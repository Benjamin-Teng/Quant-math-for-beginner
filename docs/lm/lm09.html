Linear Systems, Inverses and Rank

One system read four ways, by rows, columns, determinants and elimination, and why an exact fit on three days proves nothing.

Learning Module Overview
  • A linear system Mx=b is a set of lines or planes that must meet (rows) and a combination of columns that must reach b (columns). On 8 and 9 April 2025, 1.035 of the Semiconductor index and 0.207 of the FinancialInsurance index reproduce TAIEX's returns on both days.
  • A 2×2 determinant is the signed area spanned by the two columns: 24.753 for columns 47.15° apart, and 2.7813 for a pair only 1.52° apart, whose system needs multiples of −3.48 and 5.12.
  • Cramer's rule, xj​=detMj​(b)/detM, follows from the linearity of the determinant in each column. With cofactor expansions it needs (n+1)! terms, each a product of n entries, against about 32​n3 operations for elimination: about 5.1×1019 terms against 5,333 operations at n=20.
  • Gaussian elimination reduces the 7–9 April system to triangular form. Its exact pivots, −9.95, 4.495940 and 1.600370 to six decimals, multiply to the determinant −71.591922. NumPy's solve runs the same elimination, with row swaps.
  • The inverse exists exactly when detM=0, when rankM=n and when only x=0 solves Mx=0. In code solve comes first; inv serves concepts such as LM7's Kelly fractions Σ−1(μ−rf​1)=(5.59,−0.52)⊤.
  • A system has a solution exactly when rankM=rank[M∣b], and its solutions are then xp​+nullM. With three sectors, two days give a line of mixes, three days exactly one and four days none.
  • Any three days with a non-zero determinant fit exactly, so an exact fit carries no evidence. FinancialInsurance's multiple runs from −1.479 to 2.069 across the four triples of days, and the 7–9 April mix gives +10.17% for 10 April, against +9.25%.
  • A return matrix has rank below its number of columns exactly when a fixed non-zero combination of the columns is 0 on every date. A daily-rebalanced 50/50 mix of Semiconductor and FinancialInsurance creates one (rank 2); a buy-and-hold mix, whose Semiconductor weight drifts from 0.50 to 0.858, does not (rank 3).

1Introduction

A replicating mix asks which multiples of a few assets reproduce a target's returns. Each day gives one linear equation in the multiples, so a few days give a linear system. This module solves such systems four ways and builds the inverse and the rank from them. It also shows why an exact fit on a few days says almost nothing.

Motivating CaseThree sectors, three days

On 7 April 2025, after the 3–4 April holidays, TAIEX fell 9.70%, its largest daily move in data back to July 2007. It then moved −4.02%, −5.79% and +9.25% on 8, 9 and 10 April. Is there one mix of the Semiconductor, FinancialInsurance and Food sector indices whose returns reproduce TAIEX's on each day (Exhibit 1)?

With two days there are infinitely many such mixes. With 7–9 April there is exactly one: 0.334 of Semiconductor, 1.447 of FinancialInsurance and −0.916 of Food, which sum to 0.865. With 8–10 April the mix becomes (−0.018,2.069,−1.376)⊤. With all four days there is none, and the 7–9 April mix gives +10.17% on 10 April, not +9.25%. Why does a perfect fit say so little?

Sections 2–6 draw the two pictures of a system, build the determinant, Cramer's rule, elimination and the inverse, and solve the case. Section 7 counts solutions with the rank, and Section 8 turns rank into perfect collinearity of returns.

2Two Pictures of a Linear System

write a linear system Mx=b by rows and by columns, and read its solutions from the row picture and the column picture

Start with two days and two unknowns. Which multiple x1​ of Semiconductor and x2​ of FinancialInsurance reproduce TAIEX's returns on 8 and 9 April? Each day gives one equation:

−3.99x1​+0.53x2​−4.47x1​−5.61x2​​=−4.02=−5.79​​(8 April),(9 April).​
DefinitionLinear system

A linear system of m equations in n unknowns is Mx=b, with M an m×n matrix, x in Rn and b in Rm. Row i is the equation ∑j​mij​xj​=bi​. The augmented matrix [M∣b] appends b to M as a last column.

The row picture. With two unknowns, each equation is a line in the (x1​,x2​) plane, and a solution lies on every line at once. Here the two lines meet in one point (Exhibit 2). With three unknowns each equation is a plane, and three planes usually meet in one point.

The column picture. Read Mx by columns instead, as LM8 (8.12) read a return matrix times a weight vector:

Mx=x1​m1​+x2​m2​+⋯+xn​mn​.(9.1)

The system asks which combination of the columns reaches b. In the case, column j holds sector j's returns on the chosen days. So a solution is a mix of sectors whose returns equal TAIEX's day by day (Exhibit 3).

Example 1Two days, two sectors, two pictures

Solve the 8–9 April system above, and check the answer in both pictures.

Solution

Eliminate one unknown at a time. Adding 5.61 times the first equation to 0.53 times the second removes x2​, with

−3.99(5.61)−4.47(0.53)−4.02(5.61)−5.79(0.53)​=−24.753,=−25.6209,​
so −24.753x1​=−25.6209 and x1​=1.035062. Adding 4.47 times the first equation to −3.99 times the second removes x1​ and gives 24.753x2​=5.1327, so x2​=0.207357. Both divisions use 24.753, which Section 3 names the determinant.

In the row picture the two lines cross at (1.035062,0.207357). In the column picture,

1.035062m1​+0.207357m2​​=(−4.02−5.79​)=b.​
So 1.035 of Semiconductor and 0.207 of FinancialInsurance reproduce TAIEX's returns on both days.

Two lines in a plane can meet in one point, be parallel or coincide. So a 2×2 system has exactly one solution, none or infinitely many (Exhibit 4). Section 7 proves that the same three cases cover every linear system.

導讀兩種讀法：一列一個方程，一行一個產業

一個線性方程組（linear system）可以按列讀，也可以按行讀。按列讀，每一天是一條直線（三個未知數時是一個平面），解就是所有直線的交點。按行讀，每一行是一個產業指數在這幾天的報酬，解就是用這幾個產業「組合」出 TAIEX 報酬的倍數，也就是一個線性組合（linear combination）。兩條直線可能交於一點、平行或重合，所以解可能剛好一個、沒有或無限多個。

Knowledge Check 1

A 2×2 system has no solution. What do its two lines look like in the row picture, and where does b lie in the column picture?

Answer

The lines are parallel and distinct, so no point lies on both. In the column picture both columns lie on one line through the origin, and b lies off it, so no combination reaches b.

3The Determinant

calculate 2×2 and 3×3 determinants by cofactor expansion and by row operations, and interpret a determinant as a signed area or volume

The number 24.753 that both divisions of Example 1 used is m11​m22​−m12​m21​. It is the signed area that the two columns span. It is 0 exactly when they lie on one line, but its size also depends on their lengths (see the pitfall below).

DefinitionDeterminant of a 2×2 matrix

For a 2×2 matrix M, the determinant is

detM:=m11​m22​−m12​m21​.(9.2)
The absolute value of detM is the area ∥m1​∥∥m2​∥sinθ of the parallelogram spanned by the columns. The sign is positive when m2​ lies counter-clockwise from m1​.

The area follows from LM8's dot product. Expanding both sides shows

(detM)2+(m1⊤​m2​)2=∥m1​∥2∥m2​∥2,

and m1⊤​m2​=∥m1​∥∥m2​∥cosθ, so ∣detM∣=∥m1​∥∥m2​∥sinθ. The determinant is 0 exactly when the columns lie on one line: θ is 0° or 180°, or a column is 0.

Example 2Area and nearly parallel columns

Find the determinant of Example 1 and the angle between its columns. Then solve the system of 7 and 9 April with Semiconductor and Food, and compare.

(−9.95−4.47​−8.66−4.17​)(x1​x2​​)=(−9.70−5.79​)

Solution

For Example 1,

detM​=(−3.99)(−5.61)−(0.53)(−4.47)=22.3839+2.3691=24.753.​
The columns have lengths 5.991744 and 5.634980, so sinθ=24.753/33.7634=0.7331. The dot product of the columns, 22.962, is positive, so θ is acute and equals 47.15°.

For Semiconductor and Food, detM=41.4915−38.7102=2.7813. The columns are longer, 10.907951 and 9.611686, but sinθ=2.7813/104.8438=0.0265. Their dot product, 104.8069, is also positive, so they lie only 1.52° apart (Exhibit 5). Eliminating as in Example 1 gives x1​=−9.6924/2.7813=−3.48 and x2​=14.2515/2.7813=5.12. To reproduce TAIEX with two nearly parallel columns, the mix shorts 3.48 of Semiconductor against 5.12 of Food.

For larger matrices the cofactor expansion defines the determinant step by step, along the first row:

detM=j=1∑n​(−1)1+jm1j​detM(1j).

Each (−1)i+jdetM(ij) is a cofactor. Expanding along any other row or column gives the same value. For n=3 the absolute value is the volume of the box spanned by the columns (both stated without proof; Strang 2016).

TheoremRow operations and the determinant

Let M and K be n×n matrices.

  1. Swapping two rows changes the sign of the determinant.
  2. Multiplying one row by a number a multiplies the determinant by a.
  3. Adding a multiple of one row to another leaves the determinant unchanged.
  4. The determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries.

Moreover,

det(MK)=detMdetK,detM⊤=detM,detM−1=1/detM,​(9.3)
the last when M−1 exists. Rules 1–4 are stated without proof (Strang 2016); the Deep Dive below derives (9.3) from them.

Since detM⊤=detM, the rules hold for columns too. If one column is a combination of the others, the columns are dependent (Section 7 defines independence). Subtracting that combination (rule 3) then leaves a zero column, so detM=0. A square matrix with detM=0 is singular; Section 5 shows that its columns are dependent.

Example 3The determinant of the 7–9 April system

With rows 7, 8 and 9 April and columns Semiconductor, FinancialInsurance and Food, the augmented matrix [M∣b] of the case is

​−9.95−3.99−4.47​−9.890.53−5.61​−8.663.77−4.17​−9.70−4.02−5.79​​.
Compute detM by cofactor expansion along the first row.

Solution

The three minors are

detM(11)detM(12)​=0.53(−4.17)−3.77(−5.61)=18.9396,=3.99(4.17)+3.77(4.47)=33.4902,​
and detM(13)=24.753, the determinant of Example 1. So
detM​=−9.95(18.9396)+9.89(33.4902)−8.66(24.753)=−71.591922,​
the sum of −188.44902, 331.218078 and −214.36098. The determinant is not 0, so the three columns are independent, and Section 5 finds exactly one mix.

PitfallA small determinant does not mean nearly dependent columns

The determinant scales with the entries: det(0.1I9​)=10−9, yet the system 0.1x=b has the plain solution x=10b. Judge a determinant against the column lengths instead: 24.753 against 33.76 in Example 2 is far from 0, and 2.7813 against 104.84 is close. LM11 measures this closeness with the condition number, which tells how strongly a nearly singular matrix magnifies small errors.

Deep DiveThe product rule (9.3)optional · click to expand

A row operation on K is a product EK, where the elementary matrix E is In​ with the same operation applied. By rules 1–3, det(EK)=detEdetK. If M is invertible, Gauss–Jordan elimination (Section 6) writes it as a product E1​E2​⋯ of elementary matrices. Applying the rule once per factor gives det(MK)=detE1​detE2​⋯detK=detMdetK.

If M is singular, so is MK, whose columns combine those of M; both sides are 0. For invertible M, transposing gives M⊤=⋯E2⊤​E1⊤​, and transposing an elementary matrix keeps its determinant, so detM⊤=detM. If M is singular, its columns are dependent (Section 5). They are the rows of M⊤, so rule 3 turns one of these rows into a zero row, and detM⊤=0=detM. Finally, detMdetM−1=detIn​=1 (Strang 2016).

4Cramer's Rule

solve small systems with Cramer's rule, and compare its cost with that of elimination

With the other columns held fixed, the determinant is linear in each column, as the cofactor expansion along that column shows. It is 0 when two columns are equal, because swapping them changes the sign but not the matrix. Now suppose Mx=b, and replace column j of M by b=∑k​xk​mk​. By linearity in column j,

detMj​(b)=k∑​xk​detMj​(mk​)=xj​detM,

because for k=j the matrix Mj​(mk​) holds column mk​ twice.

TheoremCramer's rule

If M is n×n and detM=0, the system Mx=b has exactly one solution, with entries

xj​=detMdetMj​(b)​,j=1,…,n.(9.4)
The derivation above shows that no other solution exists; the Deep Dive of Section 6 shows that (9.4) solves the system.

Example 4Cramer's rule for the 7–9 April mix

Solve the system of Example 3 by (9.4).

Solution

Replacing each column of M in turn by b and expanding as in Example 3 gives detM1​(b)=−23.919201, detM2​(b)=−103.581657 and detM3​(b)=65.586258. Dividing each by detM=−71.591922,

x=(0.334105, 1.446834, −0.916113)⊤.
The mix holds 0.334 of Semiconductor, 1.447 of FinancialInsurance and −0.916 of Food, and its multiples sum to 0.865, not 1. LM7 (7.12) is the same rule for two Kelly fractions. Each numerator there is the determinant of the covariance matrix with one column replaced by the excess returns.

Cramer's rule is exact and short to write, but slow to compute. A cofactor expansion of an n×n determinant has n! terms, each a product of n entries, and (9.4) needs n+1 determinants: (n+1)! terms. Each term alone takes n−1 multiplications, so the number of terms understates the work. Elimination needs about 32​n3 operations (Section 5 counts them). At n=20 the term count alone is about 5.1×1019, against about 5,333 operations for elimination (Exhibit 6).

導讀行列式與克拉瑪法則

2×2 行列式（determinant）就是兩個行向量（column vector）張成的平行四邊形（parallelogram）面積，正負號表示方向；3×3 時是平行六面體（parallelepiped）的體積。行列式為 0，表示某一行是其他行的線性組合（linear combination），方程組不會剛好只有一個解。克拉瑪法則（Cramer's rule）把每個未知數寫成兩個行列式的比，公式漂亮，二元、三元時手算很方便。但 n 個未知數要算 n+1 個行列式、共 (n+1)! 項，n=20 時約 5×1019 項，消去法（elimination）只要約五千次運算。行列式很小也不代表接近奇異（singular），要和行向量的長度比較，LM11 用條件數（condition number）衡量。

Knowledge Check 3

How many products of four entries does (9.4) need for four unknowns when every determinant is a cofactor expansion?

Answer

Five determinants of 4!=24 terms each make 120 products. Formed one by one at three multiplications each, they take 360 multiplications. A nested cofactor expansion reuses partial products and needs 40 multiplications per determinant, 200 in all.

5Gaussian Elimination

solve a system by Gaussian elimination with back substitution and row swaps, and compute its determinant from the pivots

Three operations on the equations leave the solutions of a system unchanged, because each can be undone. They swap two equations, multiply one by a non-zero number, or add a multiple of one equation to another. Gaussian elimination uses them to make the matrix upper triangular, with zeros below the diagonal. Back substitution then solves the last equation first and works upward.

At step k the pivot dk​ is the diagonal entry of row k. For each row i below it, the multiplier ℓik​ is that row's entry in column k divided by dk​. Subtracting ℓik​ times row k from row i puts a zero there.

Step k changes the (n−k)2 entries below and to the right of the pivot, each with one multiplication and one subtraction. Summing over the steps,

k=1∑n−1​2(n−k)2=32​n3−n2+31​n≈32​n3

operations, with an error of order n2; the right-hand side and back substitution add terms of the same order.

If a pivot is 0, swap its row with a lower row that has a non-zero entry in that column. Library code goes further. At every step it brings into the pivot position the row whose entry in the pivot column is largest in absolute value. This partial pivoting keeps every multiplier between −1 and 1, which limits the growth of rounding errors (Golub and Van Loan 2013, §3.4).

Elimination also delivers the determinant. Subtracting multiples of rows leaves it unchanged (rule 3), each swap flips its sign (rule 1), and the final matrix is triangular (rule 4). So

detM=±d1​d2​⋯dn​,

with a minus sign when the number of swaps is odd. With independent columns, elimination always finds a non-zero pivot. Otherwise the current column, zero at and below the pivot row, would be a combination of the earlier columns. So detM=0 for independent columns, the converse promised in Section 3.

Example 5Elimination on the 7–9 April system

Solve the system of Example 3 by elimination, and check its determinant.

Solution

The first pivot is d1​=−9.95. The multipliers ℓ21​=−3.99/(−9.95)=0.401005 and ℓ31​=−4.47/(−9.95)=0.449246 clear column 1. Then the exact multiplier ℓ32​=−0.259557 clears column 2 (Exhibit 7). Dividing the rounded entry −1.166955 by the rounded pivot 4.495940 gives −0.259558 instead. No swap is needed, and partial pivoting would choose the same rows. Back substitution in exact arithmetic gives

x3​x2​x1​​=1.600370−1.466119​=−0.916113,=4.495940−0.130251−7.242704x3​​=1.446834,=−9.95−9.70+9.89x2​+8.66x3​​=0.334105,​
as in Example 4. With the rounded entries and the printed x3​ and x2​, the same steps give x2​=1.446835 and x1​=0.334106. The exact pivots multiply to −71.591922=detM.

NumPy's np.linalg.solve(M, b) runs this algorithm, with partial pivoting. It calls LAPACK's gesv, which stores the multipliers and the triangular result as the LU factorization of M and then substitutes (LAPACK, dgesv).

6The Inverse

compute an inverse with the 2×2 formula and by Gauss–Jordan elimination, and apply the product and transpose rules
DefinitionInverse

An n×n matrix M is invertible if some n×n matrix K satisfies MK=KM=In​. That K is unique and is written M−1.

If K and K2​ both qualify, then K=K(MK2​)=(KM)K2​=K2​, so the inverse is unique. When it exists, the system Mx=b has the one solution x=M−1b. For a 2×2 matrix with detM=0, multiplying out checks

M−1=detM1​(m22​−m21​​−m12​m11​​).(9.5)

For larger matrices, Gauss–Jordan elimination row-reduces [M∣In​] until the left block is In​; the right block is then M−1. It solves the n systems Mkj​=ej​ at once, whose solutions kj​ are the columns of M−1. For invertible M and K, two rules follow from the definition:

(MK)−1=K−1M−1,(M⊤)−1=(M−1)⊤.​

The first holds because MK(K−1M−1)=In​ and (K−1M−1)MK=In​. The second follows from transposing MM−1=M−1M=In​ with LM8 (8.15).

For an n×n matrix M the following are equivalent:

M is invertible​⟺detM=0⟺rankM=n⟺nullM={0}.​(9.6)

Here the rank is the number of independent columns, and the null space is the set of solutions of Mx=0 (Section 7). An inverse turns Mx=0 into x=0. Independent columns give n non-zero pivots and detM=0 (Section 5). The Deep Dive below builds the inverse when detM=0.

The inverse also tells how the answer moves with the data. Since x=M−1b, column j of M−1 is the change in the mix when TAIEX's return on day j rises by one point (Exhibit 8). Had TAIEX fallen 8.70% on 7 April, not 9.70%, the Semiconductor multiple would fall by 0.265 and the FinancialInsurance one rise by 0.468.

Example 6LM7's Kelly fractions through the inverse

LM7 estimated, for 0050 and 00679B, an annualized covariance matrix with σ12​=0.040425, σ22​=0.018214 and σ12​=−0.002866, and excess returns μ−rf​1=(0.22751,−0.02547)⊤ over an illustrative rf​=1.5%. Invert Σ with (9.5) and compute the Kelly fractions Σ−1(μ−rf​1).

Solution

The determinant is

The inverse is a concept first. To solve one system, np.linalg.solve factors M once and applies the elimination to b alone. np.linalg.inv applies it to all n columns of In​, so it costs more. Its M−1b also has weaker error bounds.

Solving through the inverse is not backward stable: its answer need not be the exact solution of a slightly changed system. So the residual Mx−b can be larger, although x itself is usually about as accurate (Higham 2002a, ch. 14). Use inv for concepts such as Σ−1; solve also takes many right-hand sides at once.

導讀消去法與反矩陣

高斯消去法（Gaussian elimination）用三種不改變解的列運算（row operation），把方程組化成上三角矩陣（upper triangular matrix），再從最後一列往上解回去，也就是後置法（back substitution）。每一步的對角元素是樞軸元（pivot），樞軸元相乘（加上換列的正負號）就是行列式（determinant）。NumPy 的 np.linalg.solve 做的正是這件事，而且每一步把絕對值最大的列換上來，以控制捨入誤差（rounding error）。反矩陣（inverse matrix）主要是概念工具：x=M−1b 說明解如何隨資料變動，LM7 的凱利比例就是 Σ−1(μ−rf​1)。實際解題時先用 solve，不要先求反矩陣再相乘。

Knowledge Check 5

Why is np.linalg.inv(M) @ b the wrong habit for solving one system?

Answer

Computing inv(M) @ b does more work and gives up backward stability. inv applies elimination to the n columns of In​ and then multiplies, while solve applies it to b alone. The result can have a larger residual, although x itself is usually about as accurate (Higham 2002a, ch. 14).

Deep DiveThe adjugate formula M−1=adjM/detMoptional · click to expand

Let adjM be the n×n matrix whose entry (j,i) is the cofactor (−1)i+jdetM(ij). Entry (i,i) of MadjM is ∑j​mij​(−1)i+jdetM(ij), the cofactor expansion of detM along row i. For k=i, entry (i,k) expands the matrix whose row k is replaced by row i, which has two equal rows, so it is 0. Hence MadjM=(detM)In​. Expanding along columns gives (adjM)M=(detM)In​ in the same way, so when detM=0,

M−1=detMadjM​.

For n=2 this is (9.5). Entry j of adjMb expands detMj​(b) along column j, so x=M−1b is (9.4), which therefore solves the system (Strang 2016).

7Rank and the Number of Solutions

determine the rank, column space and null space of a matrix, and classify a system as having no, one or infinitely many solutions
DefinitionIndependence, column space, null space and rank

Vectors m1​,…,mn​ are linearly independent if x1​m1​+⋯+xn​mn​=0 only for x=0. For an m×n matrix M, the column space colM is the set of all Mx. The null space nullM is the set of all x with Mx=0. The rank rankM is the largest number of independent columns. The dimension of a space of vectors is the largest number of independent vectors in it, so rankM=dimcolM.

Elimination works on any m×n matrix. When a column has no non-zero entry at or below the current row, it gets no pivot, and elimination moves to the next column. The result is an echelon form: each row starts further right than the row above, and zero rows come last. The pivot columns are independent and the other columns are combinations of them, so the rank is the number of pivots.

Each column without a pivot holds a free variable: give it any value, and back substitution finds the rest. With b=0, setting one free variable to 1 and the others to 0 gives a null-space vector xh​. These vectors are independent and span nullM, one for each free column, so

rankM+dimnullM=n.(9.7)

A system is solvable exactly when b lies in colM. In echelon form, the entries of the transformed b beside zero rows of M must then be 0: appending b adds no pivot. If xp​ is one solution and Mxh​=0, then M(xp​+xh​)=b, and two solutions always differ by a null-space vector. Hence, for the system Mx=b,

solvable⟺rankM=rank[M∣b],solutions=xp​+nullM.​(9.8)

By (9.7), a solvable system has exactly one solution when rankM=n, and infinitely many when rankM<n.

NoteResearch hygiene

Each square fit is an in-sample exact fit: when detM=0 it always succeeds, so it carries no evidence about how TAIEX is built. Comparing the four triples is a stability check, and the mix fails it. The 10 April comparison is one illustration, not an out-of-sample test. Section 8's ranks, weights and correlation are descriptive: they summarize 2008–2026 and forecast nothing.

With more days than sectors there is usually no exact solution; LM12 finds the multiples that miss by the least. Fitting over hundreds of days is a regression (LM23).

PitfallOne instrument twice: TX and MTX

Adding the Mini-TAIEX future (MTX) to TX adds no new direction. Both settle on TAIEX, and TX pays NT$200 per index point against MTX's NT$50 (TAIFEX contract specifications; LM2). Per point of the index, one TX contract moves exactly as four MTX contracts. For the same index-point moves, their columns of daily P&L per contract are proportional (200 against 50), so they have rank 1.

A TX-plus-MTX hedge is a line of answers, xp​+x2​(−41​,1)⊤, not a better hedge. This follows from the contract terms, not from settlement data (Problem 9).

Deep DiveRow rank equals column rankoptional · click to expand

The row rank is the largest number of independent rows. A row operation EM, with E invertible, keeps all combinations of the rows and hence the row rank. It also keeps every relation among the columns, since EMx=0 exactly when Mx=0, and hence the column rank. In echelon form both ranks equal the number of pivots (Strang 2016).

8Perfect Collinearity in Returns, and Python

identify perfect collinearity in a matrix of returns, and explain why a daily-rebalanced mix creates it and a buy-and-hold mix does not
solve and test linear systems in NumPy with solve, inv, det and matrix_rank, and interpret their floating-point output

Now let the rows be dates and the columns assets, as in the T×N return matrix X of LM8. Its columns are dependent exactly when some fixed non-zero combination of the columns is 0 on every date, a case called perfect collinearity. By (9.7),

rankX<N⟺Xx=0  for some x=0.(9.9)

Take the daily returns of Semiconductor and FinancialInsurance on the Volume II sector sample: T=4,382 returns from 2 December 2008 to 8 October 2026. Add a 50/50 mix of the two as a third column. A mix rebalanced daily to 50/50 returns 21​RS,t​+21​RF,t​ on every date. So x=(−21​,−21​,1)⊤ solves Xx=0, and the rank is 2.

A mix bought and held from the close of 1 December 2008 lets its weights drift with prices. Its return on date t is wt−1​RS,t​+(1−wt−1​)RF,t​, where wt−1​ is Semiconductor's weight at the previous close. That weight rose from 0.50 to 0.858 by 8 October 2026 (Exhibit 12). Along the way it fell to 0.457 on 4 August 2011 and peaked at 0.883 on 25 May 2026. No fixed x matches a changing weight, so the rank is 3.

Real indices are nearly collinear, not exactly. TWSE's Electronic index covers the stocks of its eight electronics sub-industries (TWSE 2007). Like every sector index it weights each stock by its market value (TWSE 2023), so its weights drift with prices (LM27). The 4,382×9 matrix of Electronic and its eight sub-industries therefore has full rank 9, although Electronic's correlation with Semiconductor alone is 0.952591.

LM11 measures how close such columns come to dependence with the condition number, and LM12 decides when to treat them as dependent. Volume II's sector set U28 holds the 28 leaf indices among the 30 TWSE sector indices that FinMind serves daily. It excludes the aggregates Electronic and ChemicalBiotechnologyMedicalCare, and its return matrix has rank 28.

Example 9A mix column on 7–9 April

Replace Food in Example 3 by the daily-rebalanced 50/50 mix of Semiconductor and FinancialInsurance, m3​=(m1​+m2​)/2, which is (−9.92,−1.73,−5.04)⊤. Find the rank, the null space and the solutions.

Solution

The third column is 21​m1​+21​m2​, so Mxh​=0 for xh​=(−21​,−21​,1)⊤ and detM=0. Elimination finds two pivots, so rankM=2 and nullM is the line through xh​. On 8 and 9 April only Example 1's combination of m1​ and m2​ matches b, and it gives −12.35% on 7 April, not −9.70%. So rank[M∣b]=3, and by (9.8) there is no solution.

Floating point keeps about 16 significant digits (LM2), so an exact 0 rarely survives. For the matrix of Example 9, np.linalg.det may print about 10−14 rather than 0. solve may return entries of order 1015 along xh​ without an error, because LAPACK stops only at an exactly zero pivot. matrix_rank applies a tolerance (LM12) and reports 2. So test singularity with the rank, never with det(M) == 0.

Python
Mmix = np.column_stack([M[:, 0], M[:, 1], (M[:, 0] + M[:, 1]) / 2])
print(np.linalg.matrix_rank(Mmix))        # 2: a non-zero combination is 0
print(abs(np.linalg.det(Mmix)) < 1e-10)   # True: det is tiny but may not be 0.0
four = np.vstack([M, [9.91, 8.36, 5.72]])           # add 10 April
Mb = np.column_stack([four, [-9.70, -4.02, -5.79, 9.25]])
print(np.linalg.matrix_rank(four), np.linalg.matrix_rank(Mb))   # 3 4
# np.linalg.solve(four, ...) raises LinAlgError:

Summary

  • A linear system Mx=b reads by rows, as lines or planes that meet, and by columns (9.1), as a mix that reaches b. On 8 and 9 April, 1.035 of Semiconductor and 0.207 of FinancialInsurance reproduce TAIEX.
  • The determinant (9.2) is the signed area or volume of the columns and is multiplicative (9.3). It is 0 exactly when the columns are dependent; judge a small value against their lengths.
  • Cramer's rule (9.4) solved the 7–9 April system, x=(0.334105,1.446834,−0.916113)⊤, with (n+1)! terms against about 32​n3 operations for elimination.
  • Elimination with back substitution and partial pivoting is what np.linalg.solve runs. Its pivots multiply to ±detM, here −71.591922.
  • The inverse (9.5) exists exactly under (9.6). It explains answers, such as LM7's Σ−1(μ−rf​1)=(5.59,−0.52)⊤, while solve computes them.
  • Rank, (9.7) and (9.8) separate no, one and infinitely many solutions: four, three and two days of the case.
  • An exact fit on three days carries no evidence. FinancialInsurance's multiple ran from −1.479 to 2.069 across the four triples, and the 7–9 April mix gave +10.17% for 10 April, against +9.25%.
  • A return matrix loses rank exactly when a fixed non-zero combination of its columns is 0 on every date (9.9). A column holding a daily-rebalanced mix creates one; a buy-and-hold mix does not. Floating point can turn the zero determinant of Example 9 into about 10−14, so test with matrix_rank.
