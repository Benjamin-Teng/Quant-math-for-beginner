Descriptive Statistics of Returns

A statistic of returns is a choice of divisor, quantile rule and period. On 2003–2025, TAIEX's days scale to an annual volatility of 18.56%, its calendar years give 22.21%, and the same years cut at other days give anything from 11.89% to 25.04%.

Learning Module Overview
  • TAIEX's day has five centres: mean 0.0625%, median 0.0879%, 5% trimmed mean 0.0832%, compound rate 0.055185% and mean log return 0.055170%. The median and the trimmed mean sit above the mean because large falls outnumber large rises.
  • The standard deviation divides by T (s=1.2084%) or by T−1 (su​=1.2085%), (19.1). The normal-equivalent robust scales of (19.2) give 0.873% and 0.868%: the typical day moves like a 13.7% annual volatility, against 18.96% from s.
  • Skewness and kurtosis, (19.3), hang on a few days. One more −10% day moves TR23's skewness from −0.338 to −0.425 and its excess kurtosis from 5.27 to 5.88, while the quantile measures of (19.4) move by 1.2% and 0.05%.
  • Daily log returns barely correlate with their past (ρ^​1​=0.0393, (19.5)), but absolute log returns correlate between 0.096 and 0.217 at every lag up to 30, far outside the iid band of ±0.0256, (19.6).
  • The variance of a sum of m days depends on every autocorrelation up to lag m−1, (19.7). The overlapping variance ratio of (19.8) is 1.208 at one year and 0.619 at five years on TR23, against 0.958 and 0.790 expected for iid days.
  • Means of log returns annualize by A exactly; their variances, volatilities and Sharpe ratios by A or A​ only for uncorrelated days, (19.9). On simple returns these rules are conventions: TR23 averages 15.38% a year, with volatility 18.96% and Sharpe ratio 0.0517 a day or 0.81 a year, with rf​=0.
  • On 2003–2025, TAIEX's annual volatility is 18.56% from days, 19.05% from overlapping one-year sums and 22.21% from calendar years. Cut into one-year blocks from other days, the same 23 years give 11.89% to 25.04%: most of the gap reflects how few years there are.

1Introduction

Volume III treated returns as random variables; this module describes them as data. Each statistic of a return series is a recipe: which returns, which divisor, which quantile rule, which period. The case shows how far one annual figure moves with the recipe.

Motivating CaseHow volatile is a year of TAIEX?

Take the TAIEX total-return index, with dividends reinvested and no costs, from 3 January 2003 to 31 December 2025: 5,664 daily log returns. Their standard deviation is 1.18352% a day (LM17). A year has about 246 sessions, so independent days would give a yearly standard deviation of 246​×1.18352%=18.56%. Weekly and monthly log returns, scaled to a year the same way, give 18.09% and 18.12% (Exhibit 1).

Yet the 23 calendar-year log returns have a standard deviation of 22.21% (LM15). To reach it, the variance of a year's sum would have to be 1.43 times the sum of its daily variances. Do daily variances fail to add up over a year? Or do 23 numbers pin down a standard deviation too loosely to tell?

Sections 2 to 4 describe one day, Sections 5 and 6 how days depend on each other and add up. Section 7 annualizes and resolves the case, and Section 8 turns it all into Python.

NoteNotation for this module

TR23 is the TAIEX total-return index: T=5,849 daily returns (a count), 3 January 2003 to 8 October 2026, over 23.7645 years from the first close, so A=246.12. Simple returns are Rt​, log returns rt​=ln(1+Rt​). Sample moments Rˉ, s, rˉ, sr​ divide by T and su​ by T−1, (19.1); model moments are μ, σ, g, σr​. Quantiles are F^−1(u)=R(⌈uT⌉)​ and med:=F^−1(21​) (LM14); LM5's μ^​ is Rˉ.

Counts: j a rank, k a lag, m the days in a sum. The model's ratio in (19.7) gets no symbol, so that VR(m) keeps one meaning.

NoteResearch hygiene

Every TAIEX figure describes TR23, unless the text or a caption names a limit regime or 2003–2025 (LM15's and LM17's calendar years). The figures are descriptive, without standard errors, which are LM20's subject.

The normal law with the sample's own mean and standard deviation is in-sample. The normal-law constants of (19.2), the iid band and expectation, and experiment 1 are model properties, and the extra −10% day is hypothetical. The limit regimes, and 2003–2025 against TR23, are stability checks. Nothing is tested (LM21, LM25).

2Where Returns Centre

calculate the mean, median, trimmed mean, compound rate and mean log return of a return series, and explain why they differ

A centre is one number that stands for a whole series. Returns have several, and in a week of extreme days they disagree.

Example 1The tariff week

TR23's returns on 7–11 April 2025, the week after the United States announced new tariffs (The White House 2025), were −9.6997%, −4.0161%, −5.7865%, +9.2512% and +2.7842%. With a standard deviation of 6.72%, this was the most volatile of TR23's 1,224 ISO weeks. Find the week's mean, median, 20% trimmed mean, compound daily rate and mean log return.

Solution

The sum is −7.4669%, so the mean is −1.493%. Sorted, the returns are −9.6997%, −5.7865%, −4.0161%, +2.7842% and +9.2512%, so the median is the third, −4.016%. The 20% trimmed mean drops one return at each end and averages the middle three: −2.339%.

The growth factor is ∏t​(1+Rt​)=0.91697, a loss of 8.303%. By (1.9) in days, RG​=0.916971/5−1=−1.719%, and by (1.12) the mean log return is rˉ=51​ln0.91697=−1.734%. Every centre answers a different question.

DefinitionMean, median and trimmed mean

Let R(1)​≤⋯≤R(T)​ be the sorted returns. The sample mean is Rˉ=T1​∑t​Rt​, and the sample median is med:=F^−1(21​)=R(⌈T/2⌉)​ (LM14). For a count 0≤j0​<T/2, the trimmed mean drops the j0​ smallest and the j0​ largest returns and averages the rest:

T−2j0​1​j=j0​+1∑T−j0​​R(j)​.
The 5% trimmed mean takes j0​=⌊0.05T⌋.

A statistic is robust when a few observations cannot move it far. Raising the largest return by any amount moves the mean without limit, but leaves the median and a trimmed mean unchanged.

Two more centres concern growth. By (1.12), lnG=∑t​rt​=Trˉ, so the compound daily rate of (1.9) is RG​=G1/T−1=erˉ−1, slightly above rˉ. Under F^, (14.12) gives RG​≤Rˉ, and by (3.9) the gap is about s2/2, the volatility drag of LM3.

Example 2Five centres of TAIEX's day

Compute TR23's mean, median, 5% trimmed mean, compound daily rate and mean log return, and explain their order.

Solution

The mean is Rˉ=0.062505%, or 0.0625%, and the median is the 2,925th return, 0.0879% (LM14). The 5% trimmed mean drops j0​=⌊0.05×5,849⌋=292 returns at each end and gives 0.0832%. For growth, rˉ=0.055170%, and LM14's compound rate is RG​=erˉ−1=0.055185%. Exhibit 3 lists the first three.

The median and the trimmed mean sit above the mean because large falls outnumber large rises, 108 against 67 beyond ±3% (LM15). The compound rate sits below the mean by the drag: 0.062505%−0.055185%=0.7320 bp, against s2/2=0.7301 bp with s=1.2084%.

PitfallA robust centre answers another question

On a skewed law the median and a trimmed mean differ from the mean even without sampling noise, because they describe other points of the distribution. Growth and the Kelly fraction (LM4) depend on the mean. Use a robust centre to describe the typical day, not to replace Rˉ in a formula.

Knowledge Check 1

In Example 1, lower the first return by 10 points, from −9.6997% to −19.6997%. What happens to the mean, the median and the 20% trimmed mean?

Answer

The mean falls by 10/5 = 2 points, to −3.493%. The changed return is still the lowest, so the median (−4.016%) and the 20% trimmed mean (−2.339%) do not move.

3How Far Returns Spread

calculate the standard deviation with divisor T and T−1, the mean absolute deviation, the interquartile range and the median absolute deviation, and convert the last two into normal-equivalent volatilities

Spread is measured around a centre: by squared deviations, by absolute deviations or by quantiles. The book's standard deviation divides by T, s2=T1​∑t​(Rt​−Rˉ)2, the variance of F^ (LM14). Software often divides by T−1 instead:

su2​:=T−1T​s2.(19.1)

LM20 shows that su2​ is an unbiased estimator of σ2 for iid draws, which is why software often uses it. NumPy's std divides by T by default (Section 8). On large samples the two barely differ: LM10 found a factor of 1.000114 at 4,382 days.

DefinitionAbsolute deviations and quartiles

The mean absolute deviation is T1​∑t​∣Rt​−Rˉ∣. The interquartile range is IQR:=F^−1(0.75)−F^−1(0.25). The median absolute deviation MAD is the median of ∣Rt​−med∣, t=1,…,T, by the same quantile rule.

To compare these scales with s, compute them for a normal law. Let R∼N(μ,σ2) and Z=(R−μ)/σ. Because zφ(z)=−φ′(z), symmetry gives E∣Z∣=2∫0∞​zφ(z)dz=2φ(0)=2/π​. The median z of ∣Z∣ solves Pr(∣Z∣≤z)=2Φ(z)−1=21​, so z=Φ−1(0.75)=0.6745, and by (15.6) the quartiles of R are μ±σΦ−1(0.75).

ResultNormal-equivalent scales

For R∼N(μ,σ2),

E∣R−μ∣=σ2/π​,F∣R−μ∣−1​(21​)=σΦ−1(0.75),IQR=2σΦ−1(0.75),σ=F∣R−μ∣−1​(21​)/Φ−1(0.75),σ=IQR/(2Φ−1(0.75)),σ≈1.4826F∣R−μ∣−1​(21​)≈IQR/1.349.​(19.2)
The constants 1/Φ−1(0.75)≈1.4826 and 2Φ−1(0.75)≈1.349 are rounded to five and four figures; the book's computations use them exactly.

On data, 1.4826MAD and IQR/1.349 are normal-equivalent scales: on a large normal sample they come out close to s.

Example 3The tariff week's spread

For Example 1's five returns, compute s, su​, the mean absolute deviation and the MAD.

Solution

From the mean, −1.4934% to four decimals, the deviations are −8.2063, −2.5227, −4.2931, +10.7446 and +4.2776 points. Their squares sum to 225.88, so s=225.88/5​=6.721% and su​=225.88/4​=7.515%, 1.118 times larger. The absolute deviations average 6.009%.

From the median −4.016%, the absolute deviations are 5.684, 0, 1.770, 13.267 and 6.800 points. Their median, the MAD, is 5.684%. With five days the divisor matters.

Example 4A typical day against the standard deviation

Compute TR23's s, su​, mean absolute deviation, IQR and MAD and the two normal-equivalent scales, and annualize the scales with A=246.12.

Solution

TR23 has s=1.2084% and su​=1.2085%. The mean absolute deviation is 0.836%, or 0.692 of s, against 2/π​=0.798 for a normal law. The quartiles are −0.4904% and 0.6812%, so IQR=1.1716%. The MAD is 0.5889%: as no two returns lie equally far from the median, exactly ⌈T/2⌉=2,925 days lie within one MAD of it.

The normal-equivalent scales are 1.4826×0.5889%=0.873% and 1.1716%/1.349=0.868%. Annualized by the A​ convention (Exhibit 7), they give 13.7% and 13.6%, against 18.96% from s. The typical day of TAIEX moves like a 13.7% volatility, and the large days add the rest.

Exhibit 2 repeats the comparison over rolling years. The three scales rise and fall together; s stands furthest above the other two when a few extreme days fall into a calm year.

None of these scales depends on the order of the days. A drawdown does, so it is a path statistic; LM32 studies it.

PitfallA robust scale leaves out the risk

The normal-equivalent scales treat the largest days almost as if they were absent. For a position, those days are the risk: a variance, a Kelly fraction (LM4) or a Sharpe ratio needs s. Quote a robust scale to describe the typical day.

導讀一般的一天與平均的一天

敘述統計（descriptive statistics）用幾個數字描述一組報酬。平均數（mean）會被極端的日子拉動；中位數（median）與截尾平均數（trimmed mean）幾乎不受少數幾天影響，屬於穩健統計（robust statistics）。離散程度（dispersion）也一樣：標準差（standard deviation）與平均絕對偏差（mean absolute deviation）讓大漲大跌的日子占很重的分量，四分位距（interquartile range）與中位數絕對離差（median absolute deviation）只看中間那一半。換算成常態分布（normal distribution）下等效的波動率（volatility），加權股價報酬指數「一般的一天」年化（annualized）約 13.7%，標準差換算卻是 18.96%，差額來自少數大波動日。樣本變異數（sample variance）改除以 T−1，是為了得到不偏估計量（unbiased estimator）；只有樣本很小時，除以 T 或 T−1 才有明顯差別。

By what factor does su​ exceed s for the five days of Example 1, and for TR23's 5,849 days?

Answer

By (19.1) the factor is T/(T−1)​: 5/4​=1.118 for five days and 5,849/5,848​=1.0000855 for TR23.

4Skewness and Kurtosis as Statistics

compute sample skewness and excess kurtosis, their adjusted versions and quantile-based measures, and judge which of them hang on a few days

LM14 defined skewness and kurtosis for a random variable, (14.5). Applied to F^ they become statistics, with a choice of divisor and a strong dependence on a few days.

DefinitionSample skewness and kurtosis

With Rˉ and s (divisor T), the sample skewness and sample kurtosis are

Skew:=s3T1​∑t​(Rt​−Rˉ)3​,Kurt:=s4T1​∑t​(Rt​−Rˉ)4​,Skewadj​:=T−2T(T−1)​​Skew,Kurtadj​:=3+(T−2)(T−3)T−1​×[(T+1)(Kurt−3)+6].​(19.3)
The sample excess kurtosis is Kurt−3; the last two are the adjusted versions.

Formula (19.3) is (14.5) under F^. It is what LM4 and LM5 computed for 0050, with μ^​=Rˉ and s, and SciPy's default (bias=True). SciPy returns the adjusted versions with bias=False; their factors tend to 1 as T grows. How far a sample skewness moves between samples would be measured by a standard error; LM20 explains why it gets none.

Example 5When the divisor matters

Compute the skewness and excess kurtosis, plain and adjusted, of the 23 calendar-year log returns of 2003–2025 and of TR23's daily returns. Then adjust LM4's 8.12 for 0050's 2,902 days.

Solution

By (19.3) the 23 years have a skewness of −0.99 and an excess kurtosis of 2.31. Adjusted, they become −1.06 and 3.22: the skewness factor is 23×22​/21=1.071, and the excess kurtosis is 21×2022​(24×2.31+6)=3.22.

TR23's daily values move by about 0.1% at most: −0.3384 to −0.3385 and 5.268 to 5.274. LM4's 8.12 becomes 8.14 at 2,902 days.

Quantiles give shape measures that a few days cannot move far.

DefinitionQuartile skewness and octile kurtosis

With quantiles F^−1(u) and IQR=F^−1(43​)−F^−1(41​),

SkewQ​KurtO​​:=IQRF^−1(43​)+F^−1(41​)−2med​,:=IQR1​[F^−1(87​)−F^−1(85​)+F^−1(83​)−F^−1(81​)].​(19.4)

The quartile skewness compares the distances from the median to the two quartiles. It lies between −1 and 1 and is 0 for a symmetric law. The octile kurtosis adds the range from the fifth to the seventh octile and the range from the first to the third, and divides by the IQR. By (15.6) it is 1.233 for every normal law; it rises when the outer octiles move away from the median or the inner ones toward it.

The regime columns of Exhibit 3 are a stability check. From the 7% limit to the 10% limit, s fell from 1.257% to 1.152% and the excess kurtosis rose from 3.39 to 8.07 (LM15). Yet the octile kurtosis fell from 1.534 to 1.420. On 7 April 2025 alone, TR23's running excess kurtosis rose from 4.25 to 4.94 (LM15).

Example 6One more limit-down day

Append a hypothetical −10% day to TR23's 5,849 returns. How far does each statistic of Exhibit 3 move?

Solution

See Exhibit 3's last column. Relative to the unrounded values, the mean falls 2.8%, the median and the trimmed mean 0.4%, and s rises 0.6%. The two robust scales move by less than 0.1%.

The skewness goes from −0.338 to −0.425 (−26%) and the excess kurtosis from 5.27 to 5.88 (+12%). The quartile skewness moves 1.2% and the octile kurtosis 0.05%. One day out of 5,850 moves the moment measures by 26% and 12%.

PitfallKurtosis measures the far tail, not the peak

A large excess kurtosis is often read as a peaked distribution. Yet the 10% regime has the larger excess kurtosis, 8.07 against 3.39, and the smaller octile kurtosis. Excess kurtosis averages fourth powers: the ten largest of TR23's 5,849 terms supply 35.0% of their sum (LM15). Quote it with its period, and beside a quantile measure.

導讀偏態與峰態是很脆弱的統計量

樣本偏態（sample skewness）與樣本峰態（sample kurtosis）把離差（deviation）取三次方、四次方（third and fourth powers）再平均，所以由最極端的幾天決定。只要多加一個假設的 −10% 交易日，樣本偏態就從 −0.338 變成 −0.425，超額峰態（excess kurtosis）從 5.27 升到 5.88。用四分位數（quartile）與八分位數（octile）算出的四分位數偏態（quartile skewness）與八分位數峰態（octile kurtosis）只看分布（distribution）的中段與肩部（shoulders），同一天只讓它們變動 1.2% 與 0.05%。兩類統計量（statistic）回答不同的問題：2015 年 6 月漲跌幅放寬後，超額峰態從 3.39 升到 8.07，八分位數峰態卻下降。除以 T 還是用調整後（adjusted）的公式，在 5,849 天裡只差約 0.1%，在 23 個年報酬上就差很多。

Knowledge Check 3

TR23's quartile skewness is +0.013, while its sample skewness is −0.338. Why can the signs differ? And why can the octile kurtosis fall from 1.534 to 1.420 between the regimes while the excess kurtosis rises from 3.39 to 8.07?

Answer

The quartile skewness compares the middle half, where the upper quartile lies slightly further from the median than the lower one. The sample skewness is decided by the largest moves, more of which are falls. Likewise the octile kurtosis weighs the shoulders and the excess kurtosis the far tail. After June 2015 the outer octiles moved closer to the quartiles, while a few days reached further.

5Autocorrelation

compute the sample autocorrelations of returns, absolute returns and squared returns under the book's convention, and read them against the iid band

Autocorrelation measures how a series relates to its own past, the order of the days that Sections 2 to 4 ignored.

Example 7Two ways to correlate a series with its past

For Example 1's five simple returns, compute the lag-1 autocorrelation by (19.5) below, and as the correlation of the four pairs (Rt−1​,Rt​). Then compare the two on TR23's log returns.

Solution

With the deviations of Example 3, (19.5) divides the sum of the four products of neighbours, 31.37, by the sum of all five squares, 225.88: ρ^​1​=0.139. The four pairs, each side with its own mean and standard deviation, correlate 0.237.

On TR23's 5,849 log returns the two agree: 0.0393 against 0.0394. For ∣rt​∣ at lag 20 they give 0.1224 against 0.1228, the 0.123 of LM17. statsmodels' acf agrees with (19.5) to within 10−15.

A series is weakly stationary when its mean, its variance and each lag covariance stay the same at every date; LM25 defines this and tests for it. Its autocovariance at lag k is γk​:=Cov(xt​,xt−k​), and its autocorrelation is ρk​:=γk​/γ0​, with γ0​=Varxt​.

DefinitionSample autocorrelation

For a series x1​,…,xT​ with mean xˉ and a lag 0≤k<T,

γ^​k​:=T1​t=k+1∑T​(xt​−xˉ)(xt−k​−xˉ),ρ^​k​:=γ^​0​γ^​k​​.​(19.5)

The book's convention uses the full-sample mean in both factors and divides by T at every lag, as statsmodels' acf does. Dividing by T rather than T−k shrinks ρ^​k​ by a factor of about (T−k)/T, which matters only at long lags or in short series.

ResultThe iid band

Let x1​,…,xT​ be iid with a finite variance, and T large. Then for each lag k≥1, ρ^​k​ is approximately normal with mean near 0 and

Varρ^​k​≈T1​,(19.6)
so about 95% of such autocorrelations lie within the iid band ±1.96/T​. The Deep Dive derives the variance. The approximate normality is stated without proof (Brockwell and Davis 1991, Theorem 7.2.2 and Example 7.2.1); it needs a central limit theorem for dependent terms, beyond LM17's (17.5).

Deep DiveWhy the iid band is ±1.96/T​optional · click to expand

Write x~t​:=xt​−Ext​, and replace xˉ by Ext​ in (19.5); the error is of order 1/T and is dropped. The T−k products x~t​x~t−k​, t=k+1,…,T, have mean 0 by (16.1). Two of them with different t are uncorrelated, because the latest date in their product appears once and has mean 0. Each has variance E[x~t2​x~t−k2​]=γ02​, again by (16.1). So by (16.3), Var(Tγ^​k​)≈(T−k)γ02​. For large T, γ^​0​≈γ0​, and Varρ^​k​≈(T−k)/T2≈1/T.

When the squares correlate, as when large moves follow large moves, the x~t​ are dependent and E[x~t2​x~t−k2​] exceeds γ02​. The band is then too narrow (LM25).

On TR23 the band is ±1.96/5,849​=±0.0256. In Exhibit 4, returns barely correlate with their past, but absolute returns correlate with theirs at every lag up to 30: large moves follow large moves, of either sign. This is volatility clustering, which LM26 models. Clustering also widens the band, because the products of the Deep Dive then vary more (LM25).

The limit regimes, each about its own mean, show the same pattern. Before June 2015, ρ^​1​ was 0.055 for rt​ and 0.113 for ∣rt​∣; from June 2015, 0.018 and 0.217. Squares hang on a few days: the product of 7 and 10 April 2025 alone supplies 31.5% of the lag-3 sum of products for rt2​, against 7.7% for ∣rt​∣.

PitfallThirty looks at one band

Under the iid model each lag falls outside the 95% band with probability about 5%. Thirty looks thus find about 1.5 bars outside by chance alone; LM21 counts such looks. TR23's nine of thirty for rt​ exceed 1.5, but the band assumes iid days, and clustering widens it. Read the bars as description, not as tests.

導讀報酬幾乎不相關，波動卻會群聚

自我相關（autocorrelation）衡量一個時間序列（time series）和自己過去的值有多相關，把各個落期（lag）的值排在一起就是自我相關函數（autocorrelation function）。本書的樣本自我相關（sample autocorrelation）用全樣本平均數（mean），分子（numerator）與分母（denominator）都除以 T，和 statsmodels 的 acf 相同。如果每天的報酬是獨立同分布（iid），約 95% 的樣本自我相關會落在獨立同分布區間（iid band）±1.96/T​ 之內。加權股價報酬指數的日對數報酬（log return）自我相關很小，落後 1 期只有 0.0393；對數報酬絕對值（absolute log return）的自我相關在落後 1 到 30 期卻都在 0.096 以上，大波動之後常接著大波動，這就是波動群聚現象（volatility clustering）。群聚也讓獨立同分布區間太窄，LM25 會給出更寬的區間。

6How Daily Variances Add Up

derive the variance of a sum of m days from autocorrelations, estimate the variance ratio from overlapping and non-overlapping sums, and state the overlapping estimator's bias

Take two days of a weakly stationary series of log returns. By (16.3), Var(r1​+r2​)=2γ0​+2γ1​, so the two-day variance is 1+ρ1​ times the sum of the daily variances. Independent days give 1, positively correlated days more.

ResultVariance of a sum of m days

Let rt​ be weakly stationary with autocovariances γk​, and rt(m)​:=rt−m+1​+⋯+rt​. Then

Varrt(m)​=mγ0​+2k=1∑m−1​(m−k)γk​,mγ0​Varrt(m)​​=1+2k=1∑m−1​(1−mk​)ρk​.​(19.7)

Proof. By (16.3) with all weights 1, the variance is the sum of the m2 covariances Cov(ri​,rj​) within the window. The m diagonal terms give mγ0​. Lag k occurs in m−k pairs with i>j and in as many with i<j, which gives 2(m−k)γk​. Dividing by mγ0​ gives the ratio. ■

The ratio in (19.7) is the model's. For uncorrelated days it is 1, and then m​ scales a volatility. As m grows, γ0​ times the model's ratio tends to the long-run variance that LM23's HAC errors estimate.

On data, every window of m consecutive log returns gives one sum, and the spread of these sums about mrˉ is compared with m daily variances.

DefinitionVariance ratio

For log returns r1​,…,rT​ with mean rˉ, sr2​ with divisor T, and 1≤m≤T, the variance ratio is

VR(m):=msr2​T−m+11​∑t=mT​(rt(m)​−mrˉ)2​.(19.8)
The non-overlapping VRn​(m) is the same ratio over the ⌊(T−o)/m⌋ consecutive blocks after the first o returns, 0≤o<m, divided by the number of blocks.

Under iid days, VR(m) averages about (1−m/T)/(1−1/T), its iid factor. The estimator is biased down, by about m/T.

Deep DiveThe overlapping estimator under iid daysoptional · click to expand

Let the rt​ be iid with variance σr2​. A window sum minus mrˉ is ∑window​rt​−Tm​∑all​rt​, with mean 0. The two sums share the window's m days, so by (16.3) and (17.1) its variance is

​mσr2​+T2m2​Tσr2​−2Tm​mσr2​=mσr2​(1−Tm​).​
Likewise Esr2​=σr2​(1−1/T), the bias that (19.1) removes (LM20). The ratio of these expectations is the iid factor, an approximation to the expectation of the ratio.

Experiment 1 checks it with 10,000 samples of 5,849 iid standard normal log returns from default_rng([1, 20261011]). The mean values of VR(m) are 0.960±0.002 at m=246 and 0.791±0.005 at m=1,231, against factors of 0.958 and 0.790. Each ± is one standard error of a mean (divisor 10,000).

Overlapping windows are far from independent. Under iid days, two consecutive one-year sums share 245 days and correlate 245/246=0.996. TR23's 5,604 one-year windows hold only about T/m=23.8 disjoint years, and its 4,619 five-year windows only 4.75.

7Annualizing, and the Annual Volatility

apply the annualization rules for the mean, variance, volatility and Sharpe ratio, state the condition each needs, and explain why TAIEX's annual volatility depends on how it is measured
build weekly, monthly and yearly returns from daily data, and compute every statistic of the module in Python with explicit divisors and quantile rules

Each annualization rule has a condition. A year's log return is the sum of its daily log returns, (1.12), so by (14.3) the annual mean log return is Arˉ, exactly. Variances add only when days are uncorrelated, (19.7): then the annual variance is Asr2​ and the volatility A​sr​. A Sharpe ratio with rf​=0, mean over standard deviation, then scales by A/A​=A​.

With correlated days, (19.7) changes the denominator. For sums of log returns with a constant mean, under (19.7)'s conditions, the Sharpe ratio of sums of m days is

SR(m)=Varrt(m)​​mErt​​=Varrt(m)​/(mγ0​)​m​SR​,(19.9)

estimated with VR(m) in the denominator. Lo (2002) shows that the 12​ rule for monthly Sharpe ratios holds only under special conditions. Serial correlation, he finds, can overstate an annual Sharpe ratio by as much as 65%. LM33 gives the Sharpe ratio's standard error when returns are correlated.

Weekly, monthly and yearly log returns are sums of daily log returns over calendar periods, as in Exhibit 1. Exhibit 7 applies the four rules to TR23. On simple returns these rules are conventions. Even for iid draws from F^, a year of A days has a simple return with mean 16.62%, standard deviation 22.29% and Sharpe ratio 0.746.

8Descriptive Statistics in Python

build weekly, monthly and yearly returns from daily data, and compute every statistic of the module in Python with explicit divisors and quantile rules

Each library has its own default divisor and quantile rule, so pass them explicitly.

Python
import numpy as np
import polars as pl
from scipy import stats
from statsmodels.tsa.stattools import acf

tr = pl.read_csv("data/taiex_tr.csv")["price"].to_numpy()
R = tr[1:] / tr[:-1] - 1                 # T = 5,849 simple returns
r = np.log1p(R)                          # log returns, for sums
s, s_u = R.std(ddof=0), R.std(ddof=1)    # always pass ddof
med = np.quantile(R, 0.5, method="inverted_cdf")      # R_(2925)
mad = np.quantile(np.abs(R - med), 0.5, method="inverted_cdf")
j0 = int(0.05 * len(R))                  # 292 dropped at each end
trim5 = np.sort(R)[j0:-j0].mean()
print(stats.skew(R), stats.kurtosis(R))  # -0.3384, 5.268
print(stats.kurtosis(R, bias=False))     # 5.274, adjusted


def rho(x, k):                           # (19.5): full mean, divisor T
    xc = x - x.mean()
    return (xc[k:] * xc[:-k]).sum() / (xc * xc).sum()


def vr(x, m):                            # (19.8), overlapping sums
    c = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum(x)])
    d = c[m:] - c[:-m] - m * x.mean()
    return (d**2).mean() / (m * x.var(ddof=0))


print(rho(r, 1), acf(r, nlags=30, fft=False)[1])   # 0.0393, 0.0393
print(vr(r, 246))                                  # 1.208
d = pl.read_csv("data/taiex_tr.csv", try_parse_dates=True)
d = d.with_columns(r=pl.col("price").log().diff()).drop_nulls()
yr = (d.filter(pl.col("date").dt.year() <= 2025)
       .group_by(pl.col("date").dt.year().alias("y"))
       .agg(pl.col("r").sum()).sort("y"))
print(yr["r"].std(ddof=0))               # 0.22207, divisor 23

NumPy's std divides by T unless given ddof=1, and Polars' by T−1 unless given ddof=0. SciPy's skew and kurtosis use divisor-T moments unless given bias=False. np.median averages the two middle values of an even sample, so the book's rule needs method="inverted_cdf". statsmodels' acf already uses the book's convention.

Summary

  • Robust centres describe the typical day; growth needs the mean.
  • The standard deviation divides by T or, as su​, by T−1, (19.1); (19.2) makes robust scales normal-equivalent.
  • Sample skewness and kurtosis, (19.3), hang on a few days; the quantile measures of (19.4) do not.
  • TAIEX's returns barely autocorrelate, (19.5), its absolute returns strongly; the iid band (19.6) assumes what clustering breaks.
  • The variance of a sum of m days depends on every lag up to m−1, (19.7); VR(m), (19.8), measures it, biased down by about m/T under iid days.
  • Log means annualize exactly; log variances, volatilities and Sharpe ratios only for uncorrelated days, (19.9); on simple returns the rules are conventions.
  • TAIEX's annual volatility depends on how it is measured, and 23 calendar years pin it down loosely.

Trimmed mean 截尾平均數 The mean after dropping the j0​ smallest and j0​ largest values.
Robust statistics 穩健統計 Statistics that a few observations cannot move far.
Mean absolute deviation 平均絕對偏差 The mean of ∣Rt​−Rˉ∣.
Interquartile range 四分位距 F^−1(0.75)−F^−1(0.25).
Median absolute deviation 中位數絕對離差 MAD, the median of ∣Rt​−med∣.
Normal-equivalent scale 常態等效尺度 1.4826MAD or IQR/1.349, which a normal law makes equal to σ, (19.2).
Sample skewness, kurtosis 樣本偏態、樣本峰態 (14.5) under F^, (19.3); the adjusted versions are SciPy's bias=False.
Quartile skewness, octile kurtosis 四分位數偏態、八分位數峰態 Shape measures from quantiles, (19.4).
Weakly stationary 平穩 Mean, variance and each lag covariance the same at every date (LM25).
Autocovariance 自我共變異數 γk​=Cov(xt​,xt−k​); sample version (19.5).
Autocorrelation 自我相關 ρk​=γk​/γ0​, the correlation of a series with itself k dates earlier; sample version (19.5).
Autocorrelation function 自我相關函數 The autocorrelations as a function of the lag k.
iid band 獨立同分布區間 ±1.96/T​, which holds about 95% of iid sample autocorrelations, (19.6).
Volatility clustering 波動群聚現象 Large moves followed by large moves, of either sign.
Variance ratio 變異數比 VR(m), the variance of overlapping sums of m days over m daily variances, (19.8).
iid factor 獨立同分布因子 (1−m/T)/(1−1/T), the approximate mean of VR(m) under iid days.
