Convex Optimization in Practice

Limits on short sales, caps, leverage and trading turn mean–variance problems into convex programs: solve them, read their multipliers as prices, and check that the solver is right.

Learning Module Overview
  • On the through case's last window, LM28's tangency formula puts 114.24% in 0050 and shorts two funds. Forbidding short sales and borrowing lowers the in-sample Sharpe ratio only from 2.18 to 2.11.
  • A convex problem has no false minima: every local minimum is global, (29.3). Portfolio variance is strictly convex because its Hessian 2Σ is positive definite.
  • At long-only minimum variance every held fund has a beta of 1 to the portfolio and every excluded one a beta of at least 1, (29.5). On U28, 19 sectors are excluded, the closest at 1.000019.

  • A multiplier is a rate: raising a 40% cap on R3 to 41% lowers its variance by 0.000102, against the linear prediction 0.000105.
  • Trading costs in the objective create a no-trade band. At a cost weight of 1, the penalized path plans 31.5% of R3's turnover and leaves 31 of 91 signals untraded.
  • On daily inputs CLARABEL's default tolerances miss R3's weights by up to 2.1×10−3, on annual inputs by 3.4×10−5. Every printed solution here is checked by its KKT residuals.

1Introduction

LM28 solved mean–variance problems with equality constraints, in closed form. Real mandates add inequalities: no short sales, caps, limits on leverage and on trading. This module adds those limits, a solver that handles them, and the checks that show the solver is right.

Motivating CaseWhy does the long-only portfolio drop two funds?

On 30 September 2026 the through case re-estimates its inputs from its last 504 joint trading days, 23 August 2024 to 30 September 2026. Fed those means and that covariance matrix, LM28's tangency formula puts 114.24% in 0050. It sells short 27.75% of 00646 and 48.48% of 00679B, and it buys 31.07% of 00635U and 30.92% of 00642U. Its gross exposure is 252%, for an in-sample Sharpe ratio of 2.18 (Exhibit 1).

The formula shorts at every one of the through case's 92 month-ends. A reader with a cash account can neither short nor borrow. Forbid both, and the best Sharpe ratio falls only to 2.11. That portfolio holds 62.77% in 0050, 18.37% in 00635U and 18.86% in 00642U, and nothing in the other two.

Three questions follow. Why is 00646's weight zero, when it earns 16.44 points a year above cash? What does each limit cost? And when a solver prints these weights, how can the reader tell they are right?

Sections 2 to 4 develop the theory, Sections 5 to 7 apply it to the through case, and Sections 8 and 9 solve and check in Python.

2Convex Sets and Convex Functions

prove that a convex problem's local minimum is global, and test convexity by Hessians, sums and maxima

A solver walks downhill, and on a bowl only the bottom can stop it. Convexity makes both the feasible set and the objective bowl-shaped.

DefinitionConvex set and convex function

A set C⊆RN is convex if it contains the segment between any two of its points. A function h on a convex set C is convex if every chord lies on or above its graph. For all u,v∈C and ω∈[0,1]:

ωu+(1−ω)v∈C,h(ωu+(1−ω)v)≤ωh(u)+(1−ω)h(v).​(29.1)
The function h is strictly convex if the inequality is strict whenever u=v and 0<ω<1.

LM4's definition is the case N=1, and LM10's (10.10) shows that a quadratic with a PSD matrix satisfies (29.1). The budget hyperplane, the long-only orthant w≥0, a cap's half-space wi​≤bi​ and the gross ball ∥w∥1​≤1.5 are convex, and so is any intersection of convex sets.

TheoremHessian test and gradient inequality

Let h be twice differentiable on a convex open set C. Then h is convex exactly when its Hessian H(u) is PSD at every u∈C, and strictly convex if H(u) is PD everywhere. A differentiable convex h satisfies, for all u,v∈C,

h(v)≥h(u)+∇h(u)⊤(v−u).(29.2)

Proof. Inequality (29.1) involves only the points of one segment, so h is convex exactly when each restriction ω↦h(u+ω(v−u)) is. By the chain rule its second derivative is (v−u)⊤H(⋅)(v−u). LM4's one-variable test, stated there without proof, makes the restriction convex exactly when that derivative is ≥0. Since C is open, v−u takes every direction, so the condition is H PSD everywhere; PD gives strict convexity. ■

Deep DiveThe gradient inequalityoptional · click to expand

Let h be differentiable and convex, and apply (29.1) at u+ω(v−u), 0<ω≤1:

ωh(u+ω(v−u))−h(u)​≤h(v)−h(u).​
As ω→0 the left side tends to ∇h(u)⊤(v−u), which gives (29.2) (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, §3.1.3, p. 69). For a quadratic it is (10.9) with Δ⊤MΔ≥0.

A sum of convex functions with non-negative weights is convex, by (29.1) term by term, and so is a pointwise maximum (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, §3.2.3, p. 80). So ∣x∣=max(x,−x), max(0,−x) and ∥w∥1​ are convex, though none has a derivative at 0.

Example 1Which portfolio sets are convex?

On the last window, show that long-only portfolios with a 40% cap, gross exposure at most 150% and volatility at most 25% form a convex set. Show that a volatility floor and "at most three funds" do not.

Solution

The budget, the orthant and the caps are linear, and the gross ball is convex by the triangle inequality. The variance has the PD Hessian 2Σ, so it is strictly convex by (29.2), and the ellipsoid w⊤Σw≤(25%)2 and the intersection are convex.

A floor w⊤Σw≥0.01 alone holds w and −w but not their midpoint 0. A third each in 0050, 00646 and 00679B, and a third each in 00679B, 00635U and 00642U, average to a portfolio of all five funds.

Integer limits, such as a number of funds or round lots, are not convex, and this module omits them.

TheoremConvex problems have no false minima

Let h and every ψi​ be convex on RN, and consider

wmin​ h(w)subject toψi​(w)≤bi​ for each i,Kw=b.​(29.3)
Problem (29.3) has a convex feasible set, and every local minimum is a global minimum (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, §4.2.2, p. 138). A strictly convex h has at most one minimizer.

Proof. Each set {ψi​≤bi​} is convex by (29.1). If a local minimum w∗ had a feasible v with h(v)<h(w∗), the feasible points w∗+ω(v−w∗), 0<ω≤1, would have lower values by (29.1), arbitrarily close to w∗. Two minimizers of a strictly convex h would have a midpoint of lower value. ■

By (29.2), a feasible point where ∇h=0 is also a global minimum. Theorem (29.3) does not say that a minimum exists: each optimum here is proved by exhibiting a KKT point (Section 4).

導讀凸性讓局部極小值就是全域最小值

凸集（convex set）包含任兩點之間的線段；凸函數（convex function）的弦不會低於圖形，也就是凸組合（convex combination）的函數值不超過兩端函數值的同樣加權平均。檢查方法有三種：海森矩陣（Hessian）處處半正定（positive semidefinite）、凸函數以非負權重相加、凸函數取最大值，例如絕對值與交易成本（transaction cost）。LM10 證明二次函數是凸函數若且唯若矩陣半正定，本節把同樣的結論推廣到一般函數。凸問題的局部極小值（local minimum）一定是全域最小值（global minimum），嚴格凸函數（strictly convex function）最多只有一個最小解，所以往下走的 solver 不會被假的谷底困住。

Knowledge Check 1

Show that LM27's cost of a trade, ∑i​[0.1425%∣Δi​∣+taxi​max(0,−Δi​)] with taxi​≥0, is convex, using only sums and maxima. Why is "at most three funds" not convex?

Answer

Each ∣Δi​∣=max(Δi​,−Δi​) and each max(0,−Δi​) is a maximum of linear functions, added with non-negative weights. Two allowed portfolios can average to one of five funds (Example 1).

3Quadratic and Linear Programs

write portfolio limits as linear constraints, and recognize infeasible, unbounded and non-convex problems

Most portfolio limits are linear, and the variance is a convex quadratic.

DefinitionQuadratic and linear programs

A quadratic program (QP) minimizes w⊤Mw plus a linear term, with M PSD, subject to linear equalities Kw=b and linear inequalities ψi​(w)≤bi​. A linear program (LP) has M=0 (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, (4.34), p. 152).

Each limit is a row: the budget a row of K, long-only −wi​≤0, a 40% cap wi​≤0.40, and "at least 10% in 00679B" −w3​≤−0.10. R3 with a cap is the QP

wmin​ w⊤Σwsubject to1⊤w=1,−wi​≤0,wi​≤0.40.​

A gross limit ∥w∥1​≤1.5 becomes linear after a split (LM8's gross exposure): w=w+−w−, w±≥0 and 1⊤(w++w−)≤1.5. The positive and negative parts of w are one split, and any split has 1⊤(w++w−)≥∥w∥1​. A turnover limit splits the same way.

A limit on volatility, such as w⊤Σw≤(25%)2, is a second-order cone constraint. cvxpy sends such problems to a conic solver, and this module does not develop cone programming.

Example 2A linear program: the highest mean under caps

On the last window, maximize μ⊤w subject to 1⊤w=1 and 0≤wi​≤40%. The three highest means are 48.775% (0050), 36.096% (00642U) and 20.678% (00635U).

Solution

The candidate w∗ holds 40% in 0050 and 00642U and 20% in 00635U, a mean of 0.4×48.775+0.4×36.096+0.2×20.678=38.08%. Both portfolios sum to 1, so

μ⊤w−μ⊤w∗=i∑​(wi​−wi∗​)(μi​−20.678%).​
Every term is ≤0: the top two funds have wi​≤40%=wi∗​ and higher means, the third fund's term is 0, and the other two have wi​≥0=wi∗​ and lower means. The means differ, so equality needs w∗'s weights in four funds, and the budget fixes the fifth: the optimum is unique. A linear objective concentrates; only a risk term diversifies.

Example 3Infeasible, unbounded and not convex

On the last window, give cvxpy three problems: minimum variance with a 15% cap on each fund; the highest mean with only the budget; and a volatility floor w⊤Σw≥0.01 alone.

Solution

The caps sum to 75%: infeasible, with prob.value inf and w.value None. With only the budget, buying 0050 against a short in 00679B raises the mean without limit: unbounded, again with prob.value inf and w.value None. The floor alone is not convex: cvxpy raises a DCPError.

With the budget added, the floor would exclude nothing: every fully invested portfolio has a variance of at least R3's 0.0102, because R3's bounds do not bind (Section 4).

PitfallThe status is part of the answer

infeasible, unbounded and optimal_inaccurate are answers, and Variable.value is None when there is no solution; a DCPError means that cvxpy cannot verify that the problem, as written, is convex. Read the status before the weights.

Knowledge Check 2

Write "at most 40% in any fund, at least 10% in 00679B, gross exposure at most 150%" as linear rows. Why is a 15% cap on each of five funds infeasible, and why does w≥0 bound the highest mean?

Answer

With w=w+−w− and w±≥0, the rows are wi​≤0.40, −w3​≤−0.10 and 1⊤(w++w−)≤1.5. Five caps of 15% allow 75% in all, so the budget cannot hold. Long-only weights lie in [0,1], so no mean can exceed the highest fund mean.

4KKT Conditions for Long-Only Portfolios

derive the KKT conditions of long-only minimum variance, and prove them sufficient for a convex objective with linear constraints
explain what KKT necessity needs and what a solver's status guarantees, and choose a solution method

LM7's (7.13) states the KKT conditions for a maximization, with an equality in the Lagrangian as −λ(ψ−b) and a multiplier νi​≥0 for each inequality. Applied to −h, it gives the form for a minimization.

TheoremKKT conditions for a minimization

Let h and every ψi​ be differentiable. Minimize h(w) subject to ψi​(w)≤bi​ for each i and Kw=b. A point w∗ with multipliers λ and νi​ is a KKT point if

∇h(w∗)=K⊤λ−i∑​νi​∇ψi​(w∗),Kw∗=b,ψi​(w∗)≤bi​,νi​≥0,νi​(bi​−ψi​(w∗))=0.​(29.4)
If h is convex and every ψi​ is linear, a KKT point is a global minimum.

A bound wi​≥0, written −wi​≤0, enters stationarity as +νi​, and a cap wi​≤bi​ as −νicap​.

Proof of sufficiency. Let w be feasible. By (29.2) and stationarity,

h(w)​≥h(w∗)+λ⊤K(w−w∗)−i∑​νi​∇ψi​(w∗)⊤(w−w∗).​
Here K(w−w∗)=0, and for linear ψi​ the last product is ψi​(w)−ψi​(w∗). Complementary slackness turns νi​ψi​(w∗) into νi​bi​, so each term is νi​(ψi​(w)−bi​)≤0, and h(w)≥h(w∗). ■

Boyd and Vandenberghe (2004, §5.5.3, p. 244) state sufficiency for every convex problem (29.3). The converse, that every minimum is a KKT point, needs a constraint qualification. For convex problems they state it under Slater's condition (p. 244), which for linear constraints is feasibility alone (p. 227). The converse is stated here without proof. LM7's independent binding gradients are another qualification; in a non-convex problem a KKT point need not be a minimum.

So, for a convex objective with linear constraints, the optima are exactly the KKT points. In Example 2, as min−μ⊤w, the budget's multiplier is λ=−20.678%; the caps' multipliers μi​−20.678% are 28.097 and 15.418 points, and the bounds' 20.678%−μi​ are 2.741 and 26.454, all non-negative.

Long-only minimum variance. Minimize w⊤Σw subject to 1⊤w=1 and w≥0. Stationarity is 2Σw∗=λ1+ν. Multiplying by w∗⊤, the budget leaves λ and complementary slackness removes ν, so λ=2σp2​. With (27.5), (Σw∗)i​=Cov(Ri​,Rp​):

2Σw∗=λ1+ν,ν≥0,νi​wi∗​=0,λ=2σp2​,νi​=2[Cov(Ri​,Rp​)−σp2​],βi,p​=1+νi​/λ.​(29.5)

Every held fund has βi,p​=1 and every excluded fund βi,p​≥1. A little more of a held fund leaves the variance unchanged to first order; an excluded fund would raise it. Constraining weights also acts as a shrinkage of Σ^, which LM30 studies (Jagannathan and Ma 2003).

On E5, R3's bounds never bind: over the 92 signals its smallest weight is 0.13% (0050, 2024-11-29), so R3 equals LM28's wmv at every signal. An active set needs a larger universe.

Example 4Long-only minimum variance of U28

Solve (29.5) for U28's 28 sector price indices on the full sample (T=4,382, A=245.48; covariance only), and check the excluded sectors.

Solution

Nine sectors are held, with an in-sample volatility of 12.10% and λ=0.0293. The 19 excluded sectors have βi,p​ from 1.000019 (Cement) to 1.480 (Optoelectronic), each ≥1. LM28's unconstrained wmv holds 12 shorts, with a gross exposure of 2.24. On the first half alone, the long-only portfolio again holds 9 sectors (LM30), only 7 of them the full sample's: the active set is itself an estimate.

U28's 28 bounds give 228=268,435,456 candidate active sets, too many to enumerate as LM7 enumerated its eight. SciPy's nnls, the exact solver of Section 5, is an active-set method that solves the KKT conditions of non-negative least squares (Lawson and Hanson 1995). The widget tries the eight binding sets of a two-fund example. With all three constraints binding, the system is singular by (9.6), since three gradients in R2 are dependent.

導讀KKT 條件：誰被排除、為什麼

只作多（long-only）的最小變異數問題，每個權重下限 wi​≥0 只有兩種狀態：鬆弛，乘數（multiplier）為 0；緊，乘數 νi​≥0，也就是有效限制（active constraint）。KKT 條件說，被持有的基金對投資組合的 beta 都等於 1，被排除的都至少是 1；互補鬆弛性（complementary slackness）保證乘數與權重至少一個是 0。乘數就是影子價格（shadow price）：放寬那條限制時，變異數下降的速率。對凸問題，KKT 條件是充分條件；限制條件（constraint）都是線性時，約束規範（constraint qualification）自動成立，所以也是必要條件。"active set" 本書保留英文。

Knowledge Check 3

At U28's long-only minimum variance, Cement is excluded with βi,p​=1.000019, the closest of the 19 to 1. What do (29.5)'s conditions say, and how far would its covariance with the portfolio have to fall for Cement to enter? Why must R3 satisfy (29.5)?

Answer

Cement's νi​=λ(βi,p​−1) is tiny but positive: adding Cement would raise the variance, barely. Cement would enter once its covariance fell below σp2​, a fall of about 0.0019% of it, since then βi,p​<1. R3 satisfies (29.5) because linear constraints satisfy a constraint qualification.

5R3 and R4 in the Through Case

compute R3 and R4 in their long-only form, and explain each zero weight by its multiplier
compute the shadow price of a limit, and check it by moving the limit

R3 is (29.5) on each window's Σ, solved exactly by vol5_rules.py (a Cholesky factor and nnls) and checked by its KKT conditions. R4 maximizes a ratio, which a change of variables turns into a QP.

The Sharpe ratio e⊤w/w⊤Σw​ does not change when w is scaled by a positive number. Suppose some ei​>0, so the best ratio is positive. For a long-only w with e⊤w>0, set y=w/e⊤w; then e⊤y=1 and the ratio is 1/y⊤Σy​. Maximizing it is minimizing y⊤Σy, by Boyd and Vandenberghe's change of variables (2004, §4.3.2, p. 151):

y∗ minimizes y⊤Σysubject to e⊤y=1, y≥0,w∗=y∗/1⊤y∗,SR∗=(y∗⊤Σy∗)−1/2,λ=2/SR∗2.​(29.6)

Stationarity is 2Σy∗=λe+ν, and multiplying by y∗⊤ gives λ=2y∗⊤Σy∗. Substituting y∗=w∗/(μp​−rf​), since e⊤w∗=μp​−rf​, and dividing by λ,

λνi​​=βi,p​(μp​−rf​)−ei​≥0,with equality for held funds.​(29.7)

A held fund earns exactly its beta to the portfolio times the portfolio's excess return; an excluded fund earns less, by νi​/λ. A positive excess return is not enough.

A multiplier also prices its limit. Let J(bi​) be the optimal value as one level bi​ moves.

ResultMultipliers are shadow prices

For a convex problem (29.3) with linear constraints and KKT multipliers νi​,

∂bi​∂J​=−νi​  where J isdifferentiable, andJ(bi​+Δbi​)≥J(bi​)−νi​Δbi​for every Δbi​.​(29.8)
Stated without proof. The derivative is Boyd and Vandenberghe's local sensitivity (2004, §5.6.3, p. 251); the bound is their global inequality (5.57), p. 250, valid by the refined Slater condition (p. 227).

Example 6A shadow price checked by moving the bound

In R4's QP (29.6) on the last window, relax yi​≥0 to yi​≥−0.01, for 00646 and then for 00679B, and compare each gain with (29.8).

7Costs in the Objective

add trading costs to the objective, find the no-trade band, and weigh turnover against in-sample risk

LM27's cost of a trade Δ, a fraction of pre-trade wealth, is 0.1425% of ∥Δ∥1​ plus a 0.1% tax on sales, none for 00679B ((27.2); LM34 prints the sources). In the objective:

wmin​ w⊤Σw+θcost(w−wtdrift​)subject to1⊤w=1,w≥0.​(29.9)

The cost is a sum of maxima (Section 2), so for θ≥0 the problem is convex. The weight θ is annual variance per unit of wealth paid once. In LM28's exposure form, a holding period of τ years trades 2δτ​w⊤Σw against a cost paid once, so θ=2/(δτ). Monthly trading, τ=1/12, with δ=4 gives θ=6.

Splitting each trade into buys and sells, w=wtdrift​+Δ+−Δ−, makes the cost linear (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, p. 156). The problem is then a QP, and (29.4) gives, for a fund still held after the trade,

bought: (2Σw)i​=λ−θ0.1425%,sold: (2Σw)i​=λ+θ(0.1425%+taxi​).​

An untouched fund lies in the no-trade band between the two. cvxpy receives the cost as written, with norm1 and pos.

8Solvers and How to Check Them

solve a problem in cvxpy with scaled inputs and set tolerances, and verify it by KKT residuals
explain what KKT necessity needs and what a solver's status guarantees, and choose a solution method

cvxpy is a modelling language for convex problems, embedded in Python (Diamond and Boyd 2016). A problem is built from convex atoms such as quad_form, norm1 and pos, and cvxpy refuses one whose convexity its rules cannot verify. It canonicalizes the others into the standard form of a solver it selects (Agrawal et al. 2018).

In the installed cvxpy 1.9.3 the default solver is OSQP 1.1.3 for a QP, such as R3 or (29.9), and CLARABEL 0.11.1 for an LP or a volatility limit. OSQP solves a quadratic program by operator splitting and CLARABEL by an interior-point method; neither method is derived here (Stellato et al. 2020; Goulart and Chen 2024).

Scaling. A solver stops when its residuals fall below tolerances, some of them absolute. CLARABEL also tests the gap between its objective and a lower bound from its multipliers. On daily inputs R3's optimal variance at the first signal is 1.035×10−5, so CLARABEL's default absolute gap, 10−8, is about 0.1% of it. On annual inputs the variance is 2.515×10−3, A times larger, and the gap is 0.0004% of it (Exhibit 7).

The condition number, 9.72 to 20.46 over the 92 windows, does not change with the units, so by (11.9) an exact solve is as accurate in either. The exact solver uses the Cholesky factor, whose condition number is κ(Σ)​ by (12.6).

Tolerances. The book sets OSQP's eps_abs and eps_rel to 10−10, with up to 400,000 iterations and polishing. Polishing guesses the active constraints and re-solves the KKT equations on them, with a few refinement steps. The book sets CLARABEL's tol_gap_abs, tol_gap_rel and tol_feas to 10−12 for QPs and LPs and 10−10 for cone programs (Problem 11). Tightening only one of CLARABEL's two gap tolerances leaves R3 unchanged at the first signal.

cvxpy's defaults for OSQP are 10−5 with 10,000 iterations and polishing, in place of OSQP's own 10−3, 4,000 and no polishing. CLARABEL keeps its own defaults, 10−8 with 200. Every printed solution comes from a fresh Problem, ends optimal and passes Example 9's checks.

solver 回報的 "optimal" 只表示它的殘差（residual）小於容許誤差（tolerance），而有些容許誤差是絕對值：用日資料時，R3 的目標函數（objective function）只有 1.035×10−5，預設的 10−8 就大約是它的千分之一，權重偏離 8.6×10−4，92 個訊號中最多偏離 2.1×10−3。改用年化資料，同一個容許誤差相對小得多。檢查的順序是：先看狀態，再看每條限制條件（constraint）是否滿足、KKT 殘差、第二個 solver，最後與已知的公式解（closed form）比對。cvxpy 回報的對偶變量（dual variable）就是 KKT 條件的乘數（multiplier），但等式的符號要先用公式解核對。OSQP 用 operator splitting，CLARABEL 用內點法（interior-point method），本書都不推導。

9Convex Optimization in Python

solve a problem in cvxpy with scaled inputs and set tolerances, and verify it by KKT residuals
Python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import cholesky, solve_triangular
from scipy.optimize import nnls

BOOK = {"tol_gap_abs": 1e-12, "tol_gap_rel": 1e-12, "tol_feas": 1e-12}


def qp(S, a):                       # min y'Sy, a'y = 1, y >= 0 in cvxpy
    y = cp.Variable(len(a))
    con = a @ y == 1
    prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(cp.quad_form(y, cp.psd_wrap(S))),
                      [con, y >= 0])
    prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, **BOOK)    # a fresh Problem
    assert prob.status == "optimal" and y.value is not None
    return y.value, -float(con.dual_value)    # cvxpy's dual is -lambda


def exact(S, a):                    # the same problem by nnls
    U = cholesky(S)                 # S = U'U, U upper triangular
    z, _ = nnls(U, solve_triangular(U, a, trans="T"))
    return z / (a @ z)


def kkt(S, a, y):                   # residuals of (29.5) and (29.6)
    lam = 2 * y @ S @ y
    nu = 2 * S @ y - lam * a
    held = y > 1e-6
    return np.abs(nu[held]).max(), nu.min(), np.abs(nu * y).max()

# R3: a = np.ones(5); R4: a = e; the weights are y / y.sum().
# nnls minimizes |U z - U^-T a|^2 over z >= 0, which is z'Sz - 2a'z plus a
# constant; then y = z / a'z. psd_wrap declares S PSD without testing it,
# so pass only a matrix known to be positive definite.

Glossary

Term 中文 Meaning
Convex set 凸集 A set containing the segment between any two of its points, (29.1).
Convex function 凸函數 A function whose chords lie on or above its graph, (29.1).
Strictly convex function 嚴格凸函數 A convex function whose chords lie strictly above the graph between distinct points.
Convex combination 凸組合 ωu+(1−ω)v with ω∈[0,1]; more generally, a mix with non-negative weights summing to 1.
Convex program 凸規劃 Minimizing a convex function subject to convex ψi​≤bi​ and Kw=b, (29.3).
Quadratic program 二次規劃 A convex quadratic objective with linear constraints.
Linear program 線性規劃 A linear objective with linear constraints.
Feasible set 可行解區域 The points that satisfy every constraint.
KKT conditions KKT 條件 Stationarity, feasibility, non-negative multipliers and complementary slackness, (29.4).
Complementary slackness 互補鬆弛性 Each inequality binds or has a zero multiplier.
Constraint qualification 約束規範 A condition on the constraints under which every minimum is a KKT point; linear constraints satisfy one.
Active constraint 有效限制 An inequality that holds with equality at the optimum; the active set lists them.
Shadow price 影子價格 The rate νi​=−∂J/∂bi​ at which relaxing a limit improves the optimal value, (29.8).
Homogenized QP — R4 as miny⊤Σy subject to e⊤y=1, y≥0, (29.6).
Penalty function 懲罰函數 A term added to the objective in place of a limit, such as θcost in (29.9).
No-trade band — The range of a fund's variance gradient in which a cost-penalized trade leaves the fund untouched.
Dual variable 對偶變量 A solver's multiplier of a constraint; cvxpy's dual_value.
Interior-point method 內點法 A method that approaches a KKT point while it keeps every inequality's multiplier strictly positive; its iterates need not be feasible (CLARABEL).
Solver status — The solver's verdict (optimal, optimal_inaccurate, infeasible, unbounded), read before its answer.
