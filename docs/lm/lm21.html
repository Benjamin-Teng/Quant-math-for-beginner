Hypothesis Testing and Multiple Testing

A test asks how surprising the data would be if nothing were there. Run enough tests and something always looks surprising.

Learning Module Overview
  • A test fixes a null hypothesis, a statistic and a level before the data. The p-value (21.2) is the probability, if the null were true, of a statistic at least as extreme: a probability of data, not of the hypothesis.
  • TR23's mean daily return, 0.0625%, has a t-stat of 3.96 by (21.1), a two-sided p-value of 7.6×10−5. Before June 2015 the t-stat is 2.05 and from then 3.68, yet the two means differ by only 1.08 standard errors, (21.3).
  • Power grows with the annual Sharpe ratio times the square root of the years, (21.4). At an annual Sharpe ratio of 0.5, 80% power at the 5% level takes 31.4 years of daily data.
  • The 2×2 χ2 equals nρ^​2, (21.5). TR23's signs pass, with χ2=2.69 and a p-value of 0.10, but falls of 3% or more followed such falls 13 times against 2.0 expected, a p-value of 1.2×10−7. Large moves cluster, and the independent-days null laws fail.
  • Wednesday's p-value, 0.018, is the smallest of 17 calendar tests. In such a family 0.85 false rejections are expected, and shuffled dates give a result at least as extreme 0.266 of the time. Bonferroni's adjusted p-value is 0.31, (21.6).
  • Holm's rule (21.7) controls the family-wise error rate under any dependence, and Benjamini–Hochberg (21.8) the false discovery rate for independent statistics. On ten constructed p-values they reject 3 and 5, Bonferroni 1; on the calendar none rejects anything.
  • The largest of m null statistics grows with m, (21.9): its median is 3.20 at m=1,000. The best of 10,000 random long–short rules on TR23 reaches 3.97, and a t-stat of 3 corrects for only about 18.5 tests.

1Introduction

LM20 attached a standard error to an estimate. A hypothesis test asks a sharper question: if the true value were zero, how surprising would the estimate be? This module tests a mean, a frequency and a table of counts on TAIEX data, then asks what happens when many tests run on the same data.

Motivating CaseThe best day to own the TAIEX

From 3 January 2003 to 8 October 2026 the TAIEX total-return index, with dividends reinvested and no costs, gained 0.0625% on an average day. Single days varied far more, with a standard deviation of 1.21%. Is the mean really positive? It lies 3.96 standard errors above zero: 2.05 before the daily price limit widened in June 2015 and 3.68 after it (Exhibit 1).

Now sort the same 5,849 days by calendar month and by weekday, 17 groups in all. Wednesdays stand out. They averaged 0.1332%, against 0.0446% on the other days, a gap of 2.36 standard errors with a p-value of 0.018, well below the usual 5%. Should an investor own the TAIEX only on Wednesdays?

Sections 2 to 5 test a mean and frequencies, Sections 6 to 8 count the tests, and Section 9 collects the Python.

NoteNotation for this module

The sample is TR23: T=5,849 simple daily returns Rt​, 3 January 2003 to 8 October 2026, over 23.7645 years (8,680 calendar days from the first close). So A=246.12 sessions a year. Rˉ and s divide by T, and su​, (19.1), by T−1; every t-stat uses su​. The limit regimes hold 3,077 returns before 1 June 2015 and 2,772 from that date.

Symbols: H0​, H1​, α the hypotheses and the level; μnull​ the mean under H0​ (μ0​ is LM24's prior mean); θ, θ0​, θ^ a binomial probability, its null value and a frequency; X a count, x its value; τ years of data; SR the annual Sharpe ratio, rf​=0; subscripts 1 and 2 for two groups (Rˉ1​, μ1​, su,1​, T1​); n11​,…,n22​ the cells of a 2×2 table of n pairs (row 1: yesterday down; column 1: today down), n1⋅​, n⋅1​ its margins, ρ^​ the correlation of its indicators; m tests with m0​ true nulls, ranks i and j, p-values pi​ ordered as p(i)​, q the false-discovery level, iH​, iBH​ the rejection counts of Holm and Benjamini–Hochberg; Z1​,…,Zm​ independent standard normal statistics; fi,t​=±1 rule i's position on day t; V a χ2 variable. Power and critical values get no letter.

NoteResearch hygiene

Every test here is in-sample, run on the data that suggested it. The module runs 29 tests on TR23, and the lab lists them: count the tests before you read any single p-value.

Comparing the limit regimes is a stability check. Null laws, power curves, shuffled dates and simulated searches are model results. The manager is hypothetical (LM13), and examples without data are constructed. LM16's counts are U28's (28 TWSE sector price indices, 2008–2026), as printed; Section 5 and Problem 8 run two tests on them. Nothing is tested on later data (LM34).

2What a Test Decides

state a null and an alternative hypothesis, and compute a test statistic, its p-value and the decision at a given level, naming the type I and type II errors
Example 1Eight good months in twelve

LM13's hypothetical manager beat the index in 8 of 12 months, independent trials with probability θ of a good month. Is the record evidence of skill, θ>21​? Find the p-value, the 5% rejection region and its size, and the chance of a rejection at LM13's θ=0.55.

Solution

Under H0​:θ=21​ the number X of good months is Bin(12,21​), by (15.1). Many good months speak for H1​:θ>21​, so the p-value is the upper tail Pr(X≥8)=794/4,096=0.194. The record is not significant at 5%.

The 5% test rejects when X≥x for the smallest x with Pr(X≥x)≤0.05. Since Pr(X≥9)=0.073 and Pr(X≥10)=79/4,096=0.0193, the region is X≥10. Its size, 0.0193, lies below the level.

For a skilled manager, Bin(12,0.55) gives Pr(X≥10)=0.042, so the test misses one with probability 0.958. LM13's posterior of 13.5% answers another question, the probability of skill given the record (LM24).

DefinitionHypothesis test

A null hypothesis H0​ fixes a parameter's value, such as θ=21​. The alternative H1​ says where the value lies otherwise: on one side, θ>21​ (one-sided), or on both, μ=0 (two-sided). Both are fixed before the data are seen. A test statistic is computed from the data; its null law is its distribution under H0​. The test rejects H0​ when the statistic falls in a rejection region. Its probability under H0​, the test's size, is at most the level α.

Definitionp-value

The p-value of an observed statistic is the probability under H0​ of a statistic at least as extreme, in the direction of H1​. The test at level α rejects exactly when the p-value is at most α. For a statistic that is approximately N(0,1) under H0​, the p-value is

two-sided:one-sided, H1​ above:​2Φ(−∣t-stat∣),Φ(−t-stat).​(21.2)

Under a continuous null law the p-value is uniform on (0,1), so Pr(p-value≤u)=u; this is the inverse transform (18.1) run backwards. A true null therefore gives a p-value below 0.05 one time in twenty.

Two kinds of mistake are possible.

Decision H0​ true H0​ false
Reject H0​ type I error, probability at most α correct, probability = power
Do not reject correct type II error, probability = 1 − power

A type I error rejects a true null, and a type II error keeps a false one. The power of a test is its probability of rejecting when a given alternative is true: 0.042 in Example 1. The likelihood-ratio test and the Neyman–Pearson lemma are not in this book (Wasserman 2004, §10.6 and §10.10.1).

Example 2Up days

TR23 has 3,205 up days among its 5,849 returns, and no return is exactly zero. Test H0​:θ=21​, a day as likely to rise as to fall, against H1​:θ>21​. Treat the days as independent trials.

Solution

The frequency is θ^=3,205/5,849=0.548. Under H0​ the count is Bin(5,849,21​), so θ^ has variance 1/(4T) by (15.2). By (17.5) the statistic

z=1/(4T)​θ^−21​​=7.3
is approximately standard normal. The exact binomial tail is Pr(X≥3,205)=1.2×10−13, so up days beat a fair coin. Both null laws assume independent days, which Section 5 checks.

PitfallA p-value is not the probability that H0​ is true

The p-value is a probability of data given H0​, not of H0​ given the data; LM24 computes the latter, as LM13 did for the manager. "Not rejected" is not "true" either: Example 1's test has power 4%, so it keeps H0​ for most skilled managers. And "significant" is not "large": Wednesday's edge is 0.0886% a day.

導讀假設檢定在問什麼

假設檢定（hypothesis testing）問的是：如果什麼都沒有，資料會有多令人意外？虛無假設（null hypothesis）說「沒有效果」，對立假設（alternative hypothesis）說效果在哪一邊，兩者都要在看資料之前定好。p值（p-value）是在虛無假設成立時，出現至少這麼極端的結果的機率（probability），不是虛無假設為真的機率。p值不超過顯著水準（significance level）就拒絕虛無假設。拒絕了真的虛無假設，叫第一型錯誤（type I error）；沒拒絕假的虛無假設，叫第二型錯誤（type II error）。經理人 12 個月勝 8 個月，p值 0.194，不顯著；就算他真有實力，這個檢定（test）也只有 4% 的機會拒絕。

Knowledge Check 1

A rule produced 60 up days in 100 sessions. Test θ=21​, two-sided, with the normal approximation. Do you reject at 5%? At 1%?

Answer

Here z=(0.6−0.5)/0.25/100​=2.00, so the two-sided p-value is 2Φ(−2)=0.0455. Reject at 5%, not at 1%.

3Testing a Mean Return

apply the one-sample t-test to a mean return and the two-sample test to two periods or groups of days, state their conditions, and relate a test to a confidence interval

LM20 derives SE(Rˉ)=σ/T​, (20.2), and the 95% interval (20.4). This section tests the value that the interval estimates.

ResultThe one-sample t-test

Let R1​,…,RT​ be iid with mean μ and variance σ2<∞, and test H0​:μ=μnull​ with

t-stat=su​/T​Rˉ−μnull​​.(21.1)
Under H0​, the t-stat is approximately N(0,1) for large T. If the returns are iid normal, it has exactly the tT−1​ law for every T≥2.

The large-T law follows from (17.5), because su​ settles at σ (LM20). Here "large" means thousands of days.

The exact law takes one line. For iid normal returns, Blitzstein and Hwang (2019) show that Rˉ and su2​ are independent (Example 7.5.9) and that V:=(T−1)su2​/σ2∼χT−12​ (Example 10.4.3). With Z:=T​(Rˉ−μ)/σ, their Definition 10.4.4 makes Z/V/(T−1)​ a tT−1​ variable. The σ's cancel, so this ratio is (21.1) at μnull​=μ.

The tT−1​ law has heavier tails than N(0,1), but at T=5,849 the two nearly coincide (Exhibit 2).

Example 3Is TAIEX's mean positive?

Test H0​:μ=0 against H1​:μ=0 on TR23 at the 5% level, with LM19's Rˉ=0.0625% and su​=1.2085% a day.

Solution

The standard error is su​/T​=1.2085%/5849​=0.0158%, and (21.1) gives t-stat=3.96 from unrounded inputs. By (21.2) the two-sided p-value is 7.6×10−5. The t5848​ law gives 7.7×10−5, and the one-sided p-value is 3.8×10−5. Reject H0​: the mean is positive at any usual level. With s in place of su​ the t-stat is still 3.956 to three decimals, so at this T the divisor does not matter.

LM20's 95% interval for the annual mean, 7.8% to 23.0% a year, excludes 0: the same decision at 5%. With the same standard error, a two-sided test at level α rejects μnull​ exactly when μnull​ lies outside Rˉ±Φ−1(1−α/2)SE. An interval is the set of null values that the test keeps.

The conditions matter more than normality. By (16.3), uncorrelated returns give VarRˉ=σ2/T even when volatility clusters, while autocorrelated returns change it. TR23's log returns have a lag-1 autocorrelation of 0.0393 (LM19), and LM23's HAC errors correct for autocorrelation.

ResultTwo-sample statistic with unpooled variances

Let two independent groups of T1​ and T2​ returns each be iid, with means μ1​, μ2​ and finite variances. Under H0​:μ1​=μ2​, for large T1​ and T2​,

su,12​/T1​+su,22​/T2​​Rˉ1​−Rˉ2​​(21.3)
is approximately N(0,1).

The denominator estimates Var(Rˉ1​−Rˉ2​)​, which (16.3) gives for independent groups. SciPy computes (21.3) as ttest_ind(..., equal_var=False), with a t reference law.

Example 4Did the mean change in June 2015?

Before 1 June 2015 TR23's mean was 0.0464% a day, with a t-stat of 2.05; from that date it was 0.0804%, with 3.68. The standard errors are 0.0227% and 0.0219% (Exhibit 1). Treat the regimes as independent groups. Is the difference significant at 5%?

Solution

The standard error of the difference is 0.02272+0.02192​=0.0315%. So (21.3) gives (0.0804−0.0464)/0.0315=1.08, a two-sided p-value of 0.28; SciPy's t reference gives 0.280. One period passes 1% and the other 5%, yet the difference between them is not significant. Stability check.

A change in variance between the regimes needs LM20's standard error of a volatility, and volatility clustering needs LM22's blocks of days. That standard error needs a finite fourth moment, which LM15's Student t fit (ν=3.065) lacks, so it is nominal. LM20 gives the kurtosis no standard error at all.

PitfallSignificant in one period, not in the other

"Significant after 2015, not before" does not show that the mean changed. Test the difference itself: Example 4 gives 1.08.

Knowledge Check 2

A strategy averages 0.04% a day with su​=1% over 900 days. Find its t-stat. With the same mean and su​, how many days give a t-stat of 2?

Answer

By (21.1), 0.04/(1/900​)=1.20. A t-stat of 2 needs T​=2/0.04=50, that is 2,500 days, about ten years.

4Power and the Years a Sharpe Ratio Needs

calculate the power of a test of a mean and the years of data needed to detect a given annual Sharpe ratio

Let SR=A​μ/σ be the annual Sharpe ratio of iid returns with known σ and rf​=0. Over τ years, T=Aτ, so the statistic T​Rˉ/σ is approximately N(T​μ/σ,1), and T​μ/σ=SRτ​. The two-sided test rejects when ∣t-stat∣≥Φ−1(1−α/2), and (5.5) gives the probability of each tail.

ResultPower of the test of a mean

Let returns be iid with known σ and annual Sharpe ratio SR (rf​=0), observed for τ years. At level α, the two-sided test of μ=0 has

powerτ​≈Φ(SRτ​−Φ−1(1−2α​))+Φ(−SRτ​−Φ−1(1−2α​)),≈(SRΦ−1(1−2α​)+Φ−1(power)​)2,​(21.4)
the second when the first line's second term is negligible.

LM19 annualizes a daily Sharpe ratio by A​, a convention on simple returns; even for log returns it needs uncorrelated days (Lo 2002). With s in place of σ the identity is exact on data: T​Rˉ/s=SRτ​.

Example 5Years of data for 80% power

How many years of daily data give 80% power at the 5% level against annual Sharpe ratios of 1, 0.5 and 0.25? And against TR23's own, LM19's 246.12​×0.0625%/1.2084%=0.81?

Solution

With Φ−1(0.975)=1.959964 and Φ−1(0.8)=0.841621, (21.4) gives τ=(2.801585/SR)2: 7.85, 31.4 and 125.6 years. TR23's ratio, 0.8115 to four decimals, needs 11.9 years. Its t-stat is 0.8115×23.7645​=3.956, by the identity above. If 0.81 were the true ratio, TR23's 23.76 years would give power 0.98; at 0.5 the power would be only 0.68 (Exhibit 3). Model.

PitfallMore frequent data do not buy power

Power depends on SRτ​, the span in years, not on the number of observations. Hourly returns over the same 23.76 years would not shrink the standard error of the annual mean; LM20 shows why (Merton 1980).

導讀檢定力：為什麼 23.8 年還不夠

檢定力（power）是對立假設（alternative hypothesis）為真時，檢定（test）拒絕虛無假設（null hypothesis）的機率（probability）。平均報酬（mean return）的 t 統計量（t-statistic）約等於年化夏普比率（annualized Sharpe ratio）乘以資料年數的平方根，所以標準誤（standard error）只隨年數縮小，與每天或每小時抽樣（sampling）無關。在 5% 的顯著水準（significance level）下要有 80% 的檢定力，夏普比率為 0.5 需要 31.4 年的日資料，0.25 更需要 125.6 年。加權股價報酬指數有 23.8 年資料，對 0.5 的檢定力也只有 0.68。回測（backtest）幾年就宣稱找到優勢，多半還分不出真假。

Knowledge Check 3

At an annual Sharpe ratio of 0.5, what power does the two-sided 5% test have with ten years of data? How many years make the expected t-stat equal to 3 at SR=1 and at SR=0.5?

5Testing Frequencies and Independence

test a frequency against a binomial model and the independence of a 2×2 table with the χ2 test, and explain what dependence between days does to both tests

LM13 counted 1,226 down days after a down day, against 1,195.0 under independence: 31 days in 5,848. Is the gap larger than chance? Exhibit 4 tests it and one more gap.

ResultThe chi-square test of a 2×2 table

Classify n pairs by two indicators, with cells n11​, n12​, n21​, n22​, row totals n1⋅​, n2⋅​ and column totals n⋅1​, n⋅2​. By (13.6), the expected count of a cell under independence is its row total times its column total over n. Then

χ2:=cells∑​expected(observed−expected)2​=nρ^​2,​(21.5)
with ρ^​ the correlation of the two indicators over the n pairs. If the pairs are iid and the two indicators independent, χ2 is approximately χ12​ for large n.

For one series on consecutive days, ρ^​ is, up to its first and last day, LM19's lag-1 autocorrelation ρ^​1​ of the indicator series. Under independent days T​ρ^​1​ is approximately standard normal; this is LM19's band (19.6). So nρ^​2 is approximately a squared standard normal, which is χ12​ by LM16's definition. For two series on the same days, the χ12​ law is stated without proof.

Deep DiveWhy the 2×2 χ2 is nρ^​2optional · click to expand

Let D:=n11​n22​−n12​n21​. Cell (1,1) has observed minus expected n11​−n1⋅​n⋅1​/n=D/n, because nn11​−(n11​+n12​)(n11​+n21​)=D. The fixed margins force the other three cells to ±D/n as well. The reciprocals of the four expected counts sum to

​n1⋅​n⋅1​n​+n1⋅​n⋅2​n​+n2⋅​n⋅1​n​+n2⋅​n⋅2​n​=n1⋅​n2⋅​n⋅1​n⋅2​n3​,​
since n1⋅​+n2⋅​=n⋅1​+n⋅2​=n. Hence χ2=nD2/(n1⋅​n2⋅​n⋅1​n⋅2​). The covariance of the two indicators is D/n2, and their variances are n1⋅​n2⋅​/n2 and n⋅1​n⋅2​/n2 (LM16). So ρ^​=D/n1⋅​n2⋅​n⋅1​n⋅2​​, and χ2=nρ^​2.

Example 6Down after down

Test the independence of TR23's signs on LM13's 5,848 pairs. There were 1,226 downs after a down; yesterday's margins are 2,643 down and 3,205 up, and today's 2,644 and 3,204.

Solution

The expected counts are 1,194.95, 1,448.05, 1,449.05 and 1,755.95, as exact fractions. Each observed count differs from its expected count by 31.05. Then (21.5) gives χ2=2.69, or 5,848×0.021432, with ρ^​=0.0214, LM15's ρ1​. Its χ12​ upper tail is a p-value of 0.10: no evidence against independent signs at 5%. SciPy's chi2_contingency applies Yates' continuity correction by default and prints 2.60, with a p-value of 0.107.

Example 7Falls after falls

On the pair space, 108 days follow a fall of 3% or more, and 13 of them fell 3% or more too (LM13). Test whether such falls are independent of the day before.

Solution

Under independence each of the 108 days falls with probability θ0​=108/5,848. The count is then Bin(108,θ0​), with mean 1082/5,848=2.0. The exact one-sided tail is Pr(X≥13)=1.2×10−7. Large moves cluster, so the days are not independent. The null laws of Example 2 and Example 6 hold for the signs only approximately.

Clustering of large moves breaks the count tests of extreme days, such as Example 7's and LM16's. A count is a sum of indicators, and (16.3) adds twice their covariances to their variances. Clustered extreme days make those covariances positive, so the count spreads more than the binomial says and the test rejects too often. LM22's block bootstrap is the tool for this, and LM26 models the clustering.

On U28, LM16 found 177 days outside the normal model's whitened circle of radius 3, against a binomial mean of 48.68 and standard deviation of 6.94. From those printed counts, z=(177−48.68)/6.94=18.5. Clustering would have to widen the spread 6.2-fold, to 42.8 days, to bring z down to 3.

LM16's five-sector counts read as 4,382 tests on χ52​ at the 1% level, one a day: 43.8 rejections expected, 291 found. Its crash correlation, 0.788 against the model's 0.860, needs the sampling law of a conditional correlation, which LM22's block bootstrap estimates.

PitfallTwo-proportion tests on clustered days

Comparing the share of extreme days in two regimes, as LM16's lab does, invites a test of two proportions. That test treats clustered days as independent, so its p-value is too small; LM22's block bootstrap is the tool for this.

Knowledge Check 4

In 10,000 day pairs, how large must ∣ρ^​∣ be for the 2×2 χ2 to exceed the χ12​ 5% point, 3.84?

Answer

By (21.5), 10,000ρ^​2>3.84 needs ∣ρ^​∣>0.0196, which is 1.96/n​. The χ2 test and the two-sided band (19.6) agree.

6Many Tests at Once

compute the family-wise error rate and the expected number of false rejections in a family of tests, and apply the Bonferroni correction

Wednesday was not a hypothesis fixed in advance. It was found by sorting the days, so its p-value belongs to a family: 12 months and 5 weekdays, each tested against all other TR23 days with (21.3). That makes 17 tests (Exhibit 5).

TR23 also holds 23 Saturday sessions (2006–2018), which belong to no weekday and to every "rest". TWSE's history page states that the market stopped trading on Saturday make-up workdays from 2019; that the 23 sessions were such days is an inference.

If every null were true, (14.1) and (14.3) would make the family expect 17×0.05=0.85 false rejections at 5%, whatever the dependence. One test rejects: Wednesday's, at 2.36. February (0.1405% a day, 1.33) and December (0.1270%, 1.53) have the highest monthly means (Exhibit 5).

The family-wise error rate (FWER) is the probability of at least one false rejection in the family. For 17 independent tests of true nulls at 5%, (13.7) gives 1−0.9517=0.58.

ResultBonferroni's correction

Let m tests include m0​ true nulls, and run each test at level α/m. Then, under any dependence,

FWER≤m0​mα​≤α.(21.6)
For m independent tests of true nulls, each of size α (as under a continuous null law), FWER=1−(1−α)m. Under any dependence, the expected number of false rejections is the sum of the true nulls' sizes, at most m0​ times each test's level.

The bound is (13.2) over the m0​ events "true null i is rejected", each of probability at most α/m. The independent case is (13.7), and the expected count is (14.1) with (14.3). For the calendar, Bonferroni's level is 0.05/17=0.00294, or ∣z∣≥2.974. Equivalently, multiply each p-value by m and compare with α.

Solution

The tests are dependent, because the months partition the days, and so do the weekdays. Experiment 8 shuffles the returns over TR23's fixed dates, so every group keeps its size. 導讀一次做很多個檢定

多重假設檢定（multiple testing）的問題是：檢定（test）做得越多，純屬偶然的「顯著」（significant）結果就越多。17 個檢定各在 5% 下進行，若虛無假設（null hypothesis）全為真，平均會有 0.85 個錯誤拒絕（false rejection）。若檢定彼此獨立（independent），至少錯一次的機率（probability），也就是族系誤差率（family-wise error rate），高達 0.58。星期三的 p值（p-value）0.018 是 17 個裡最小的，放回這 17 個檢定之中就不稀奇：打亂日期之後，有 0.266 的機會出現至少這麼極端的結果。Bonferroni 校正（Bonferroni correction）把每個檢定的顯著水準（significance level）降為 0.05/17，不論檢定是否獨立，族系誤差率都不超過 5%。

7Holm and the False Discovery Rate

apply Holm's procedure and the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure, and state which error each controls and under which condition

Bonferroni gives every test the same level. Holm's rule starts at the same level and relaxes it after each rejection.

ResultHolm's procedure

Order the p-values, p(1)​≤⋯≤p(m)​, and let iH​ be the largest i such that

p(j)​≤m−j+1α​for every j≤i.(21.7)
Reject the hypotheses of p(1)​,…,p(iH​)​, and none if p(1)​ misses. Then FWER≤α under any dependence (Holm 1979).

Holm compares p(1)​ with α/m, then p(2)​ with α/(m−1), and stops at the first miss. Its levels are never below Bonferroni's.

Deep DiveWhy Holm controls the family-wise error rateoptional · click to expand

Suppose Holm rejects a true null, and look at the first true null it rejects, at rank i. The i−1 hypotheses rejected before it are false nulls, so i−1≤m−m0​, that is m−i+1≥m0​. This true null has the smallest p-value among the true nulls, and it passed: that p-value is at most α/(m−i+1)≤α/m0​.

So a false rejection by Holm requires some true null with a p-value at most α/m0​. Each true null's p-value is that small with probability at most α/m0​. By (13.2) the event has probability at most m0​⋅α/m0​=α, whatever the dependence.

When many tests run, avoiding every false rejection can cost too many true ones. The false discovery rate (FDR) is the expected share of false rejections among all rejections, counted as zero when nothing is rejected.

ResultThe Benjamini–Hochberg procedure

Order the p-values and let

iBH​:=max{i: p(i)​≤miq​},(21.8)
or 0 if no rank passes. Reject the hypotheses of p(1)​,…,p(iBH​)​. For independent test statistics the FDR is then at most q (stated without proof; Benjamini and Hochberg 1995).

The rule steps up: it looks for the largest rank that passes, so a p-value above its own level can still be rejected. When every null is true, every rejection is false, and the FDR equals the FWER (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995). SciPy's false_discovery_control with method="by" (Benjamini and Yekutieli 2001, Theorem 1.3) runs (21.8) with q divided by ∑i=1m​1/i (3.44 for m=17), and controls the FDR under any dependence. LM23 applies these corrections to 28 sector alphas.

Example 9Three rules on ten p-values

Ten tests give the p-values 0.002, 0.0054, 0.0061, 0.011, 0.024, 0.031, 0.09, 0.22, 0.47 and 0.86 (constructed). At α=q=5%, how many hypotheses do Bonferroni, Holm and Benjamini–Hochberg reject?

Solution

Bonferroni's level is 0.05/10=0.005, and only 0.002 passes: 1 rejection. Holm's first four levels are 0.005, 0.00556, 0.00625 and 0.00714. The p-values 0.002, 0.0054 and 0.0061 pass, and 0.011 misses: 3 rejections.

Benjamini–Hochberg's levels iq/m run 0.005, 0.010, …, 0.050. The fifth rank passes, 0.024≤0.025, and every later one misses, from 0.031>0.030 and 0.09>0.035 onwards: 5 rejections.

On the calendar, p(1)​=0.018 exceeds 0.00294, the first level of Bonferroni and Holm at 5%, so neither rejects anything. Benjamini–Hochberg steps up, so every rank must be checked: each p(i)​ exceeds its level iq/m, at least threefold even at q=10% (Exhibit 6). Its adjusted p-values, minj≥i​mp(j)​/j (at most 1), start at 0.308.

PitfallChoosing the rule after seeing the p-values

Benjamini–Hochberg rejects at least as many hypotheses as Holm, and Holm at least as many as Bonferroni. Picking the rule that delivers the hoped-for result is one more search: fix the family, the error and the level before the data. Benjamini–Hochberg's guarantee also needs independent statistics. The calendar's 17 are dependent, though the shuffle shows that the FWER barely moves (0.580 against 0.582).

導讀偽發現率：控制錯誤的比例

族系誤差率（family-wise error rate）要求一個都不能錯，檢定（test）一多就太嚴，真正的效果也會被漏掉。偽發現率（false discovery rate）改為控制錯誤拒絕（false rejection）在所有拒絕中所占的比例：拒絕 20 個、錯 1 個，比例是 5%。Benjamini–Hochberg 的方法把排序後第 i 小的 p值（p-value）和 iq/m 比較，在各檢定彼此獨立（independent）時，偽發現率不超過 q。Holm 的方法從最小的 p值往上逐一比較，遇到第一個不通過就停；不論檢定是否獨立，都能控制族系誤差率。月份與星期的 17 個檢定，三種方法都一個也不拒絕。

8Searching Many Strategies: the Hurdle of 3

explain why the largest t-stat of a search grows with the number of strategies tried, and interpret the t > 3 hurdle

A strategy search is a family too, usually a large one whose members go unreported. Model it as m tests whose null statistics are independent and standard normal. The largest of them exceeds a level when any one does, and (16.1) multiplies the probabilities that none does.

ResultThe largest of m null statistics

For independent standard normal Z1​,…,Zm​ and every x,

Pr(imax​Zi​≤x)=Φ(x)m.(21.9)
The largest has median Φ−1(0.51/m) and u quantile Φ−1(u1/m).

The median of the best grows with m: 1.75 at m=17, 2.46 at 100, 3.20 at 1,000 and 3.81 at 10,000. At m=1,000 its middle 90% runs from 2.75 to 3.88, and it exceeds 3 with probability 0.74 (Exhibit 7).

Example 10Ten thousand random rules on TAIEX

Experiment 10 draws every position in one call on default_rng([10, 20261011]), integers(0, 2, size=(10000, T), dtype=np.int8), with 1 for long and 0 for short. Rule i of the 10,000 is long or short one unit each day by this fair coin, fi,t​=±1. Its daily return is fi,t​Rt​, and (21.1) gives its t-stat with μnull​=0. How large is the best t-stat?

Solution

The 10,000 t-stats average −0.01 (standard error 0.01), with a standard deviation of 0.995 (batch standard error 0.007, from 20 batches of 500, LM18). They behave like standard normal draws. 13 reach 3, against 10,000Φ(−3)=13.5 expected.

The best after 100 rules is 2.95 (rule 90), and it is still 2.95 after 1,000. Rule 1,022 reaches 3.28, and the best of all 10,000 is 3.97 (rule 7,840). This is one search, a single draw with no standard error. Under (21.9) the best of 1,000 has median 3.20 and exceeds 3 with probability 0.74. Model on TR23's returns.

Harvey, Liu and Zhu (2016) argue that a new factor needs "a t-statistic greater than 3.0". The two-sided p-value at ∣t-stat∣=3 is 2Φ(−3)=0.0027, Bonferroni's level for about 18.5 tests at 5%. So a hurdle of 3 corrects for a search of about 18.5 tests, while the median best of 1,000 null rules already passes it. TR23's mean clears both bars: its t-stat, 3.96, exceeds 3 and Bonferroni's 3.134 for this module's 29 tests.

LM4 warned that backtests overstate Sharpe ratios. LM33 deflates a Sharpe ratio for the number of trials, and LM34 tests the best rule of a search on later data (White 2000; Romano and Wolf 2005).

導讀資料偵伺：最好的那一個為什麼不可信

資料偵伺（data snooping）是在同一份資料上試很多策略，只報告最好的那一個。就算每個策略都毫無預測力，最好的 t 統計量（t-statistic）也會隨嘗試的數目變大：在獨立（independent）的情形下，1,000 個裡最好的中位數（median）是 3.20。在加權股價報酬指數上用擲硬幣決定每天作多（long）或放空（short），10,000 條隨機規則中最好的 t 統計量達到 3.97。Harvey、Liu 和 Zhu 主張新因子（factor）的 t 統計量要超過 3，這只相當於對 18.5 個檢定（test）做 Bonferroni 校正（Bonferroni correction）。看到漂亮的回測（backtest），先問它試了幾個。

run these tests in Python by hand and with SciPy and statsmodels, checking each function's defaults

Python
import numpy as np
import polars as pl
from scipy import stats
from statsmodels.stats.multitest import multipletests

tr = pl.read_csv("data/taiex_tr.csv")["price"].to_numpy()
R = tr[1:] / tr[:-1] - 1                     # T = 5,849 simple returns

print(2 * stats.norm.sf(abs(tstat)))         # 7.6e-05, normal
print(2 * stats.t.sf(abs(tstat), T - 1))     # 7.7e-05, t with T - 1
print(stats.binom.sf(3205 - 1, T, 0.5))      # 1.2e-13, up days
print(stats.chi2.sf(2.69, 1))                # 0.10, signs, (21.5)
p = np.array([0.002, 0.0054, 0.0061, 0.011, 0.024,
              0.031, 0.09, 0.22, 0.47, 0.86])
for method in ("bonferroni", "holm", "fdr_bh"):  # 1, 3, 5
    print(method, multipletests(p, alpha=0.05, method=method)[0].sum())
print((stats.false_discovery_control(p) <= 0.05).sum())  # 5, BH

Family-wise error rate 族系誤差率 The probability of at least one false rejection in a family, (21.6).
Bonferroni correction Bonferroni 校正 Running each of m tests at level α/m, (21.6).
False discovery rate 偽發現率 The expected share of false rejections among all rejections, zero if none, (21.8).
Data snooping 資料偵伺 Searching many models or rules on one data set and reporting the best, (21.9).
