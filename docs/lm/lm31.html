When means cannot be trusted, allocate by risk alone: equal risk contributions, a tree of correlations, and a book scaled by a volatility forecast.

Learning Module Overview
  • With two funds, equal risk contributions (ERC) give inverse-volatility weights whatever the correlation. With five they do not: on the first window inverse volatility leaves the bond fund 00679B only 9.8% of the risk.
  • A long-only risk-budget portfolio exists and is unique for a positive definite covariance matrix and positive budgets: it is the normalized minimizer of a strictly convex function. Newton's method finds R6's first weights in 6 steps and coordinate descent in 12 sweeps; a fixed point with equal shares settles at negative weights.
  • HRP links funds by single linkage on the distance between columns of (1−ρij​)/2​. Linking on that distance itself changes the quasi-diagonal order at 17 of 92 signals.
  • On U28's second half, with weights from the first, HRP's volatility, 14.96%, is the lowest of 1/N and the three risk-based portfolios, and above both minimum-variance portfolios.
  • Out of sample R6 and R7 had volatilities of 10.32% and 10.14%. The mean of R6's target weights carried 18.0% to 23.1% of the realized risk, not 20% each.
  • R8 scales R6 by min(1.5, σˉt​/σt+1∣t​): its exposure averaged 1.14 over 92 trades and was at the cap at 14 of them.
  • Over 91 holding months the median ratio of realized to target volatility rose from R6's 0.83 to R8's 0.98, while the SD of its logarithm stayed at 0.421. R8 entered April 2025 at 1.33 and realized 5.78 times its target.
  • The EWMA forecast beat the window's volatility in 56 of 91 holding months, yet its mean QLIKE was the higher, 0.791 against 0.691, because of one holding month.

1Introduction

LM27 split a book's volatility into risk contributions. LM28 to LM30 traded mean against variance, and their minimum-variance portfolios used the covariance matrix alone. This module uses risk alone in three new ways. It gives each fund a chosen share of the risk, builds a portfolio from a tree of correlations, and scales the whole book by a volatility forecast.

Motivating CaseEqual risk, then a forecast

An investor who cannot trust estimated means can still share risk evenly. On 31 March 2025 rule R6 did that for E5's five funds (LM27). It put 30.7% in the bond fund 00679B and 12.0% in the oil-futures fund 00642U, so each fund carried a fifth of the risk. Rule R8 then scales R6 by a forecast.

R6's book had been calm: its EWMA forecast was 5.89% a year, against 7.84% over the 504 joint dates before it. So on 1 April R8 held 1.33 times its equity in the funds, borrowing the extra 0.33 at 3.5% a year. On 7 April, the first session after the Qingming holiday, the forecast jumped to 23.58%, an implied exposure of 0.33. R8 trades only after a month-end, so it kept its leverage until 2 May.

Over that holding month, 2 April to 2 May (Section 7), its realized volatility was 5.78 times its target (Exhibit 1). That ratio was the worst of all 91. Does a forecast keep risk steady? And what does an equal share of risk buy?

Sections 2 to 5 treat R6 and R7, Sections 6 and 7 treat R8, and Section 8 the code.

2Risk Contributions as Targets

derive the budget conditions (31.1) and show when ERC gives inverse-volatility weights
explain what risk-based weights assume, and why risk shares set in advance differ after the fact

On the first window, 13 February 2017 to 27 February 2019, R1 held 20% of each fund. Yet 00642U carried 50.2% of its risk (Exhibit 2), as LM27's note on its trailing windows also shows. For σp​>0, (27.5) and (27.6) give

MRCi​=σp​(Σw)i​​,RCi​=wi​MRCi​,i∑​RCi​=σp​.​

Here MRCi​ is LM7's marginal variance divided by 2σp​, and RCi​/σp​ is fund i's risk share.

DefinitionRisk budgets

Let Σ be positive definite, and let budgets bi​>0 sum to 1. A fully invested portfolio, 1⊤w=1, meets them when RCi​=bi​σp​ for every i. Multiplying by σp​ and using (27.6), that is

wi​(Σw)i​=bi​w⊤Σw,i=1,…,N.(31.1)
The risk-budget portfolio is the long-only solution, w≥0, which exists and is unique by (31.2), Section 3. Equal risk contributions (ERC) take bi​=1/N.

Example 1Two funds

On the first window 0050 and the bond fund 00679B have volatilities 13.50% and 8.54% and correlation −0.30 (LM28). Find their ERC weights.

Solution

With two funds, (31.1) at bi​=21​ reads

w1​(w1​σ12​+w2​σ12​)=w2​(w1​σ12​+w2​σ22​).
The covariance terms cancel, so w12​σ12​=w22​σ22​, and positive weights give w1​σ1​=w2​σ2​ whatever ρ. So w1​=8.54/(13.50+8.54)=0.3875 and w2​=0.6125, with risk shares 50/50. LM7's minimum-variance weight (7.7) puts 0.331 in 0050, at 6.1% against ERC's 6.2%.

ResultWhen ERC is inverse volatility (Maillard, Roncalli and Teïletche 2010)

Let Σ be positive definite. The inverse-volatility weights

wi​=∑j​1/σj​1/σi​​(31.3)
are an ERC portfolio for two assets, whatever their correlation, and for N assets whose correlations all equal ρ, with −1/(N−1)<ρ<1. By (31.2), Section 3, they are then the only long-only one.

Proof. Two assets are Example 1; at ρ=−1 the book's σp​ would be 0. With one correlation ρ and the weights (31.3),

(Σw)i​=j∑​ρij​σi​σj​wj​=∑j​1/σj​σi​(1+(N−1)ρ)​,

so wi​(Σw)i​ is the same for every i. The correlation matrix has eigenvalues 1+(N−1)ρ and 1−ρ (LM11), so Σ is positive definite exactly when −1/(N−1)<ρ<1. ■

By (29.5), each fund that long-only minimum variance holds has the rate (Σw)i​=σp2​, so its beta βi,p​ is 1 and its share wi​βi,p​ is its weight. ERC equalizes the products wi​(Σw)i​ instead. The rate (Σw)i​ is half of LM10's gradient entry, and a share is a weight times that rate over σp2​. Maillard, Roncalli and Teïletche (2010) order the volatilities:

σmv​≤σerc​≤σ1/N​,

with σmv​ the volatility of long-only minimum variance (stated without proof; their 2009 working paper, Appendix A.3). Bruder and Roncalli (2012, §2.3.4, p. 10) put a risk-budget portfolio between minimum variance and the budgets used as weights (stated without proof; their Appendix A.3).

Example 2Five funds on the first window

The five funds' volatilities are 13.50, 11.87, 8.54, 9.39 and 23.17% (0050, 00646, 00679B, 00635U, 00642U; LM28). Compare inverse volatility with ERC.

Solution

Inverse-volatility weights are 0.174, 0.198, 0.275, 0.251 and 0.102. With them every wi​σi​ is equal, so each share is proportional to the fund's row sum of correlations, as in the proof of (31.3). 00679B correlates −0.30 and −0.26 with the two equity funds, so its row sum is the lowest and its share is 9.8%. 00642U's row sum, the highest, gives 24.8%. R6 holds 0.1561, 0.1828, 0.3386, 0.2372 and 0.0854 (rounded separately; 1.0001 as printed), and each fund carries 20%.

The volatilities, 5.02% for R3, 5.45% for R6 and 7.38% for R1 (R3's and R1's from LM28), follow Maillard et al.'s order. R3 equals (28.2)'s wmv here, since every weight of wmv is positive. With every correlation set to the mean, ρˉ​=0.0533, ERC equals inverse volatility. All use the window's unrounded Σ^.

PitfallA risk share is neither a weight nor a share of losses

R6 holds 33.9% in 00679B, which carries 20% of its risk. A risk share splits one covariance matrix's variance before the fact. It is no share of a day's loss, and it moves afterwards (Section 5).

導讀風險預算：每檔基金分擔指定的風險

權重相等不代表風險相等：第一個視窗裡，等權重投資組合有一半的風險來自石油期貨基金。風險貢獻（risk contribution）把投資組合波動率拆成各資產的部分，加總剛好是投資組合波動率。風險預算（risk budget）事先指定每檔資產分擔的比例，每檔都是 1/N 時稱為等風險貢獻（equal risk contribution，ERC），是最常見的風險平價投資組合（risk-parity portfolio）。本模組的風險預算是分給每一檔資產的份額，LM7 的風險預算則是兩個策略合計的波動率上限，兩者不同。最小變異數投資組合（minimum-variance portfolio）讓持有資產的 (Σw)i​ 相等，ERC 讓權重乘上它的乘積相等，所以低波動又與股票負相關的債券基金權重很大，風險份額（risk share）卻只有五分之一。

Knowledge Check 1

On the first window R3 holds 41.4% in 00679B, and 41.4% of its risk is there (LM28). R6 holds 33.9% in 00679B, yet that fund carries only 20% of R6's risk. Why less than its weight?

Answer

Minimum variance equalizes the rates (Σw)i​ of held funds, (29.5), so each share equals its weight; ERC equalizes the products wi​(Σw)i​. 00679B's rate is low, since it is the calmest fund (8.5% a year, LM28) and moves against the equity funds. So a large weight buys a fifth of the risk.

3Budgets: Existence, Uniqueness and Computation

prove that a long-only risk-budget portfolio exists and is unique when Σ is positive definite
compute equal-risk and risk-budget weights by Newton's method and coordinate descent, and check them with cvxpy

A change of variables makes the N nonlinear equations (31.1) the first-order conditions of a convex problem.

TheoremThe risk-budget portfolio (Bruder and Roncalli 2012, §2.3.3, pp. 9–10)

Let h be the function on the open set y>0 given by

h(y)=21​y⊤Σy−i∑​bi​lnyi​,
and suppose that

  1. the matrix Σ is positive definite;
  2. every budget is positive, bi​>0;
  3. the budgets sum to 1.

Then h has exactly one minimizer y∗, and its normalized form

w∗=1⊤y∗y∗​(31.2)
is the only long-only solution of (31.1).

Proof. The Hessian of h is Σ+diag(bi​/yi2​), which is positive definite on y>0. So h is strictly convex there by (29.2) and has at most one minimizer by (29.3)'s argument; one exists (Deep Dive). At it the gradient Σy−b/y vanishes (b/y entry by entry), so

yi​(Σy)i​=bi​,y⊤Σy=i∑​bi​=1.

Dividing both by (1⊤y∗)2 shows that w∗ satisfies (31.1). Conversely, let a long-only w with 1⊤w=1 solve (31.1). Each wi​(Σw)i​=bi​w⊤Σw is then positive, so every wi​>0, and y=w/w⊤Σw​ has yi​(Σy)i​=bi​. So the gradient of h vanishes there, and a stationary point of a convex function is its minimizer, by (29.2)'s tangent inequality. So y=y∗ and w=w∗; Bruder and Roncalli only sketch this argument. ■

Deep DiveDeep Dive: why the minimizer existsoptional · click to expand

By (11.4), y⊤Σy is at least ∥y∥2 times the smallest eigenvalue of Σ, which is positive. Each yi​ is at most ∥y∥ and the budgets sum to 1, so −∑i​bi​lnyi​≥−ln∥y∥. Hence h→∞ as ∥y∥→∞, and also as any yi​→0+ on a bounded set. So the sublevel set {y>0:h(y)≤h(1)} is closed and bounded, and the continuous h attains a minimum on it (LM11's extreme value theorem).

Newton's method, LM7's in five variables, solves a linear system for each step Δ:

(Σ+diag(bi​/yi2​))Δ=−(Σy−b/y).

The rules' erc() starts at yi​=1/σi​ rescaled to y⊤Σy=1/N. It halves a step until y>0 and, while the largest gap ∣yi​(Σy)i​−bi​∣ exceeds 10−6, until h falls enough. It stops when that gap is below 10−13.

Cyclical coordinate descent (CCD; Griveau-Billion, Richard and Roncalli 2013) minimizes h along one yi​ at a time. With the others fixed, yi​∂h/∂yi​=0 is a quadratic whose positive root is the update:

σi2​yi2​+(j=i∑​σij​yj​)yi​−bi​=0,yi​←2σi2​(∑j=i​σij​yj​)2+4σi2​bi​​−∑j=i​σij​yj​​.​(31.4)

The sum is (Σy)i​−σi2​yi​. A sweep updates y1​ to yN​ in turn, each update lowers h, and the sweeps converge (Tseng 2001, Theorem 5.1, as Griveau-Billion et al. cite it; stated without proof).

A fixed point. At the solution wi​βi,p​=bi​ by (27.6), so wi​∝bi​/βi,p​ (Bruder and Roncalli, eq. (7), p. 9). Iterating that property gives the fixed-point iteration that Griveau-Billion et al. attribute to Chaves et al. (2012); it is no descent of h. cvxpy minimizes h as an exponential-cone program, a cone program of the kind LM29 names but does not develop. CLARABEL solves that program to about 10−6, so it checks Newton to 10−5.

PitfallA fixed point is not an algorithm

The property wi​∝bi​/βi,p​ holds at the solution, but iterating it need not settle, and it can settle at negative weights. Newton's method and CCD descend a strictly convex h. Check an answer by its risk shares and signs, not by a solver's status.

導讀先保證唯一，再談演算法

風險預算條件 (31.1) 是一組非線性方程式，本身不保證只有一個解。把問題改寫成在 y>0 上最小化 h(y) 後，嚴格凸函數（strictly convex function）h 的最小值點最多只有一個；任一 yi​ 趨近 0 或 ∥y∥ 趨近無窮大時 h 都趨近無窮大，所以最小值點一定存在（見 Deep Dive）。把這個最小值點正規化，就得到只作多（long-only）的風險預算投資組合（risk-budget portfolio），而且只作多的解只有這一個，也就是唯一性（uniqueness）。牛頓法（Newton's method）與座標下降法（coordinate descent）每一步都讓 h 下降；座標下降法的收斂引用 Tseng（2001）的定理，未加證明。不動點（fixed point）迭代 wi​∝bi​/βi,p​ 不是下降法，可能停在含負權重的解，也可能一直不收斂。檢查答案要看風險份額（risk share）是否等於預算、權重是否全為正，不能只看 solver 回報的狀態。

Knowledge Check 2

R2 holds no 00642U. Can its weights serve as risk budgets for all five funds?

Answer

No. A zero budget removes that fund's log term from h, so (31.1) can have several solutions (Bruder and Roncalli 2012, §2.2, pp. 4–5), and theorem (31.2) does not apply. CCD's update (31.4) still runs, but it sets that yi​ to 0 whenever (Σy)i​−σi2​yi​≥0, outside y>0. Drop the fund instead (Example 4).

4Hierarchical Risk Parity

build an HRP portfolio from a correlation matrix, and explain why implementations can give different trees

Hierarchical risk parity (HRP; López de Prado 2016) needs no optimizer and no inverse of Σ. It builds a tree of the assets from their correlations, orders the assets along it, and splits the weight from the top down. Its distance is half of LM8's per-date distance in (8.10):

dij​=21​(1−ρij​)​=21​2(1−ρij​)​,column distance ∥di​−dj​∥.​(31.5)

LM8's distance is Euclidean, so dij​ satisfies the triangle inequality (8.8). HRP clusters on the column distance, the norm (8.5) of the difference between columns i and j of (dij​). Assets are close when their distances to all assets are alike.

  1. Tree. Single linkage merges the two closest clusters until one is left, the distance between clusters being the smallest distance between their members. Numbered from 1 in SciPy's order, the assets are 1 to N and the clusters N+1, N+2, … as they form; each merge lists its lower-numbered member first.
  2. Order. From the root down, replace each cluster by its two members, in that order. This quasi-diagonal order puts correlated assets side by side.
  3. Bisection. Start with weight 1 on the ordered list. Split every list of n>1 items into its first ⌊n/2⌋ items and the rest, until single assets remain. Each half's inverse-variance portfolio, weights in proportion to 1/σi2​, has variance σ(1)2​ or σ(2)2​.

Each split multiplies the weights of the two halves by

σ(1)2​+σ(2)2​σ(2)2​​andσ(1)2​+σ(2)2​σ(1)2​​.(31.6)

The calmer half gets more, and inverse variance is minimum variance for a diagonal covariance matrix (López de Prado, Appendix A.2). Single linkage only compares distances, so the factor 2 to LM8's distance doubles the heights and changes no tree. Other linkages, and splits along the tree's clusters (an option López de Prado notes), are not in this book.

Example 5HRP for three funds by hand

Build HRP for 0050, 00646 and 00679B on the first window, whose correlations are 0.54, −0.30 and −0.26 (LM28).

Solution

By (31.5), d is 0.480, 0.806 and 0.794, and three decimals are kept from here on. The column distances are 0.679 (0050, 00646), 1.182 (0050, 00679B) and 1.169 (00646, 00679B). So 0050 and 00646 merge at 0.679, 00679B joins at min(1.182,1.169)=1.169, and the order is (00679B, 0050, 00646).

The first cut is [00679B] | [0050, 00646], whose inverse-variance portfolios have variances 72.96 and 121.95 (%² a year), so 00679B takes 121.95/(72.96+121.95)=0.6257. In the second cut 0050's 182.29 meets 00646's 140.88, so 0050 takes 0.4359 of the remaining 0.3743. The weights are 0.1632, 0.2111 and 0.6257. The variances use the window's unrounded Σ^: squaring Example 1's 13.50% and 8.54% would give 182.25 and 72.93.

PitfallTwo codes, two trees

The author's code passes the square matrix (dij​) to SciPy's linkage, which reads its rows as observations and warns. So it builds the column-distance tree, at all 92 windows and on U28. Code that passes the condensed dij​ links on d itself, and the quasi-diagonal order differs at 17 of the 92 windows. The bisection cuts the list, not the tree (Example 6).

導讀先分群，再由上而下分配

層次風險平價（hierarchical risk parity，HRP）不需要反矩陣（inverse matrix），也不需要最佳化（optimization）。第一步用相關係數（correlation coefficient）定義距離 dij​，再算距離矩陣各行之間的歐氏距離（Euclidean distance），以單一連接叢集法（single linkage clustering）做層次叢集法（hierarchical clustering），畫成樹形圖（dendrogram）。第二步準對角化（quasi-diagonalization），讓相關高的資產排在一起。第三步遞迴二分（recursive bisection）：把排好的清單從中間切開，風險較低的一半分到較多權重。切的是清單而不是樹，所以樹上最先合併的一對也可能被切開。

LM26's recursion extends to a covariance matrix, and for fixed weights its quadratic form is (26.2) on the book's returns:

Σt+1∣t​w⊤Σt+1∣t​w​=λΣt∣t−1​+(1−λ)Rt​Rt⊤​,=λw⊤Σt∣t−1​w+(1−λ)(w⊤Rt​)2.​(31.8)

The evidence. Moreira and Muir (2017) scale factor returns each month by a constant over the previous calendar month's realized variance. The constant equates the managed volatility with buy-and-hold's over the full sample, 1926–2015 for the market, size, value and momentum. Caps of 1 and 1.5 appear only as robustness checks. Sharpe ratios rise, because expected returns do not rise in proportion to volatility (their abstract).

Harvey et al. study more than 60 assets with daily data from as early as 1926. Their Sharpe ratio improves slightly for equity indexes and credit, hardly for bonds, currencies and commodities. The volatility of volatility falls and the left tail improves for almost all their futures, forwards and credit. For US 10-year Treasury futures the left tail is similar. Neither paper tests E5's funds.

PitfallA target that moves with the book

R8 has no fixed target. It targets R6's own volatility over the last 504 joint dates, which ran from 5.37% to 11.24%. So R8 aims low after calm years and high after a crash.

導讀用預測波動率調整曝險

波動率目標（volatility targeting）在預測波動率低時加碼、高時減碼。R8 的目標不是固定水準，而是 R6 自己過去 504 個共同交易日的波動率；預測來自指數加權移動平均模型（EWMA），兩者的比值就是曝險（exposure），上限 1.5 倍。曝險超過 1 就是槓桿（leverage），借款年息 3.5%；低於 1 的部分是現金，年息 1.5%。Moreira 與 Muir 用上個月的實現變異數（realized variance）縮放，常數由全樣本決定；Harvey 等人以固定 10% 為目標，研究 60 多種資產。這些研究都沒有檢驗 E5 的基金，結果不能直接套用。

8Risk-Based Allocation in Python

compute equal-risk and risk-budget weights by Newton's method and coordinate descent, and check them with cvxpy
build an HRP portfolio from a correlation matrix, and explain why implementations can give different trees
Python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np
from scipy.cluster.hierarchy import leaves_list, linkage
from scipy.spatial.distance import squareform

def ccd(S, b, tol=1e-12):                 # (31.4), S annualized
    y = 1 / np.sqrt(np.diag(S))
    while np.abs(y * (S @ y) - b).max() >= tol:
        for i in range(len(b)):
            r = S[i] @ y - S[i, i] * y[i]  # the other funds' part
            y[i] = (np.sqrt(r * r + 4 * S[i, i] * b[i]) - r) / (2 * S[i, i])
    return y / y.sum()

def newton(S, b):                         # as vol5_rules.erc
    y = 1 / np.sqrt(np.diag(S))
    y *= np.sqrt(1 / (len(b) * (y @ S @ y)))
    h = lambda v: 0.5 * v @ S @ v - b @ np.log(v)
    while np.abs(y * (S @ y) - b).max() >= 1e-13:
        g = S @ y - b / y
        d = np.linalg.solve(S + np.diag(b / y**2), -g)
        t, far = 1.0, np.abs(g * y).max() > 1e-6
        while (y + t * d).min() <= 0 or (
                far and h(y + t * d) > h(y) + 1e-4 * t * (g @ d)):
            t /= 2                         # halve until h falls enough
        y = y + t * d
    return y / y.sum()

# S: the annualized covariance, b: the budgets, x: the window's returns
y = cp.Variable(len(b))                  # the check: h in cvxpy
h = 0.5 * cp.quad_form(y, cp.psd_wrap(S)) - b @ cp.log(y)  # S is PSD
prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(h))
prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, tol_gap_abs=1e-10, tol_gap_rel=1e-10,
           tol_feas=1e-10)
assert prob.status == "optimal" and y.value is not None

C = np.corrcoef(x, rowvar=False)          # x: T x N returns
C = (C + C.T) / 2                         # exactly symmetric
np.fill_diagonal(C, 1.0)
d = np.sqrt(np.clip((1 - C) / 2, 0, None))        # (31.5)
D2 = np.sqrt(((d[:, :, None] - d[:, None, :]) ** 2).sum(axis=0))
D2 = (D2 + D2.T) / 2
np.fill_diagonal(D2, 0.0)
Z = linkage(squareform(D2), method="single")      # checks pass
order = leaves_list(Z)                            # quasi-diagonal
# linkage(d, "single") reads the rows of the square d as observations,
# warns (ClusterWarning) and builds the column-distance tree as well

squareform requires exact symmetry and a zero diagonal, which np.corrcoef does not guarantee.
