Linear Regression

A regression coefficient is a measurement with an error bar. The error bar depends on what the errors do, and one significant intercept among 28 sectors is what chance delivers.

Learning Module Overview
  • OLS solves β^​=(X⊤X)−1X⊤y, (23.1). It is unbiased when the errors have mean zero given X, and its variance is σε2​(X⊤X)−1 when they are also homoskedastic and uncorrelated, (23.2). The estimate sε2​=RSS/(T−d) is unbiased, (23.3).
  • Semiconductor's market model on 4,382 daily price returns has beta 1.207 and intercept 2.10 bp a day (5.16% a year). The beta's standard error is 0.0088 classical, 0.0129 White and 0.0185 Newey–West.
  • A day far from the average return has a large hat value: 7 April 2025 had 21 times the average in FinancialInsurance's 2025 fit, and moved its beta by 2.8 classical standard errors.
  • White's sandwich (23.6) allows heteroskedastic errors. On LM18's model paths the classical standard error is 43% of the true one.
  • Overlapping 20-day sums make residuals autocorrelated (0.953 at lag 1). Newey–West (23.7) allows for it, and a standard error that keeps growing with the lag signals a drifting beta.
  • A raw intercept on price indices mixes Jensen's alpha with interest and dividend terms, (23.8). The halves' betas differ by more than 1.96 Newey–West standard errors in 25 of 28 sectors, and FinancialInsurance's first-half hedge ratio fails in the second half.
  • Of 28 sector intercepts one clears 1.96, against 1.4 expected by chance; Bonferroni, Holm and Benjamini–Hochberg reject none. Correlated sectors keep the expected count but change its spread.
  • An electronics factor named in advance raises Semiconductor's R-squared from 0.8112 to 0.9163. The variance inflation factor (23.9) is 16.7 for the raw pair of regressors and 1.15 for the factor.

1Introduction

LM12 fitted lines by least squares and read their coefficients as geometry. Here the same coefficients are estimates, which another sample would change. Each gets a standard error, which depends on what the model's errors do.

Motivating CaseIs Semiconductor's alpha real?

From 1 December 2008 to 8 October 2026 the TWSE Semiconductor price index rose 38.1-fold, against 10.9-fold for the TAIEX price index. Regress Semiconductor's 4,382 daily returns on the TAIEX's. The slope, the sector's beta, is 1.207. The intercept is 2.10 bp a day, 5.16% a year, with a classical t-stat of 2.10. That clears the familiar 1.96 (Exhibit 1).

Before calling it skill, answer three questions. How sure is the beta? Its standard error is 0.0088 by the textbook formula, 0.0129 by White's and 0.0185 by Newey–West's. The beta was 1.089 in the sample's first half and 1.294 in the second (Newey–West standard errors 0.023 and 0.029). What does the intercept measure, when price indices omit dividends and the regression omits interest? And how many sectors were tried? Of 28, one intercept clears 1.96; chance alone predicts 1.4.

Sections 2–5 build the model and its classical and robust standard errors. Sections 6–8 apply them to betas, hedges, the 28 intercepts and a second factor; Section 9 uses Python.

2The Linear Model and Its Assumptions

write the linear regression model in matrix form, compute OLS estimates, residuals and sε2​=RSS/(T−d), and state the assumptions under which OLS is unbiased and best among linear unbiased estimators

LM12's four points, regressor values 0, 1, 2, 3 and responses y=(1,3,2,6)⊤, gave the line α^=0.9, β^​=1.4. Four other points from the same process would give another line. How far the line can move is a question about a model of the data.

Write the model and its least-squares estimate as

y=Xβ+ε,β^​=(X⊤X)−1X⊤y.(23.1)

Here X is the T×d design with first column 1, and ε is the unobserved error. The estimate is LM12's solution of (12.1), the ordinary least squares (OLS) estimator. It is unique when X has full column rank, so that X⊤X is invertible (LM10).

TheoremMean and variance of OLS

Let y=Xβ+ε, where

  1. the design has full column rank d, and
  2. the errors have conditional mean zero, E[ε∣X]=0.

Then E[β^​∣X]=β. If also 3. the errors are homoskedastic and uncorrelated, Var(ε∣X)=σε2​I,

then

E[β^​∣X]=β,Var(β^​∣X)=σε2​(X⊤X)−1.​(23.2)

Proof. Substituting the model gives β^​−β=(X⊤X)−1X⊤ε, a matrix fixed given X times ε. Its conditional expectation is 0 by condition 2, so E[β^​∣X]=β. By (16.4) and condition 3,

Var(β^​∣X)​=(X⊤X)−1X⊤(σε2​I)X(X⊤X)−1=σε2​(X⊤X)−1.■​

The model itself is the linearity assumption; normal errors are an optional fourth. In a simple regression, d=2, and the slope's entry of (X⊤X)−1 is 1/(Tsx2​), so Var(β^​∣X)=σε2​/(Tsx2​). The variance falls like 1/T, the rate LM18 assumed for a slope's batch errors.

The error variance is unknown. Its estimate divides the residual sum of squares by T−d:

sε2​:=T−dRSS​,E[sε2​∣X]=σε2​.(23.3)

The residual is ε^=(I−Π)y=(I−Π)ε, because ΠX=X. Since I−Π is symmetric and idempotent, RSS=ε⊤(I−Π)ε. Its expectation sums each entry of I−Π times the matching entry of Var(ε∣X). By condition 3 those entries are σε2​ on the diagonal and 0 off it, which gives σε2​tr(I−Π)=σε2​(T−d) by (12.2). For normal errors the maximum-likelihood estimate divides RSS by T, as LM20's normal MLE (20.5) does.

Example 1Four points with an error bar

For LM12's four points (α^=0.9, β^​=1.4, RSS=4.2), compute sε2​, the standard errors of both coefficients and a 95% interval for β under normal errors.

Solution

Here T−d=2, so sε2​=4.2/2=2.1. The regressor has xˉ=1.5 and Tsx2​=5. By (23.2), SE(β^​)=2.1/5​=0.648 and SE(α^)=2.1(1/4+1.52/5)​=1.212. The t-stat of β^​ is 1.4/0.648=2.16.

With normal errors the t-stat follows t2​ (Section 3), whose 97.5% quantile is 4.303. The interval 1.4±4.303×0.648 runs from −1.39 to 4.19 and contains 0. The normal quantile 1.96 would give 0.13 to 2.67 and exclude 0. With two residual degrees of freedom, the normal reference misleads.

TheoremGauss–Markov

Under the three conditions of (23.2), let My be any linear estimator of β that is unbiased for every β, with M a d×T matrix fixed given X. Then Var(My∣X)−Var(β^​∣X) is positive semi-definite: OLS is the best linear unbiased estimator (Greene 2018, ch. 4; proof in the Deep Dive).

No linear unbiased estimator gives any combination a⊤β a smaller variance. The theorem says nothing about nonlinear estimators, nor about errors that break condition 3, as daily returns do (Section 4).

Deep DiveWhy OLS is best among linear unbiased estimatorsoptional · click to expand

Since E[My∣X]=MXβ, unbiasedness for every β requires MX=I. Write M=(X⊤X)−1X⊤+K; then KX=0. By (16.4),

Var(My∣X)​=σε2​MM⊤=σε2​(X⊤X)−1+σε2​KK⊤,​
because the cross terms (X⊤X)−1X⊤K⊤ vanish with KX=0. The extra term is positive semi-definite, since for every a
a⊤KK⊤a=∥K⊤a∥2≥0.■

PitfallRegress returns, not prices

Regressing one trending price series on another can show a strong fit with no relation at all. LM25 treats this spurious regression; this module regresses returns.

導讀迴歸是帶誤差的量測

迴歸（regression）把資料寫成 y=Xβ+ε，其中係數（coefficient）是要估計的常數，誤差（error）是看不到的隨機部分。普通最小平方法（ordinary least squares）的估計量（estimator）就是 LM12 用投影（projection）求得的解；只要誤差在給定 X 時平均為零，它就是不偏估計量（unbiased estimator）。殘差（residual）只剩 T−d 個維度（dimension），所以誤差的變異數（variance）要用 RSS/(T−d) 估計。誤差若變異數齊一（homoskedastic）且互不相關（uncorrelated），高斯-馬可夫定理（Gauss–Markov theorem）保證 OLS 是最佳線性不偏估計量（best linear unbiased estimator）。這個保證只比較線性不偏的估計量，而且誤差變異數一旦逐日不同就不再成立。

Knowledge Check 1

LM12 fitted TAIEX's 22 daily returns of December 2008 on the 28 sectors with an intercept: 29 columns of rank 22, an exact fit. What is sε​?

Answer

The estimate sε​ does not exist. The fit is exact, so RSS=0, and the rank leaves 22−22=0 residual degrees of freedom, so (23.3) reads 0/0. An exact fit has no error estimate, hence no standard errors: it is no evidence.

3Standard Errors, Intervals and Hat Values

compute standard errors, t-stats, confidence intervals and prediction intervals for a regression, identify days with large hat values, and interpret R-squared as a statistic

Replace σε2​ by sε2​ in (23.2): the square roots of the diagonal of sε2​(X⊤X)−1 are the classical standard errors. A coefficient divided by its standard error is a t-stat, as in LM21's test of a mean (21.1).

Resultt-stats and intervals

Under the conditions of (23.2), test H0​:βj​=βj,null​ with

t-stat(β^​j​)=SE(β^​j​)β^​j​−βj,null​​,interval  β^​j​±1.96SE(β^​j​).​(23.4)
With normal errors the t-stat follows tT−d​ under H0​ (stated without proof; it is the regression form of LM20's tT−1​ law). For large T, with finite fourth moments, it is approximately N(0,1) without normality (stated without proof; (17.5) is the reason). The interval then covers βj​ with probability close to 95%.

Later t-stats and p-values are two-sided with the normal reference. Daily returns may lack a finite fourth moment (LM19), so these normal references are approximations. Every later t-stat comes from a fit with T−d≥183 (the widget's 2026 window). The 97.5% quantile of t183​, 1.973, exceeds the normal 1.960 by 0.7%. Each t-stat names its standard error: classical, White or Newey–West.

Example 2The case by hand

Compute Semiconductor's classical standard error of beta from sε​ and sx​, the 95% intervals with the classical and Newey–West errors, and the intercept's p-value.

Solution

The residual standard deviation is sε​=0.66239% with divisor T−2 (0.66224% with divisor T), and the TAIEX's returns have sx​=1.137%. So

SE(β^​)​=T​sx​sε​​=4382​×1.137%0.66239%​=0.0088.​
The classical interval 1.2073±1.96×0.0088 runs from 1.190 to 1.225. With the Newey–West error 0.0185 it runs from 1.171 to 1.244 (Exhibit 5).

The intercept's classical t-stat is 2.10, with p-value 2Φ(−2.10)=0.036 by (21.2). It is one test of 28 on the same data, which Section 7 counts.

R-squared, (12.4), is 0.8112 here. As a statistic it has its own sampling error, and it never falls when a column is added. Adjusted R-squared, 1−(1−R-squared)(T−1)/(T−d), charges for columns: 0.81119 against 0.81124 (from unrounded values). The F statistic tests that all coefficients but the intercept are zero; with one regressor it is the squared t-stat, 137.202=18,824. R-squared describes a fit and tests nothing: LM12's 0.99912 for TAIEX on its own sectors was an accounting identity.

A new observation y0​=x0⊤​β+ε0​ has an error of variance σε2​, independent of the sample. By (16.4), the fitted value x0⊤​β^​ and the prediction error have

Var(x0⊤​β^​∣X)=σε2​h0​,h0​:=x0⊤​(X⊤X)−1x0​,Var(y0​−x0⊤​β^​∣X)=σε2​(1+h0​).​(23.5)

Under (23.2)'s conditions, with sε​ in place of σε​, ±1.96 standard errors give a confidence interval for the fitted line, approximately for large T. The prediction interval for a new day also needs normal errors, because one day's error does not average out.

At a sample point, ht​:=xt⊤​(X⊤X)−1xt​ is the diagonal entry of Π in row t, the hat value of date t. It is the weight of yt​ in its own fitted value y^​t​, a measure of influence. The hat values sum to trΠ=d, so their average is d/T. Leverage keeps the book's meaning, f>1.

Example 3One day in 2025

Fit FinancialInsurance on the TAIEX over the 243 returns of 2025 (Exhibit 2). Find the 95% confidence and prediction intervals at a TAIEX return of −5%, and the hat value and influence of 7 April 2025.

Solution

The fit gives β^​=0.529, with classical standard error 0.0350, and sε​=0.840%. At x0​=(1,−0.05)⊤, a fall that only 2 days of 2025 reached, the fitted return is −2.642% with standard error 0.1865 points. The confidence interval runs from −3.01% to −2.28%. The prediction standard error is 0.860 points, so the prediction interval, −4.33% to −0.96%, is over four times as wide.

On 7 April 2025 the TAIEX fell 9.70% and FinancialInsurance 9.89%. That day's hat value is ht​=0.1709, against the average d/T=2/243=0.0082: 21 times. Without it, β^​=0.432, so one day moves the year's beta by 2.8 classical standard errors. 0050 closed and TX settled at their −10% limits that day (LM2), so the limits may have capped the recorded moves.

導讀標準誤、信賴區間與 hat value

係數（coefficient）除以它的標準誤（standard error）就是 t 統計量（t-statistic）；誤差（error）為常態分布（normal distribution）時它服從 tT−d​，樣本大時近似標準常態分布。估計值（estimate）加減 1.96 個標準誤，就是 95% 信賴區間（confidence interval）。判定係數（R-squared）只描述配適（fit），本身也有抽樣誤差（sampling error），不是檢定（test）。預測新的一天時，除了係數的誤差，還要加上當天自己的誤差，所以預測區間（prediction interval）寬得多。hat value 衡量一個點對自己配適值（fitted value）的影響力（influence）：2025 年 4 月 7 日的 hat value 是當年平均的 21 倍，這一天就讓全年的 beta 移動 2.8 個標準誤。

Knowledge Check 2

Test H0​:β=1 for Semiconductor (β^​=1.2073) with the classical standard error 0.0088 and with the Newey–West one, 0.0185. Do both reject at the 5% level?

Answer

By (23.4) the t-stats are (1.2073−1)/0.0088=23.6 and (1.2073−1)/0.0185=11.2. Both exceed 1.96 by far: Semiconductor moves more than one for one with the TAIEX, whichever standard error one trusts.

4Heteroskedasticity and White's Standard Errors

explain why heteroskedastic or autocorrelated errors invalidate the classical standard errors, and compute White and Newey–West standard errors, choosing the lag and checking its effect

The classical standard errors rest on condition 3 of (23.2): every day's error has the same variance. Daily returns break it: large errors come on days of large index moves, far from the regressor's mean, where they move the slope most.

Keep conditions 1 and 2, and let Σε​:=Var(ε∣X) be any covariance matrix. Applying (16.4) to β^​−β=(X⊤X)−1X⊤ε gives the sandwich

Var(β^​∣X)=(X⊤X)−1X⊤Σε​X(X⊤X)−1.(23.6)

The outer factors are the bread and the middle the filling. When the errors are uncorrelated, Σε​ is diagonal and the filling is ∑t​Var(εt​∣X)xt​xt⊤​.

White's estimator replaces each unknown variance by the squared residual, so the filling becomes ∑t​ε^t2​xt​xt⊤​ (statsmodels' HC0). One squared residual estimates its variance badly; the sum over T days averages the errors out. White (1980) shows that the estimate is consistent for independent rows with finite moments as T grows (stated without proof). Variance that changes from day to day is what LM26 models; White's standard errors need no model of it.

Example 4LM18's slope on 10,000 paths

LM18 simulated 10,000 five-year paths of iid normal log returns (m=1,231, default_rng([3, 20261011])) and regressed the growth factor G on lnG. It reported β^​=2.18 with batch standard error 0.01. Compare the classical, White and batch standard errors with the model's value.

Solution

OLS gives β^​=2.1759, with classical standard error 0.0067 and White's 0.0152. LM18's batch value from 20 batches of 500 is 0.0113. In the model lnG is exactly normal, so (23.6) with the model's moments in place of the sample's, for 10,000 paths, is an integral over a normal law. Quadrature gives 0.0157 around the true slope EG=2.1584.

The classical formula gives 43% of that value, and White's 0.97 of it. The batch value is 0.72 of it. By LM18's rule, a standard deviation of 20 values errs by about 1/40​=16%, so the batch value misses by under two such errors.

Since G=elnG is convex, the line misses most at both ends of lnG, where the regressor is farthest from its mean. The residuals' root mean square is 0.260, 0.179 and 0.378 in the lower, middle and upper thirds of lnG (cut at F^−1(1/3) and F^−1(2/3)). The classical formula ignores where the large errors sit.

Across U28's 28 market models, White's standard error of beta is 1.19 to 1.91 times the classical one. In FinancialInsurance's 2025 fit (Example 3) it is 0.106, three times the classical 0.0350, and 0.097 without 7 April 2025. That year's errors are largest on its few days of large index moves.

So Example 3's bands are too narrow at −5%: White's standard error of the fitted value there is 0.557 points, not 0.1865. The two 2025 days with the TAIEX at or below −5% have residuals of −5.7 and −3.0 sε​, outside the prediction band.

Knowledge Check 3

LM16's slope of FinancialInsurance on Semiconductor, 0.4591, has standard errors 0.0099 classical, 0.0165 White and 0.0229 Newey–West (ℓ=9). Give the Newey–West 95% interval. LM12 reported a residual standard deviation of 0.996%. How does it relate to sε​?

Answer

The interval is 0.4591±1.96×0.0229, from 0.414 to 0.504. LM12 divided by T and got 0.99603%; (23.3) divides by T−2 and gives 0.99626%. They differ by the factor 4382/4380​, and both round to 0.996%.

5Overlapping Returns and Newey–West

explain why heteroskedastic or autocorrelated errors invalidate the classical standard errors, and compute White and Newey–West standard errors, choosing the lag and checking its effect

A regression of 20-day returns can start a sum on every day: days 1–20, 2–21 and so on. Neighbouring sums share 19 days, so their errors are autocorrelated, and condition 3 fails again. Semiconductor's 4,363 overlapping log-return windows leave residuals with sample autocorrelation (19.5) ρ^​1​=0.953, close to the 19/20 of iid days.

With autocorrelated rows the filling of (23.6) also collects covariances between days. Divided by T, it tends to the long-run variance of the products εt​xt​,

Γ0​+k≥1∑​(Γk​+Γk⊤​),Γk​:=E[εt​εt−k​xt​xt−k⊤​],​

the matrix form of (19.7)'s limit. Newey and West (1987) estimate it with declining weights:

Γ^0​+k=1∑ℓ​(1−ℓ+1k​)(Γ^k​+Γ^k⊤​),Γ^k​:=T1​t=k+1∑T​ε^t​ε^t−k​xt​xt−k⊤​,​(23.7)

which replaces X⊤Σε​X/T in (23.6). statsmodels calls 1−k/(ℓ+1) Bartlett weights. With them the estimate is positive semi-definite. It is consistent when the products' dependence fades fast enough, their moments are bounded and ℓ grows more slowly than T1/4 (both stated without proof; Newey and West 1987).

The book fixes the lag in advance as ℓ=⌊4(T/100)2/9⌋, whose exponent 2/9 is below 1/4: 9 for 4,382 returns, 7 for a half, 4 for a year. It is statsmodels' default HAC lag and the pilot lag of Newey and West (1994), not the data-based lag they recommend. A fixed rule avoids choosing the lag that gives the wanted answer.

Example 5Overlapping 20-day sums

Deep DiveWhy overlapping sums need a long lagoptional · click to expand

Let the daily errors and the regressor's daily returns be iid with mean zero and variances σε2​ and σx2​, the two series independent. Two sums of m days that end k<m days apart share m−k days. By (16.4) the error sums then have covariance (m−k)σε2​, and the regressor sums (m−k)σx2​. By (16.1) the slope's entry of Γk​ is their product:

(m−k)2σε2​σx2​(k<m),0(k≥m).​
The classical formula keeps Γ0​ alone, m2σε2​σx2​. The long-run variance adds the other lags,
​2k=1∑m−1​(m−k)2σε2​σx2​=3(m−1)m(2m−1)​σε2​σx2​,​
so the true variance is (2m2+1)/(3m) times the classical one. At m=20 that is 13.35, a factor 3.65 in the standard error. A lag below m−1 drops terms, and Bartlett weights shrink the rest: in large samples ℓ=19 recovers a factor 3.17, and ℓ=38 a factor 3.41.

The lag also diagnoses a drifting beta (Exhibit 4). With the full-sample residuals, Semiconductor's daily Newey–West standard error keeps growing: 0.0129 at ℓ=0 (White's), 0.0185 at 9, 0.0275 at 50 and 0.0390 at 200. With a drifting beta, the products ε^t​xt​ of the full-sample fit keep their sign for long stretches. Residuals from each calendar year's own line give 0.0152, 0.0160 and 0.0123 at lags 9, 50 and 200 instead.

PitfallA standard error that grows with the lag

When a Newey–West standard error keeps growing with ℓ, the constant coefficient itself is in doubt: the full-sample beta averages a moving target (Section 6). On overlapping sums, use ℓ≥m−1 or non-overlapping sums, and report both.

LM22's block bootstrap is the resampling alternative to (23.7), and LM33 applies the long-run variance to a Sharpe ratio.

導讀變異數非齊一與自我相關

古典標準誤（classical standard error）假設每天誤差（error）的變異數（variance）相同、互不相關（uncorrelated）。日報酬違反前一項：大盤大漲大跌的日子誤差也大，這就是變異數非齊一性（heteroskedasticity）。White 的夾層估計量（sandwich estimator）用殘差（residual）平方取代未知的變異數，得到穩健標準誤（robust standard error）。重疊的 20 日報酬相鄰兩個視窗（window）共用 19 天，殘差有很強的自我相關（autocorrelation）；Newey–West 的變異數非齊一與自我相關一致估計量（HAC）再把各落期（lag）的共變異數（covariance）加權納入。落期越長、標準誤一路上升，表示 beta 本身有參數漂移（parameter drift），問題不只出在標準誤。

6The Market Model: Beta, Alpha and Hedging

estimate a market model for a sector index, interpret its beta and intercept, and explain why a raw-return intercept on price indices is not Jensen's alpha
size a hedge by regression, and judge a beta's stability across halves, calendar years and rolling windows

The market model regresses a sector's return on the market's:

Ri,t​=α+βRmkt,t​+εt​.

The market model's slope estimates the sector's beta, the population slope of (16.8). The model describes co-movement and makes no claim about expected returns.

The capital asset pricing model (CAPM) makes one. With total returns and a risk-free rate rf​, it states E[Ri​]−rf​=βi​(E[Rmkt​]−rf​) for every asset (Campbell, Lo and MacKinlay 1997, ch. 5; Sharpe 1964 and Lintner 1965 are its origins). Jensen's alpha αJ​ is the market model's intercept on total excess returns (Jensen 1968), zero under the CAPM.

A total return adds the dividend yield, qi​/A a day if spread evenly, so Jensen's regression reads

Ri,t​+Aqi​−rf​​=αJ​​+β(Rmkt,t​+Aqmkt​−rf​​)+εt​.​

Collecting constants gives U28's raw intercept

α=αJ​+A(1−β)rf​​−Aqi​−βqmkt​​(23.8)

per day, with rf​, qi​ and qmkt​ simple rates a year.

Over U28's window the TAIEX total-return index rose 20.23-fold and the price index 10.91-fold. Their mean daily return gap, times A, gives qmkt​=3.46% a year. For Semiconductor (β=1.207), a 2% risk-free rate, LM6's proxy, adds 0.17 bp a day to Jensen's alpha, and equal dividend yields subtract 0.29 bp. Jensen's alpha is then 1.98 bp. Each point of sector yield below the index's subtracts another 0.41 bp; the sectors' own yields are not in the data.

A beta is an average over the sample. Exhibit 5 sets the 28 full-sample betas beside the halves'. With Newey–West errors (ℓ=7 in each half), the change between the halves in standard errors,

z=SEfirst2​+SEsecond2​​β^​second​−β^​first​​,

exceeds 1.96 in absolute value for 25 of 28 sectors. FinancialInsurance fell from 1.156 to 0.578 and Cement from 0.982 to 0.463; Semiconductor rose from 1.089 to 1.294. The halves' betas correlate 0.653.

Exhibit 6 follows two betas over rolling 250-return windows. FinancialInsurance's fell from 1.377 (window ending 23 February 2010) to 0.251 (26 May 2026), and ends at 0.308. Semiconductor's ranged from 0.914 (18 May 2011) to 1.658 (17 January 2020), and ends at 1.168.

7Twenty-Eight Alphas

test 28 sector alphas with Bonferroni, Holm and Benjamini–Hochberg, and explain how correlation across sectors changes the count of false positives

導讀28 個 alpha 與多重假設檢定

同一份資料在 5% 顯著水準（significance level）下做 28 次檢定（test），即使真實的 alpha 全是零，平均也有 1.4 個 t 統計量（t-statistic）的絕對值超過 1.96，所以看到一個顯著並不稀奇。多重假設檢定（multiple testing）控制族系誤差率（family-wise error rate）或偽發現率（false discovery rate）：Bonferroni 校正（Bonferroni correction）把每個檢定的門檻（threshold）降到 0.05/28，Holm 逐步放寬，Benjamini–Hochberg 控制偽發現率。三種方法都沒有拒絕任何類股。類股殘差（residual）彼此相關（correlated），不改變錯誤拒絕（false rejection）的平均個數，卻讓「至少一個」的機率（probability）從 0.762 降到 0.696。把許多類股、規則或期間都試一遍再挑最好的，就是資料偵伺（data snooping）。

The factor comes from (23.2): the slopes' block of (X⊤X)−1 is (TΣ^)−1 by (12.3), and inverting a 2×2 correlation matrix gives diagonal entries 1/(1−ρ^​2). 9Regression in Python

compute OLS with classical and robust standard errors in NumPy, and check them against statsmodels with every option passed explicitly

NumPy computes every number here. The checks use statsmodels with every option passed, because its defaults differ from the book's.

Python
import numpy as np
import statsmodels.api as sm
from statsmodels.stats.multitest import multipletests

# y: a sector's returns, x: the TAIEX's (U28, T = 4,382)
X = np.column_stack([np.ones(len(x)), x])
b = np.linalg.lstsq(X, y, rcond=None)[0]
e = y - X @ b
s2 = e @ e / (len(y) - X.shape[1])          # divisor T - d
XtXi = np.linalg.inv(X.T @ X)
se_classical = np.sqrt(s2 * np.diag(XtXi))
u = X * e[:, None]
se_white = np.sqrt(np.diag(XtXi @ (u.T @ u) @ XtXi))   # HC0

white = sm.OLS(y, X).fit(cov_type="HC0")
nw = sm.OLS(y, X).fit(cov_type="HAC",
                      cov_kwds={"maxlags": 9, "use_correction": False})
print(nw.bse, nw.use_t)                     # use_t False: normal p-values
# pvals: the 28 intercepts' two-sided p-values (Problem 12)
holm = multipletests(pvals, alpha=0.05, method="holm")[0]

get_robustcov_results() without arguments returns HC1, which multiplies HC0's variance by T/(T−d). In OLS.fit the HAC option needs maxlags and leaves use_correction False; cov_hac defaults to True. multipletests defaults to Holm–Šidák ("hs"), so name the method.

Summary

  • OLS is unbiased when the errors have mean zero given X, (23.2); sε2​ divides RSS by T−d, (23.3).
  • Classical standard errors (23.4) assume homoskedastic, uncorrelated errors; hat values measure influence, (23.5).
  • White's sandwich (23.6) allows heteroskedasticity, and Newey–West (23.7) autocorrelation too.
  • A Newey–West standard error that grows with the lag signals a drifting coefficient.
  • A raw intercept on price indices is not Jensen's alpha, (23.8), and betas move.
  • One significant intercept among 28 is what chance gives.
  • A factor named in advance avoids collinearity, measured by (23.9).

  8. With xt​=(1,xt​)⊤, the matrix X⊤X has entries T, Txˉ, Txˉ and T(sx2​+xˉ2), with determinant T2sx2​. Hence

    ht​​=T2sx2​T(sx2​+xˉ2)−2Txˉxt​+Txt2​​=T1​+Tsx2​(xt​−xˉ)2​.​
    Summing over t gives 1+Tsx2​/(Tsx2​)=2=d. For 7 April 2025,
    ht​=2431​+243×1.540529.80592​=0.1709,
    21 times the average 2/243. The lab computes all 243 from Π.

  9. The contracts are 0.75×50,000,000/(200×20,000)=9.375, about 9, against 12.50 for the notional hedge. The hedged variance is 1−0.5 of the unhedged, so the standard deviation is 0.5​=0.7071 of it.   10. Each design's columns are combinations of the other's, since Rel​=(Rel​−Rmkt​)+Rmkt​. So both span the same space, and Π, the fitted values and the residuals agree. Equating the fits day by day,

    ​βmktraw​Rmkt​+βelraw​Rel​=βmkt​Rmkt​+βel​(Rel​−Rmkt​),​
    gives βel​=βelraw​ and βmkt​=βmktraw​+βelraw​. For Semiconductor, −0.515+1.552=1.037. The VIFs are 1/(1−0.96962)=16.7 and 1/(1−0.3652)=1.15.

  11. Both give β^​=0.8234, with standard errors 0.0103 classical, 0.0183 HC0 and 0.0288 HAC; NumPy and statsmodels agree to 12 digits. With use_correction=True the HAC value is 0.02885. get_robustcov_results() defaults to HC1, which scales HC0's variance by T/(T−2).

  12. The t-stats run from −1.935 (Optoelectronic) to 2.097 (Semiconductor); the smallest p-value is 0.036. Bonferroni, Holm and Benjamini–Hochberg reject none. Term 中文 Meaning
Regression 迴歸 A model y=Xβ+ε of a response on regressors, (23.1).

Residual 殘差 ε^=y−Xβ^​.
Unbiased estimator 不偏估計量 An estimator whose expectation is the parameter, (23.2), (23.3).

Best linear unbiased estimator 最佳線性不偏估計量 OLS under the Gauss–Markov conditions.
Standard error 標準誤 The estimated standard deviation of an estimator.
Confidence interval 信賴區間 β^​j​±1.96SE(β^​j​), (23.4).
R-squared 判定係數 The share of the centred response's squared length that the fit reproduces, (12.4).
Hat value — ht​, the diagonal entry of Π in row t: a point's influence on its own fit, (23.5).
Heteroskedasticity 變異數非齊一性 Error variances that differ across observations.
Robust standard error 穩健標準誤 A standard error from the sandwich (23.6), such as White's (HC0).

Autocorrelation 自我相關 Correlation of a series with its own past.
