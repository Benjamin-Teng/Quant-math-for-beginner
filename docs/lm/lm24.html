Bayesian Inference and Shrinkage

A prior turns a past mean into a weighted average of the data and a belief. Twenty-eight sector means carry almost nothing beyond their average, and fractional Kelly is the Bayes bet of a stated sceptic.

Learning Module Overview
  • Bayes' rule for a parameter, (24.1), multiplies a prior density by the likelihood. A beta prior and a binomial record give a beta posterior, (24.2): under a sceptic's Beta(82, 82), eight good months of twelve raise the probability that a manager's monthly chance is at least 0.55 from 10.0% to 15.3%.
  • For iid normal returns with a known variance, the posterior mean is the prior mean plus the data's weight c times the gap to the sample mean, (24.3), and precisions add. With a prior standard deviation of 10 points a year, TAIEX's 23.8 years get c=0.869, and the posterior mean is 13.4% a year against the sample's 15.38%.
  • A credible interval is a probability statement about the parameter; a confidence interval describes a procedure. Under a flat prior they coincide, 7.8% to 23.0% a year, and Pr(μ≤0∣data)=3.8×10−5 equals LM21's one-sided p-value to the printed digits.
  • James–Stein's estimate (24.4) shrinks many means toward their grand mean by an estimated weight. In Experiment 1, with 28 means whose spread equals their noise, it cuts the total squared error from 28.1 to 15.5; the Bayes rule that knows the prior reaches 14.0.
  • On the 28 TWSE sector price indices, each sector's first-half mean predicts its second half worse than the grand mean does, 9.8 against 8.5 points a year. The means spread less than 0.945 times their noise, so James–Stein shrinks all the way, even with the common component removed.
  • With a normal prior centred at zero, log utility and a known volatility, the Bayes Kelly fraction is c times the plug-in fraction, (24.5). On TAIEX, quarter and half Kelly are the Bayes bets of sceptics whose prior standard deviation is 2.25 or 3.89 points a year.
  • The quadratic rule overstates the exact fraction by 0.14 at full Kelly and by 0.10 at c=0.869, while the prior shaves 13%. A prior centred away from zero breaks the fraction reading: centred at 8% a year, the Bayes bet is 0.76 of the plug-in, not c=0.50.

1Introduction

LM20 gave a past mean a standard error. Here the unknown mean itself is uncertain: a prior distribution states a belief before the data, and Bayes' rule updates it to a posterior distribution. The posterior mean is a weighted average of the data and the prior, so it shrinks the sample mean toward a target.

Motivating CaseLast decade's winners

Split the 28 TWSE sector price indices of LM10 (U28, no dividends) at LM11's midpoint. From December 2008 to October 2017, Automobile led with a mean return of 21.65% a year. From October 2017 to October 2026 it ranked 20th, at 3.67%. ShippingTransportation went from last, 1.89%, to fifth, 17.57%. Across the 28 sectors, the two halves' means correlate −0.05 (Exhibit 1).

Predict each sector's second half by its own first-half mean, and the typical miss is 9.8 points a year. Predict every sector by the first half's average of all 28, 10.49%, and the miss is 8.5 points.

The TAIEX's total return averaged 15.38% a year over 23.8 years (LM19), and its plug-in Kelly leverage is 4.28 (LM20). LM4 advised betting a quarter to a half of that. Should you believe a past mean, and what would a sceptic have to believe to bet so little?

Sections 2 to 4 apply Bayes' rule to a parameter and to TAIEX's mean. Sections 5 and 6 shrink many means and test the result on the sectors. Section 7 derives the Bayes Kelly fraction, and Section 8 collects the Python. Shrinking a covariance matrix, the subject of LM30, follows the same idea.

2Bayes' Rule for a Parameter

apply Bayes' rule to an unknown parameter, and compute the beta–binomial posterior of a success probability and the data's weight in it

LM13's manager beat the index in 8 of 12 months. LM13 allowed two types: 10% of managers win a month with probability 0.55, the rest with 0.5. But a manager's monthly chance θ can be any number between 0 and 1, so a prior should be a density on [0,1].

Given θ, months are independent, so by (13.7) a given record of j good months in n has probability θj(1−θ)n−j. As a function of θ, this is the record's likelihood L(θ) (LM20). Split [0,1] into short intervals of width Δ. By (13.9) and (13.8), the interval at xk​, with prior probability about fθ​(xk​)Δ, gets the posterior probability about

∑l​L(xl​)fθ​(xl​)ΔL(xk​)fθ​(xk​)Δ​.

Dividing by Δ and letting Δ→0 turns the sum into an integral; the limit is taken as given here (Wasserman 2004, ch. 11).

TheoremBayes' rule for a parameter

Let a parameter θ have a prior density fθ​, and let the data have the likelihood L(θ), with 0<∫L(x)fθ​(x)dx<∞. Then the posterior density is

fθ∣data​(θ)=∫L(x)fθ​(x)dxL(θ)fθ​(θ)​.(24.1)

The denominator does not depend on θ, so constant factors of the likelihood or the prior cancel.

DefinitionBeta distribution

For α0​,β0​>0, the beta distribution Beta(α0​,β0​) has the density, for 0<x<1,

fθ​(x)​=Γ(α0​)Γ(β0​)Γ(α0​+β0​)​×xα0​−1(1−x)β0​−1,​
with Γ from (15.8). That this density integrates to 1 is stated without proof.

Multiplying the density by x gives a constant times the Beta(α0​+1,β0​) density, and the recursion of (15.8) for Γ gives the mean α0​/(α0​+β0​). The same step twice gives the variance

Varθ=(α0​+β0​)2(α0​+β0​+1)α0​β0​​.
ResultBeta prior, binomial record

Let θ∼Beta(α0​,β0​), and let a record have j successes in n independent trials with probability θ. Then the posterior is Beta(α0​+j,β0​+n−j), with mean

E[θ∣data]c​=α0​+β0​α0​​+c(nj​−α0​+β0​α0​​),=n+α0​+β0​n​.​(24.2)

Proof. By (24.1), the posterior is proportional to xj(1−x)n−j⋅xα0​−1(1−x)β0​−1, the shape of the stated beta density. Its mean (α0​+j)/(α0​+β0​+n) rearranges into (24.2). ■

A prior whose posterior stays in its family is conjugate. The data's weight c sets n trials against the prior's worth of n0​:=α0​+β0​ trials: c=n/(n+n0​).

To match LM13, choose a symmetric prior Beta(a,a) under which 10% of managers have θ≥0.55: a=81.96. The integer 82 still gives 10.0% and keeps every probability an exact fraction. This sceptic's prior has standard deviation 0.039 and is worth n0​=164 months.

Example 1The manager under a sceptic's prior

Under the prior Beta(82, 82), find the posterior after LM13's 8 good months of 12, its mean, the data's weight and Pr(θ≥0.55∣record).

Solution

By (24.2), the posterior is Beta(90, 86), with mean 90/176=45/88=0.5114. The data's weight is c=12/176=3/44=0.068: twelve months weigh 6.8% against the prior's 164. So the record moves the mean from 0.5 by only 443​×(128​−21​)=881​=0.0114. Exactly, Pr(θ≥0.55∣record)=15.3% (Exhibit 2), against LM13's two-type 13.5%.

Example 2A flat prior, and how long a record convinces the sceptic

Repeat Example 1 with the flat prior Beta(1, 1). Then find how many months of two good in three raise the sceptic's Pr(θ≥0.55) above one half.

Solution

The posterior is Beta(9, 5), with mean 9/14=0.643 and c=12/14=6/7: the data dominate. Exactly, Pr(θ>0.5)=7,099/8,192=86.7% and Pr(θ≥0.55)=77.2%. The central 95% of the posterior lies between 0.39 and 0.86.

Under Beta(82, 82), a two-in-three record of n months gives Beta(82+2n/3,82+n/3). The probability first exceeds one half at n=72 months (51.2%; 49.4% at 69), when c=72/236=0.305: six years barely convince the sceptic.

PitfallA flat prior is a choice too

Beta(1, 1) looks neutral, but it puts 45% of managers at θ≥0.55, four and a half times LM13's share. Under it, the twelve months dominate the posterior (c=6/7); under the sceptic's prior they hardly move it (c=0.068). State the prior, and say why it was chosen.

導讀參數的貝氏推論

貝氏推論（Bayesian inference）把未知的參數（parameter）當成有分布（distribution）的量：事前分布（prior distribution）描述看資料之前的信念，乘上概度函數（likelihood function）再除以常數，就是事後分布（posterior distribution），即 (24.1)。貝他(β)分布（beta distribution）配上二項資料（binomial data），事後仍是貝他(β)分布，所以它是共軛事前分布（conjugate prior）。事後平均數（posterior mean）是事前平均數與資料頻率的加權平均（weighted average），資料權重（data's weight）c=n/(n+n0​)，其中 n0​=α0​+β0​ 是事前分布相當的月數。懷疑性事前分布（sceptical prior）Beta(82, 82) 相當於 164 個月，所以 12 個月的紀錄只占 6.8% 的權重。均勻的事前分布看似中立，其實也是一種選擇。

Knowledge Check 1

A prior Beta(2, 2) meets a record of 3 good months in 4. Find the posterior, its mean and the data's weight.

Answer

By (24.2), the posterior is Beta(5, 3), with mean 5/8=0.625. The data's weight is c=4/(4+4)=21​: the prior is worth four months.

3The Normal Mean with Known Variance

derive the posterior of a normal mean with known variance, and explain why the posterior precision is the prior precision plus the data precision

Now let the parameter be TAIEX's mean daily return μ, with daily returns iid N(μ,σ2) and σ known. Expanding each square around Rˉ, the cross term vanishes because ∑t​(Rt​−Rˉ)=0:

t=1∑T​(Rt​−μ)2=t=1∑T​(Rt​−Rˉ)2+T(Rˉ−μ)2.

So the likelihood L(μ)=∏t​φ((Rt​−μ)/σ)/σ is a constant times e−T(Rˉ−μ)2/(2σ2). It depends on the data only through Rˉ, and by (24.1) so does the posterior.

Before the data, μ∼N(μ0​,σ02​), and Rˉ=μ+εˉ with noise εˉ∼N(0,σ2/T) independent of μ. So (μ,Rˉ) is a normal pair by (16.7), with VarRˉ=σ02​+σ2/T and Cov(μ,Rˉ)=σ02​ by (16.3) and (16.4). In (16.8), with X=Rˉ and Y=μ, the slope is σ02​/(σ02​+σ2/T), written c, and the residual variance is σ02​(1−c)=cσ2/T.

ResultPosterior of a normal mean

Let the returns R1​,…,RT​ be iid N(μ,σ2) with σ known, and let the prior be μ∼N(μ0​,σ02​). Then

μ∣data∼N(μ0​+c(Rˉ−μ0​),cσ2/T),c=σ02​+σ2/Tσ02​​=T+n0​T​,n0​=σ02​σ2​.​(24.3)

Inverting the posterior variance gives 1/σ02​+T/σ2: the precision of the posterior, one over its variance, is the prior's precision plus the data's (Gelman et al. 2013, §2.5). The prior is worth n0​ returns.

Deep DiveCompleting the squareoptional · click to expand

Let m and v>0 be numbers. By (24.1), the log posterior density of μ is a constant plus

−2σ02​(μ−μ0​)2​−2σ2T(Rˉ−μ)2​.
This log density is a quadratic in μ with leading coefficient −21​(1/σ02​+T/σ2). Its derivative vanishes at
1/σ02​+T/σ2μ0​/σ02​+TRˉ/σ2​=μ0​+c(Rˉ−μ0​).
A density whose logarithm is a constant minus (μ−m)2/(2v) is the normal density φ((μ−m)/v​)/v​. So the posterior is normal with the mean and variance of (24.3), without (16.8).

On TR23, the model sets σ=s=1.2084%, the normal maximum-likelihood estimate (20.5). LM19's annualization exhibit gives ARˉ=15.38% and A​s=18.96% a year, and LM20's (20.2) gives the standard error As/T​=3.89 points. On the annual scale, c=(Aσ0​)2/((Aσ0​)2+3.892). A prior SD equal to the standard error gives c=21​: the posterior mean halves the sample's, to 7.69%, with SD 3.89/2​=2.75 points (Exhibit 3).

For μ0​=0, the posterior mean cRˉ is LM20's scaled mean ηRˉ with η=c. By (20.1), MSE(ηRˉ)=η2σ2/T+(1−η)2μ2, so the best η needs the unknown μ. Averaged over the prior, μ2 becomes σ02​, and the minimizing η is c. LM20's η∗=0.940 is c at a prior SD equal to the mean itself.

Example 3TAIEX's mean under a prior of 10 points

Take a prior centred at zero with Aσ0​=10 points a year. Find the data's weight, the posterior mean and SD of the annual mean, and the prior's worth in years.

Solution

On the annual scale, c=102/(102+3.892)=0.869. By (24.3), the posterior mean is 0.869×15.38%=13.4%, and the posterior SD is 0.869​×3.89=3.6 points. The prior is worth n0​=s2/σ02​ returns, or n0​/A=(18.96/10)2=3.59 years of data.

The model assumes normal days, which TAIEX's are not; (17.5) still makes Rˉ approximately normal. The iid standard error ignores volatility clustering, and robust errors of a mean are LM22's and LM23's.

Example 4Where the prior's variance goes

Check the law of total variance (16.6) for μ and Rˉ at Aσ0​=10 points, on the annual scale.

Solution

By (16.6), Varμ=Var(E[μ∣Rˉ])+E[Var(μ∣Rˉ)]. Here E[μ∣Rˉ]=cRˉ has variance c2(σ02​+σ2/T)=cσ02​. The posterior variance is (1−c)σ02​, whatever Rˉ is. In points², 100=86.9+13.1. Before the data arrive, most of the prior's variance is variance of the coming posterior mean.

PitfallA prior SD per day or per year

The formulas take σ0​ per day, while the text quotes Aσ0​ a year. A prior SD belongs to a mean, so it annualizes by A like the mean, not by A​ like a volatility. Dividing 10 points by A​ gives a daily σ0​ of 0.637% instead of 0.0406%, and c=0.9994 instead of 0.869: the sceptic disappears. Also, c is a ratio of variances, never of standard deviations (Problem 2).

導讀精確度相加

在變異數（variance）已知的常態模型裡，若平均數（mean）μ 的事前分布（prior distribution）是常態分布（normal distribution），事後分布（posterior distribution）也是常態分布。事後平均數（posterior mean）是 Rˉ 與事前平均數 μ0​ 的加權平均（weighted average），資料權重（data's weight）c 等於事前變異數除以（事前變異數加上 σ2/T）。事後變異數的倒數稱為精確度（precision），它等於事前的精確度加上資料的精確度 T/σ2。事前分布相當於 n0​=σ2/σ02​ 筆報酬，所以 c=T/(T+n0​)。加權股價報酬指數 23.8 年的資料，碰上標準差（standard deviation）每年 10 個百分點的事前分布，權重是 0.869，事後平均數由 15.38% 縮為 13.4%。

Knowledge Check 2

With Aσ0​=10 points, how many years of TAIEX data is the prior worth? How many years of data would raise c to 0.9?

Answer

The prior is worth (18.96/10)2=3.59 years. By (24.3), c=T/(T+n0​)=0.9 needs T=9n0​, nine times the unrounded worth, (18.957/10)2=3.594: 32.3 years.

4Credible and Confidence Intervals

distinguish a credible interval from a confidence interval, and compute both for the TAIEX's mean return

A posterior answers probability questions about μ directly. Below, 1.96 is Φ−1(0.975), as in LM15.

DefinitionCredible interval

A 95% credible interval is an interval to which the posterior gives probability 95%. For a normal posterior, the central one is the posterior mean ±1.96 posterior SD.

LM20's large-sample 95% confidence interval, Rˉ±1.96s/T​, says something else. The procedure that builds it covers the fixed, unknown μ in about 95% of samples, while one computed interval either holds μ or misses it. As σ0​→∞, the prior flattens and c→1. The credible interval then becomes numerically the confidence interval, and the two differ only in what they claim (Exhibit 4).

Example 5The chance of a negative mean

Under the flat prior, find the 95% credible interval for TAIEX's annual mean and Pr(μ≤0∣data), and compare them with LM20 and LM21. Repeat at Aσ0​=10 points.

Solution

With c=1, the posterior is N(Rˉ,s2/T). On the annual scale, 15.38%±1.96×3.89 runs from 7.8% to 23.0%, numerically LM20's confidence interval. The posterior probability of a negative mean is

Pr(μ≤0∣data)​=Φ(−s/T​Rˉ​)=3.8×10−5.​
LM21's one-sided p-value, (21.2) at its t-stat 3.96, divides by su​ instead of s, and the two agree to the printed digits. Yet the posterior probability is a statement about μ, and the p-value a statement about the data under H0​:μ=0.

At Aσ0​=10 points, the interval is 6.3% to 20.5% and the probability 1.1×10−4 (Exhibit 4). Even under the prior of the first row, the posterior puts 97.6% on μ>0.

The widget varies the prior SD, the prior mean and the years of data, with TAIEX's annual mean and volatility held at their sample values. It starts at the third row of Exhibit 4.

PitfallA confidence interval is not a probability for μ

The sentence "μ lies between 7.8% and 23.0% with probability 95%" belongs to a credible interval, and it needs a prior: here the flat one. Under the prior with Aσ0​=3.89 points, the same data give 2.3% to 13.1%. A confidence interval's 95% belongs to the procedure, not to the interval in hand.

導讀可靠區間與信賴區間

可靠區間（credible interval）是事後分布（posterior distribution）的機率（probability）敘述：給定資料與事前分布（prior distribution），μ 落在區間內的機率是 95%。信賴區間（confidence interval）則是程序的性質：重複抽樣（repeated sampling）時，用同一作法建出的區間有 95% 會蓋住固定的 μ，手上這一個區間則只有蓋住或沒蓋住兩種情形。事前分布越平，c 越接近 1，兩者在數字上就越接近，例如加權股價報酬指數年平均報酬（mean return）的 7.8% 到 23.0%。同樣地，Pr(μ≤0∣data) 是關於 μ 的機率；p值（p-value）則是在虛無假設（null hypothesis）成立時，關於資料的機率。兩者在這裡數值幾乎相同，意義卻不同。

5Shrinking Many Means: James–Stein

state the James–Stein estimator with its conditions, and derive its empirical-Bayes reading as an estimated data's weight

Now take N sector means Rˉ1​,…,RˉN​ of T returns each, under one prior: the true means μi​ are iid N(μ0​,σ02​). Given the μi​, the Rˉi​ are independent, with Rˉi​∼N(μi​,σ2/T) and one common noise variance. By (24.3), with Rˉi​ as sector i's data, every Bayes estimate μ0​+c(Rˉi​−μ0​) moves toward μ0​ by the same weight c.

The data can estimate μ0​ and c themselves: before the μi​ are known, the Rˉi​ are iid N(μ0​,σ02​+σ2/T) by (16.7). Their grand mean Rˉˉ:=N1​∑i​Rˉi​ estimates μ0​, and the square of their spread estimates σ02​+σ2/T, the denominator of 1−c. Plugging both in gives an empirical Bayes estimate (Efron and Morris 1973).

TheoremJames–Stein estimator toward the grand mean

Let N≥4 estimates Rˉ1​,…,RˉN​ satisfy:

  1. each Rˉi​∼N(μi​,σ2/T);
  2. the Rˉi​ are independent;
  3. the noise variance σ2/T is common to all of them and known.

Define

μ^​iJS​=Rˉˉ+c^(Rˉi​−Rˉˉ),c^=max(0, 1−∑i​(Rˉi​−Rˉˉ)2(N−3)σ2/T​).​(24.4)
Then, for every (μ1​,…,μN​), the expected total squared error E∑i​(μ^​iJS​−μi​)2 is below the raw means' Nσ2/T. Stated without proof (Efron and Hastie 2016, §7.1 and §7.5).

When the means spread several times their noise, c^ is near 1. When their spread (divisor N) is at most (N−3)/N​ times the noise, 0.945 for 28 means, c^=0, and every estimate is the grand mean. James and Stein (1961) shrank toward a fixed point. The max with 0, the positive part, stops the weight from turning negative and pushing estimates past the grand mean.

How can a biased rule beat the raw means? In the normal model each Rˉi​ attains the Cramér–Rao bound (20.7), so no unbiased estimator has a smaller variance. James–Stein accepts bias toward Rˉˉ and removes more variance than the bias adds, as (20.1) allows. Applied by Efron and Morris (1975) to 18 players' batting averages after their first 45 at-bats of 1970, it had less than half the raw averages' squared error.

Deep DiveWhy N−3optional · click to expand

Under the model, the Rˉi​ are iid N(μ0​,σ02​+σ2/T). By Blitzstein and Hwang's Example 10.4.3, ∑i​(Rˉi​−Rˉˉ)2/(σ02​+σ2/T) then has the law χN−12​ of LM16. The χν2​ density is xν/2−1e−x/2/(2ν/2Γ(ν/2)) for x>0 (stated without proof). For ν>2, the substitution x=2y in the integral of (15.8) and its recursion for Γ give

E[χν2​1​]​=2ν/2Γ(ν/2)∫0∞​xν/2−2e−x/2dx​=2ν/2Γ(ν/2)2ν/2−1Γ(ν/2−1)​=ν−21​.​
With ν=N−1, the expectation of (N−3)(σ2/T)/∑i​(Rˉi​−Rˉˉ)2 is (σ2/T)/(σ02​+σ2/T)=1−c. So 1−c^, before the clip at 0, is an unbiased estimate of 1−c. The grand mean uses one degree of freedom, and the expectation needs ν>2: hence N≥4.

Example 7James–Stein on the sector halves

On U28's first half (T=2,191, divisor T), take the common noise variance to be the average of the 28 values si2​/T. Find the noise and the spread of the 28 annual means, c^, and the out-of-sample misses at c=0 and c=1. Repeat after removing each day's mean of the 28 returns.

Solution

The noise of one annual mean is A times the root of the average si2​/T: 7.18 points. The 28 annual means spread by only 4.50 points (divisor 28). By (24.4), 1−c^=25×7.182/(28×4.502)=2.27, so c^=0, and every sector is shrunk to the grand mean, 10.49%.

Judged on the second half, the grand mean misses by 8.5 points and the own means by 9.8 (Exhibit 6). The best weight in hindsight, the slope of second-half deviations on first-half ones, is −0.09: the deviations reversed slightly.

Removing each day's mean of the 28 returns leaves the deviations of the 28 means unchanged, but it cuts the noise to 4.79 points. Then 1−c^=1.01, and c^=0 again, by a hair. The second half's means spread by 8.49 points, almost twice the first half's: the true means moved, and no first-half estimate could know where.

Example 7 uses one noise variance for all 28 sectors, but the sectors' noise variances differ by a factor of about 4 (half-widths 9 to 19 points in Exhibit 5). Their average, estimated from 2,191 returns, is treated as known. With the total noise, (24.4) gives c^=0 even if every sector had the smallest noise, 4.8 points (the largest is 9.7), so c^=0 does not depend on the averaging.

Exhibit 5 is consistent with the zero weight: every first-half interval contains the grand mean.

The theorem assumes independent estimates, but sectors share the market's moves. The common part of the noise cancels in the deviations Rˉi​−Rˉˉ yet stays in σ2/T, so c^ is biased toward full shrinkage. Subtracting each day's cross-sectional mean removes it, and the verdict stands; a market-model alpha (LM23) is another way.

But the 28 subtracted series sum to zero each day, still co-move and have unequal noise, so no dominance theorem covers them. "James–Stein shrank fully" describes these 28 means, not a law. Jorion (1986) carried an empirical-Bayes estimate of means into portfolio choice (LM30).

The misses, the hindsight slope and the case's correlation come from one fixed split of 28 co-moving sectors, so they carry no standard error. The reverse direction and the lab's thirds show how much the misses move; in the thirds the correlation is 0.13 and 0.24, and the hindsight slope 0.09 and 0.61.

導讀收縮到總平均數

收縮估計量（shrinkage estimator）把每個估計值（estimate）往目標拉近。James-Stein 估計量（James–Stein estimator）的目標是總平均數（grand mean），拉近的程度由資料自己估計，所以它是一種經驗貝氏估計量（empirical Bayes estimator）。關鍵是比較兩個數字：單一產業平均報酬（mean return）的雜訊（noise）有多大，以及 28 個平均報酬彼此差多少。前半段的雜訊每年 7.18 個百分點，彼此的差異只有 4.50 個百分點，所以 c^=0，全部收縮到總平均數；用後半段檢驗，總平均數的誤差（error）8.5 點，小於各自平均數的 9.8 點。產業報酬彼此相關（correlated），共同成分（common component）在離差（deviation）中抵銷，卻留在雜訊的估計裡；扣掉每天的橫斷面平均（cross-sectional mean）後，結論不變。這是這 28 個平均數的結果，不是定律。

7Kelly with an Uncertain Mean

derive the Bayes Kelly fraction under log utility in a stated model, and compute the prior that makes fractional Kelly the Bayes bet
explain what breaks the Bayes Kelly result, and measure the size of its quadratic approximation on TAIEX data

LM4 advised betting a quarter to a half of the Kelly fraction. Thorp (2006, §7.3) and MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba (2010) give prudential reasons, and Rising and Wyner (2012) trace the benefit to reduced estimation error. A stated model turns the rule of thumb into a result:

  1. the index follows geometric Brownian motion with known volatility σ and unknown drift μ;
  2. the prior is μ∼N(0,σ02​), centred at zero;
  3. the investor holds a fixed fraction f and maximizes expected log wealth;
  4. borrowing costs nothing, rf​=0.

Under geometric Brownian motion (LM38) a fixed fraction grows at exactly fμ−21​f2σ2 a day, and LM42 derives Kelly in continuous time. Given μ, expected log growth over τ years is therefore (fμ−21​f2σ2)Aτ. The investor does not know μ and averages over the posterior, in which the growth is linear:

E[lnW0​Wτ​​​data]​=(fE[μ∣data]−21​f2σ2)Aτ.​
ResultBayes Kelly fraction

In the model above, the fraction that maximizes posterior-expected log growth, over any horizon τ, is

fB​=σ2E[μ∣data]​=cf^​,f^​=σ2Rˉ​,(24.5)
where the second form uses the prior centred at zero and (24.3). Applying (24.3) to the daily simple returns is an approximation under geometric Brownian motion. For daily returns the same follows from (4.2) without its μ2 term.

Fractional Kelly is thus the Bayes bet, and its fraction is the data's weight c. Browne and Whitt (1996) derive the general rule under log utility: the bet is linear in the posterior mean.

By (24.5), fB​=0.869×4.28=3.72. The exact Kelly condition (4.5) on TR23's returns, each shifted by one constant to the posterior mean (LM4's band method), gives 3.62. Averaging the sample's growth over the posterior of μ before maximizing gives 3.62 too.

The quadratic rule overstates the exact fraction by 0.14 at c=1 (4.28 against LM17's f^​∗=4.14), by 0.10 at c=0.869, and by 0.03 at c=21​ (2.14 against 2.11). In the model, the posterior-expected log growth is about 25% a year at fB​. The plug-in bet f^​=fB​/c keeps 1−(1−1/0.869)2=97.7% of it, by (4.15) with 1/c as its fraction.

Four departures break the result:

  • A prior centred away from zero. The Bayes bet is still E[μ∣data]/σ2, but no longer cf^​ (knowledge check).
  • An unknown σ. Under the conjugate prior of Gelman et al. (2013, §3.3), whose prior SD of μ is proportional to σ, the posterior of μ is a Student t. At T=5,849 this matters little. Volatility that changes from day to day is modelled in LM26.
  • Non-normal days. Fat tails and a negative skewness make the quadratic rule overstate the exact fraction (Example 9).
  • Other utilities. Power utility, under which fractional Kelly expresses risk aversion, is LM44's subject, and sizing from a bootstrap distribution is LM45's.
PitfallThe prior is the decision

Under (24.5) the fraction is c, so choosing the prior is choosing the bet. A prior picked to justify a wanted fraction repeats Section 5's error of a target chosen after the data. State the prior and its worth in years, as Exhibit 4 does, and read the result as a model result, not advice. Haircuts for a backtest's Sharpe ratio are LM33's.

導讀分數凱利是懷疑者的貝氏下注

第 7 節的模型假設波動率（volatility）已知、事前分布（prior distribution）以零為中心、目標是期望對數成長（expected log growth）。在這個模型裡，使事後期望對數成長最大的部位是 fB​=E[μ∣data]/σ2=cf^​，而且與投資期間長短無關。所以分數凱利（fractional Kelly）就是貝氏下注（Bayes bet），比例恰好是資料權重（data's weight）c。要得到 LM4 的四分之一或一半凱利，懷疑者的事前標準差（prior standard deviation）必須是每年 2.25 或 3.89 個百分點，相當於 71.3 年或 23.8 年的資料。在半凱利懷疑者的事後分布（posterior distribution）下，直接用估計值（estimate）下注就是兩倍凱利，期望成長為零。事前分布若不以零為中心，這個對應就不成立；報酬有厚尾（heavy tail）與負偏態（negative skewness）時，它也只是近似。

8Bayesian Inference in Python

compute posteriors, shrinkage factors and out-of-sample errors in Python, passing every divisor explicitly

SciPy's stats.beta gives a beta law's mean, quantiles and tail probabilities; for integer parameters, the lab computes them exactly with Fraction. The functions below pass every divisor explicitly.

Python
import numpy as np
from scipy import optimize, stats

post = stats.beta(82 + 8, 82 + 4)      # sceptic's prior, 8 good months of 12
print(post.mean(), post.sf(0.55))      # 0.5114, 0.1526


def posterior(Rbar, s, T, sigma0, mu0=0.0):     # (24.3), daily units
    c = sigma0**2 / (sigma0**2 + s**2 / T)
    return c, mu0 + c * (Rbar - mu0), np.sqrt(c) * s / np.sqrt(T)


def james_stein(X):                    # X: T x N daily returns, (24.4)
    T, N = X.shape
    m = X.mean(axis=0)
    noise = (X.var(axis=0, ddof=0) / T).mean()  # common noise variance
    c_hat = max(0.0, 1 - (N - 3) * noise / ((m - m.mean()) ** 2).sum())
    return c_hat, m.mean() + c_hat * (m - m.mean())


def kelly_exact(R, target):            # root of (4.5), returns shifted
    x = R - R.mean() + target
    return optimize.brentq(lambda f: (x / (1 + f * x)).mean(),
                           1e-6, 1 / abs(x.min()) - 1e-6)

Summary

  • Bayes' rule for a parameter (24.1) multiplies a prior density by the likelihood; a beta prior and a binomial record give a beta posterior (24.2).
  • Each posterior mean moves from the prior mean toward the data by the data's weight c; for a normal mean with known variance, precisions add (24.3).
  • A credible interval is a probability statement about the parameter; a confidence interval describes a procedure.
  • James–Stein (24.4) estimates the weight from the spread of many means and beats the raw means under its conditions.
  • On U28's halves c^=0: the 28 sector means carry almost nothing beyond their average.
  • In the stated model the Bayes Kelly fraction is cf^​ (24.5). Quarter and half Kelly are the bets of sceptics whose priors are worth three times and once the sample.
