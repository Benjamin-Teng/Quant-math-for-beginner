Learning Module Overview
  • On 7 April 2025 the TAIEX total-return index fell 9.70%: 2.3 to 3.6 times the one-day 99% value at risk that six models had forecast on 2 April.
  • Historical VaR is minus an order statistic, and historical ES, (32.2), is the tail mean of the empirical distribution. On TR23's 5,849 returns the 99% VaR and ES are 3.57% and 4.69%, against a normal model's 2.75% and 3.16%.
  • From 5,849 days an iid bootstrap gives the 1% quantile a standard error of 0.119 points. A block bootstrap, which keeps the order of days, gives 0.257. From 250 iid returns no order statistic is a 95% lower bound.
  • Two constructed, independent bonds held half and half raise VaR from −2.00% to 29.00%, while ES falls from 47.60% to 29.20%: ES is coherent, and it is the minimum of a convex function, (32.7).
  • Under GARCH with normal innovations 7 April was a once-in-3.7-million-years day. With Student-t innovations it was once in 32.6 years. The law's tail decides.
  • Over 4,609 days historical simulation had 56 exceedances against 46.09 expected, but they clustered. The filtered models clustered far less and exceeded too often.
  • The eight rules' maximum drawdowns run from 18.42% to 33.56%, and three of them breach a hypothetical 20% limit. Permuting the returns finds no rule's order of days unusual at the 5% level.

1Introduction

LM27 measured a book's typical risk by its volatility σp​, (27.4). This module measures the tail, by value at risk and expected shortfall, and the path, by drawdowns.

Motivating CaseDid any model see 7 April coming?

Wednesday 2 April 2025 was the last session before the Qingming holiday. That evening six models forecast the next session's one-day 99% value at risk of the TAIEX total-return index. Their answers ran from 2.66% (a normal law on its last 1,231 returns) to 4.14% (GARCH with Student-t innovations). The next session came after four days without trading.

On Monday 7 April the index fell 9.70%, the worst of its 5,849 daily returns since 2003. That loss was 2.3 to 3.6 times each model's value at risk, and 1.8 to 3.2 times its expected shortfall. Over the ten sessions from 7 April it lost 8.92% in all. It was also the worst day for seven of the eight allocation rules of the through case. What each rule lost is LM35's replay.

Was every model wrong, or was the day just rare? How precise is a 99% value at risk in the first place? And what does a one-day number say about the fall from a peak?

Exhibit 1 lists the forecasts.

Sections 2 to 7 define, fit, forecast and backtest the measures. Section 8 measures the rules' drawdowns. Extreme value theory beyond Hill's estimator, backtests of ES, multivariate GARCH and conditional drawdown measures are not in this book.

2Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall

derive the historical, normal and Student-t forms of VaR and ES from their definitions by the quantile function
compute historical, normal, Student-t and Hill-tail VaR and ES, and diagnose what each model misses

A 99% one-day value at risk is a loss exceeded with probability 1%. Expected shortfall is the mean loss beyond it. Both come from LM14's quantile function FR−1​(u)=min{x:FR​(x)≥u}.

DefinitionValue at risk and expected shortfall

Let R be the simple return of a position over m sessions, with distribution function FR​, and let 0<u<1. The value at risk and, when E∣R∣<∞, the expected shortfall at tail probability u are

VaRu​:=−FR−1​(u),ESu​:=−u1​∫0u​FR−1​(v)dv.​(32.1)
Both are positive for a loss. "99% VaR" is u=0.01, and the horizon is one day unless m is named.

For a continuous FR​ the integral is a tail mean. By (18.1), FR−1​(U) with U∼Unif(0,1) has the law of R, and U≤u exactly when FR−1​(U)≤FR−1​(u), up to probability 0. So the integral is E[R1{R≤FR−1​(u)}], and since Pr(R≤FR−1​(u))=u,

ESu​=−E[R∣R≤FR−1​(u)]for continuous FR​.​

This tail mean, with the sign reversed, is LM5's (5.11) and LM15's at the VaR level.

The historical estimators apply (32.1) to the empirical distribution F^, mass 1/T on each return. Its quantile function is the step F^−1(v)=R(i)​ for (i−1)/T<v≤i/T, so the integral takes j−1 whole steps and part of step j:

VaR=−R(j)​,j=⌈uT⌉,ES=−u1​[T1​i<j∑​R(i)​+(u−Tj−1​)R(j)​].​(32.2)

When uT is whole, ES is the mean of the j worst returns. Form (32.2) is Acerbi and Tasche's (2002a) tail mean applied to F^, the book's one historical ES.

導讀風險值與預期短缺：同一個分位數的兩種讀法

風險值（value at risk，VaR）是報酬分布下方分位數（quantile）的負值：99% 的單日風險值，是一天只有 1% 機率會超過的損失。預期短缺（expected shortfall，ES）則問：落到那 1% 的壞日子時，平均損失多少。歷史模擬法（historical simulation）直接用經驗分布（empirical distribution），把報酬由小到大排成順序統計量（order statistic），第 j=⌈uT⌉ 小的報酬取負號就是風險值。預期短缺把前 j−1 個壞日子全部計入、第 j 個按比例計入再平均，這就是 (32.2)。樣本很小時，幾種平均方法差距不小，本書一律用 (32.2)。

Example 3Normal and Student t on TR23

Find TR23's 1% VaR and ES under a normal law with Rˉ=0.0625% and s=1.2084% (LM19), and under LM20's t fit: ν=3.065, location 0.0997%, scale 0.7761%.

Solution

By (32.3), Φ−1(0.01)=−2.3263 and φ/u=2.6652 give VaR 2.75% and ES 3.16%. By (32.4), the unrounded fit's q=−4.4644 gives 3.37% and 5.19%. The data give 3.57% and 4.69%. The normal misses both. The t nearly matches the VaR but overshoots the ES, "too heavy from −5%" (LM15).

VaR needs no moment, ES a first (ν>1, or α>1 for a power tail), and the normal fit a variance. TR23's t, ν=3.065, has no fourth moment, so no standard error built on kurtosis applies (LM5's warning).

LM20's Hill estimate (20.10) (Hill 1975) fits a power tail beyond x0​,

Pr(−R>x)≈Tk​(x0​x​)−α,x≥x0​,

whose density is proportional to x−α−1. Setting the fitted tail equal to u gives the VaR, and for α^>1 Section 2's tail mean gives the ES:

VaRu​=x0​(uTk​)1/α^,u≤Tk​,ESu​=∫VaRu​∞​x−α^−1dx∫VaRu​∞​x−α^dx​=α^−1α^​VaRu​.​

At x0​=3%, k=108 and α^=3.63 the 1% figures are 3.55% and 4.90% (Problem 11).

The horizon. The m​ rule multiplies a one-day VaR by m​. It is exact only for a log return over m iid normal days with zero mean, whose sum (19.7) gives m times the daily variance. A non-normal law changes the quantile's scaling, a mean adds m times, and compounding to er−1 bends the result.

常態分布（normal distribution）低估 TR23 的尾部風險（tail risk）：1% 的風險值（value at risk）少了 0.82 個百分點，預期短缺（expected shortfall）少了 1.53 個百分點。Student t 分布有厚尾（heavy tails），風險值接近資料，預期短缺卻偏高。風險值不需要任何動差（moment），預期短缺只需要一階動差存在，也就是自由度（degrees of freedom）或尾部指數（tail index）大於 1。所以即使尾部很厚，只要這個條件成立，預期短缺仍有定義。把單日風險值乘上 m​ 的 m​ 法則（m​ rule），只在每日對數報酬（log return）獨立同分布（iid），且服從平均為零的常態分布時才精確。

4How Precise Is a Value at Risk?

derive a quantile's distribution-free interval, compute its standard error, and explain why 250 returns are too few

A historical VaR is one order statistic, so it moves from sample to sample. Its precision needs no model for FR​.

PropositionA distribution-free interval for a quantile

Let T returns be iid with a continuous distribution function FR​, let X∼Bin(T,u), and let j1​<j2​ be ranks. Then

Pr(R(j1​)​≤FR−1​(u)<R(j2​)​)=Pr(j1​≤X≤j2​−1).​(32.5)

Proof. By continuity each return lies at or below FR−1​(u) with probability u, independently, so the count of such returns has X's law. And R(j)​ is at or below FR−1​(u) exactly when at least j returns are. So the left event is {X≥j1​}∩{X≤j2​−1}. ■

The equal-tail 95% interval takes j1​ the largest rank with Pr(X≤j1​−1)≤0.025 and j2​ the smallest with Pr(X≤j2​−1)≥0.975, so its coverage is at least 95%. For large T,

SE(R(⌈uT⌉)​)≈fR​(FR−1​(u))u(1−u)/T​​,(32.6)

for iid returns with a density fR​ positive and continuous at the quantile (stated without proof; Glasserman 2003, (9.7)). The interval's ranks estimate the density by their spacing, (j2​−j1​)/(T(R(j2​)​−R(j1​)​)).

5Coherence and Expected Shortfall

derive ES as a convex minimum, state the coherence axioms, and show VaR failing subadditivity
explain what the m​ rule, Basel's ES and the Brownian drawdown result assume, and choose a measure

A risk measure should not punish diversification. Artzner, Delbaen, Eber and Heath (1999) wrote this down as four axioms.

DefinitionA coherent risk measure

A risk measure R(⋅) gives a number to a position's profit and loss, written here as a return on one unit of capital. It is coherent if, for all positions R1​ and R2​,

  1. translation invariance: R(R1​+ζ)=R(R1​)−ζ for a sure return ζ;
  2. subadditivity: R(R1​+R2​)≤R(R1​)+R(R2​);
  3. positive homogeneity: R(ηR1​)=ηR(R1​) for every η≥0;
  4. monotonicity: R(R2​)≤R(R1​) when R1​≤R2​ in every outcome.

VaR meets axioms 1, 3 and 4: a quantile moves with a sure return, scales with a positive multiple and keeps the order of outcomes. For jointly normal returns and u<21​ it is also subadditive. By (16.7) the sum is normal, and by (16.4) its standard deviation is at most σ1​+σ2​, as LM10 shows for a long-only book. Since −Φ−1(u)>0, (32.3) then gives subadditivity, which can fail without normality.

Example 6Two bonds

Two independent bonds each lose 60% with probability 0.8% and gain 2% otherwise (constructed). Compare one bond with half in each at u=1%.

Solution

Default is rarer than 1%, so one bond's 1% quantile is its gain: VaR −2.00%. By (32.1) its ES is −[0.008×(−60%)+0.002×2%]/0.01=47.60%.

With half in each, some default has probability 1−0.9922=1.59%, above 1%. So the quantile is one default, (−60%+2%)/2: VaR 29.00%, against the halves' −2.00% summed. The ES is 29.20%, below the halves' 47.60%. Diversifying raised VaR and lowered ES.

ES is coherent (Acerbi and Tasche 2002a; stated without proof). It is also a convex minimum, which makes it easy to optimize.

TheoremExpected shortfall as a minimum (Rockafellar and Uryasev 2000)

Let R have a continuous distribution function and E∣R∣<∞. Then

ESu​=θmin​{θ+u1​E[(−R−θ)+]},(32.7)
attained at θ=VaRu​. The function minimized is convex in θ, and jointly convex in θ and the weights when R=w⊤Rt​.

Proof. Each (−w⊤Rt​−θ)+ is the larger of 0 and an affine function, so it is convex by (29.1), and so is a mean of such terms. The minimum is the Deep Dive's. ■

Deep DiveDeep Dive: why the minimum is the expected shortfalloptional · click to expand

For continuous FR​ the derivative in θ is 1−Pr(−R>θ)/u, which rises with θ and is zero at θ=VaRu​. There the value is VaRu​ plus E[(−R−VaRu​)+]/u, which equals E[−R1{−R>VaRu​}]/u, so it is ESu​ (Theorem 1 of Rockafellar and Uryasev's 1999 working paper).

Under F^ the function is piecewise linear, with kinks at the losses −Rt​. Its slope 1−#{t:−Rt​>θ}/(uT) changes sign at the loss of rank j, counted from the largest. When uT is whole it is zero between ranks j+1 and j. At rank j the value is (32.2): on TR23, 4.689% at 3.567%.

So minimizing ES over long-only weights is a convex problem of LM29's (29.3), and under F^ a linear program (Problem 12).

Basel's 2019 market-risk standard uses ES at 97.5% (BCBS 2019, MAR33.3), where the 1996 rules used a 99% VaR (BCBS 1996b, B.4(b)). Under a normal law with zero mean, ES0.025​=2.338σ and VaR0.01​=2.326σ.

導讀一致性：分散投資不該增加風險

一致性風險量數（coherent risk measure）要滿足四個公理（axiom），其中劣可加性（subadditivity）要求合併兩個部位的風險不超過各自風險之和。風險值（value at risk）不一定滿足：兩檔獨立債券各持一半，至少一檔違約的機率超過 1%，風險值反而從兩半合計的 −2.00% 升到 29.00%。預期短缺（expected shortfall）滿足劣可加性，在這個例子裡從 47.60% 降到 29.20%；它也叫條件風險值（conditional value at risk，CVaR）。預期短缺還能寫成凸函數（convex function）的最小值 (32.7)，所以用歷史報酬的經驗分布（empirical distribution）時，最小化投資組合（portfolio）的預期短缺是線性規劃（linear programming）問題。

7Backtesting Value at Risk

Day t is an exceedance when Rt​<−VaRt​, with a guard of 10−12. The smallest margin ∣Rt​+VaRt​∣ is 0.011 points for HS and 0.0012 over all six models, so no return ties with its forecast. Under a correct model exceedances are independent with probability u, so their count is X∼Bin(n,u).

導讀回測：次數與時機

回測（backtest）逐日比較前一天的風險值（value at risk）預測與當天報酬。超過 VaR 的日子（exceedance）在模型正確時彼此獨立，次數服從二項分布（binomial distribution）。歷史模擬法（historical simulation）超過 VaR 的日子有 56 天，與模型正確時應有的 46.09 天相去不遠，卻常常連續出現，所以通不過獨立性檢定（independence test）。EWMA 與 GARCH 模型超過 VaR 的日子，連續出現的情形比歷史模擬法少得多，天數卻太多。同一批預測做了 12 個檢定，所以用 Bonferroni 的水準 0.0042 判讀。

9Risk Measures in Python

compute historical, normal, Student-t and Hill-tail VaR and ES, and diagnose what each model misses
Python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np
from scipy import integrate, stats

def var_es(R, u):                         # (32.2): historical VaR and ES
    x = np.sort(R)
    T = len(x)
    j = int(np.ceil(u * T - 1e-12))       # j = ceil(uT), with a guard
    assert x[j - 1] == np.quantile(R, u, method="inverted_cdf")
    tail = x[: j - 1].sum() / T + x[j - 1] * (u - (j - 1) / T)
    return -x[j - 1], -tail / u

def es_t(R, u):                           # (32.4) on an ML fit, in percent
    nu, loc, sc = stats.t.fit(100 * R)
    q = stats.t.ppf(u, nu)
    es = -loc + sc * (nu + q * q) * stats.t.pdf(q, nu) / ((nu - 1) * u)
    tail = integrate.quad(lambda y: y * stats.t.pdf(y, nu), -np.inf, q,
                          epsabs=1e-14, epsrel=1e-13)[0]
    assert abs(-loc - sc * tail / u - es) < 1e-12     # the Deep Dive
    return es

def min_es(Rw, u):                        # (32.7) as a linear program
    T, N = Rw.shape                       # Rw: T x N returns in percent
    w, th, s = cp.Variable(N), cp.Variable(), cp.Variable(T)
    cons = [s >= 0, s + Rw @ w + th >= 0, w >= 0, cp.sum(w) == 1]
    prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(th + cp.sum(s) / (u * T)), cons)
    prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, tol_gap_abs=1e-12,
               tol_gap_rel=1e-12, tol_feas=1e-12)
    assert prob.status == "optimal" and w.value is not None
    return w.value, prob.value

In min_es two linear constraints on each slack s[t] replace (−w⊤Rt​−θ)+, which makes (32.7) a linear program. The lab refits the forecasts with LM26's arch code.

Summary

    The minimum exists: the weights lie in a closed bounded set, and for fixed weights the objective grows without bound as ∣θ∣→∞. Within 10−9 of the minimum no weight moves by more than 10−7, so it is unique here. The weights are plug-in estimates with an unmeasured error.
