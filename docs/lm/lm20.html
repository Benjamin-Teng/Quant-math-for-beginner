Estimation Theory and Maximum Likelihood

An estimate is one draw from a sampling distribution. Its standard error says how far another sample could move it: TAIEX's 23.8 years pin the volatility to 18.3%–19.6% but leave the mean anywhere from 7.8% to 23.0% a year (95% intervals).

Learning Module Overview
  • An estimator is a random variable, and its mean squared error is its variance plus its squared bias, (20.1). A biased estimator can win: with one year of TAIEX-like days, 0.397Rˉ has 0.397 times the mean squared error of Rˉ.
  • The standard error of a mean is σ/T​, (20.2). Annualized it is A​σ/τ​: the years of data decide it, not the sampling frequency. TAIEX's 23.8 years give 3.89 points; ±1 point at 95% would need 1,381 years.
  • The interval Rˉ±1.96s/T​ is a large-sample one; for normal days the tT−1​ interval with su​ is exact, (20.4). At T=5 the first covers the mean in 84.5% of simulated samples, the second in 95.0%.
  • Maximum likelihood gives Rˉ and s (divisor T) for the normal model, (20.5). For TAIEX it fits a Student-t with ν=3.065 (standard error 0.144), whose log-likelihood exceeds the normal's by 561.8.
  • The observed information gives standard errors, (20.6), and no unbiased estimator beats the Cramér–Rao bound, (20.7). The profile log-likelihood puts ν between 2.80 and 3.37.
  • The delta method, (20.8) and (20.9), gives TAIEX's annual volatility 18.96% (SE 0.33), the Kelly fraction 4.28 (SE 1.11; 2.10 to 6.46) and the Sharpe ratio 0.81 (SE 0.21) a year, all under independent days and a finite fourth moment.
  • The Hill estimator, (20.10), puts TAIEX's tail index at 3.63 (SE 0.35) from the 108 losses of 3% or more. It rises from 2.49 at 2% to 4.93 at 4%: the threshold trades bias against variance.

1Introduction

Every number computed from data is an estimate: another sample would give another value. This module measures how far estimates move between samples, and how to choose an estimator.

Motivating CaseWhat 23.8 years pin down

From 3 January 2003 to 8 October 2026 the TAIEX total-return index, dividends reinvested, had calendar years from −43.07% (2008) to +83.34% (2009) (LM14). Its average day, 0.0625%, is 15.38% a year at A=246.12 sessions. Its daily standard deviation, 1.2084%, is 18.96% a year (LM19).

Treat the 5,849 days as independent draws from one distribution. They then pin the volatility to 18.3% to 19.6% a year, but the mean only to 7.8% to 23.0%, both at 95% (Exhibit 1). Five-minute data would not narrow that range; only more years would. Why is the mean so much harder to estimate? And how far can the Kelly fraction of 4.28 that these numbers suggest be trusted?

Sections 2 to 4 treat estimators and intervals, 5 and 6 maximum likelihood, 7 the delta method, 8 the Hill estimator and 9 Python.

2Estimators, Bias and Mean Squared Error

explain an estimator as a random variable with a sampling distribution, and calculate its bias, variance and mean squared error, including the trade-off of a scaled mean

Before the data are drawn, the sample mean is a random variable: each possible sample gives its own value. Exhibit 2 draws 10,000 one-year samples of 246 days from two models with TR23's mean and standard deviation, a normal and LM15's Student-t, and estimates each sample's annual mean and volatility. Whether TAIEX's days are independent is a question about data, which LM19 answers; here the model assumes it.

The annual mean's standard deviation depends only on the variance: 18.9 points for the normal model, 18.7 for the Student-t and 18.96 in theory. The volatility's depends on the tails: 0.86 points for normal days (theory 0.855), about 4 for Student-t days. Its 95% range, F^−1(0.025) to F^−1(0.975), is 17.3% to 20.6% (normal) and 14.6% to 26.8% (Student-t, median 17.7%).

DefinitionEstimator, bias and mean squared error

An estimator θ^ of a parameter θ is a function of the sample, and its value on the data is the estimate. Its distribution across samples is its sampling distribution, and its standard deviation the standard error SE(θ^). The bias is Bias(θ^):=Eθ^−θ; the estimator is unbiased when the bias is 0. The mean squared error is MSE(θ^):=E[(θ^−θ)2].

ResultMean squared error

If Eθ^2<∞, then

MSE(θ^)=Var(θ^)+Bias(θ^)2.(20.1)

Proof. Write θ^−θ=(θ^−Eθ^)+Bias(θ^) and square. The cross term is the constant bias times θ^−Eθ^, whose expectation is 0 by (14.3). ■

Example 1Shrinking the sample mean

Let T iid days have mean μ and standard deviation σ. Find the MSE of ηRˉ and the η that minimizes it. Evaluate η∗ for one year (T=246) and for TR23's T=5,849, with TR23's daily Rˉ/s=0.051727 as the model's SR=μ/σ.

Solution

By (14.3), (14.4) and (17.1), E(ηRˉ)=ημ and Var(ηRˉ)=η2σ2/T, so Bias=(η−1)μ. By (20.1),

MSE(ηRˉ)=Tη2σ2​+(η−1)2μ2.
The derivative in η vanishes at
η∗=μ2+σ2/Tμ2​=1+TSR2TSR2​,MSE(η∗Rˉ)=η∗Tσ2​.​
For one year, TSR2=246×0.0517272=0.658 and η∗=0.397: the shrunk mean has 0.397 times the mean squared error of Rˉ. For the whole sample, TSR2=15.65 and η∗=0.940. The best η depends on the unknown μ; LM24 supplies it from a prior.

An estimator is consistent when it converges in probability to θ as T→∞ (LM17). By (17.2), Rˉ is consistent for iid days with finite variance. The strong law, applied to Rt​ and to Rt2​, makes s2=T1​∑t​Rt2​−Rˉ2 converge to σ2, so s is consistent too.

PitfallUnbiased is not the same as best

An unbiased estimator is right on average but can still spread widely: in Example 1 the biased η∗Rˉ beats Rˉ. Report the bias and the standard error separately.

導讀估計量是隨機變數

樣本抽出之前，樣本平均數（sample mean）本身就是隨機變數（random variable）；用樣本算參數（parameter）的公式叫估計量（estimator），它在不同樣本間的分布（distribution）叫抽樣分布（sampling distribution）。估計量的期望值（expectation）減去真值是偏誤（bias），偏誤為零的叫不偏估計量（unbiased estimator）。好壞常用均方誤差（mean squared error）衡量，它等於變異數（variance）加偏誤的平方，所以稍有偏誤、變異數卻小得多的估計量可能更好：一年的資料下，0.397Rˉ 的均方誤差只有 Rˉ 的 0.397 倍。樣本越大越接近真值的叫一致估計量（consistent estimator）。模擬一萬個一年的樣本，年化平均報酬（annualized mean return）的標準差（standard deviation）約 19 個百分點，與尾部（tails）無關；年化波動率（annualized volatility）的分散卻由尾部決定。

3The Standard Error of a Mean

derive the standard error σ/T​ of a sample mean and the bias of s2, and explain why the standard error of an annualized mean depends on the years of data, not on the sampling frequency

For uncorrelated days with a common mean and variance, the sum rule (16.3) and (17.1) give VarRˉ=σ2/T. A mean over τ=T/A years scales by A, so its standard error does too.

ResultStandard error of a mean

Let R1​,…,RT​ be uncorrelated with a common mean μ and a common variance σ2<∞. Then

SE(Rˉ)=T​σ​,SE(ARˉ)=T​Aσ​=τ​A​σ​,​(20.2)
estimated by replacing σ with s.

Example 2The standard error of TAIEX's mean

Compute the standard error of TR23's daily mean and of its annual mean ARˉ=15.38%.

Solution

By (20.2), s/T​=1.2084%/5,849​=0.01580% a day, the value that LM18's (18.8) gave for its bootstrap model. Times A=246.12, it is 3.89 points a year, a quarter of the 15.38%. Equivalently, 18.96%/23.7645​=3.89. LM7's 7.36 and 6.09 points over 4.95 years follow the same rule.

Merton (1980) drew the consequence: finer sampling does not help the mean. By (1.12), ∑t​rt​=ln(PT​/P0​), so the annual mean log return Arˉ=ln(PT​/P0​)/τ depends only on the first and last price. Five-minute log returns over the same years give the same number.

Exhibit 3 measures both standard errors at eight sampling intervals, from one day to one year. The mean's stays between 3.87 and 4.53 points, around the iid line. The volatility's grows from 0.34 points with daily data to 4.43 with annual sums, and the normal model's from 0.18 to 2.80. Fine sampling sharpens the volatility, never the mean. A mean known to ±1 point at 95% needs (1.96×18.96)2=1,381 years of data.

The mean's standard error survives volatility clustering if the days are uncorrelated: with unequal variances σt2​, (16.3) gives VarRˉ=T21​∑t​σt2​. Correlated days break (20.2). LM19's variance ratios measure how far daily variances fail to add up; LM23's HAC errors and LM22's block bootstrap then repair the standard error.

An expansion around μ gives the bias of s2. Since ∑t​(Rt​−Rˉ)=0,

t=1∑T​(Rt​−μ)2=t=1∑T​(Rt​−Rˉ)2+T(Rˉ−μ)2.

The left side has expectation Tσ2, and by (20.2) the last term has σ2, so E∑t​(Rt​−Rˉ)2=(T−1)σ2.

ResultThe bias of the sample variance

Under the conditions of (20.2),

Es2=TT−1​σ2,su2​:=T−1T​s2,Esu2​=σ2.​(20.3)

The conditions of (20.2) are those under which LM10's divisor T−1 removes a small bias. NumPy's ddof=1 and Polars' default compute su​, which LM19 introduced.

Example 3How much the divisor matters

Compute su​/s for TR23's 5,849 days and for the 23 annual log returns of 2003–2025, whose s is 22.207% (LM15).

Solution

The ratio is T/(T−1)​: 1.0000855 for TR23, a change in the fifth significant figure. For 23 values it is 23/22​=1.0225, so su​=22.706%, and Es2 is only 22/23=0.9565 of σ2. LM10's 4,382 returns gave 1.000114. The divisor matters for calendar years and other small samples, not for daily data.

PitfallAnnualizing the mean's error by the square root

A mean scales by A, so its standard error does too. The daily 0.0158% times A​ gives 0.25 points, A​=15.69 times less than the correct 3.89. Only a standard deviation scales by A​, and exactly only for log returns on uncorrelated days (LM19).

導讀平均數的標準誤只看年數

樣本平均數（sample mean）的標準誤（standard error）是 σ/T​，年化之後等於年化波動率（annualized volatility）除以資料年數的平方根，與抽樣頻率（sampling frequency）無關。加權股價報酬指數 23.8 年的資料，年化平均報酬（annualized mean return）的標準誤約 3.89 個百分點；改用五分鐘資料也不會變小，因為對數報酬（log return）的總和只取決於頭尾兩個價格。要讓年化平均報酬的 95% 信賴區間（confidence interval）縮到正負 1 個百分點，需要約 1,381 年的資料。樣本變異數（sample variance）除以 T 時，期望值（expectation）只有 σ2 的 (T−1)/T 倍，除以 T−1 才不偏（unbiased）；日資料只差在第五位有效數字（significant figure），23 個年報酬的標準差（standard deviation）則差 2.2%。

Knowledge Check 2

Three years of independent daily returns have an annual volatility of 18.96%. What is the standard error of the annual mean? Ten years? What if the returns were monthly over the same years?

Answer

By (20.2), 18.96/3​=10.9 points and 18.96/10​=6.0 points. Monthly data over the same years give the same, because only the years enter.

4Confidence Intervals

construct and interpret confidence intervals for a mean, with the normal approximation and with the tT−1​ interval of a normal model, and check their coverage by simulation

A standard error becomes an interval once the shape of the estimator's distribution is known.

DefinitionConfidence interval

A 95% confidence interval for θ is an interval computed from the sample that contains θ with probability 0.95 for every θ, exactly or approximately for large T. The probability is over samples, with θ fixed; it is the interval's coverage.

By (17.5), T​(Rˉ−μ)/σ is approximately standard normal for iid days with finite variance. Replacing σ by the consistent s keeps the approximation (stated without proof; Wasserman 2004). With Φ−1(0.975)=1.96, (15.6), this gives a large-sample interval; for normal days an exact one exists.

ResultConfidence intervals for a mean

Let the days be iid with mean μ and variance 0<σ2<∞. For large T, an approximate 95% interval for μ is Rˉ±1.96s/T​. If the days are iid normal and T≥2, an exact 95% interval is

Rˉ±FY−1​(0.975)T​su​​,Y∼tT−1​.(20.4)

Proof of the exact form. Let Z=T​(Rˉ−μ)/σ and V=(T−1)su2​/σ2. For iid normal days, Z∼N(0,1) and V∼χT−12​ (LM16; Blitzstein and Hwang 2019, Example 10.4.3), and they are independent (their Example 7.5.9). By their Definition 10.4.4, whose density is (15.9) (they state it without proof),

V/(T−1)​Z​=su​T​(Rˉ−μ)​∼tT−1​,

so ∣Rˉ−μ∣≤FY−1​(0.975)su​/T​ with probability 0.95. ■

Example 4TAIEX's annual mean

Give 95% intervals for TR23's annual mean with the normal approximation and with the tT−1​ form. How wide is the interval from one year of data with the same volatility?

Solution

By (20.4) and Example 2, 15.38%±1.96×3.89 runs from 7.8% to 23.0%. At T−1=5,848 the t quantile is 1.96037 against Φ−1(0.975)=1.95996, and su​/s=1.0000855, so the t interval differs by 0.002 points. One year of data gives a standard error of 18.96 points and a half-width of 1.96×18.96=37.2 points.

Exhibit 4 checks both intervals by simulation. With s and 1.96, small samples cover too rarely, 84.5% at T=5, because the normal quantile ignores the error in s, and divisor T shrinks s. The tT−1​ interval is exact for normal days and covers slightly too often for t3​ days. By T=246 every column is between 94.8% and 95.2%.

Exhibit 5 varies the years of data, with TR23's annual mean and volatility. The interval runs from −21.8% to 52.5% after one year, 3.6% to 27.1% after ten and 11.7% to 19.1% after a hundred.

The widget repeats the experiment of Exhibit 4 with 1,000 samples per click and draws the first 100 intervals.

PitfallThe 95% belongs to the procedure

Once computed, 7.8% to 23.0% either contains Aμ or does not. The 95% says that intervals built this way cover Aμ in 95% of samples, if the days are iid. A probability for Aμ itself needs LM24. An interval that excludes a value is the start of a test, LM21's subject; this module runs none.

5Maximum Likelihood

derive the maximum-likelihood estimators of the normal model, and fit a Student-t by maximum likelihood numerically

LM15 fitted a Student-t to TAIEX with SciPy's t.fit, by maximum likelihood. Let the days be iid with a density fR​(x;θ), known up to the parameters θ.

DefinitionLikelihood

The likelihood of iid observations R1​,…,RT​ is L(θ):=∏t=1T​fR​(Rt​;θ), and the log-likelihood is ℓ(θ):=lnL(θ)=∑t​lnfR​(Rt​;θ). The maximum-likelihood estimator (MLE) θ^ maximizes ℓ over the parameters the model allows.

The likelihood holds the data fixed and ranks parameter values by the density they give the sample. For the normal model, fR​(x;μ,σ)=φ((x−μ)/σ)/σ, and Section 3's expansion gives

ℓ(μ,σ)=​−2T​ln(2π)−Tlnσ−2σ2Ts2+T(Rˉ−μ)2​.​
ResultNormal maximum likelihood

For iid normal days with T≥2, not all equal, the MLE is

μ^​=Rˉ,σ^=s.(20.5)
For iid NN​(μ,Σ) vectors Rt​, the MLE is Rˉ and LM10's Σ^ of (10.2), whenever Σ^ is positive definite (Deep Dive 1).

Proof (one variable). For every σ, ℓ is largest at μ=Rˉ, where it is −2T​ln(2π)−Tlnσ−Ts2/(2σ2). Its derivative in σ, −T/σ+Ts2/σ3, is positive below s and negative above. ■

So the maximum-likelihood variance s2 divides by T and is biased, by (20.3): maximum likelihood aims at large-sample precision, not at unbiasedness.

Example 5LM5's normal fit is maximum likelihood

LM5 fitted N(μ,σ2) to 0050's 2,902 daily total returns with μ^​=0.0871% and σ^=s=1.2385%. Is that the MLE? What is TR23's?

Solution

Yes, by (20.5): LM5's "population standard deviation" of the sample divides by T. The mean's standard error is 0.0230% (LM4). For TR23 the MLE is μ^​=0.0625% and σ^=1.2084%, not su​=1.2085%.

Example 6A Student-t for TAIEX by maximum likelihood

Fit the Student-t to TR23's daily returns by maximum likelihood, and compare its maximal ℓ with the normal model's.

Solution

SciPy's t.fit returns ν=3.06522, and a tighter Nelder–Mead search from there reaches 3.06526, location 0.0997% and scale 0.7761% (Exhibit 6). The maximal ℓ exceeds the normal model's by 561.8. By (15.11), the fit's standard deviation is 0.7761%×3.065/1.065​=1.317%, against s=1.2084%.

PitfallThe Student-t's location is not the mean

The fit centres a symmetric model on skewed data (skewness −0.34). Its location, 0.0997%, sits near LM14's median, 0.0879% (its standard error is LM32's), and more than half above the mean, 0.0625%. Use Rˉ for the drift.

Knowledge Check 4

Why must the Student-t's maximal log-likelihood be at least the normal's, and what does the gain of 561.8 not yet show?

Answer

The normal is the Student-t's limit as ν→∞, so the Student-t's maximum cannot be lower. Whether 561.8 exceeds what a third parameter buys by chance needs a likelihood-ratio test, which this book does not cover (Section 6).

6Fisher Information and the Cramér–Rao Bound

compute standard errors from the observed information, and state asymptotic normality and the Cramér–Rao bound with their conditions

A sharp peak in ℓ pins the parameter down; a flat top leaves many values almost as likely.

DefinitionScore and Fisher information

For one observation with density fR​(x;θ), the score is ∂θ​lnfR​(R;θ) and the Fisher information is

I(θ):=−E[∂θ2∂2​lnfR​(R;θ)].
The observed information of a sample is −ℓ′′(θ^), and for a parameter vector the matrix −H at θ^.

A sample of T iid observations carries TI(θ), which the observed information estimates. Deep Dive 2 shows that I(θ) is also the variance of the score.

TheoremAsymptotic normality of the MLE

Let the following hold:

  • the observations are iid with density fR​(x;θ), the model is correct, and distinct θ give distinct densities;
  • the true θ is an interior point of the parameter set, and the support of fR​ does not depend on θ;
  • the density is smooth in θ, with 0<I(θ)<∞.

Then, for large T,

θ^≈N(θ,TI(θ)1​),SE(θ^)≈(−ℓ′′(θ^))−1/2,​(20.6)
and for a vector Cov(θ^)≈(−H)−1 at θ^. Stated without proof (Wasserman 2004).

Example 7The normal model's information

Compute I(μ) and I(σ) for N(μ,σ2), and the standard errors of TR23's μ^​ and σ^.

Solution

From lnfR​=−lnσ−(x−μ)2/(2σ2) plus a constant, ∂μ2​lnfR​=−1/σ2 and ∂σ2​lnfR​=1/σ2−3(x−μ)2/σ4, whose expectation is −2/σ2. The mixed derivative has expectation 0, so the two parameters can be treated one at a time: I(μ)=1/σ2 and I(σ)=2/σ2. By (20.6),

SE(μ^​)=T​s​=0.0158%,SE(σ^)=2T​s​=0.0112%.​
The first is (20.2) again, and the second is LM18's sY​/2n​ for near-normal draws.

The profile log-likelihood of one parameter maximizes ℓ over the others at each of its values. Exhibit 7 draws it for ν, beside the parabola of the observed information. Where the curve falls 1.962/2=1.92 below its top it gives 2.80 to 3.37; the parabola gives ν^±1.96×0.144, 2.78 to 3.35. TheoremCramér–Rao bound

Let the following hold:

  • the conditions of (20.6);
  • the identity ∫fR​(x;θ)dx=1 may be differentiated twice under the integral sign, and Eθ^ once;
  • the estimator θ^ of θ from T iid observations is unbiased.

Then

Var(θ^)≥TI(θ)1​.(20.7)

Deep Dive 2 proves the bound (Rao 1992, a reprint). In the normal model Rˉ attains it: VarRˉ=σ2/T=1/(TI(μ)). Inside the Student-t model, Rˉ's standard error is the fit's 1.317%/T​=0.0172%, and the location's is 0.0124%, a ratio of 0.72. That efficiency holds only if the days are Student-t; on TR23's skewed days the location estimates a different centre from the mean (Section 5).

PitfallAn information-based standard error assumes the model

導讀概度、訊息與下界

把資料固定、讓參數（parameter）變動，樣本的聯合密度（joint density）就成了概度函數（likelihood function），使它最大的參數值就是最大概度估計（maximum likelihood estimation）。常態模型（normal model）的最大概度估計正是樣本平均數（sample mean）與除以 T 的標準差（standard deviation）；t 分布（Student-t distribution）則要用數值方法（numerical methods）求解，加權股價報酬指數的自由度（degrees of freedom）約為 3.065。單筆觀察的對數概度（log-likelihood）二階導數（second derivative）取負號後的期望值（expectation）是費雪訊息（Fisher information）；實務上用樣本對數概度在頂點的彎曲程度，即負的 Hessian 矩陣，也就是觀察訊息矩陣（observed information matrix），其反矩陣（inverse matrix）對角元素（diagonal entries）的平方根就是近似的標準誤（standard error）；樣本大時，估計量（estimator）具漸近常態性（asymptotic normality）。克萊姆-饒歐下界（Cramér–Rao lower bound）說不偏估計量（unbiased estimator）的變異數（variance）不會低於 1/(TI)，達到下界的估計量最有效率（efficiency）。剖面概度（profile likelihood）比拋物線（parabola）更忠實：自由度的區間（interval）2.80 到 3.37 並不對稱。

7The Delta Method

apply the delta method to the annualized volatility, the Kelly fraction and the Sharpe ratio of independent returns

The annual volatility, the Kelly fraction and the Sharpe ratio are smooth functions of Rˉ and s2. Their standard errors follow from a first-order expansion.

TheoremDelta method

Let θ^ be approximately normal with mean θ and covariance matrix Cov(θ^), and let h be differentiable at θ with ∇h=0. Then h(θ^) is approximately normal with mean h(θ) and

Varh(θ^)≈∇h⊤Cov(θ^)∇h.(20.8)

Why. By (7.1), h(θ^)≈h(θ)+∇h⊤(θ^−θ), and (16.4) gives the variance of this linear function, as (10.4) gave a portfolio's (Wasserman 2004).

Up to a term of order 1/T, s2 averages the iid terms (Rt​−μ)2, each with variance (Kurt−1)σ4 by (14.5). So Var(s2)≈(Kurt−1)σ4/T by (17.1), and h(v)=v​, with derivative 1/(2σ) at σ2, gives by (20.8)

SE(s)≈σ4TKurt−1​​,

which needs a finite fourth moment. For normal days Kurt=3, and this is Example 7's σ/2T​.

8The Hill Estimator of a Tail Index

estimate a tail index with the Hill estimator and its standard error, and explain how the threshold trades bias against variance

LM15 read TAIEX's tail from chords between loss levels, 2.09 to 5.03, and its Student-t fit implies a tail index of ν=3.065. A tail index can also be estimated from the largest losses alone.

Let X=−R be a day's loss and x0​>0 a loss level, the threshold. Model the losses beyond x0​ as an exact power tail, Pr(X>x∣X≥x0​)=(x/x0​)−α for x≥x0​. This is a Pareto distribution, with density αx0α​x−α−1 on x≥x0​. Its support does not depend on α, so (20.6) applies. For k iid losses x1​,…,xk​≥x0​,

ℓ(α)ℓ′(α)​=klnα+kαlnx0​−(α+1)i=1∑k​lnxi​,=αk​−i=1∑k​lnx0​xi​​.​

The root of ℓ′ is a maximum, since ℓ′′=−k/α2<0. One loss carries the information 1/α2.

ResultHill estimator

Let the losses at or beyond x0​ be iid with an exact power tail of index α, and let k of them be observed. The MLE of α and its standard error are

α^=∑i=1k​ln(xi​/x0​)k​,SE(α^)≈k​α^​.(20.10)

Hill (1975) based tail inference on the conditional likelihood given the largest order statistics; here x0​ is a fixed loss level, as in LM15's counts.

Example 10TAIEX's tail index

Estimate the tail index of TR23's daily losses at x0​=3%, and then at 2%, 4% and 5%.

Solution

At 3% the guard R <= -0.03 + 1e-12 finds k=108 losses, LM13's count. By (20.10), α^=3.63 with standard error 3.63/108​=0.35, so the interval is 2.9 to 4.3. At 2%, 4% and 5% the estimates are 2.49 (0.16), 4.93 (0.74) and 4.76 (1.23), from k = 252, 44 and 15 (Exhibit 9). The estimate rises from 2% to 4%, as LM15's chords did, then wanders once k falls below 45. The Student-t's 3.065 sits near the low thresholds.

PitfallThe threshold trades bias against variance

A low threshold brings many losses and a small standard error, but losses near 2% belong to the body of the distribution, not to a power tail. A high one fits the tail model better but leaves few losses. Big losses also come in runs (LM19), so the iid α^/k​ is likely too small. Value at risk from a tail model is LM32's; extreme value theory beyond Hill's estimator is not in this book.

導讀希爾估計量：尾部指數

尾部指數（tail index）描述極端損失出現的頻率隨幅度遞減的速度，數值越小尾巴越厚。若門檻（threshold）x0​ 以上的損失服從帕累托分布（Pareto distribution），最大概度估計（maximum likelihood estimation）就是希爾估計量（Hill estimator）k/∑ln(xi​/x0​)，標準誤（standard error）約為 α^/k​。加權股價報酬指數在 3% 門檻有 108 個損失日，估計值（estimate）3.63，標準誤 0.35；門檻 2% 時是 2.49，4% 時升到 4.93。門檻低則樣本多、標準誤小，卻混入不屬於尾部（tails）的損失而產生偏誤（bias）；門檻高則偏誤小、變異數（variance）大。大跌日又常成群出現，所以獨立（independent）假設下的標準誤可能偏小。

9Estimation in Python

compute estimates, standard errors and intervals in Python with explicit divisors and seeded simulations

Every estimate here takes a few library calls; the divisor, the tolerance and the seed must be explicit.

Python
import numpy as np
import polars as pl
from scipy import optimize, stats

tr = pl.read_csv("data/taiex_tr.csv")["price"].to_numpy()
R = tr[1:] / tr[:-1] - 1          # T = 5,849 simple returns
T = len(R)
s, s_u = R.std(ddof=0), R.std(ddof=1)   # 1.208364%, 1.208468%
se_mean = s / np.sqrt(T)                # 0.01580% a day
q = stats.t.ppf(0.975, T - 1)           # 1.96037

x = 100 * R                       # percent, for the optimizer


def nll(p):                       # minus the Student-t log-likelihood
    return -stats.t.logpdf(x, *p).sum()


fit = optimize.minimize(nll, stats.t.fit(x), method="Nelder-Mead",
                        options={"xatol": 1e-12, "fatol": 1e-12})


def hessian(f, p, h):             # central differences
    k = len(p)
    H = np.zeros((k, k))
    for i in range(k):
        for j in range(k):
            a, b = np.eye(k)[i] * h[i], np.eye(k)[j] * h[j]
            H[i, j] = (f(p + a + b) - f(p + a - b) - f(p - a + b)
                       + f(p - a - b)) / (4 * h[i] * h[j])
    return H


for h in ([1e-4, 1e-5, 1e-5], [1e-3, 1e-4, 1e-4]):   # two step sizes
    cov = np.linalg.inv(hessian(nll, fit.x, h))
    print(np.sqrt(np.diag(cov)))  # 0.144, 0.0124, 0.0138 both times

rng = np.random.default_rng([1, 20261011])   # experiment 1, once
sims = R.mean() + s * rng.standard_normal((10_000, 246))
vol = np.sqrt(T / 23.7645) * sims.std(axis=1, ddof=0)
print(np.quantile(vol, [0.025, 0.975], method="inverted_cdf"))

NumPy's std divides by T by default and Polars' by T−1, so pass ddof every time. SciPy's Nelder–Mead stops at a tolerance of 10−4 unless told otherwise. The last lines repeat the normal block of Exhibit 2: 17.3% to 20.6%.

Glossary

Term 中文 Meaning
Estimator 估計量 A function of the sample that estimates a parameter; its value on the data is the estimate.
Sampling distribution 抽樣分布 The distribution of an estimator across samples.
Bias 偏誤 Eθ^−θ.
Unbiased estimator 不偏估計量 An estimator with bias 0.
Mean squared error 均方誤差 E[(θ^−θ)2], variance plus squared bias, (20.1).
Consistent estimator 一致估計量 An estimator that converges in probability to the parameter.
Standard error 標準誤 The standard deviation of an estimator, (20.2) for a mean.
Confidence interval 信賴區間 An interval rule that covers the parameter with a stated probability across samples, (20.4).
Likelihood function 概度函數 L(θ), the density of the observed sample as a function of the parameters.
Maximum likelihood estimation 最大概度估計 Choosing the parameters that maximize the likelihood, (20.5).
Fisher information 費雪訊息 I(θ), the expected curvature of one observation's log-likelihood.
Observed information matrix 觀察訊息矩陣 −H, the negative Hessian of ℓ at the estimate; its inverse estimates the covariance, (20.6).
Asymptotic normality 漸近常態性 The approximately normal law of an estimator in large samples, (20.6).
Profile likelihood 剖面概度 The likelihood of one parameter, maximized over the others at each of its values; its logarithm is the profile log-likelihood.
Cramér–Rao lower bound 克萊姆-饒歐下界 1/(TI), below which no unbiased estimator's variance falls, (20.7).
Efficiency 效率 Precision of an estimator relative to the Cramér–Rao bound or to another estimator.
Delta method delta 法 Standard errors of smooth functions by a first-order expansion, (20.8).
Pareto distribution 帕累托分布 The exact power tail Pr(X>x∣X≥x0​)=(x/x0​)−α.
Hill estimator 希爾估計量 The Pareto MLE of a tail index from the losses beyond a threshold, (20.10).
