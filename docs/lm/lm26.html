Learning Module Overview

1Introduction

LM13 to LM18 found that large moves come in clusters, and LM19 measured it: TR23's squared log returns correlate 0.194 with the previous day's. This module forecasts tomorrow's variance from that dependence, measures a day's variance from intraday index data, and judges the forecasts against that noisy measurement.

Motivating CaseThe day after the worst day

On Wednesday 2 April 2025, the last session before the Qingming holiday, a GARCH(1,1) model refitted on the previous five years forecast a daily standard deviation of 1.72%. That is 27.0% a year. An exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) said 1.56%. On Monday 7 April the TAIEX total-return index fell 9.70%, a log return of −10.20%: 5.9 of GARCH's standard deviations, and TR23's largest fall (LM15).

On the evening of 7 April the forecasts for 8 April diverged: GARCH 3.60%, EWMA 2.92%, and 7 April's own realized volatility, read as a forecast, 10.09%. The day's realized measure, Vt​​ from intraday TAIEX data, then put 8 April at 3.39% (Exhibit 1). Which forecast should a risk manager trust, how fast should it fall back, and how can a forecast be judged when a day's true variance is never observed?

Section 2 tests for clustering, Sections 3 to 5 build the forecasts, Section 6 measures a day's variance, and Section 7 judges them.

NoteNotation for this module

Daily data are TR23: T=5,849 log returns rt​=ln(1+Rt​) from 3 January 2003 to 8 October 2026, A=246.12 (LM17). Models use rt​ in percent, so variances are in %² a day. Intraday data are the TAIEX price index every 5 to 60 seconds, 5,351 sessions from 3 January 2005. Moments divide by T. Here α and β are GARCH coefficients, so a significance level is written "the 5% level".

Counts: t days, i intraday returns, j and k lags.

2Volatility Clustering and the ARCH Test

explain volatility clustering as dependence between the sizes of returns, and test for it with Engle's ARCH test

The evidence so far concerns the sizes of returns, not their signs. After a fall of 3% or more TR23 fell again 12.04% of the time, against 1.85% overall (LM13). Next-day log returns correlate only 0.0393, but their sizes correlate 0.161 (LM19). LM25's Ljung–Box statistic for ∣rt​∣, (25.5), is 1,820 on ten lags.

Volatility clustering is this dependence between sizes. A model for it describes the conditional variance σt2​, the variance of day t given the returns before it:

rt​=g+εt​,εt​=σt​ξt​.

Here σt​>0 depends only on earlier returns, and the ξt​ are iid with mean 0 and variance 1, each independent of the past. By the tower property (16.5), E[εt​εt−k​]=E[E[εt​∣past]εt−k​]=0 for k≥1. So the innovations are uncorrelated, yet εt2​ and εt−12​ correlate whenever σt​ grows with ∣εt−1​∣ and fourth moments are finite.

ResultThe ARCH test

Let ε^t​=rt​−rˉ. Regress ε^t2​ by OLS, (23.1), on a constant and ε^t−12​,…,ε^t−K2​, with coefficients a,b1​,…,bK​, over the T−K days that have K lags. Under H0​ the εt​ are iid with a finite fourth moment, and then, for large T, the statistic

(T−K)×R-squaredis approximately χK2​.​(26.1)
The test uses the upper tail. Stated without proof (Engle 1982).

Example 1The ARCH test on TR23

Test TR23's log returns, in percent, with K=1 and with K=5.

Solution

With K=1 the intercept is a=1.1779 and the slope b1​=0.196. The slope is close to LM19's ρ^​1​ of rt2​, 0.194, because regressing a stationary series on its lag gives about its lag-1 autocorrelation. R-squared is 0.03844, so the statistic is 5,848×0.03844=224.8, with a p-value of 8.2×10−51 from χ12​.

With K=5 the statistic is 696.3, far above the 5% point 11.07 of χ52​; its p-value is below 10−100. These χ2 p-values are nominal: (26.1) needs a finite fourth moment, which LM15's fit (ν=3.065) lacks (LM19). The five slopes sum to 0.529.

Experiment 3 shuffles TR23's days 20,000 times (default_rng([3, 20261011])), as LM21's experiment 8 shuffled dates; no shuffle reaches 224.8 or 696.3. A constant variance is rejected beyond doubt.

PitfallThe ARCH test names no model

Any change in variance makes the test reject. After a calm decade and a turbulent one, squared residuals correlate with their lags, because a day and its lag usually share a decade. A rejection says only that the variance is not constant.

導讀大小會群聚，方向不會

報酬的正負號幾乎無法預測，報酬的大小卻會成群出現，這就是波動群聚現象（volatility clustering）。模型描述的是條件變異數（conditional variance）：在已知昨天以前所有報酬的條件下，今天報酬的變異數。新息（innovation）的條件期望值（conditional expectation）是零，所以互不相關（uncorrelated）；但只要今天的變異數取決於昨天的意外有多大，它們的平方就會相關。Engle 的自我迴歸條件變異數非齊一檢定（ARCH test）把殘差（residual）平方對自己前幾期的值（lagged values）做迴歸（regression），對立假設（alternative hypothesis）是自我迴歸條件變異數非齊一模型（ARCH model）；在虛無假設（null hypothesis）下，若四階動差（fourth moment）有限，統計量（statistic）近似卡方分布（chi-square distribution）。檢定拒絕只代表變異數不是常數，並沒有指出是哪一種模型。

Knowledge Check 1

The ARCH regression with K=1 has a=1.1779 and b1​=0.1960. What variance does it predict for tomorrow after a surprise of 3% today, and after one of 0.5%?

Answer

After 3%: 1.1779+0.1960×9=2.94 %², a standard deviation of 1.72%. After 0.5%: 1.1779+0.1960×0.25=1.23 %², or 1.11%. Today's size moves tomorrow's spread, as LM16 described.

3The Exponentially Weighted Moving Average

compute an EWMA variance by hand, with its weights, effective window and RiskMetrics' choice λ=0.94

The simplest variance forecast is the mean of the last 60 squared returns, in which a crash counts fully for 60 days and then drops out at once. An exponentially weighted average lets old days fade instead.

DefinitionEWMA variance

With a decay 0<λ<1 and returns of mean zero, the exponentially weighted moving average forecast of tomorrow's variance is

σt+1∣t2​​=λσt∣t−12​+(1−λ)rt2​=(1−λ)j≥0∑​λjrt−j2​.​(26.2)

The second form follows by substituting the recursion into itself (Problem 7). The weights (1−λ)λj sum to 1 and halve every ln21​/lnλ days. The effective window 1/(1−λ) is the number of equal weights that would give today the same weight.

RiskMetrics (1996, §5.3.2.2) fixed λ=0.94 for daily data. That is a weighted average, over 480 series, of the λ that minimizes each series' root mean squared error of variance forecasts, weighted by forecast accuracy. Its §5.3.1.2 counts the effective days as those that leave out a tolerance level of the weight: at 1%, ln0.01/ln0.94=74.4, which its Table 5.7 lists as 74 days. RiskMetrics sets the mean return to zero (its Table 5.9), as in (26.2).

Example 2EWMA across 7 April 2025

The EWMA forecast for 7 April was 2.4351 %², or 1.5605%. Update it with 7 April's log return, −10.2029%, and describe the weights at λ=0.94.

Solution

In %², rt2​=104.0999. By (26.2),

σt+1∣t2​​=0.94×2.4351+0.06×104.0999=2.2890+6.2460,​
so the forecast for 8 April is 8.5350​=2.9215%, nearly double.

The effective window is 16.7 days, the weights halve every 11.2 days, and the last 75 days carry 99.0% of the weight. The recursion started at 1.84 %², the mean of rt2​ over the first 246 returns. In the forecast for 2 January 2008, Section 7's first test day, that start carries a weight of 0.941,240=5×10−34.

Exhibit 2 shows the EWMA forecast by week beside Section 6's realized volatility: it rises within days of a crash and decays over weeks.

Example 3Choosing λ in hindsight

Section 7 scores forecasts by QLIKE, (26.8), against Vt​ on 4,609 days. Which λ would have scored best?

Solution

On a grid from 0.800 to 0.995 in steps of 0.005, QLIKE is 0.5153 at 0.94, 0.5436 at 0.97 and lowest, 0.5087, at 0.905. GARCH scores 0.5032. Choosing 0.905 uses the test days, so that choice is in-sample.

PitfallA decay chosen on the test days

The best λ of Example 3 beats 0.94 on the days it was chosen on, by construction. Only a value fixed in advance, as RiskMetrics' 0.94 was, gives an out-of-sample score.

導讀記憶逐漸淡去，而不是一刀切

60 天移動平均（moving average）把每一天都當成同等重要，滿 60 天就一次丟掉，所以預測值（forecast）會突然跳動。指數加權移動平均模型（EWMA）讓每一天的權重（weight）以 λ 的比例逐日遞減：昨天的平方報酬占 1−λ，越早的日子越不重要，但不會一下子歸零。RiskMetrics 對日資料用 λ=0.94，有效視窗（effective window）約 16.7 天，權重每 11.2 天減半。崩盤隔天，波動率（volatility）預測立刻上升，之後慢慢回落；回落到哪裡，取決於最近的平靜程度，因為 EWMA 沒有長期水準。

Knowledge Check 2

With λ=0.97, what is the effective window, and in how many days do the weights halve?

Answer

The effective window is 1/0.03=33.3 days, and the weights halve in ln21​/ln0.97=22.76 days.

4GARCH(1,1): Model, Forecasts and Half-Life

state the GARCH(1,1) model with its conditions, and derive its unconditional variance, its multi-step forecasts and the half-life of a shock

EWMA has no long-run level, so its forecast for every later day equals tomorrow's (Deep Dive 1). GARCH, generalized ARCH (Bollerslev 1986), adds a constant that pulls the variance back toward a level.

DefinitionGARCH(1,1)

Let rt​=g+εt​ and εt​=σt​ξt​, with

σt2​=ω+αεt−12​+βσt−12​.(26.3)
The model's conditions are:

  1. the ξt​ are iid with mean 0 and variance 1, each independent of the returns before day t;
  2. a positive constant, ω>0;
  3. non-negative coefficients, α≥0 and β≥0;
  4. a persistence below 1, α+β<1.

Tomorrow's variance is a constant plus a share of today's squared surprise and a share of today's variance. The ξt​ may be normal, or Student t scaled to variance 1 by (15.11).

Given the returns up to day t, σt+12​ is known, so σt+1∣t2​=σt+12​. For j≥1, condition 1 and the tower property (16.5) give E[εt+j2​∣day t]=σt+j∣t2​. Taking that expectation of (26.3) for day t+j+1 gives

σt+j+1∣t2​=ω+(α+β)σt+j∣t2​.

A stationary model has a constant unconditional variance σ2=Eεt2​, and the same step on unconditional expectations gives σ2=ω+(α+β)σ2. Subtracting, each step multiplies the forecast's distance from σ2 by α+β.

ResultUnconditional variance, forecasts and half-life

Under the conditions of (26.3), with εt​ stationary,

σ2=1−α−βω​,σt+m∣t2​=σ2+(α+β)m−1(σt+1∣t2​−σ2),half-life=ln(α+β)ln21​​.​(26.4)
The half-life is (25.2)'s: the number of sessions in which a shock's effect on the forecast halves. That a stationary solution exists when α+β<1 is stated without proof (Tsay 2010, ch. 3).

Deep DiveDeep Dive 1: the GARCH variance unrolledoptional · click to expand

Here ηt​ is LM25's white noise. Apply (26.3) k times:

σt2​=ωj=0∑k−1​βj+αj=0∑k−1​βjεt−1−j2​+βkσt−k2​.
For β<1 and bounded Eσt−k2​ the last term vanishes as k→∞. So σt2​=ω/(1−β)+α∑j≥0​βjεt−1−j2​: past surprises weighted with decay β, plus a floor. EWMA is the case ω=0, α=1−λ, β=λ, which breaks conditions 2 and 4. With ω=0 and α+β=1 the forecast recursion gives σt+m∣t2​=σt+1∣t2​ for every m; with ω>0 it rises by ω a step.

Next, let ηt​:=εt2​−σt2​, which has mean 0 given the past and is white noise when Eεt4​<∞. Replacing σt2​ and σt−12​ in (26.3) by εt2​−ηt​ and εt−12​−ηt−1​ gives

εt2​=ω+(α+β)εt−12​+ηt​−βηt−1​,
an ARMA(1,1) in the squares. For k≥2, ηt​ and ηt−1​ are uncorrelated with εt−k2​. So from lag 2 on, each autocovariance of εt2​ is α+β times the one before, like (25.1)'s AR(1).

Example 4How fast the forecast falls back

Take the full-sample normal fit of Exhibit 3, with α+β=0.98443 and σ2=1.5971 %². On the evening of 7 April 2025 it forecast σt+1∣t​=3.517% (12.3695 %²). Trace the forecasts.

Solution

By (26.4) the forecasts fall to 3.42%, 3.10%, 2.66% and 1.35% at m = 5, 20, 44 and 250 sessions, toward σ=1.264% a day. The half-life is ln21​/ln0.98443=44.2 sessions. The average forecast variance over the next 20 days is a volatility of 51.8% a year, and over 250 days 32.6%.

On 8 October 2026, the last day, the forecast was 1.092% (17.13% a year), below 19.83%, so it rises toward that level. arch's forecast gives the same values to 10−12.

Starting from Example 4, the widget draws (26.4) for any α+β<1 at the fit's unconditional variance. LM31 scales a position by such a forecast, and LM32 turns it into a value-at-risk. In continuous time a variance that moves at random is LM39's stochastic volatility.

PitfallA​ times a one-day forecast is not a year's forecast

Annualizing tomorrow's forecast by A​ assumes that every later day has the same variance. On the evening of 7 April 2025 that gave 55.18% a year, while the average forecast over the next 250 sessions was 32.57%. Average the forecasts over the horizon instead, as the widget does.

導讀衝擊會衰退，回到長期水準

廣義自我迴歸條件變異數非齊一模型（GARCH）說：明天的變異數（variance）等於一個常數，加上今天意外的平方的一部分，再加上今天變異數的一部分。只要 α+β<1，就有一個長期的無條件變異數（unconditional variance），預測值（forecast）與它的差距每天乘上 α+β。差距減半所需的天數就是半衰期（half-life）。加權股價報酬指數的 α+β=0.98443，半衰期約 44 個交易日。把明天的預測乘上 A​ 當成年化波動率（annualized volatility），等於假設往後每天都一樣，在崩盤之後會高估很多。

Knowledge Check 3

What half-life does α+β=0.98 give? What does TR23's t fit, α+β=0.99098, give?

Answer

By (26.4), ln21​/ln0.98=34.3 sessions, and ln21​/ln0.99098=76.5 sessions.

5Fitting GARCH by Maximum Likelihood

fit GARCH(1,1) by maximum likelihood with normal and Student t innovations, compute the log-likelihood by hand, and check the fit with standardized residuals

The parameters θ=(g,ω,α,β)⊤ are fitted by maximum likelihood (LM20). Given a start value σ12​, the recursion (26.3) turns each candidate θ into a path σ12​,…,σT2​. For normal ξt​ the log-likelihood is LM20's normal one, with a variance that changes each day:

ℓ(θ)=−21​t=1∑T​[ln2π+lnσt2​+σt2​(rt​−g)2​].(26.5)

The arch package starts from its backcast, a mean of the first 75 squared residuals about the sample mean with weights proportional to 0.94j, and sets σ12​=ω+(α+β)×backcast. It maximizes (26.5) numerically. With Student t innovations the density becomes (15.9)'s, scaled by (15.11), and ν joins θ.

Classical standard errors invert −ℓ′′ as in LM20's (20.6). Robust ones use a sandwich that does not assume the density is right (arch's cov_type options "classic" and "robust", its default). Their validity for large T under the dependence of (26.3) is stated without proof; LM20 gives the iid theory.

Example 5GARCH on TR23

Fit (26.3) to TR23's 5,849 log returns, in percent, with normal and with t innovations, and give the half-life with an interval.

Solution

Exhibit 3 lists both fits. For the normal fit, the NumPy likelihood (26.5), started from arch's backcast of 2.78 %², equals arch's ℓ=−8,647.25 to 10−6. Its α+β=0.98443 gives σ2=1.597 %², 19.83% a year (robust delta-method standard error 2.3 points) against the sample's 18.99% (LM19).

The half-life is 44.2 sessions. The delta method (20.8) with robust errors gives it a standard error of 16.1, so a 95% interval runs from 13 to 76 sessions. Mapping instead the robust interval for α+β, 0.98443±1.96×0.0056 (0.97342 to 0.99544 unrounded), gives 26 to 152 sessions: near 1 the half-life is poorly determined.

With t innovations, ν^=5.54, ℓ rises by 183.35, and the half-life is 76.5 sessions; mapped from α+β (0.98343 to 0.99853), its interval runs from 41 to 471 sessions.

Example 6Standardized residuals

Check the normal fit with its standardized residuals ξ^​t​=(rt​−g^​)/σ^t​.

Solution

A right model leaves ξ^​t​ close to iid normal. The ξ^​t​ have excess kurtosis 2.57 against rt​'s 5.428 (LM17), and skewness −0.59 against rt​'s −0.47 (computed here), both by (19.3).

The Ljung–Box statistic (25.5) of ∣ξ^​t​∣ on ten lags is 27.6 (p-value 0.002), against 1,820 for ∣rt​∣. The ARCH test with K=5 on ξ^​t​ gives 14.3 (p-value 0.014). Both p-values are approximate, since the tests take residuals of an estimated model as data. GARCH removes most of the dependence between sizes, not all, and leaves fat tails.

LM5's lab asked, for 0050, whether changing volatility explains the tail beyond −4.107 standard deviations. On TR23, at or below −4.107 lie 17 days standardized by s and 9 standardized by GARCH, against 0.117 under the normal model. Changing volatility explains part of the tail, not all.

​Eσt4​(1−(α+β)2−(Eξt4​−1)α2)=σ4(1−(α+β)2).​
Since Eεt4​=Eξt4​Eσt4​, when the bracket on the left is positive,
Kurt(εt​)=1−(α+β)2−(Eξt4​−1)α2Eξt4​(1−(α+β)2)​.
For normal ξt​, Eξt4​=3 by (14.6). At α^=0.091254 and α^+β^​=0.984432 the denominator is 0.014239, so the kurtosis is 6.51, an excess of 3.51. For the t fit, Eξt4​=3+6/(ν−4)=6.89 by (15.10) at ν^=5.544, and (α+β)2+(Eξt4​−1)α2=1.008>1: that model has no finite fourth moment.

Example 7What GARCH explains

How much of the clustering that LM13, LM15 and LM16 found does the normal fit reproduce?

Solution

After the 108 falls of 3% or more, 13 next days fell too (LM13; LM21 tests it). GARCH, with the residuals' own distribution for ξt​, expects 7.8, against 2.0 for independent days. After the 67 rises above 3% it expects 5.4, where 3 fell. The model raises the risk after any large move; the data raise it more after falls. GARCH(1,1) cannot express that asymmetry, which Hansen and Lunde (2005) call a "leverage effect"; the lab fits it.

After the 489 days with ∣Rt​∣≥2% the next day's size averaged 1.27%, against 0.80% after other days, LM16's pattern on TR23. GARCH predicts 1.35% and 0.79%.

Experiment 1 draws 1,000 paths of 5,849 days from the normal fit, with normal ξt​ and σt2​ started at the unconditional variance (default_rng([1, 20261011])). On TR23's 286 months, the variance-to-mean ratio of monthly counts of ∣R∣≥3% has median 3.15 (batch standard error 0.035, LM18), with 5% to 95% of paths from 2.04 to 5.14. TR23 has 3.02 (LM15), and resampled days about 1 (LM18's 0.598 over LM15's mean, 0.612).

Experiment 1's paths have a median excess kurtosis of only 1.63, and 5% reach TR23's 5.428 (standard errors 0.045 and 0.7 points). Experiment 2 (default_rng([2, 20261011])) repeats it from the t fit: median 7.0 (standard error 0.11).

PitfallA robust standard error does not repair a wrong model

The sandwich keeps the standard errors honest when ξt​ is not normal, but the estimates still belong to the fitted model. If the variance reacts more to falls than to rises, both kinds of error describe the wrong parameters.

導讀用最大概度估計配適 GARCH

給定起始值之後，GARCH 的遞迴（recursion）把每一組參數（parameter）變成一條條件變異數（conditional variance）的路徑，概度函數（likelihood function）就是每天一個常態密度（normal density）的乘積，只是每天的變異數不同。最大概度估計（maximum likelihood estimation）找出讓這個乘積最大的參數。標準誤（standard error）有兩種：古典標準誤（classical standard error）假設模型的分布（distribution）正確；穩健標準誤（robust standard error）用夾層估計量（sandwich estimator），不需要這個假設。檢查配適（fit）時看標準化殘差（standardized residual）：加權股價報酬指數的標準化殘差還有超額峰態（excess kurtosis）2.57，GARCH 解釋了部分厚尾（heavy tail），但不是全部。

Knowledge Check 4

Why is the robust standard error of α^ 1.80 times the classical one?

Answer

The classical error assumes that the normal density is right. The sandwich does not, and the standardized residuals of Example 6 have excess kurtosis 2.57, not 0.

6Realized Variance and the Signature Plot

compute realized variance from intraday data, read a volatility signature plot, and state the hypotheses (microstructure noise, stale prices) that could explain how it moves with the sampling interval

A day's variance is never observed, but intraday prices measure it. Take a grid of interval h from 09:30:00 to 13:30:00, with xi​ the log index at point i. The realized variance of day t is LM3's RV on these intraday log returns. A return over Jh sums J returns over h, so for returns with mean zero and autocorrelations ρk​ that hold all day, (19.7) gives, ignoring the day's edges,

RVt​(h)=i=1∑n​(xi​−xi−1​)2,n=h14,400 s​,ERV(h)ERV(Jh)​=1+2k=1∑J−1​(1−Jk​)ρk​.​(26.6)

For J=2 the ratio is 1+ρ1​.

The grid does not start at 09:00:00 because FinMind prints the previous close there, and the index needs about a minute to absorb the opening gap. It starts at 09:30:00 because the 240 minutes from there to 13:30:00 divide evenly into every interval used here, 5 seconds to 30 minutes.

The opening return ro,t​ runs from the previous close to 09:30:00, and the daily target Vt​:=ro,t2​+RVt​(15 min), in %², splits the day into 17 squared pieces. A day has 2,880 five-second returns, or 16 fifteen-minute ones. Dividends change TR23's squared returns by less than 0.01% in total on these days.

Without noise, a finer grid only helps: if day t's n returns were iid normal with variance σt2​/n each, RVt​(h) would have mean σt2​ and variance 2σt4​/n by (14.6). Exhibit 4 plots the volatility signature, a period's mean RV(h) over its mean RV(5 min). On the 5-second days of 2015–2026, RV(5 s) averaged 11.7 times RV(5 min), and in 2025, 24.3 times. In 2005–2010 RV(1 min) averaged only 0.483 of it.

Example 8The variance ratio read backwards

Each day's own ρ^​1​ ran from −0.52 to −0.19, the middle half from −0.41 to −0.33. The ratio RV(5 s)/RV(5 min) was 25.2 on the median day and up to 62.6.

Exhibit 5 shows the change by year. By (26.6) with J=5, a top bar below 1 says that 1-minute returns within five minutes correlate positively on balance. The bars run from 0.397 in 2008 to 2.968 in 2025, below 1 in 2005–2012 and 2020 only. The bottom bars approximate ρ^​1​ of 5-minute returns (J=2): from 0.256 in 2007 to −0.257 in 2025, below −0.1 only in 2024 (−0.177) and 2025.

One model of the reversal separates the observed price from an efficient one.

ResultA noise model (a hypothesis)

Let xi​=xie​+ui​, where the efficient returns xie​−xi−1e​ are iid with variance σe2​, the noise ui​ is iid with variance σu2​, and the two are independent. Then the log index is LM25's persistent part plus noise, (25.9), with a random walk as the persistent part, and its returns form an MA(1), (25.3). Their lag-1 autocorrelation and mean realized variance are

ρ1​=−σe2​+2σu2​σu2​​∈[−21​,0],ERV(h)=nσe2​+2nσu2​.​(26.7)
Here nσe2​ is the window's efficient variance, the same for every h, while the noise term grows with n.

Deep DiveDeep Dive 3: noise and the variance ratiooptional · click to expand

Each observed return is (xie​−xi−1e​)+ui​−ui−1​, with variance σe2​+2σu2​. Adjacent returns share ui​ with opposite signs, so their covariance is −σu2​; returns further apart share nothing. That gives ρ1​ of (26.7), the MA(1) shape of (25.3), and ERV(h) as a sum of n variances. The noise share of a return's variance is 2σu2​/(σe2​+2σu2​)=2∣ρ1​∣.

If (26.7) held, the mean RV would rise by the same 2σu2​ for every extra return a day. On the 5-second days, between neighbouring intervals from 5 seconds to 5 minutes, the rise per extra return runs from 3.1×10−3 to 4.9×10−3 of RV(5 min). Each has a Newey–West standard error across days of about a tenth of itself. Day by day the two extremes move together (correlation 0.93), and the Newey–West t-stat of their per-day difference is 10.6.

The Newey–West errors need a finite fourth moment of returns (LM19), so they are nominal. Yet the largest rise exceeds the smallest on 88% of the days.

So (26.7), with iid efficient returns and iid noise, does not fit. In the 30 Dow Jones stocks, Hansen and Lunde (2006) find noise that is serially dependent and correlated with the efficient returns.

7Judging Forecasts Out of Sample

evaluate variance forecasts out of sample with MSE and QLIKE against a noisy realized-variance target, and with Mincer–Zarnowitz regressions

Six forecasts are fixed in advance and made each evening for the next session. GARCH with normal and with t innovations is refitted at each month's first session on the previous 1,231 returns, five years by LM17's rule (226 refits). The others are EWMA, the 60-day mean of squared returns, yesterday's V, and an EWMA of V, both EWMAs with λ=0.94. Forecasts run from 2 January 2008 to 8 October 2026: 4,609 days.

DefinitionLosses for a variance forecast

For a target Vt​>0 and a forecast σt∣t−12​>0, the losses of day t are

squared error=(Vt​−σt∣t−12​)2,QLIKE=σt∣t−12​Vt​​−lnσt∣t−12​Vt​​−1 ≥0.​(26.8)
A forecast's MSE is the mean of its squared errors over the test days, in %⁴, and its QLIKE score the mean of its daily QLIKE. QLIKE has no unit and is zero for a perfect forecast.

The target Vt​ is only a noisy measurement of the variance. MSE and QLIKE still rank forecasts as the true variance would if E[Vt​∣day t−1]=σt2​, a conditionally unbiased target. That result is stated without proof (Patton 2011; his 2006 working paper, Section 3). QLIKE depends only on the ratio of target to forecast, so a calm day counts as much as a crash.

DefinitionMincer–Zarnowitz regression

Regress the target on a constant and the forecast by OLS, (23.1):

E[Vt​∣day t−1]=a+bσt∣t−12​.(26.9)
A conditionally unbiased forecast has a=0 and b=1. The target's errors grow with the forecast, so the standard errors are White's HC0, (23.6).

Example 10Six forecasts, 2008–2026

Rank the six forecasts against Vt​ (Exhibit 7), test the differences and check the best one for bias.

Solution

GARCH normal has the lowest QLIKE, 0.5032, and the lowest MSE, 10.37 %⁴. To test a difference, take each day's loss minus GARCH normal's. Its mean over its Newey–West standard error, (23.7) with 9 lags for 4,609 days, is a t-stat with a normal reference. EWMA is worse by 0.0121 in QLIKE (t-stat 1.83, p-value 0.067) and by 0.428 in MSE (t-stat 2.98). GARCH t differs by 1.40 and 0.62 standard errors; yesterday's V is worst (t-stat 11.96 in QLIKE).

For GARCH normal, (26.9) gives a=−0.015 (HC0 standard error 0.135) and b=0.980 (0.114), so the t-stat for b=1 is −0.18; R-squared is 0.172, and 0.119 against rt2​. Andersen and Bollerslev (1998) found 0.047 and 0.026 against squared returns for two exchange rates, and about half against 5-minute realized volatility. Our target lies between, because over the 4,609 days 68% of it is, on average, one squared opening return.

The target itself falls short of the close-to-close day: it averages 0.933 of rt2​ over the 5,351 days (Newey–West t-stat −3.26), 0.918 in 2025 and 1.011 in 2008. So the QLIKE order is also checked against rt2​, unbiased up to the tiny squared mean; there only the two EWMAs swap places.

The MSE t-stats, the HC0 errors of b and the t-stat −3.26 need a finite fourth moment of returns, which LM15's fit lacks (LM19); they are nominal.

Exhibit 8 splits QLIKE by year. GARCH beats EWMA in 12 of 19 years; in 2025 it scored 0.611 against 0.733, and in 2020 0.666 against 0.785. With windows of 1,000 and 2,461 returns instead of 1,231, GARCH normal again has the lowest QLIKE, 0.5029 and 0.5332, against EWMA's 0.5126 and 0.5421. Those forecasts run from 1 February 2007 and from 3 December 2012.

Exhibit 9 follows 2025, whose realized volatility was 23.33%, week by week. In the week of 7 April it was 100.16% a year; GARCH forecast 55.86% on average, EWMA 47.22% and yesterday's V 99.52%. Over the 20 sessions from 7 April it was 54.3%, against 19.8% in the 20 before; GARCH forecast 48.9%, EWMA 50.1%. Neither forecast wins every stretch.

Forecasting a whole covariance matrix is LM30's and LM31's subject.

PitfallA noisy, unbiased target lowers R-squared, not the ranking

GARCH's R-squared of 0.119 against rt2​ does not make it a poor forecast: a single squared return is a noisy target, and its noise caps R-squared. Given the past, rt2​ is unbiased up to the tiny squared mean, so a robust loss still ranks forecasts as the true variance would.

導讀用有雜訊的目標值評估預測

真正的每日變異數（variance）看不到，只能拿實現變異數（realized variance）這類有雜訊（noise）的量來比，所以損失函數（loss function）的選擇很重要。Patton 證明，只要目標值（target）條件不偏（conditionally unbiased），均方誤差（mean squared error，MSE）與 QLIKE 的排序會和用真實變異數排序相同。QLIKE 只看目標值與預測值（forecast）的比率，對預測偏低的懲罰比偏高重。在 4,609 天的樣本外預測（out-of-sample forecasting）中，GARCH 的 QLIKE 最低，但只比 EWMA 好 1.83 個標準誤（standard error）。條件不偏的目標值，其雜訊會壓低判定係數（R-squared），卻不會改變排序。

8Volatility Models in Python

run these tools in Python with arch and statsmodels on NumPy arrays, matching each package result to a calculation by hand
Python
import numpy as np
import polars as pl
from arch import arch_model
from statsmodels.stats.diagnostic import acorr_ljungbox, het_arch

tr = pl.read_csv("data/taiex_tr.csv")["price"].to_numpy()
y = 100 * np.log1p(tr[1:] / tr[:-1] - 1)      # log returns in percent
res = arch_model(y, mean="Constant", vol="GARCH", p=1, q=1).fit(
    disp="off", cov_type="classic")
g, omega, alpha, beta = res.params.to_numpy()  # a pandas Series in arch
fc = res.forecast(horizon=250, reindex=False).variance.to_numpy()[0]
xi = (y - g) / res.conditional_volatility      # an array for array input
arch5 = het_arch(y - y.mean(), nlags=5, result_object=True)
print(arch5.lm)                                # 696.3
print(acorr_ljungbox(np.abs(xi), lags=[10]).to_numpy())   # 27.6
rv = pl.read_csv("data/vol4_rv.csv", comment_prefix="#")
V = 1e4 * (rv["o2"] + rv["rv_15m"]).to_numpy()  # the target, %^2

Summary

  • Returns are nearly uncorrelated, their sizes are not; the ARCH test (26.1) detects that but names no model.
  • EWMA (26.2) weights squared returns by (1−λ)λj; RiskMetrics uses λ=0.94.
  • GARCH(1,1) (26.3) pulls forecasts toward σ2 with half-life ln21​/ln(α+β), (26.4): 44.2 sessions on TR23.
  • The likelihood (26.5) follows from the recursion; for ω, α and β of the normal fit, robust errors are 1.7 to 1.8 times the classical ones.
  • Standardized residuals keep fat tails; GARCH(1,1) misses the asymmetry after falls.
  • Realized variance (26.6) depends on the interval; under the noise model (26.7) fine returns reverse.
  • MSE and QLIKE (26.8) rank forecasts as the true variance would if the target is conditionally unbiased; GARCH leads, but not every year.

Unconditional variance 無條件變異數 σ2=ω/(1−α−β), the level GARCH forecasts return to, (26.4).
