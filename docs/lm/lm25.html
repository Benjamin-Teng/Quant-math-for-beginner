Time Series I: ARMA, Unit Roots and Cointegration

A price index wanders with no level to return to, while the futures–spot basis keeps coming back. Stationarity, ARMA models, unit-root tests and cointegration say which is which, and how fast.

Learning Module Overview
  • A random walk's variance grows like t, so a price level has no mean to return to; its changes can be stationary. TAIEX rose 10.9-fold in 2003–2026, and its log shows no evidence against a unit root (ADF −0.77, p-value 0.97).
  • An AR(1) has autocorrelations ϕ1k​ and a half-life ln21​/lnϕ1​, (25.1)–(25.2); an MA(1) remembers one day, (25.3). The TX–TAIEX log basis has an AR(1) coefficient of 0.7758, a half-life of 2.73 sessions.
  • Volatility clustering widens the band of a sample autocorrelation, (25.4). TAIEX's lag-1 value 0.0393 has a t-stat of 3.01 against the iid band and 1.92 against the robust one; Ljung–Box (25.5) and the variance-ratio test (25.6) need the same care.
  • Under a unit root without drift the Dickey–Fuller t-stat (25.7) is shifted left: its 5% point is −2.86, and a true random walk falls below −1.645 in 46.7% of simulated samples. The basis rejects a unit root (ADF −9.00).
  • Two independent random walks look related: their regression has a t-stat beyond ±1.96 in 96.6% of 1,000 simulated pairs. The log basis is a cointegrating combination with the known vector (1, −1).
  • The basis has slow parts: convergence at expiry, roll jumps (−144.7 bp at the June roll) and the dividend season, with June's mean −74.80 bp of which −67.43 bp is carry and dividends.
  • Read as a persistent AR(1) plus one-day noise, (25.9), the basis's ARMA(1,1) fit puts 64% of the variance of a day's change in noise; the persistent part halves in 7.04 sessions (SE 0.556).

1Introduction

A price index has no level it must return to, while a futures price is tied to its index by carry and dividends. This module turns that difference into models and tests, applied to the TX near-month future and the TAIEX.

Motivating CaseA basis that comes back

From 2 January 2003 to 8 October 2026 the TAIEX price index rose from 4,524.87 to 49,313.44, 10.9 times its start, and its log never settled around a level. The near-month TX future followed it. Their log basis, bt​=lnFt​−lnSt​, averaged −25.4 bp with a standard deviation of 58.2 bp (Exhibit 1). It reached −793 bp on 27 October 2008, when TX settled 7.0% lower and the index 4.6% lower, and +248 bp on 5 May 2009.

The future settled below the index on 4,051 of 5,850 days. A day's change in the basis had a standard deviation of 38.9 bp, yet the basis did not wander off. Next-day values correlate 0.776, so an AR(1) would halve a gap in about 2.7 sessions. LM6 split one season's basis into carry, dividends and a residual. Is the basis stationary while the index is not? How fast does it return, and how much of a day's move is noise?

Sections 2 to 6 build the tools, from stationarity to cointegration. Section 7 resolves the case, and Section 8 runs the tools in Python.

NoteNotation for this module

TX23 holds 5,850 dates. On each, Ft​ is the settlement of the TX near-month contract held from that close, and St​ the TAIEX price close. The contract rolls at the close of the day before its last trading day. The log basis bt​:=lnFt​−lnSt​ is in basis points (bp, 10−4); LM6's F−S was in points. A rolled change differences the stored series; a roll-free change uses one contract on both days.

D holds the 5,834 dates, to 14 September 2026, whose contract expires within the sample (the last, 202609, on 16 September). TR23's T=5,849 log returns rt​ serve the autocorrelation tests.

NoteResearch hygiene

Basis figures use TX23, or D where a caption says so. The residual of (6.25) uses dividends that went ex up to each contract's expiry: a look-ahead that suits a decomposition, never a trading rule.

Tests and fits are in-sample, the limit regimes a stability check, and the simulations and widget a model. The figures of the case, Example 1 and the expiry, season and roll figures are descriptive, without standard errors: they describe the sample, and neighbouring days correlate about 0.78. Nothing is tested on later data (LM34).

Tests counted (LM21's rule): 36 on TR23's log returns, one of them Problem 6's QLB​(5), with 1.8 false rejections expected at the 5% level. Two are on ∣rt​∣ and 24 on TX23. Every DF and ADF verdict lies far from its critical value.

Bonferroni's correction for 24 tests needs a KPSS statistic above 1.03, the 0.05/24 upper point of its limit law (Kwiatkowski et al. 1992). That law is the law of ∫01​Vt2​dt for a Brownian bridge Vt​ (LM36), the Cramér–von Mises limit law (Csörgő and Faraway 1996, p. 222), which SciPy evaluates.

The five KPSS statistics for the basis, 0.62 to 0.96, fall short, and so does α^F​'s t-stat, −2.9, against the two-sided normal point 3.08. The yearly means also show a moving mean (Example 6).

2Stationarity, White Noise and the Random Walk

define weak stationarity, white noise and a random walk, and explain why a price level is not stationary while its changes can be

A time series is a sequence x1​,…,xT​ observed at equal steps, here trading sessions, and treated as one draw of random variables xt​. Its autocovariance at lag k is Cov(xt​,xt−k​), (16.2).

DefinitionWeak stationarity

A series xt​ is weakly stationary when

  1. its mean Ext​ is the same for every t;
  2. its variance γ0​:=Varxt​<∞ is the same for every t;
  3. its autocovariance γk​:=Cov(xt​,xt−k​) depends only on the lag k.

The series' autocorrelations ρk​:=γk​/γ0​ then depend only on k as well.

DefinitionWhite noise

A series ηt​ is white noise with variance ση2​ when Eηt​=0, Varηt​=ση2​<∞ and Cov(ηt​,ηt−k​)=0 for every lag k≥1.

Independent draws with mean 0 and a common finite variance are white noise by (16.1). The converse fails, because white noise only needs zero correlation. TAIEX's daily log returns are close to uncorrelated, but their sizes are not: the lag-1 autocorrelation of ∣rt​∣ is 0.161 (LM19). Volatility that changes over time is modelled in LM26; white noise here only needs a constant variance.

A random walk adds white noise to its last value, xt​=xt−1​+ηt​; with a drift c it is xt​=c+xt−1​+ηt​. After t steps xt​−x0​=ct+η1​+⋯+ηt​, and the terms are uncorrelated, so (16.3) gives

Var(xt​−x0​)=tση2​.

This variance grows without bound, so a random walk is not stationary, while its changes Δxt​=c+ηt​ are. LM3 treated lnS as such a walk.

Example 1A level and a spread across two regimes

TWSE's daily price limit widened from 7% to 10% on 1 June 2015, which splits TX23 into 3,078 and 2,772 dates. Compare the mean of St​ and the mean and standard deviation of bt​ in the two regimes. Then give the standard deviation of a random walk after t sessions.

Solution

The index averaged 7,302 before the change and 16,042 after it: a level's mean depends on the window. The basis averaged −28.6 bp and −21.8 bp, with standard deviations of 65.7 and 48.2 bp, so the spread moved much less. A random walk's standard deviation after t sessions is ση​t​, so it has no fixed variance.

PitfallA sample mean of a level is not a mean to return to

The mean TAIEX close over 2003–2026 describes the path the index happened to take. A random walk has no mean to return to, so a level "below its long-run average" is no forecast. Only a stationary series has a mean that pulls it back.

導讀價格水準沒有回得去的平均，變動才可能有

時間序列（time series）是依時間排列的一串觀測值。平穩（weakly stationary）要求平均數（mean）與變異數（variance）固定，兩期之間的共變異數（covariance）只跟間隔有關。白噪音（white noise）只要求平均為零、變異數固定、互不相關（uncorrelated）；平均為零、變異數有限的獨立同分布（iid）序列一定是白噪音，反之則不然，例如報酬幾乎互不相關，大小卻會成群出現。隨機漫步（random walk）把白噪音一期一期加上去，t 期後的變異數是 tση2​，越走越散，所以價格指數沒有一個會拉它回去的平均。它的變動才是平穩的，這就是分析報酬而不分析價格水準的原因。

Knowledge Check 1

A random walk has a daily standard deviation of 1.2%. What is the standard deviation of its change over 25 and over 100 sessions? Compare xt​=0.9xt−1​+ηt​ with the same ση​, whose variance settles at ση2​/(1−0.92) (Section 3 derives it).

Answer

By ση​t​, 6.0% after 25 sessions and 12.0% after 100, growing without bound. The second series' standard deviation settles at 1.2%/0.19​=2.753%, however long the horizon.

3Autoregressive and Moving-Average Models

derive the autocorrelations and partial autocorrelations of AR(1) and MA(1) models, estimate an AR(1) by least squares and convert its coefficient into a half-life

The basis remembers yesterday: next-day values correlate 0.776. The simplest model with such memory regresses today's value on yesterday's.

ResultThe AR(1) model

Let ηt​ be white noise with variance ση2​, uncorrelated with xt−1​,xt−2​,…, and let

xt​=c+ϕ1​xt−1​+ηt​.(25.1)
If xt​ is weakly stationary, then ∣ϕ1​∣<1, Ext​=c/(1−ϕ1​), Varxt​=ση2​/(1−ϕ12​) and ρk​=ϕ1k​. At ϕ1​=1 the model is a random walk with drift c.

Take expectations of (25.1): Ext​=c+ϕ1​Ext​. Take variances: γ0​=ϕ12​γ0​+ση2​, because ηt​ is uncorrelated with xt−1​, and a positive γ0​ needs ϕ12​<1. Multiplying the centred equation by xt−k​ and taking expectations gives γk​=ϕ1​γk−1​ (Problem 7).

When the ηt​ are independent of the past, today's gap from the mean is expected to shrink by the factor ϕ1​ each session. For 0<ϕ1​<1 the gap halves after

half-life=lnϕ1​ln21​​ sessions.(25.2)

A moving-average model remembers shocks rather than values. The MA(1) is xt​=c+ηt​+θ1​ηt−1​, stationary for every θ1​. Its variance is (1+θ12​)ση2​ and Cov(xt​,xt−1​)=θ1​ση2​ by (16.3) and (16.4), while values two or more sessions apart share no η. So

ρ1​=1+θ12​θ1​​,ρk​=0(k≥2).(25.3)

The partial autocorrelation at lag k is the last coefficient of an OLS regression of xt​ on a constant and xt−1​,…,xt−k​. It measures the link with xt−k​ that the nearer lags leave unexplained.

An AR(1) has autocorrelations that decay and partial autocorrelations that stop after lag 1: given xt−1​, earlier lags add nothing, because ηt​ is uncorrelated with all of them. An MA(1) is the reverse: with ρ2​=0, the normal equations of the two-lag regression give a lag-2 coefficient of −ρ12​/(1−ρ12​)=0.

The ARMA(1,1) model combines the two, xt​=c+ϕ1​xt−1​+ηt​+θ1​ηt−1​ with ∣ϕ1​∣<1. Its autocorrelations fall by the factor ϕ1​ per lag from

ρ1​=1+2ϕ1​θ1​+θ12​(1+ϕ1​θ1​)(ϕ1​+θ1​)​

(stated without proof; Hamilton 1994, ch. 3). OLS and its classical standard error are LM23's, (23.1)–(23.3); the AR(1) estimate is (12.1) with a constant and one lagged value.

Example 2An AR(1) for the basis

Regress bt​ (bp) on a constant and bt−1​ by OLS over TX23's 5,849 rolled changes. Report c^, ϕ^​1​ with its classical standard error, sε​, the half-life and the implied mean.

Solution

OLS gives c^=−5.66 bp, ϕ^​1​=0.7758 (classical SE 0.00824) and sε​=36.7 bp. By (25.2) the half-life is ln21​/ln0.7758=2.73 sessions, and the implied mean is c^/(1−ϕ^​1​)=−25.25 bp. The classical standard error assumes white-noise errors, which the partial autocorrelation of 0.230 at lag 2 denies. So the half-life gets no interval here; Example 10 gives one. Roll-free changes give 0.7748 and 2.72 sessions.

Exhibit 2 compares the basis with two models. Its partial autocorrelations of 0.230 and 0.109 at lags 2 and 3 exclude a pure AR(1). Beyond lag 5 the sample autocorrelations decay more slowly than either model, because the mean itself moves (Section 7).

PitfallA half-life from a noisy series is too short

One-day noise on top of a persistent series lowers ρ^​1​, and with it the AR(1) half-life. The basis's AR(1) gives 2.7 sessions; Section 7 separates the noise and finds 7.0 sessions for the persistent part.

導讀自我迴歸與移動平均：記住昨天，或記住昨天的衝擊項

自我迴歸模型（autoregressive model, AR）把今天的值寫成昨天的值乘上 ϕ1​ 再加白噪音（white noise），所以自我相關函數（autocorrelation function, ACF）是 ϕ1k​，一期期衰減。偏離平均數（mean）的缺口每期縮成 ϕ1​ 倍，縮到一半所需的期數就是半衰期（half-life）ln21​/lnϕ1​。移動平均模型（moving average model, MA）記住的是昨天的衝擊項（shock），自我相關只到第一期就歸零。偏自我相關函數（partial autocorrelation function, PACF）扣掉較近各期的影響後再看第 k 期，AR(1) 的 PACF 在第一期之後歸零，MA(1) 則相反。自我迴歸移動平均模型（ARMA）結合兩者，第 7 節會用它把基差（basis）拆成持續的部分與一日雜訊（noise）。

Knowledge Check 2

An MA(1) has θ1​=−0.36. Find ρ1​ and ρ2​. What is the half-life of an AR(1) with ϕ1​=0.5?

Answer

By (25.3), ρ1​=−0.36/1.1296=−0.319 and ρ2​=0. By (25.2), ln21​/ln0.5=1 session.

4Testing for Autocorrelation

test a return series for autocorrelation with the iid and the robust bands, the Ljung–Box test and the variance-ratio test, and explain how volatility clustering changes the verdict

Is TAIEX's lag-1 autocorrelation of log returns, 0.0393 (LM19), evidence of memory? LM19's iid band ±1.96/T​, (19.6), rests on a normal approximation that LM19 states without proof, beyond LM17's (17.5). Tests and p-values are LM21's (21.2), and each p-value comes with the number of tests run on the same data.

The iid band assumes independent returns. Clustered returns pair large moves with large moves, so the products r~t​r~t−k​ vary more than under independence, and ρ^​k​ with them.

ResultA robust standard error of ρ^​k​

Let the returns rt​ have conditional mean Er given the past (a null hypothesis that implies no autocorrelation) and finite fourth moments, and let r~t​:=rt​−rˉ. Then, for large T,

SErob​(ρ^​k​)=∑t​r~t2​∑t>k​r~t2​r~t−k2​​​.(25.4)
For iid returns it is close to SEiid​=1/T​.

Formula (25.4) is HC0-type, the lag-k version of LM23's White errors (23.6); the Deep Dive derives it.

Example 3Is the lag-1 autocorrelation evidence?

TR23's log returns have ρ^​1​=0.0393 with T=5,849. Test ρ1​=0 at the 5% level (two-sided, normal reference) with SEiid​ and with (25.4), whose value at lag 1 is 0.0205.

Solution

The iid standard error is 1/5849​=0.013076, so the t-stat is 0.0393/0.013076=3.01, and the test rejects. The robust t-stat is 0.0393/0.0205=1.92, below 1.96: once clustering is allowed for, the lag-1 correlation is no evidence at the 5% level. Over lags 1 to 30, 9 values leave the iid band and 2 leave the robust one, against 0.05×30=1.5 expected under the null (Exhibit 3).

Formula (25.4) needs a finite fourth moment, which TR23 may lack (LM19), so its t-stats are approximate. Leaving the product of 9 and 10 April 2025 out of (25.4)'s sum lowers the standard error to 0.01954 and lifts the t-stat to 2.01.

The Ljung–Box test combines lags 1 to K in one statistic,

QLB​(K)=T(T+2)k=1∑K​T−kρ^​k2​​.(25.5)

For iid data with a finite variance and large T it is approximately χK2​ (stated without proof; Ljung and Box 1978), and large values reject.

LM19's identity column shows VR(m)−1 close to 2∑k<m​(1−k/m)ρ^​k​. If the ρ^​k​ are uncorrelated, that sum has the variance SErob2​ of (25.6), and the variance-ratio test is

t-stat=SEVR(m)−1​,SEiid2​=3mT2(2m−1)(m−1)​,SErob2​=k=1∑m−1​[2(1−mk​)]2SErob​(ρ^​k​)2.​(25.6)

The iid form puts 1/T for each SErob​(ρ^​k​)2. The test is two-sided with a normal reference, a large-sample approximation; its robust form also assumes that the Deep Dive's cross-lag terms vanish.

Example 4Ljung–Box and variance ratios for TAIEX

On TR23's log returns, compute QLB​(10) and the t-stats (25.6) at m = 2, 5, 10 and 20, each at the 5% level.

Solution

The statistic QLB​(10) is 37.1 against the χ102​ 5% point 18.31, a p-value of 5.4×10−5. Its χ2 law needs iid data, so clustering may cause the rejection (Problem 6).

LM19's variance ratios, with iid and robust t-stats, are 1.0392 (3.00, 1.91) at m=2, 1.0465 (1.62, 1.02) at 5, 1.0065 (0.15, 0.09) at 10 and 1.0359 (0.55, 0.37) at 20. Only the iid test at m=2 rejects. Keeping the cross-lag terms widens the robust standard error by up to 25% and changes no verdict (t-stats 0.90, 0.08 and 0.30 at m = 5, 10 and 20). For ∣rt​∣, QLB​(10) is 1,820: sizes cluster (LM26).

Deep DiveWhere the robust band comes fromoptional · click to expand

Here et​:=rt​−Er is a return's deviation from its true mean. Under the null each et​ has conditional mean 0 given the past. The product et​et−k​ then has conditional mean 0 given days up to t−1, which fix every earlier product, so the products are uncorrelated. By (16.3),

Var(t>k∑​et​et−k​)=t>k∑​E[et2​et−k2​].
Dividing by (∑t​et2​)2, then replacing Er by rˉ and the expectations by sample values, a large-T approximation, gives (25.4). Formula (25.4) is (23.6)'s White form for a regression of r~t​ on r~t−k​, evaluated under the null. For iid returns each expectation is (Varr)2, and (25.4) is close to 1/T​. For two lags j=k the argument leaves cross terms E[et2​et−j​et−k​], which the robust form of (25.6) sets to zero.

PitfallAn iid band on clustered data

The iid band is too narrow for clustered returns, and Ljung–Box's χK2​ law has the same flaw. Use (25.4).

導讀波動群聚讓獨立同分布區間太窄

自我相關（autocorrelation）的獨立同分布區間（iid band）±1.96/T​ 是在獨立（independent）報酬的虛無假設（null hypothesis）下算出來的。可是加權指數有波動群聚現象（volatility clustering）：大漲大跌成群出現，相乘項 r~t​r~t−k​ 的波動比獨立時大，樣本自我相關也跟著跳得更大。穩健標準誤（robust standard error）把這些相乘項的平方直接加總，落後一期的標準誤從 0.0131 變成 0.0205，t 統計量（t-statistic）從 3.01 降到 1.92，不再顯著（significant）。Ljung–Box 檢定（Ljung–Box test）與用 iid 標準誤的變異數比（variance ratio）檢定也假設獨立，同樣會誤判。報酬幾乎互不相關（uncorrelated），但大小彼此相關（correlated），這是 LM26 的主題。

Knowledge Check 3

LM19 found a lag-1 autocorrelation of 0.161 for ∣rt​∣, and (25.4) gives it a robust standard error of 0.0240. Is it significant at the 5% level?

Answer

Yes. The robust t-stat is 0.161/0.0240=6.7, and 0.161 lies far outside the iid band ±0.0256 too. Clustering is dependence of sizes, which LM26 models.

5Unit Roots and the Dickey–Fuller Test

explain why the Dickey–Fuller t-stat does not follow a t distribution, and apply the augmented test with MacKinnon p-values and the KPSS test of the reverse null

Is lnSt​ a random walk, or an AR(1) with ϕ1​ just below 1? Subtract xt−1​ from both sides of (25.1):

Δxt​=c+δxt−1​+ηt​,δ:=ϕ1​−1.(25.7)

A unit root, ϕ1​=1, is δ=0. The Dickey–Fuller test (Dickey and Fuller 1979) tests H0​:δ=0 against H1​:δ<0, one-sided. Its statistic, the DF t-stat, is δ^/SE(δ^) from OLS with the classical standard error, and small values reject. Under H0​ with no drift (c=0 too) it follows neither the normal nor the t law of (23.4): its limit is a functional of Brownian motion (LM36), simulated here.

With a drift, the statistic of a regression with a constant only is asymptotically normal instead (stated without proof; Hamilton 1994, §17.4, Case 3). With a trend in the regression, it follows the trend case's own Dickey–Fuller law, not the one simulated here, whatever the drift (Case 4, also stated without proof). So a drifting series is tested with a trend (below).

Example 5The Dickey–Fuller null by simulation

Experiment 1 (Model, seed [1, 20261011]) draws 10,000 random walks xt​=z1​+⋯+zt​ of 5,850 levels, with zt​ iid standard normal. Each walk gives a DF t-stat with a constant and no lags. Find the 1%, 5% and 10% points and the share below −1.645.

Solution

The points are −3.44, −2.86 (batch SE 0.014, LM18) and −2.58, and the mean is −1.54 (Exhibit 4). For 5,849 observations, statsmodels gives MacKinnon's (2010) points as −3.43, −2.86 and −2.57. A true unit root falls below the normal 5% point −1.645 in 46.7% of the draws (SE 0.5%), and below −1.96 in 30.7%.

When the changes are autocorrelated, the errors of (25.7) are not white noise. The augmented Dickey–Fuller (ADF) test adds J lagged changes, which approximate an ARMA by an autoregression (Said and Dickey 1984):

Δxt​=c+δxt−1​+j=1∑J​aj​Δxt−j​+ηt​.(25.8)

statsmodels' adfuller picks J by the Akaike information criterion (AIC), which trades fit against lags, from 0 to ⌈12(T/100)1/4⌉, 34 here. It reports MacKinnon's p-values (MacKinnon 1994, 2010). A drifting series also gets a trend term, regression="ct", because its alternative is stationarity around a trend.

The KPSS test (Kwiatkowski et al. 1992) reverses the null: H0​ is stationarity around a constant, and large values reject (one-sided). Its statistic grows with the squared partial sums of the demeaned series, which wander under a unit root. statsmodels interpolates its p-value in a table of four points (0.347, 0.463, 0.574 and 0.739 at 10%, 5%, 2.5% and 1%), so p-values stop at 0.01 and 0.10.

Example 6Unit-root tests on the index and on the basis

Test lnSt​ and bt​ on TX23 at the 5% level: the DF regression by hand, then ADF, then KPSS.

Solution

For lnSt​ the DF t-stat with a constant and no lags is 1.19, with ϕ^​1​=1.000392. The no-drift p-value, 0.996, does not apply to a drifting index, but a positive statistic rejects a unit root under neither law. ADF with a trend (32 lags by AIC) gives −0.77, p-value 0.97: no evidence against a unit root. KPSS gives 10.356, p-value below 0.01.

For bt​ the DF t-stat is −27.20, and ADF with a constant (28 lags) gives −9.00, p-value below 10−14. KPSS gives 0.664, between the 2.5% and 1% points, p-value 0.017. Both tests reject for the basis: it returns within days to a mean that moves across years, from −80 bp in 2008 to +34 bp in 2026 to date.

Deep DiveWhy the DF t-stat shifts leftoptional · click to expand

Under δ=0 and c=0 the regressor xt−1​ is a random walk, so E∑t​xt−12​ grows like T2, not like T. The estimate δ^ settles at the rate T, and the t-stat converges to a ratio of functionals of a standard Brownian motion Bt​ (LM36), not to N(0,1). With a constant the limit is negative on average: experiment 1's mean is −1.54. Stated without proof (Hamilton 1994, ch. 17; MacKinnon 1996 for the tables).

PitfallReading the DF t-stat against −1.645

Against −1.645, a test at the nominal 5% level rejects a true unit root in 46.7% of samples. For a series without drift, use MacKinnon's point, −2.86 with a constant and no trend, or the p-value of adfuller.

導讀單根檢定：別拿 −1.645 當標準

單根（unit root）就是 ϕ1​=1，序列成了隨機漫步（random walk）。單根檢定（unit root test）的虛無假設（null hypothesis）是 δ=0，對立假設（alternative hypothesis）是 δ<0，屬於單邊檢定（one-sided test）。問題在於虛無假設成立、序列又沒有漂移項（drift）時，水準的平方和以 T2 的速度成長，t 統計量（t-statistic）不再近似常態分布（normal distribution），而是整體往左移：模擬一萬條隨機漫步，有 46.7% 落在 −1.645 以下。所以要用 MacKinnon 的臨界值（critical value），5% 約為 −2.86。增廣 Dickey-Fuller 檢定（augmented Dickey–Fuller test）加入前幾期的變動（lagged changes），吸收殘差（residual）的自我相關（autocorrelation）；KPSS 檢定（KPSS test）則反過來，以平穩性（stationarity）為虛無假設。

Knowledge Check 4

Adding 28 lagged changes moves the basis's statistic from −27.20 to −9.00. Why does it move, and why does the verdict stay?

Answer

The lagged changes absorb the part of the reversion that the partial autocorrelations show at lags 2 and 3 (0.230 and 0.109), so δ^ alone carries less of it. Both statistics lie far below MacKinnon's 1% point, −3.43.

6Spurious Regression and Cointegration

explain why regressions between independent random walks look significant, and test cointegration with a known vector and with an estimated one

Regress one random walk on another, independent one: OLS finds a link almost every time.

Example 7Two unrelated random walks

Experiment 2 (Model, seed [2, 20261011]) draws 1,000 independent pairs of random walks of 5,850 sessions, xt​ then yt​. Regress yt​ on a constant and xt​ by OLS with the classical standard error. How often is ∣t-stat∣>1.96?

Solution

The first pair (Exhibit 5) gives a slope of 0.61, a t-stat of 23.5 and an R-squared of 0.086. Over 1,000 pairs, 96.6% (SE 0.6%) have ∣t-stat∣>1.96; the median ∣t-stat∣ is 36 (batch SE 0.9) and the median R-squared 0.18 (batch SE 0.007). On one year of 246 sessions (experiment 4) the share is still 81.7%. Regressing Δyt​ on Δxt​ for the first pair gives a t-stat of −0.98.

The classical standard error (23.2) assumes white-noise errors, but here the residual is itself close to a random walk. Such a fit is a spurious regression (Granger and Newbold 1974; Greene 2018, ch. 21). Differencing restores a valid test, but it discards the levels.

DefinitionCointegration

Two series xt​ and yt​, each with a unit root, are cointegrated when some combination yt​−βxt​ is stationary. The vector (1,−β) is their cointegrating vector.

The log basis is such a combination with the known vector (1,−1), so a unit-root test on bt​ is a cointegration test, and Example 6 rejects a unit root. Whether futures and spot prices are cointegrated depends on the time-series properties of the cost of carry (Brenner and Kroner 1995). With an estimated vector, the Engle–Granger test applies a unit-root test to the OLS residual, with its own critical values (Engle and Granger 1987; MacKinnon 2010).

In an error-correction model each series' change responds to yesterday's gap bt−1​ through an adjustment coefficient, αF​ for the future and αS​ for the index (Engle and Granger 1987).

Example 8Engle–Granger and error correction for TX and TAIEX

Regress lnFt​ on a constant and lnSt​ over TX23 and test the residual. Then regress the roll-free ΔlnFt​ and ΔlnSt​ on a constant and bt−1​, with White (HC0) standard errors.

Solution

OLS gives lnFt​=−0.0283+1.0028lnSt​+ε^t​. statsmodels' coint gives −9.42 against its 5% point −3.34 (one-sided), a p-value below 10−14: it rejects the null of no cointegration. The residual's lag-1 autocorrelation is 0.764, so the slope's classical SE of 0.000154 and its "t-stat" of 18 against β=1 mean nothing. Theory fixes the vector at (1,−1); β^​>1 fits a basis whose mean drifts up with the index level (Example 6).

The adjustment coefficients are α^F​=−0.1597 (SE 0.055) and α^S​=+0.0655 (SE 0.040). A high basis predicts a fall in F and, less clearly, a rise in S: the future does most of the correcting. Their difference, −0.2252, is the δ^ of the DF regression on roll-free changes.

With more than two series, Johansen's (1988, 1991) test counts the cointegrating vectors. Threshold models let the basis revert only outside a band (Dwyer, Locke and Yu 1996; Lin, Chen and Hwang 2003, for Taiwan). Pairs trading is not in this book.

PitfallTesting β^​=1 with the classical standard error

In a cointegrating regression the regressor has a unit root and the residual is autocorrelated, so OLS's classical standard error is meaningless. When theory fixes the vector, test bt​ for a unit root instead.

導讀虛假迴歸與共整合

兩條互相獨立（independent）的隨機漫步（random walk）做迴歸（regression），t 統計量（t-statistic）卻常常大得驚人：一千組模擬中有 96.6% 的 ∣t-stat∣ 超過 1.96，這就是虛假迴歸（spurious regression）。原因是殘差（residual）本身也像隨機漫步，古典標準誤（classical standard error）假設的白噪音（white noise）根本不成立。共整合（cointegration）是例外：兩條都有單根（unit root），但某個線性組合（linear combination）是平穩（stationary）的。期貨與現貨的對數基差（log basis）就是向量（vector）(1,−1) 的組合，所以對基差做單根檢定（unit root test），就是在檢定共整合。誤差修正模型（error correction model）描述兩者如何把昨天的缺口拉回來，在台指期上，主要是期貨在修正。

Knowledge Check 5

The first pair of Exhibit 5 gives a t-stat of 23.5 in levels and −0.98 in differences. Why not difference lnFt​ and lnSt​ as well?

Answer

Differencing removes the spurious fit, but on lnFt​ and lnSt​ it would also remove the long-run link that the error-correction term bt−1​ keeps.

7The TX Basis as a Time Series

decompose the TX–TAIEX log basis into convergence at expiry, roll jumps, the dividend season and one-day noise, and measure how fast it reverts

In logs, LM6's (6.22) reads bt​=(rf​−q)τ. Take the dividends that actually went ex, qreal​τ from the total-return index (LM6). Then the fair log basis is rf​τ−qreal​τ, and the basis minus it is the residual of (6.25) in log form. The basis has three slow parts and one fast one.

Convergence at expiry. Carry and dividends shrink with τ (Exhibit 6). Twenty sessions before the last trading day the basis averaged −37.95 bp (standard deviation 69.17) against a fair −14.78; two sessions before, −6.56 bp (35.13) against −1.84. The standard deviation falls only to about 35 bp, of which one-day noise (22.2 bp, Example 10) is a part.

Roll jumps. On a roll date the series switches to a contract with a longer life. The 285 roll jumps, rolled minus roll-free change, averaged −37.5 bp (standard deviation 58.7).

The dividend season. Exhibit 7 splits the calendar-month means. June's −74.80 bp is −67.43 fair plus −7.37 residual, and July's −70.17 is −65.88 plus −4.29. Over D the basis averaged −25.57 bp: carry +9.73, dividends −17.54 and residual −17.76.

Example 9Four versions of the basis

On D, test four series for a unit root and fit AR(1) and ARMA(1,1) models: bt​; bt​−rf​τ; the residual bt​−rf​τ+qreal​τ; and bt​ minus its calendar-month mean.

Solution

Every version rejects a unit root (DF −27.2 to −32.3, ADF −7.75 to −9.15) and fails KPSS at the 5% level (Exhibit 8). The AR(1) half-lives run from 1.92 to 2.72 sessions and the ARMA ones from 5.05 to 7.01. Removing slow parts shortens the half-lives by up to 29%, but every version still reverts within days.

Now suppose the basis is a persistent AR(1) plus one-day noise: bt​=xt​+ut​, with xt​=c+ϕ1​xt−1​+ζt​ and ζt​, ut​ independent white noise. The quasi-difference

bt​−ϕ1​bt−1​=c+ζt​+ut​−ϕ1​ut−1​

is correlated with its own lag only. An MA(1) ηt​+θ1​ηt−1​ has the same autocovariances when

σu2​=−ϕ1​θ1​ση2​​,σζ2​=(1+θ12​)ση2​−(1+ϕ12​)σu2​​(25.9)

(Problem 8). So bt​ has ARMA(1,1) autocorrelations with θ1​<0, and a fitted ARMA(1,1) splits it into the two parts. statsmodels fits it by maximum likelihood (LM20), with standard errors from the curvature −ℓ′′, (20.6). Their large-sample law for a stationary ARMA that is invertible (∣θ1​∣<1) is stated without proof.

Example 10A persistent basis plus one-day noise

Fit an ARMA(1,1) to bt​ (bp, TX23) at the likelihood maximum and read it with (25.9). Give the persistent part's half-life with a delta-method interval.

Solution

statsmodels' default fit stops 0.006 below the maximum log-likelihood, so the fit starts from method="innovations_mle", and a restart does not raise ℓ. At the maximum the mean is −25.5 bp (SE 3.2), ϕ^​1​=0.90620 (SE 0.0071), θ^1​=−0.3553 (SE 0.017) and σ^η2​=1,257.0 bp².

By (25.9), σ^u​=22.2 bp and σ^ζ​=22.8 bp, and the persistent part has standard deviation σ^ζ​/1−ϕ^​12​​=53.8 bp. The noise is 64% of the variance of a day's change and 14.5% of the level's. The model's daily-change standard deviation, 39.11 bp, matches the sample's 38.91.

The persistent part's half-life is ln21​/ln0.90620=7.04 sessions. The delta method (20.8) with SE(ϕ^​1​)=0.007055 gives a standard error of 0.556 and a 95% interval of 5.95 to 8.13 sessions.

The standard errors of Example 10 assume that the model is right, yet the basis's volatility changes across years (Example 1). The likelihood's sandwich, the analogue of (23.6) (stated without proof), gives SE(ϕ^​1​)=0.01462 and an interval of 4.78 to 9.30 sessions. LM26 meets it as the robust standard errors of a GARCH fit.

Stability check: before and from 1 June 2015, ϕ^​1​ is 0.90 and 0.91 and σ^u​ 25.74 and 17.45 bp (Problem 8); the DF t-stats are −20.20 and −17.88. In 2025 alone σ^u​=23.4 bp, about 56 index points at the year's mean close of 23,919.

Measured in points, the 242 rolled daily changes within 2025 had a standard deviation of 81.4 (divisor 242), and the roll-free ones 76.7. LM6's 83.4 (divisor 137) used same-contract pairs from April to October only, with another roll rule.

At the 5% level KPSS rejects every version, consistent with a mean that moves across years. A fixed rf​=2% also leaves 23 years of rate changes in the residual, which FinMind's data cannot measure. One candidate source of the noise is timing: TAIEX closes on a 13:25–13:30 call auction, while TX settles from the last minute before 13:45 (LM6). This book does not measure that gap. In continuous time a mean-reverting spread is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (LM39).

The widget simulates the model over 246 sessions, with σζ​=22.8 bp around −25.5 bp. Each path's DF t-stat is compared with MacKinnon's 5% point for 245 changes, −2.87. At the initial ϕ1​=0.91 the half-life is 7.35 sessions.

PitfallAnnualizing or ranking a one-day basis

In Example 10's model, 64% of the variance of a day's change is noise. Annualizing a one-day basis, or ranking days by it, magnifies that noise (LM6). Average over days, or model the persistent part.

導讀基差：慢的三塊加上快的雜訊

對數基差（log basis）裡有三個慢慢變的部分：到期前逐漸收斂、轉倉（roll）時跳到存續期較長的合約、每年夏天的除息旺季；6 月的平均 −74.80 bp 裡有 −67.43 bp 是持有成本（carry）與股利。剩下的是快的部分。把基差看成「持續的 AR(1)」加上「一日白噪音（white noise）」，ARMA(1,1) 的估計顯示一天變動的變異數（variance）有 64% 來自雜訊（noise），持續部分的半衰期（half-life）約 7 個交易日，而不是直接配 AR(1) 得到的 2.7 個交易日。基差幾天內就回到平均數（mean），所以單根檢定（unit root test）拒絕單根；但這個平均數會隨年份移動，所以 KPSS 也拒絕平穩（stationary）的虛無假設（null hypothesis）。13:30 收盤與 13:45 結算的時間差，可能是雜訊的來源之一。

Knowledge Check 6

Rolls are grouped by the calendar month of the roll date, so the June roll moves from the June into the July contract. It lowers the near-month basis by 144.7 bp on average, the July roll by 104.9 bp and the other rolls by 19.8 bp. Why?

Answer

The contract entered at the June roll lives through the heart of the ex-dividend season. Over 2003–2025, on average 45% of a year's dividends went ex in its life, and 39% in the July roll's contract. So its fair basis lies far below the expiring contract's, which is near zero. The other rolls add a month's carry, but the residual discount widens with time to expiry (Exhibit 6) and outweighs it.

from statsmodels.stats.diagnostic import acorr_ljungbox

rho = acf(r, nlags=30, fft=False)[1:]        # (19.5), divisor T

eg = coint(np.log(F), np.log(S), trend="c", return_results=True)

Summary

  • A random walk's variance grows like t, so a price level has no mean to return to; white noise needs zero correlation, not independence.
  • An AR(1) has ρk​=ϕ1k​ and half-life ln21​/lnϕ1​, (25.1)–(25.2); an MA(1) has ρk​=0 beyond lag 1, (25.3).
  • Clustering widens the band of ρ^​k​, (25.4); Ljung–Box (25.5) and the iid variance-ratio test (25.6) need iid data.
  • Under a unit root without drift, the DF t-stat (25.7) is shifted left: use MacKinnon's −2.86, not −1.645. ADF (25.8) adds lagged changes; KPSS reverses the null.
  • Independent random walks give spurious regressions; the log basis is a cointegrating combination.
  • As an AR(1) plus noise, (25.9), the basis puts 64% of the variance of a day's change in noise, and its persistent part halves in about 7 sessions.
  • statsmodels matches each hand calculation; an ARIMA fit's standard errors depend on its cov_type.

Glossary

Term 中文 Meaning
Time series 時間序列 Observations of one quantity at equal time steps.
Weak stationarity 平穩 Constant mean and variance, and autocovariances that depend only on the lag.
White noise 白噪音 Mean 0, constant variance, zero correlation between dates.
Random walk 隨機漫步 xt​=xt−1​+ηt​ (with drift, +c); its variance grows like t.
Autocorrelation function (ACF) 自我相關函數 ρk​ as a function of the lag k.
Partial autocorrelation function (PACF) 偏自我相關函數 The last coefficient of an OLS regression on lags 1 to k.
Autoregressive model, AR(1) 自我迴歸模型 xt​=c+ϕ1​xt−1​+ηt​, (25.1).
Moving-average model, MA(1) 移動平均模型 xt​=c+ηt​+θ1​ηt−1​, (25.3).
ARMA(1,1) 自我迴歸移動平均模型 An AR(1) with an MA(1) error.
Half-life 半衰期 ln21​/lnϕ1​ sessions, (25.2).
Robust standard error 穩健標準誤 (25.4): valid under clustering with finite fourth moments, for no autocorrelation.
Ljung–Box test Ljung–Box 檢定 (25.5): a joint test of lags 1 to K, approximately χK2​ for iid data.
Variance-ratio test 變異數比檢定 (25.6): VR(m)−1 over its standard error.
Unit root 單根 ϕ1​=1 in an AR(1), a random walk.
Augmented Dickey–Fuller test 增廣 Dickey-Fuller 檢定 (25.8): (25.7) with lagged changes; one-sided, MacKinnon's points.
KPSS test KPSS 檢定 A test of stationarity against a unit root; large values reject.
Spurious regression 虛假迴歸 A significant-looking regression between unrelated nonstationary series.
Cointegration 共整合 A stationary combination of series that each have a unit root.
Error-correction model 誤差修正模型 Changes that respond to yesterday's gap from the cointegrating relation.
