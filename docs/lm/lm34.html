Backtesting Without Fooling Yourself

A backtest is an experiment with hidden choices: fix them in advance, put the costs inside, use only what was known, and correct for the search.

1Introduction

A backtest is an experiment with hidden choices: dates, universe, costs, and the versions tried. LM27, LM28, LM29, LM30 and LM31 built eight rules, LM33 measures their Sharpe ratios, and this module tests the procedure behind them.

Motivating CaseThe grid's winner

From March 2019 to October 2026, the through case's eight rules grew between 8.70% (R3) and 14.49% (R2) a year after Taiwan's commission and tax. Now vary each rule's window (126, 252 or 504 joint dates) and how often it trades (every one, three or six month-ends). R1 and R2 use no window. That gives 60 configurations. The best, maximum Sharpe on a 252-date window traded every six month-ends, grew 18.62% a year (Exhibit 1).

The median configuration grew 10.96%, and maximum Sharpe as fixed in advance (R4) grew 10.51%. Would you trade the winner? Three questions come first. Did the test use only what was known at each decision? Did its universe include the funds that later vanished?

Of the six gold, oil and VIX futures funds trading in January 2017, two were delisted. Equal amounts in all six lost 27.7%, while the four survivors gained 5.4%. And could the search alone have produced 18.62%?

Sections 2–3 set the protocol and costs, 4–5 show two biases, and 6–8 correct and read the search.

2The Walk-Forward Protocol

run a walk-forward backtest fixed in advance, and report its net wealth, growth, turnover and costs

LM11 defined an out-of-sample test: fit on earlier data, and judge on later data that the fit never used. A walk-forward test repeats that step at every decision.

DefinitionWalk-forward protocol

A walk-forward test fixes in advance its decision dates, estimation windows, trade timing, costs, valuation and criteria. At each decision it estimates from data known by then and trades at the first close it can reach.

The through case's protocol is LM27's:

  1. Signal. Each month's last session, from the trailing 504 joint dates of the estimation frame. Its close is known only after 13:30, when matching ends on TWSE (Operating Rules, Art. 3) and on TPEx, 00679B's market (Business Rules, Art. 40; ETF rules, Art. 4 ¶2).
  2. Trade. At the close of the next session on which every fund trades. The first signal, 27 February 2019, traded on 4 March, and the last, 30 September 2026, on 1 October.
  3. Start and end. Every rule starts in cash, so its first purchase pays costs. Valuation on 8 October 2026 sells nothing, so no final sale is charged.

The window rolls: each signal uses only the latest 504 joint dates, where an expanding window keeps all earlier dates. Choosing leverage under parameter uncertainty is LM45's subject, and each rule's exposure is taken as given.

A model choice is tested the same way. LM26 scored six forecasts out of sample by QLIKE (26.8), but named the best on the same 4,609 days. A later test would score that choice on new days.

A "fixed" protocol changed after its result and rerun is a second trial on the same data: count it, as LM21 counts tests.

導讀回測要先把規則寫死

回測（backtest）是一場實驗，結果取決於許多事後看不見的選擇。滾動前進測試（walk-forward test）在每個決策日只用當時已知的資料：月底的收盤價要到 13:30 以後才知道，所以交易排在下一個每檔基金都有成交的交易日收盤。視窗、日期、成本與評比標準都在看到任何結果之前寫下，結果才算樣本外（out of sample）。看過結果再改規則重跑，等於在同一份資料上多試了一次。

Use LM27's arithmetic for single trades, then sum each rule's costs.

Solution

The first purchase from cash costs 0.001425/1.001425=0.1423% of wealth for R1–R7 (LM27), less for R8, whose first exposure is below 1. Switching a hypothetical NT$1,000,000 from 0050 into 00679B costs about NT$3,850, and back about NT$2,850, since only 0050's sale is taxed (LM27).

ResultGross against net wealth

Let the gross path trade to the net path's targets on the same dates, without commission and tax. Suppose that

  1. costs are scale-free, cost(ax)=acost(x) for a>0, as (27.2)'s are;
  2. cash interest and borrowing accrue in proportion to wealth, at rates set by each path's exposure.

Then

lnWendnet​Wendgross​​​=−trades∑​ln(1−Wprecost​)≥trades∑​Wprecost​.​(34.1)

Deep DiveDeep Dive: the log gap between gross and net wealthoptional · click to expand

After each trade both paths hold the same exposures, as shares of their own wealth, and the rest in cash. By hypothesis 2 their ratio then stays fixed, and both reach the next trade t with the same wtdrift​. Write Wt​=(1−xt​)Wtpre​ for the net path. Dividing (27.2) by Wtpre​ gives, for scale-free costs,

xt​=cost((1−xt​)ft​−wtdrift​),
so xt​=cost/Wtpre​ depends on ft​ and wtdrift​ alone, while the gross path keeps Wt​=Wtpre​. Each trade multiplies Wgross/Wnet by 1/(1−xt​), which gives the equality. The bound follows from ln(1−x)≤−x for 0≤x<1.

For R1, (34.1) gives a log gap of 0.0080768 against the cost sum 0.0080755, and on every rule it holds to below 2×10−14. Exhibit 3 splits gross growth into net growth and costs.

PitfallOne constant subtracted at the end

An average cost subtracted from final growth charges every rule alike, yet R4's costs are 4.22% of wealth and R2's 0.64%. Costs belong in (27.2), at each trade.

導讀成本要放進每一筆交易

交易成本（transaction cost）包括買賣兩邊各 0.1425% 的手續費（commission），以及賣出時的證券交易稅（securities transaction tax）：一般 ETF 與期貨 ETF 是 0.1%，債券 ETF 在 2026 年底前免徵。0.1425% 是參考費率，券商收得比它高時必須事先告知客戶，所以它不是上限。成本在每次交易時從財富扣除，週轉率（turnover）高的規則付得多，不能在最後一次扣掉一個平均值。每筆交易的成本除以交易前的財富，稱為成本比例（cost share）。若成本與交易金額成正比、利息與財富成正比，且扣除成本前後的兩條財富路徑交易到相同的目標，兩者財富的對數差距（log gap）就等於各次交易 −ln(1 − 成本比例) 的總和（式 (34.1)），比成本比例直接相加略大。

4Look-Ahead Bias

detect look-ahead, and measure it by rerunning with only what each decision knew
DefinitionLook-ahead bias

A backtest has look-ahead bias when a decision uses information not known when it acted, such as a later price, a revised series or a parameter fitted on later data. The bias is measured by rerunning the test with only what each decision knew.

Adjusted prices are a common case. A series adjusted for distributions and splits is built backward from its last day t′:

Pt∣t′adj​=Gt,t′​Pt′​​,Gt,t′​=(1+Rt+1​)(1+Rt+2​)⋯(1+Rt′​).​(34.2)

Each R is Volume I's total return: the close plus any distribution, over the previous close, restated per current share on a split day. So Pt∣t′adj​/Pt−1∣t′adj​=1+Rt​ for every build date t′: returns do not depend on t′, but levels do. A level is look-ahead for any decision before its build date.

Example 4One close, three build dates (descriptive)

0050 closed at 72.30 on 17 January 2017. What does that day read in adjusted series built later?

Solution

That close reads 65.12 in a series built on 2 January 2020 and 55.20 in one built on 31 December 2024. Built on 8 October 2026, after 20 distributions and the 4-for-1 split of 18 June 2025, it reads 13.25. February 2017's NT$1.70 yielded 2.32% of the previous close but 12.65% in 2026's series (Exhibit 4).

Move each of the 91 later trades to its signal day's own close, known only after 13:30. Keep the first purchase on 4 March 2019, so that both versions cover the same 7.5975 years.

PitfallA full-sample statistic inside a rule

A Kelly root or a volatility fitted on all the data and used at every date is look-ahead (Problem 9). So is a daily scale that uses the day's own return (Problem 8).

5Survivorship Bias

measure survivorship bias by fixing the universe from a dated list that includes delisted funds

A list of today's funds omits those that vanished. FinMind's TaiwanStockDelisting names the delisted ETFs, and price snapshots list the funds with a row on a date.

Example 6The funds of January 2017 (descriptive)

How many ETFs with a row on 17 January 2017 vanished, and what did the six futures funds earn?

Solution

Of 69 ETFs listed on TWSE or TPEx with a row that day, 10 had none on 8 October 2026, and each is in the delisting list. Equal amounts in the six futures funds grew to 0.7234, or −27.7% (log growth −3.33% a year), and in the four survivors to 1.0539, or +5.4% (+0.54% a year).

Over the 9.7221 years from that close the gap is 3.87 points a year. These means use the unrounded factors, while Exhibit 5's printed factors average 0.7233 and 1.0540.

The six are futures-trust ETFs on futures indices, all listed on TWSE. Three carry a daily multiple: 00672L +2× crude oil, 00673R −1× crude oil and 00674R −1× gold. A multiple holds for one day only, so under daily compounding their long-run returns depart from it (TWSE's ETF overview, §四). So the survivorship gap mixes product types. When far contracts cost more (正價差, contango), a roll may lose, in TWSE's word (可能).

E5 itself was chosen in 2026 from funds still listed (LM27): 00635U and 00642U are survivors, while the oil fund 00672L, delisted in 2020, never entered it.

The repair is a data policy set before any result: fix the universe from a dated list of the funds listed on TWSE or TPEx (Research hygiene). A delisted fund's last close is an approximation, since FinMind does not record its holders' payout. Funds that disappear tend to do so after poor performance (Elton, Gruber and Blake 1996, abstract).

導讀只看今天還在的基金會高估過去

前視偏誤（look-ahead bias）是決策用到當時還不知道的資訊，例如用日後的配息回頭調整過的價格，或用全部資料估計的參數。存活者偏誤（survivorship bias）是只從今天還在交易的基金挑選，看不到已經下市（delisting）的基金。2017 年 1 月的六檔黃金、石油與 VIX 期貨 ETF 有兩檔後來下市：等金額持有六檔虧損 27.7%，只看存活的四檔卻賺 5.4%。其中三檔是單日正向兩倍或反向一倍，長期報酬會偏離倍數；期貨 ETF 遇到正價差（contango）時，轉倉（roll）可能產生損失。

6Correcting a Search on the Same Data

test whether the best of many rules beats a benchmark, using White's reality check
choose among the reality check, Romano–Wolf, Hansen's test, PBO and deflation, and say when no rule is supported

LM21's Experiment 10 judged the best of 10,000 random rules on TR23, the TAIEX total-return index, by (21.9) for independent rules. White's (2000) reality check corrects this data snooping while keeping the rules' dependence.

His null is that no rule beats the benchmark: every true mean difference is at most 0. He resamples blocks of days with the stationary bootstrap (22.3), so the rules' correlations survive, and subtracts from each resampled mean the sample mean Rˉk​. The reality-check p-value is the share of the B resamples in which

kmax​T​(Rˉk,boot​−Rˉk​) ≥ kmax​T​Rˉk​.(34.3)

Dividing each rule's terms by its su,k​ gives the studentized form, which compares largest t-stats (Romano and Wolf 2005, §4.1; Hansen 2005).

ResultWhite's reality check (White 2000, Corollary 2.4; stated without proof)

Let the rules be fixed in advance, with no estimated parameters, and let their number stay fixed as T grows. Suppose that

  1. each difference from the benchmark has a finite moment of order 6+ε, for some ε>0;
  2. the differences form a strictly stationary series whose dependence, measured by α-mixing coefficients, falls with the lag at least as fast as a power whose exponent exceeds 3(6+ε)/ε;
  3. their long-run covariance matrix is positive definite (White notes that a singular one also works);
  4. the mean block length grows more slowly than T: ℓ→∞ and ℓ/T→0.

Then, as T→∞, the law of the resampled maximum in (34.3), given the data, approaches that of the observed maximum when every true mean difference is 0.

Deep DiveDeep Dive: why the resamples are recentredoptional · click to expand

A p-value must hold at every point of the null, and the hardest point is where every mean difference is 0. A resample's means scatter around the sample means Rˉk​, so subtracting them makes the resampled maxima behave as at that point. White's Corollary 2.4 gives their law for large T.

Large T here means 5,849 days for Experiment 10, whose 10,000 rules outnumber them, outside White's fixed number of rules. For the grid's 59 differences it means 1,851, closer to his setting in the number of rules. The grid's estimates are fixed functions of past data, so this book reads its rules as fixed in advance.

White's moment condition, hypothesis 1, may fail on both searches. LM20's Student-t fit to TR23 has 3.065 degrees of freedom, so its moments exist only below order 3.065. Experiment 10's returns ±Rt​ have the same absolute values as TR23's. E5's rules may lack even a fourth moment (LM33), so the corollary does not cover either reality-check p-value, and each is a practical guide.

Example 7Experiment 10, corrected (model)

The best of Experiment 10's 10,000 rules has a t-stat of 3.9738 (LM21 prints 3.97). Correct it for the search.

Solution

The best rule's one-sided p-value (21.2) is 3.54×10−5. Bonferroni's bound (21.6) is 10,000×3.54×10−5=0.354, and (21.9) gives 1−(1−3.54×10−5)10,000=0.298.

With ℓ=145 by LM22's rule and B=10,000, the reality check gives 0.283, and its studentized form 0.281, both with MC SE 0.0045 by (18.2) (Exhibit 6). The bootstrap's 95% point for the largest t-stat is 4.77, which no t-stat reaches. The best of 10,000 random rules is what chance gives.

Romano and Wolf (2005) ask which rules beat the benchmark. Their stepdown (Algorithm 3.1; studentized, Algorithm 4.1) rejects each rule above the bootstrap 95% point of the largest centred statistic among the rules left, and repeats. The studentized first step is Example 7's 95% point, with the sample's su​ (their footnote 22) and centred at the sample means (their Remark 3.4).

Hansen's (2005) test for superior predictive ability (SPA) floors the largest t-stat at 0. It recentres only the rules whose t-stat is at least −2lnlnT​, the version with a consistent p-value (his Corollary 2). On Experiment 10 that threshold is −2.079, so it recentres 9,814 rules and gives 0.277 (MC SE 0.0045). The code's iid su​ replaces his kernel estimate, and his strict count gives the same value.

Both tests rest on large-T bootstrap theory beyond this book and are stated without proof. Other data-snooping tests are not covered, and Harvey, Liu and Zhu's hurdle of 3 is LM21's. Sullivan, Timmermann and White (1999) applied the reality check to technical trading rules on 100 years of daily Dow Jones data (their abstract).

PitfallBest, not which

The reality check asks whether the best rule beats the benchmark. A large p-value does not show that the rules are equal, and a small one does not say which rules win: that is the stepdown's question.

導讀在同一份資料上挑出最好的

在同一份資料上試很多規則、只留下最好的一個，就是資料偵伺（data snooping）：試得愈多，最好的那個愈可能只是運氣。Bonferroni 校正（Bonferroni correction）控制族系誤差率（family-wise error rate），卻不利用規則之間的相關係數（correlation coefficient）。White 的 reality check 用平穩自助重抽法（stationary bootstrap）整段重抽日期，保留規則之間的相關係數，再從每個規則重抽的平均數（mean）扣掉它的樣本值，看最好的統計量（statistic）是否超出偶然能解釋的範圍。它只回答最好的規則是否勝過基準（benchmark），不回答哪些規則勝過基準。

DefinitionCombinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV)

Take n strategies' daily returns on the same T days (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu 2017, Algorithm 2.3, in this book's words).

  1. Cut the T days into S equal blocks, S even, dropping the earliest days that do not fill a block.
  2. For each choice of S/2 blocks, call those blocks, in time order, the in-sample set and the others the out-of-sample set.
  3. On the in-sample set find the best strategy k∗ by Sharpe ratio.
  4. On the out-of-sample set rank k∗ among the n, with j=1 the worst, and set ω=j/(n+1).

The probability of backtest overfitting (PBO) is the share of splits in which k∗ falls in the lower half out of sample:

PBO=(S/2S​)#{splits with logit≤0}​,logit=ln1−ωω​.​(34.4)

With S=16 there are (816​)=12,870 splits (the manuscript prints 12,780). Dropping the earliest days is this book's convention. Daily returns do not overlap, so cross-validation that removes overlaps is not needed.

Deep DiveDeep Dive: counting the splitsoptional · click to expand

A split chooses 8 of 16 blocks, and sorting the choices by whether block 1 is among them gives Pascal's rule,

(816​)​=(715​)+(815​)=6,435+6,435=12,870,​
the terms equal by symmetry. Each split's out-of-sample set is itself a choice of 8, so every split appears with its halves swapped. A strategy's out-of-sample sum is then its full-sample sum minus the in-sample one.

導讀樣本內最好的設定，到樣本外表現如何

過度配適（overfitting）是指樣本內（in-sample）挑出的最佳設定，大半只是配合了雜訊（noise）。CSCV 是一種交叉確認（cross-validation）：把日期切成 16 段，每次取 8 段當樣本內、其餘當樣本外（out of sample），共 12,870 種切法。每種切法找出樣本內最好的設定，看它在樣本外的秩（rank）；這裡的秩就是名次，1 是最差。名次換成對數勝算（logit）後小於或等於 0，就表示排在後半段，而 PBO 是這種切法的比例：毫無技巧的策略約為一半，本例的 60 個設定是 0.663。

9Backtesting in Python

run a walk-forward backtest fixed in advance, and report its net wealth, growth, turnover and costs
test whether the best of many rules beats a benchmark, using White's reality check
estimate the probability of backtest overfitting by CSCV on a declared grid, and read its logits

vol5_rules.wealth_path(fr, sigs, rule, cost_mult) applies (27.2) at every session, with cost_mult=0 for the gross path. The reality check uses LM22's stationary_idx, which draws in arch's StationaryBootstrap order (the lab checks it), and CSCV needs only block sums.

Python
import numpy as np
from itertools import combinations

def reality_check(d, ell, B, rng):    # d: T days x rules, minus benchmark
    T = len(d)
    dm, se = d.mean(0), d.std(0, ddof=1) / np.sqrt(T)
    mx = np.empty(B)
    for b in range(B):                 # stationary_idx: LM22's
        cnt = np.bincount(stationary_idx(rng, T, ell), minlength=T)
        mx[b] = ((cnt @ d / T - dm) / se).max()
    return (mx >= (dm / se).max() - 1e-12).mean()   # (34.3), studentized

def cscv(ret, S=16):                  # ret: T x n daily excess returns
    T, n = ret.shape
    blen = T // S                      # block length; the earliest rows go
    blk = ret[T - blen * S:].reshape(S, blen, n)
    s1, s2 = blk.sum(1), (blk**2).sum(1)            # S x n block sums
    cmb = list(combinations(range(S), S // 2))
    mask = np.zeros((len(cmb), S))
    for r, chosen in enumerate(cmb):
        mask[r, list(chosen)] = 1.0
    half = blen * (S // 2)             # days in each half
    i1, i2 = mask @ s1, mask @ s2      # in-sample sums, splits x n
    o1, o2 = s1.sum(0) - i1, s2.sum(0) - i2
    sr_i = i1 / half / np.sqrt(i2 / half - (i1 / half) ** 2)
    sr_o = o1 / half / np.sqrt(o2 / half - (o1 / half) ** 2)
    best = sr_i.argmax(1)              # k*, the first on a tie
    so = sr_o[np.arange(len(cmb)), best]
    j = 1 + (sr_o < so[:, None]).sum(1)             # rank, 1 = worst
    omega = j / (n + 1)
    return np.log(omega / (1 - omega))  # PBO = (logits <= 0).mean()

On the grid's 59 differences, ell=75, B=10000 and default_rng([4, 20261011]) give Example 8's 0.375.

Summary

  • A walk-forward test fixes its dates, windows, timing, costs and criteria in advance.
  • Costs belong in (27.2), and (34.1) ties the gross–net log gap to them.
  • Adjusted levels depend on their build date, (34.2), but returns do not.
  • A universe of survivors hides losses, 3.87 points a year among the futures funds of 2017.
  • The reality check (34.3) tests a search's best under dependence, and Romano and Wolf's stepdown names winners.
  • PBO (34.4) measures whether selection persists: 0.663 here, about one half without skill.
