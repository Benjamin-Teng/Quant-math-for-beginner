Portfolio Return and Risk

A book's weights drift between trades, every trade costs money, and its risk splits among its funds in one way that adds up.

Learning Module Overview
  • A book earns its funds' returns weighted at the previous close, and between trades its weights drift: an equal-weight E5 book bought on 4 March 2019 and held had 48.6% in 0050 by 8 October 2026.
  • Costs come out of wealth, so the post-trade wealth solves a self-financing equation, (27.2). A trade costs about 0.285% of its one-way turnover, plus the tax on taxed sales.
  • R1's largest monthly reset, on 1 April 2026, traded 8.06% of the book and cost 0.031% of it: NT$3,100 on NT$10 million.
  • Daily joint statistics of E5 use the 2,362 dates on which every fund has a one-session return; wealth uses all 2,368 sessions, including 0050's halt of June 2025.
  • The funds close at 13:30 Taipei time, after the previous New York session, so daily correlations can mislead. Of five pairs, one daily–monthly gap reaches two standard errors: 00679B–00642U's, −0.18 daily against −0.36 monthly.
  • Euler's identity splits a book's volatility into contributions that add up: equal weights put 45.9% of the risk in 00642U and 2.6% in 00679B.
  • A negative contribution is a hedge only at the margin; 00679B's was negative in 22 of R1's 92 trailing windows.
  • With market betas, 83.4% of an equal-weight U28 book's variance is systematic; most of the residual part comes from residual covariances.

1Introduction

LM7 priced two funds at fixed weights, and LM8, LM10, LM16 and LM23 gave a book's return, its variance and the market model. This module adds time and attribution. Weights drift between trades, trades cost money out of wealth, five funds need two sets of dates, and the risk splits among them in one meaningful way.

Motivating CaseWhich fund carries the risk?

A reader holds LM7's two funds, 0050 and 00679B, and adds three: 00646 (the S&P 500), 00635U (gold futures) and 00642U (crude-oil futures). She puts a fifth of her money in each. On the 2,362 joint dates from January 2017 to October 2026, their volatilities ran from 13.49% to 37.43% a year. Yet the mix, reset to a fifth each day, had a volatility of 11.49% on the same dates.

Which fund carried that risk? 00642U held a fifth of the money and 45.9% of the risk; 00679B held a fifth and 2.6%. Left alone, the weights drift. Bought on 4 March 2019 and held, 0050 grew to 48.6% of the book by 8 October 2026.

Resetting the weights each month costs money. The largest of 91 monthly resets, on 1 April 2026, traded 8.06% of the book and cost 0.031% of its value. How should the risk be split, and what does each trade cost?

Exhibit 1 lists the five funds and the risk shares of two mixes, R1 and R2 (Section 3).

Sections 2 to 4 cover returns, trades and E5's dates; Sections 5 to 8 measure and split the risk; Section 9 does it in Python.

2The Return of a Book

compute a book's return from the weights set at the previous close, and track how buy-and-hold weights drift

A book's return uses the weights held at the previous close, as an index's does (LM12). LM8's (8.3) and LM1's (1.13) are that sum for weights adding up to 1. A book may also hold cash, or borrow.

DefinitionA book's return and the drift of its weights

Let wt−1​ hold each fund's value over wealth after the close of date t−1, and let cash earn rf​ a year. The book's simple return on date t, and its weights after that day's returns and before any trade, are

Rp,t​=wt−1⊤​Rt​+(1−1⊤wt−1​)Aw​rf​​,wt,idrift​=1+Rp,t​wt−1,i​(1+Ri,t​)​.​(27.1)
A negative cash balance, 1⊤wt−1​>1, pays LM31's financing rate in place of rf​. The drift needs 1+Rp,t​>0; otherwise the book is ruined on date t.

Fund i's holding grows from Wt−1​wt−1,i​ to Wt−1​wt−1,i​(1+Ri,t​), and cash by the factor 1+rf​/Aw​. Their sum is the new wealth, Wt−1​(1+Rp,t​), and each holding over it is the drifted weight. For weights summing to 1 the cash term vanishes, and (27.1) is (8.3).

Between trades (27.1) applies daily, so a fund that beats the book gains weight. A book reset at every close is LM8's Xw, (8.12). A book that never trades keeps its holdings, (8.13), and drifts, as does a cap-weighted index between changes of its members or share counts (LM12). In LM9's buy-and-hold mix of two sectors, Semiconductor's weight drifted from 0.50 to 0.858.

Exhibit 2 shows the drift of an E5 book bought at a fifth each at the close of 4 March 2019. On 8 October 2026 it had 48.619%, 19.734%, 5.422%, 14.513% and 11.712% in the five funds. 0050's weight ran from 19.1% (15 August 2019) to 49.1% (1 July 2026). 00642U's fell from 21.3% (23 April 2019) to 7.0% (2 November 2020), and 00679B's peaked at 28.0% on 23 March 2020.

Example 1Six sessions across 0050's halt

0050 stopped trading after 10 June 2025 for its 4-for-1 split and resumed on 18 June. Over those six sessions the five funds returned +0.864%, −1.689%, −0.792%, +2.226% and +12.816%. What did a book earn that held a fifth of each from the close of 10 June?

Solution

By (27.1) applied once to the span, the book earns the funds' average, 2.685%: across funds, simple returns add (LM3). The average log return, 0.02524, gives e0.02524−1=2.56%, too low, as (1.14) says of a long-only book.

Across time, log returns add. R1 itself, with its weights drifted since its trade of 2 June, earned 2.73%. Its log return is the sum of its six daily log returns, (1.12).

The drift needs 1+Rp,t​>0, the domain of ln(1+Rp,t​). R1 and R2, long-only and fully invested, never leave it, since every fund's return exceeds −100%; LM35 shows a book that can.

PitfallAveraging log returns across funds

For a long-only book the average of its funds' log returns understates its log return, (1.14). Average simple returns across funds and add log returns across days.

導讀權重會漂移，報酬要分兩個方向加

帳戶當天的報酬，是前一天收盤時的權重（weights）乘上各基金當天的報酬再加總。不交易的時候，漲得比整個帳戶多的基金權重變大，落後的變小，這就是權重漂移（drift）。交易回到目標權重叫做再平衡（rebalancing），每天收盤都再平衡，權重就固定不變。完全不交易叫做買進持有（buy-and-hold）；按市值加權的指數在成分股與股數不變時就是買進持有。跨資產彙總要用簡單報酬（simple return），跨時間彙總要用對數報酬（log return）。把各基金的對數報酬平均，會低估只作多帳戶的報酬。

3Trades, Turnover and Costs

derive the wealth recursion with trades, turnover and costs, and solve it for the post-trade wealth

The through case, fixed on 24 October 2026 before any result was computed, runs eight rules on E5. R1 holds a fifth of each fund; R2 holds a constructed mix of 40% 0050, 20% 00646, 30% 00679B and 10% 00635U. R3 to R8 are defined in LM28 to LM31, and every rule trades by (27.2).

A rule reads its window at a month's last close, known only after 13:30 (Research hygiene). It trades at the next close on which every fund trades, from wtdrift​ to target exposures ft​. The costs come out of wealth, so the book is self-financing.

DefinitionThe wealth recursion

With Wt−1​ the wealth after date t−1's trade, the wealth before and after date t's trade and the cost of trades x in money are

Wtpre​Wt​cost(x)​=Wt−1​(1+Rp,t​),=Wtpre​−cost(Wt​ft​−Wtpre​wtdrift​),=0.1425%∥x∥1​+∑i​taxi​max(0,−xi​).​(27.2)
After a trade wt​=ft​. On a date without a trade, Wt​=Wtpre​ and wt​=wtdrift​.

The trade depends on the post-trade wealth, so Wt​ sits on both sides. The commission is 0.1425% of the value traded on each side, and the tax 0.1% of every sale except 00679B's, a bond ETF exempt through 2026. These are Taiwan's posted commission (TWSE's Operating Rules, Art. 94; TPEx's Business Rules, Art. 64, which its ETF rules apply) and transaction tax (Securities Transaction Tax Act, Arts. 2 and 2-1). LM34 discusses them. No slippage is charged.

ResultOne post-trade wealth

If the factor (0.1425%+maxi​taxi​)∥ft​∥1​ is below 1, then (27.2) has exactly one solution Wt​. If selling the whole book would also cost less than Wtpre​, then 0<Wt​≤Wtpre​ (Deep Dive 1).

For E5's rules the factor is below 0.4%: 0.2425% for a fully invested long-only book. To size a trade, replace Wt​ by Wtpre​ inside the cost:

TOt​Wtpre​cost​∣error∣​=21​∥ft​−wtdrift​∥1​,≈0.285%TOt​+∑i​taxi​max(0,wt,idrift​−ft,i​),≤Wtpre​cost​(0.1425%+imax​taxi​)∥ft​∥1​.​(27.3)

The one-way turnover TOt​ is half LM8's 1-norm, since for weights summing to 1 before and after a trade the buys equal the sales. A trade then costs 0.285% of its turnover with no taxed sale, and up to 0.385% when every sale is taxed. Problem 8 derives the bound.

Deep DiveDeep Dive 1: one post-trade wealthoptional · click to expand

Here h(W)=W+cost(Wft​−Wtpre​wtdrift​), so (27.2) reads h(Wt​)=Wtpre​. The cost is Lipschitz in x with constant 0.1425%+maxi​taxi​: a change in x moves it by at most that constant times the change's 1-norm. So a move of W moves it by at most the result's factor times as much. Hence h is continuous, with slope at least 1 minus that factor, and takes the value Wtpre​ once.

The root is at most Wtpre​, since a cost is never negative. It is positive when h(0), the cost of selling the whole book, is below Wtpre​, as for every E5 rule. The iteration W↦Wtpre​−cost(⋅) multiplies the distance to the root by at most the same factor at each step, so it converges geometrically.

Example 2R1's first purchase

R1 starts with cash of 1 and buys a fifth of each fund at the close of 4 March 2019. Find the wealth after the trade, the cost and the turnover.

Solution

Every trade is a buy, so no tax is due, and the trades sum to Wt​ in money. By (27.2), Wt​=1−0.001425Wt​, so Wt​=1/1.001425=0.998577. The cost is 0.1423% of the pre-trade wealth of 1, and TOt​=21​∥ft​∥1​=0.5, half the buys, since cash pays for them. R2's first purchase is the same.

Example 3R1's trade of 1 April 2026

After the signal of 31 March 2026, R1's drifted weights at the close of 1 April were 17.954%, 18.611%, 18.697%, 16.680% and 28.058%. It traded back to a fifth each. Find the turnover and the cost, by (27.3) and exactly.

Solution

The turnover is half the sum of the five gaps to 20%: TOt​=8.058%. Only 00642U is sold, by 8.058 points, so (27.3) gives, as a share of the pre-trade wealth,

​0.285%×8.058%+0.1%×8.058%=0.03102%.​
The exact root of (27.2) gives 0.03100%: NT$3,100 on a book of NT$10,000,000 (Exhibit 3). It was the largest of R1's 91 rebalances by turnover.

Cash earns an illustrative rf​=1.5% a year, rf​/Aw​ a session; R1 and R2 hold none after the first purchase. LM31 sets R8's exposure, which may exceed 1, and the rate at which R8 borrows. No final sale is charged on 8 October 2026.

PitfallTurnover counted on both sides

Adding the buys and the sales doubles the one-way turnover, which is half the 1-norm. The commission falls on both sides, so (27.3)'s rate is 2×0.1425% of TOt​; the tax falls on sales only.

導讀交易成本從財富裡扣

再平衡要買賣，每一筆都付手續費（commission），賣出還要付證券交易稅（securities transaction tax）。這些交易成本（transaction cost）直接從帳戶財富扣掉，不另外放錢進來，所以叫做自我融資（self-financing）。麻煩在於交易後的財富決定要買多少，買多少又決定成本，所以它同時出現在等式兩邊；只要成本率乘上總曝險（gross exposure）小於 1，就只有一個解，可以反覆代入求出。週轉率（turnover）是單邊的，等於權重差的 L₁範數（1-norm）的一半；把買進和賣出加總會算成兩倍。

4E5: Two Frames and Asynchronous Closes

explain why E5 needs an estimation frame and a wealth frame, and why its daily correlations can mislead

E5's funds track the indices of Exhibit 1; those of 00635U and 00642U follow COMEX gold and NYMEX WTI crude-oil futures. Returns are total returns in New Taiwan dollars, Ri,t​=(Pt​+Dt​)/Pt−1​−1, and on 0050's 4-for-1 split of 18 June 2025 the return is (Pt​+Dt​)/(Pt−1​/4)−1. The sample runs from the base close of 17 January 2017, 00679B's first, to 8 October 2026: 3,551 days, or 9.7221 years.

The funds were chosen in 2026 among funds still listed; LM34 shows what such a choice can hide. Joint statistics need every fund's return over the same session, but wealth must cover every session. The estimation frame holds the dates on which every fund has a one-session return: T=2,362, and A=2,362×365.25/3,551=242.951 as LM10 counts a year. The wealth frame holds all 2,368 sessions, Aw​=243.569; a halted fund earns 0 there until it trades again.

Every daily covariance, correlation, mean and risk share uses the estimation frame, "on the 2,362 joint dates". Weekly and monthly returns, and every book's return, drift, trade and wealth, use the wealth frame.

Example 4Two frames, one halt

Why do E5's frames differ by six dates, and what does that do to 00642U's growth factor?

Solution

The six dates 11 to 18 June 2025 are in the wealth frame only. 0050 earns 0 on the first five and 4×47.57/188.65−1=0.864% on 18 June, from its close of 10 June. Lacking 0050's one-session returns, the estimation frame drops all six. So 00642U's growth factor is 1.593 over the wealth frame and 1.412 over the estimation frame, which misses its +12.816% of Example 1.

All five funds trade in Taipei, 00679B on TPEx and the others on TWSE. Both markets match orders from 09:00 to 13:30 (TWSE's Operating Rules, Art. 3; TPEx's Business Rules, Art. 40, which its ETF rules apply). Each closes with a call auction from 13:25, as its trading-system page says. New York's core session ends at 4:00 p.m. (NYSE): 04:00 Taipei time in US summer time, 05:00 otherwise.

So the 13:30 closes of 00646 and 00679B follow the previous New York session, and 0050 reacts to it too. TWSE's ETF overview page adds that a futures-trust ETF on a foreign futures index is valued on European and US futures, so the time difference is a risk. Across markets that close at different times, correlations are understated (Burns, Engle and Mezrich 1998). Here the closes coincide but reflect different sessions, so daily correlations can mislead either way.

Exhibit 4 compounds the wealth frame's returns within ISO weeks and calendar months, so each is a return from close to close. It drops the two weeks across 0050's halt, since 0050 has no close on 13 June 2025. Weeks are annualized as LM19 counts a year. The 500 weeks with a session span 9.6975 years, from the close of 20 January 2017 to that of 2 October 2026, so A=500×365.25/3,542=51.56; months use A=12.

Example 5Correlations by frequency

Does a daily correlation in Exhibit 4 differ from the weekly or monthly one by more than chance?

Solution

Daily, weekly and monthly, 0050 correlates −0.105, −0.022 and −0.015 with 00679B, 0.599, 0.588 and 0.549 with 00646, and 0.180, 0.231 and 0.191 with 00635U. With 00642U, 0050 correlates 0.092, 0.072 and 0.022. 00679B–00642U gives −0.18, −0.19 and −0.36.

The normal-theory errors are (1−ρ^​2)/T​; the plug-in ones of LM20's Problem 6 drop normality but need finite fourth moments, and both assume independent periods. So read 00642U's plug-in errors with care: its raw kurtosis (19.3), 26.6 daily and 12.3 monthly, is the largest (the others 11.2 to 21.0 and 4.3 to 5.7).

Of the five pairs, no daily–weekly gap reaches two weekly standard errors of either kind; the largest, 0.0833 for 0050–00679B, is 1.9 normal-theory ones. The daily figure is higher for 0050–00646, 0050–00642U and 00679B–00642U, and lower for the other two. Of the five pairs, one daily–monthly gap reaches two monthly standard errors: 00679B–00642U's 0.1807 (from the unrounded correlations), 2.2 normal-theory ones (0.0807) and 2.0 plug-in ones (0.0890). The data name no cause.

A daily correlation and a weekly or monthly one come from the same days, so their gap has its own standard error. Resampling whole weeks or months (LM22) gives about 0.046 for 0050–00679B's daily–weekly gap, 1.8 of them, and about 0.08 for 00679B–00642U's daily–monthly gap, which stays above two of them. Experiment 1 draws 20,000 resamples of each, weeks first, from default_rng([1, 20261011]), with Monte Carlo errors below 0.001.

Monthly plug-in errors reach 0.16. R1's volatility is 11.49%, 10.57% and 10.03% a year at the three frequencies, and 00642U's share 45.9%, 41.9% and 44.9%. The rules use daily Σ^.

導讀同一個收盤時間，不同的海外交易時段

五檔指數股票型基金（ETF）都在台北 13:30 收盤，但 00646 和 00679B 的價格反映前一晚的紐約交易時段，0050 在同一個收盤也受那個時段影響。期貨型 ETF 依歐美期貨評價，時差本身就是風險。這種非同步（asynchronous）收盤會讓日報酬的相關係數（correlation coefficient）和週、月報酬的不同，而且方向不一定。比較不同頻率時要附上標準誤（standard error）：兩種標準誤都假設各期獨立，plug-in 標準誤還需要四階動差（fourth moment）有限，所以要同時看各基金的峰態（kurtosis）。

5Portfolio Mean and Variance

compute a portfolio's mean, variance and volatility from μ and Σ, and measure its diversification

Let R have mean μ and covariance matrix Σ, and hold constant weights w. The return Rp​=w⊤R is linear in R, so its mean is w⊤μ, and (16.4) with M=w⊤ gives its variance:

μp​=w⊤μ,σp2​=w⊤Σw,a year: Aμp​ and A​σp​.​(27.4)

In sample the same steps give (10.4), sp2​=w⊤Σ^w, exact for a book reset at every close. The annual forms are conventions on simple returns, since variances add only for log returns on uncorrelated periods, (19.7). Every matrix here is a plug-in estimate. Section 4 measures the standard errors of five of the daily correlations; LM30 measures the error of Σ^ and of R1's risk shares.

By (8.11), for w≥0 the book's volatility is at most the weighted average of the funds' volatilities. Their ratio therefore measures diversification:

sp​w⊤s​≥1(w≥0, sp​>0),(27.7)

with equality only when every correlation among the held assets is 1. Choueifaty and Coignard (2008, eq. (1)) name it the diversification ratio. For a long-only book that is not equivalent to a single asset, it exceeds 1.

Example 6R1 and R2: mean, variance and diversification

From Exhibit 5, find μp​, sp​ and the ratio (27.7) of R1 and R2 at the full-sample Σ^.

6Which Fund Carries the Risk

derive each asset's covariance and beta with the portfolio, its risk contributions, and Euler's identity
choose a meaningful split of portfolio risk, and interpret a negative or unstable contribution

By (16.2) and the bilinearity of covariance, Cov(Ri​,Rp​)=∑j​σij​wj​, entry i of Σw, as LM7 found for two assets. Dividing by σp2​ gives fund i's beta to the portfolio, the slope of its line on Rp​ by (12.3):

Cov(Ri​,Rp​)=(Σw)i​,βi,p​=σp2​(Σw)i​​,i∑​wi​βi,p​=1.​(27.5)

The last identity holds because ∑i​wi​(Σw)i​=w⊤Σw=σp2​. Both ratios need σp​>0.

The marginal risk contribution MRCi​ is the rate at which the book's volatility changes with fund i's weight, the other weights fixed. By (10.8), ∇σp2​=2Σw, and for σp​>0 the chain rule gives ∂σp​/∂wi​=(Σw)i​/σp​. Multiplying by wi​ gives the risk contribution RCi​:

MRCi​=∂wi​∂σp​​=σp​(Σw)i​​,RCi​=wi​MRCi​,∑i​RCi​=σp​,RCi​/σp​=wi​βi,p​.​(27.6)

The sum is Euler's identity, which holds because σp​ is homogeneous of degree 1 in w: doubling every weight doubles the volatility (Deep Dive 2). Maillard, Roncalli and Teïletche (2010) define the contributions this way. The risk share wi​βi,p​ is RCi​ over σp​. LM10 warned that entry i of 2Σ^w is a rate of change, not a share: divided by 2sp​ it is MRCi​, and the share is wi​MRCi​/sp​.

Here h is differentiable at x, with h(ax)=ah(x) for every a>0. Differentiating in a at a=1 gives ∑i​xi​∂h/∂xi​=h(x). The volatility w⊤Σw​ is such a function wherever σp​>0, and not differentiable at a book of zero variance. The variance, homogeneous of degree 2, gives ∑i​wi​∂σp2​/∂wi​=2σp2​, which by (10.8) reads ∑i​wi​Cov(Ri​,Rp​)=σp2​. The working paper of Maillard et al. states the split this way (§2.1, p. 4, and its footnote).

7Negative and Moving Contributions

choose a meaningful split of portfolio risk, and interpret a negative or unstable contribution

A risk contribution is negative exactly when wi​Cov(Ri​,Rp​)<0, as for a long position in a fund that moves against the book. It lowers the book's volatility at the margin, not at any size: with the other weights fixed,

∂wi​∂MRCi​​​=σp​σi2​−βi,p2​σp2​​=σp​σi2​(1−ρi,p2​)​≥0​

(Problem 5). So MRCi​ never falls as wi​ grows. It tends to σi​>0 as wi​ grows without bound, so a hedge at the margin stops hedging at a large enough weight.

8Systematic and Residual Risk

split a portfolio's variance into systematic and residual parts with market betas

LM23's market model, fitted by (23.1), splits each sector's return into a part that moves with the market and a residual:

Ri,t​=αi​+βi​Rmkt,t​+εi,t​.

A book with weights w then has the beta w⊤β and the residual ∑i​wi​εi,t​. If the errors are uncorrelated with Rmkt,t​, (16.4) gives

σp2​=(w⊤β)2σmkt2​+w⊤Σres​w,RCi​=σp​wi​βi​(w⊤β)σmkt2​+wi​(Σres​w)i​​.​(27.8)

In sample (27.8) is exact when each βi​ is the OLS slope with an intercept, because the normal equations make each residual series' sample covariance with Rmkt,t​ zero (Problem 9). The first term is the systematic part, the second the residual part, and both split by fund.

U28 holds price indices, without dividends, and only their covariances are used. Under (23.8)'s assumption that dividends accrue evenly, the dividend gap moves the intercepts, not the covariances.

導讀系統性風險與殘差風險

市場模型（market model）把每個類股的報酬拆成跟著大盤動的部分和自己的殘差（residual）。帳戶的 beta 是各類股 beta 的加權平均，帳戶變異數於是分成兩塊：beta 平方乘上大盤變異數是系統性風險（systematic risk），其餘來自殘差的共變異數矩陣（covariance matrix）。用最小平方法（least squares）加截距（intercept）估計時，殘差和大盤的樣本共變異數剛好是 0，所以這個變異數分解（variance decomposition）在樣本內完全成立。單一指標模型（single index model）假設殘差彼此不相關，但等權重類股帳戶的殘差平均正相關，略去它們會低估波動率：14.73%，實際是 15.75%。

9Portfolio Accounts in Python

compute risk contributions and the wealth recursion in NumPy, and check them against their identities

The lab's files hold E5's frames in long form, one row per date and fund, and the block below works from them.

Python
import numpy as np
import polars as pl

funds = ["0050", "00646", "00679B", "00635U", "00642U"]
est = pl.read_csv("data/e5_estimation.csv",
                  schema_overrides={"fund": pl.String})
X = (est.pivot(on="fund", index="date", values="R")
     .sort("date").select(funds).to_numpy())
A = 2362 * 365.25 / 3551                       # 242.951
S = A * np.cov(X, rowvar=False, ddof=0)        # divisor T
w = np.full(5, 0.2)
sp = np.sqrt(w @ S @ w)                        # 0.11493
rc = w * (S @ w) / sp                          # (27.6)
assert abs(rc.sum() - sp) < 1e-15              # Euler's identity
wf = pl.read_csv("data/e5_wealth.csv",
                 schema_overrides={"fund": pl.String})
d = pl.col("date").str.to_date()
wk = (wf.group_by("fund", d.dt.iso_year().alias("y"),
                  d.dt.week().alias("k"))
      .agg((pl.col("R") + 1).product() - 1)   # weekly returns
      .sort("fund", "y", "k"))   # 502 weeks; Section 4 keeps 498

def post_trade(W_pre, drift, f, tax, k=0.001425):
    W = W_pre                                  # (27.2) by iteration
    while True:
        x = W * f - W_pre * drift
        new = W_pre - k * np.abs(x).sum() - tax @ np.clip(-x, 0, None)
        if abs(new - W) < 1e-15 * W_pre:
            return new
        W = new

np.cov divides by T−1 unless given ddof=0. select(funds) fixes the column order, and the sort fixes the order of the groups, which group_by does not keep.

Summary

  • A book's return uses the previous close's weights, (27.1); between trades the weights drift.
  • Costs come out of wealth, so the post-trade wealth solves (27.2), which has one root.
  • One-way turnover is half the 1-norm; a trade costs about 0.285% of it plus taxes, (27.3).
  • Daily joint statistics use E5's 2,362 joint dates, wealth its 2,368 sessions; daily correlations can mislead either way.
  • A book's mean and variance are w⊤μ and w⊤Σw, (27.4); the ratio (27.7) is at least 1 for a long-only book.
  • Euler's contributions (27.5)–(27.6) add up to σp​ and price a switch; a negative one hedges only at the margin.
  • With market betas the variance splits exactly in sample into systematic and residual parts, (27.8).

Buy-and-hold 買進持有 A book that keeps its holdings, so its weights drift with prices.
Rebalancing 再平衡 A trade back to target weights.
Self-financing 自我融資 Costs are paid out of wealth; no money comes in or goes out, (27.2).
Wealth recursion — (27.2): the pre-trade wealth, the trade and the post-trade wealth.
One-way turnover 週轉率 TOt​=21​∥ft​−wtdrift​∥1​, (27.3).
Transaction cost 交易成本 Commission on both sides and tax on sales, paid at the trade.
Estimation frame — E5's 2,362 dates on which every fund has a one-session return.
Wealth frame — E5's 2,368 sessions; a halted fund earns 0 until it trades again.
Asynchronous closes 非同步收盤 Prices that reflect different sessions abroad at the same close.
Diversification ratio — w⊤s/sp​, at least 1 for a long-only book, (27.7).
Beta to the portfolio — βi,p​=Cov(Ri​,Rp​)/σp2​, (27.5).
Marginal risk contribution 邊際風險貢獻 MRCi​=∂σp​/∂wi​=(Σw)i​/σp​, (27.6).
Risk contribution 風險貢獻 RCi​=wi​MRCi​; the contributions sum to σp​.
Risk share 風險份額 RCi​/σp​=wi​βi,p​.
Euler's identity — ∑i​wi​∂σp​/∂wi​=σp​, since σp​ is homogeneous of degree 1.
Euler's theorem Euler 定理 A differentiable h with h(ax)=ah(x) for every a>0 has ∑i​xi​∂h/∂xi​=h(x) (Deep Dive 2).
Systematic risk 系統性風險 (w⊤β)2σmkt2​, the market part of the variance, (27.8).
Residual risk 殘差風險 w⊤Σres​w, the rest of the variance, (27.8).
Single index model 單一指標模型 A market model whose residuals are taken as uncorrelated.
