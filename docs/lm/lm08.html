Vectors and Matrices

Holdings, prices and returns are vectors and a book of returns is a matrix: the dot product gives P&L and portfolio return, and the correlation of two return series is the cosine of the angle between their centred vectors.

Learning Module Overview
  • A vector is an ordered list with a unit for each entry and an index that says which asset, and which date, each entry belongs to. Two vectors add only when their orders, units and dates match.
  • The dot product x⊤y=∑k​xk​yk​ gives a book's P&L, ΔW=h⊤ΔP. On 7 April 2025 a book short one TX contract and long 0050 and 00679B made NT$57,400: the future and 0050 nearly cancelled, and the bond line made NT$54,000.
  • Simple returns combine with weights, Rp​=w⊤R, and log returns do not. The same day's P&L is exactly W0​f⊤R, 0.574% of equity, with net exposure 1⊤f=0.295 and gross exposure ∥f∥1​=1.147.
  • The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality ∣x⊤y∣≤∥x∥∥y∥ holds with equality only for parallel vectors, so every angle between non-zero vectors is well defined.
  • A sample correlation is the cosine of the angle between centred return vectors: 13.0° for 0050 and 00631L, 96.1° for 0050 and 00679B, and 41.0° to 77.4° across the 378 pairs of the 28 leaf TWSE sector indices that FinMind serves daily.
  • For long-only weights the triangle inequality gives sp​≤∑i​wi​si​: the 42.3/57.7 mix of 0050 and 00679B in LM7 had 10.91% annualized volatility against a weighted average of 16.29%.
  • In a data matrix X, with a row per date and a column per asset, Xw is a daily-rebalanced portfolio's return series and Yh a fixed book's daily P&L. Over 7–11 April the case book lost NT$124,200: 1⊤Yh=−124,200.
  • Matrix products follow a shape rule, are associative but not commutative, and transpose in reverse order. Summed daily returns are no period return: for a 60/40 mix of 0050 and 00679B the week's returns sum to −5.98% and compound to −6.32%.

1Introduction

A trading book holds several instruments at once. Its holdings, prices, weights and returns are therefore lists, and its P&L and return are sums over them. Linear algebra writes such sums as products of vectors. It also adds geometry: a length that measures exposure and an angle that measures how alike two return series are. This module builds those tools on one real book and one real week.

Motivating Case7 April 2025: two limit-downs and a bond that rose

At the close of 2 April 2025 a trader with equity W0​= NT$10 million holds the book h=(−1, 24,000, 100,000)⊤. It is short one April TX contract and long 24,000 shares of 0050 and 100,000 shares of 00679B, the US Treasury ETF of LM7. The short future roughly offsets the 0050 line: NT$4.26 million of index exposure against NT$4.22 million.

Taiwan's markets were closed on 3 and 4 April and reopened on Monday 7 April, after the United States had announced new tariffs. TX and 0050 closed at their −10% limits, and 00679B rose (Exhibit 1).

How much did the book make on 7 April, and which line made it? Why did the "hedged" book still lose NT$124,200 over the week? How alike are the series, over a week and over a decade?

Sections 2 and 3 introduce vectors and the dot product and answer the first question. Sections 4–6 add length and angle and show that a correlation is the cosine of an angle, which answers the third. Sections 7 and 8 stack the week into a matrix and answer the second.

2Vectors: Holdings, Prices, Weights and Returns

represent holdings, prices, weights and returns as vectors with a stated shape, unit per entry and index, and form linear combinations of them

A vector is an ordered list of numbers, written as a column x=(x1​,…,xN​)⊤. Entry k always refers to the same thing, here the same asset, so the order is part of the vector. Exhibit 2 draws the weights w=(0.6,0.4)⊤ of a mix of 0050 and 00679B as an arrow from the origin to the point (0.6,0.4). The arrow is a sum of two scaled basis vectors:

w=(0.60.4​)​=0.6(10​)+0.4(01​)=0.6e1​+0.4e2​.​

Shape, unit and index. A vector from a market needs three labels besides its numbers. Its shape is the number of entries. Each entry has a unit, and an index says which asset each entry belongs to and, for prices and returns, on which date. In the case every vector lists (TX, 0050, 00679B) in that order:

h=​−124,000100,000​​,P2 Apr​=​200×21,296176.0029.88​​,R7 Apr​=​−0.0999718−0.10000000.0180723​​.​

Addition and multiples. Vectors of the same shape add entry by entry, and a number a multiplies every entry: (x+y)k​=xk​+yk​ and (ax)k​=axk​. A sum such as w1​x1​+⋯+wN​xN​, with numbers wj​ and vectors xj​, is a linear combination. Holding two books together adds their holdings, and Section 7 builds every portfolio's return series in the same way.

Two vectors can be added only when they agree in asset order, in the unit of each entry and, for prices and returns, in date. The sum h+P2 Apr​ has the right shape but no meaning.

Example 1Shapes, units and index of the case's vectors

State the shape, the unit of each entry and the index of h, Pt​, the exposures f (position values ÷ equity, LM2) and Rt​ in the case. A second trader holds (−1, 20,000, 100,000)⊤. Write the desk's combined book, and the difference between the two books as a multiple of a basis vector.

Solution

All four have three entries in the order (TX, 0050, 00679B). The holdings count contracts, shares and shares, and the prices are NT$ per contract (200Ft​) and per share at the close of date t. The exposures and returns are pure numbers: f is measured at the close of 2 April, and Rt​ covers the session that ends on date t.

The combined book is (−2, 44,000, 200,000)⊤. The difference h−(−1, 20,000, 100,000)⊤ is 4,000e2​: the books differ only in 0050.

PitfallTwo lists in different orders or on different dates

Two columns of closes can look alike and still be misaligned. 0050 was halted from 11 to 17 June 2025 for its split while 00679B traded. Stacked by position, the two files would pair returns of different days. Join on the date first: an inner join keeps the 2,364 dates that both funds share from 17 January 2017, which give the 2,363 returns of LM7. Fix the asset order once and keep it in every vector.

Answer

Not as sent: its first entry counts 00679B shares, not TX contracts. Reordered to (TX, 0050, 00679B), it equals h, so the sum is 2h=(−2, 48,000, 200,000)⊤.

3The Dot Product: P&L and Portfolio Return

compute a book's P&L as h⊤ΔP and a portfolio's return as w⊤R, and explain why the second needs simple returns

Multiply two vectors entry by entry and add the products: the result is one number.

DefinitionDot product

The dot product of two vectors of the same shape is

x⊤y:=k=1∑N​xk​yk​=x1​y1​+⋯+xN​yN​.(8.1)
The dot product is also written x⋅y. The form x⊤y reads as a row times a column, a matrix product (Section 8).

P&L. A book's P&L on date t adds, over its lines, the holding times the price change:

ΔWt​=i=1∑N​hi​ΔPi,t​=h⊤ΔPt​.(8.2)

Each term is contracts times NT$ per contract, or shares times NT$ per share, so every term and the sum are in NT$. The units must match line by line, which is why TX enters at 200Ft​ NT$ per contract. Interest on cash and on margin deposits is ignored.

Example 2The book's P&L on 7 April 2025, line by line

Use Exhibit 1 to compute h⊤ΔP for 7 April, and say which line made the money.

Solution

The price changes are 200×(19,167−21,296)=−425,800 NT$ per TX contract, 158.40−176.00=−17.60 and 30.42−29.88=0.54 NT$ per share. So

h⊤ΔP​=(−1)(−425,800)+24,000(−17.60)+100,000(0.54)=425,800−422,400+54,000=57,400.​
The book made NT$57,400. The short future and the 0050 line nearly cancel, leaving NT$3,400, so the bond line made almost all of the gain. The hedge is sized by notional value; LM23 sizes hedges by regression, with the least-squares tools of LM12.

Portfolio return. LM1 (1.13) showed that simple returns add across assets with weights. Take a fully invested book, so that W0​=∑i​hi​Pi,0​, with weights wi​:=hi​Pi,0​/W0​. Then

1+Rp​​=∑i​hi​Pi,0​∑i​hi​Pi,1​​=i∑​W0​hi​Pi,0​​Pi,0​Pi,1​​=i∑​wi​(1+Ri​)=1+w⊤R,​

where the last step uses ∑i​wi​=1. Hence

Rp​=w⊤R.(8.3)

Log returns do not combine this way: a portfolio's log return is ln(1+w⊤R), not w⊤r (LM1 (1.14)).

Exposures. A book with futures breaks the identity W0​=∑i​hi​Pi,0​, because a futures contract costs nothing to enter. Define each line's exposure as its position value over equity, fi​:=hi​Pi,0​/W0​, the f of LM2. Line by line, hi​ΔPi​=W0​fi​Ri​, because

fi​Ri​=W0​hi​Pi,0​​⋅Pi,0​ΔPi​​=W0​hi​ΔPi​​.

Summing over the lines gives

ΔW=h⊤ΔP=W0​f⊤R,(8.4)

exactly, for one period and whatever the net exposure 1⊤f is. For a fully invested book f=w, and (8.4) is (8.3) times W0​.

Example 3The same P&L from exposures and returns

Compute the exposures f at the close of 2 April, and check (8.4) for 7 April.

Solution

The position values hi​Pi,0​ are −4,259,200, 4,224,000 and 2,988,000 NT$, so f=(−0.42592, 0.4224, 0.2988)⊤. With the returns of Exhibit 1, the products fi​Ri​ are 0.042580, −0.042240 and 0.005400. They sum to f⊤R=0.005740, a return of 0.574% on equity, and W0​f⊤R= NT$57,400. No rounding is involved: each product fi​Ri​ equals hi​ΔPi​/W0​ exactly.

Two rules make the dot product easy to use. It is symmetric, x⊤y=y⊤x, and it is linear in each vector:

(ax+y)⊤ΔP=ax⊤ΔP+y⊤ΔP.

So two books held together make the sum of their P&Ls. The P&L over several days is likewise the sum of the daily P&Ls, which Exhibit 3 shows for the week.

PitfallA limit-locked P&L is a mark, not cash

TX may move at most 10% from the previous regular session's settlement price, in ticks of one index point (TAIFEX). On 7 April that limit, 0.9×21,296=19,166.4, fell between two ticks. TX traded and settled at 19,167, a rounding that the data show and TAIFEX's pages do not state. The settlement price is the volume-weighted price of the last minute's trades, and TAIFEX states no special rule for a locked market.

The April contract's regular-session volume fell from 66,423 contracts on 2 April to 2,736, so the settlement of 19,167 came from thin trading. On 10 April, when TX settled at its +10% limit, the volume was 2,382.

In a market locked at its lower limit, sell orders queue while a buy order fills at once. A buy order would have closed the short TX line at 19,167 and realized its +425,800. A sell order for the long 0050 line, however, would only have joined the queue at 158.40, TWSE's 10% limit. The book could not be closed at its marks, so treat a limit day's P&L as a valuation, not as cash.

導讀內積就是損益

內積（dot product）把兩個向量（vector）逐項相乘再加總。持有部位乘上價格變動，加總就是整本帳的損益 ΔW=h⊤ΔP。權重乘上簡單報酬（simple return），加總就是投資組合報酬；對數報酬（log return）不能這樣加權相加。4 月 7 日這本帳賺了新臺幣 57,400 元：期貨空單與 0050 幾乎互相抵銷，賺錢的是美債 ETF。跌停鎖死時，賣單排隊、難以成交，買單卻能立刻成交：期貨空單可以在 19,167 回補、實現獲利，0050 多單的賣單卻只能在 158.40 排隊，所以整本帳的損益只是評價。

4Length, Distance and Exposure

calculate norms, distances and unit vectors, and distinguish net exposure 1⊤f from gross exposure ∥f∥1​

The dot product of a vector with itself is a sum of squares, and its square root is the vector's length.

DefinitionNorm, distance and unit vector

The Euclidean norm (length) of x is

∥x∥:=x⊤x​=x12​+⋯+xN2​​,(8.5)
and the distance between x and y is ∥x−y∥. A unit vector has length 1, and for x=0 the unit vector in the direction of x is u=x/∥x∥. The 1-norm is ∥x∥1​:=∑k​∣xk​∣.

In two dimensions (8.5) is Pythagoras' theorem: the arrow of Exhibit 2 has length 0.62+0.42​=0.7211. A norm scales with its vector, ∥ax∥=∣a∣∥x∥, and it is zero only for x=0.

Net and gross exposure. Two numbers summarize a book's exposures f:

net exposuregross exposure​=1⊤f=i∑​fi​,=∥f∥1​=i∑​∣fi​∣.​

Net exposure lets a short cancel a long, while gross exposure adds both. The two agree only when every position is long. Exhibit 4 draws both norms for the case's TX and 0050 exposures.

Example 4Net and gross exposure, and the distance between two mixes

(a) Compute the net and gross exposure of f=(−0.42592, 0.4224, 0.2988)⊤. (b) How far apart are the weight vectors (0.6,0.4)⊤ and (0.5,0.5)⊤?

Solution

(a) Net: −0.42592+0.4224+0.2988=0.29528. Gross: 0.42592+0.4224+0.2988=1.14712. On equity of NT$10 million, the book has NT$2,952,800 of net and NT$11,471,200 of gross exposure. The hedge cancels in the net figure but not in the gross one.

(b) The difference is (0.1,−0.1)⊤, so the distance is 0.12+0.12​=0.1414. Moving 10 points of weight from 00679B to 0050 is a step of length 0.1414.

PitfallNet exposure is not gross exposure

A limit on net exposure lets a short offset more longs, while a limit on gross exposure counts the short as more exposure. The case book's net 0.295 hides a gross 1.147. Before comparing a book with a limit, check which of the two the limit names; LM7 drew the same distinction for a leverage cap.

Knowledge Check 3

A long–short pair has exposures f=(0.5, −0.5)⊤. Find its net and gross exposure, its Euclidean norm and the unit vector in its direction.

Answer

Net 0.5−0.5=0, gross 0.5+0.5=1 and norm 0.25+0.25​=0.7071. The unit vector is f/0.7071=(0.7071, −0.7071)⊤.

5Angles, the Cauchy–Schwarz Inequality and Orthogonality

prove the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with its equality case, and use it to define angles, orthogonality and the triangle inequality

In the plane, the law of cosines links the sides of the triangle formed by x, y and x−y to the angle θ between x and y. Expanding the squared length of x−y with the dot product gives the same formula, with x⊤y in place of ∥x∥∥y∥cosθ:

∥x−y∥2​=∥x∥2+∥y∥2−2x⊤y=∥x∥2+∥y∥2−2∥x∥∥y∥cosθ.​

So in the plane x⊤y=∥x∥∥y∥cosθ. In N dimensions this equation becomes the definition of the angle, once we know that the ratio of x⊤y to ∥x∥∥y∥ lies between −1 and 1.

TheoremCauchy–Schwarz inequality

For any two vectors x and y of the same shape,

∣x⊤y∣≤∥x∥∥y∥,(8.6)
with equality exactly when one of the two vectors is a scalar multiple of the other (Strang 2016).

Proof. If y=0, both sides are 0, and y=0⋅x. Otherwise the squared length of x−ay is non-negative for every number a, and the choice a∗:=x⊤y/∥y∥2 gives

0≤∥x−a∗y∥2​=∥x∥2−2a∗x⊤y+a∗2∥y∥2=∥x∥2−∥y∥2(x⊤y)2​.​

Multiplying by ∥y∥2 gives (x⊤y)2≤∥x∥2∥y∥2, and square roots give (8.6). Equality holds exactly when ∥x−a∗y∥=0, that is, when x=a∗y. Conversely, if x=ay for some number a, both sides of (8.6) equal ∣a∣∥y∥2. ■

The quadratic ∥x∥2−2ax⊤y+a2∥y∥2 is smallest where its derivative in a is zero, at a∗. So a∗y is the multiple of y closest to x, the projection of x on y (LM12). Exhibit 5 draws it: the leftover x−a∗y is perpendicular to y.

With entries indexed by dates, (8.6) is the sum form that LM7 stated without proof:

​t∑​xt​yt​​≤t∑​xt2​​t∑​yt2​​.
DefinitionAngle and orthogonality

For non-zero vectors x and y, the angle θ between them, from 0° to 180°, is defined by

cosθ:=∥x∥∥y∥x⊤y​.(8.7)
Two vectors are orthogonal, x⊥y, when x⊤y=0.

By (8.6) the ratio in (8.7) lies between −1 and 1, so (8.7) defines an angle for every pair of non-zero vectors. The angle is 0° when y is a positive multiple of x, 180° for a negative multiple and 90° for orthogonal vectors. A dollar-neutral book, with net exposure 1⊤f=0, is orthogonal to 1.

Triangle inequality. The length of a sum is at most the sum of the lengths. Expand the square and apply (8.6) to the middle term:

∥x+y∥2​=∥x∥2+2x⊤y+∥y∥2≤∥x∥2+2∥x∥∥y∥+∥y∥2=(∥x∥+∥y∥)2,​

so that

∥x+y∥≤∥x∥+∥y∥.(8.8)
Example 5Two angles: two weight mixes, and a hedge against the vector of ones

(a) Find the angle between x=(0.6,0.4)⊤ and y=(0.5,0.5)⊤, and check (8.6). (b) The case's equity lines have exposures (fTX​,f0050​)=(−0.42592, 0.4224). Find their angle with 1=(1,1)⊤.

Solution

(a) The dot product is 0.30+0.20=0.50, and ∥x∥∥y∥=0.72111×0.70711=0.50990, so (8.6) holds. Then cosθ=0.50/0.50990=0.98058 and θ=11.31∘.

(b) The dot product with 1 is the net exposure, −0.42592+0.4224=−0.00352. The lengths are 0.59986 and 2​=1.41421, so cosθ=−0.00352/0.84833=−0.00415 and θ=90.24∘. The pair is almost orthogonal to 1, because the short future nearly cancels the 0050 line. An exactly dollar-neutral pair such as (−0.4224, 0.4224) makes exactly 90°.

導讀夾角與 Cauchy–Schwarz 不等式

在平面上，兩個箭頭的內積（dot product）等於兩個長度相乘、再乘上夾角（angle）的餘弦（cosine）。Cauchy–Schwarz 不等式（Cauchy–Schwarz inequality）保證內積的絕對值不超過兩個長度的乘積，所以在任何維度都能用 cosθ=x⊤y/(∥x∥∥y∥) 定義夾角。證明只用一件事：x−ay 的長度平方不會是負的，挑最好的 a 就得到不等式；等號只在其中一個向量（vector）是另一個的倍數時成立。內積為零就是正交（orthogonal），淨曝險為零的多空部位與全為 1 的向量正交。三角不等式（triangle inequality）也是它的直接結果：和的長度不超過長度的和。

Knowledge Check 4

For x=(0.6,0.4)⊤ and y=(0.5,0.5)⊤, find the number a for which x−ay is orthogonal to y. Interpret x−ay.

Answer

The proof's choice works: a∗=x⊤y/∥y∥2=0.50/0.50=1. Then x−y=(0.1, −0.1)⊤, and (0.1)(0.5)+(−0.1)(0.5)=0. The 60/40 mix is the 50/50 mix plus a dollar-neutral tilt toward 0050.

6Correlation as a Cosine

interpret a sample correlation as the cosine of the angle between centred return vectors, and bound the standard deviation of a long-only portfolio

A return series over T dates is a vector with one entry per date. Subtracting its mean from every entry centres it: x~:=x−xˉ1, with xˉ=1⊤x/T. With divisor T, the standard deviation is sx​=∥x~∥/T​, and the covariance of two series is x~⊤y~​/T, the average product of deviations in LM7.

ResultA sample correlation is a cosine

For two return series whose entries are not all equal, the sample correlation is

ρ:=sx​sy​x~⊤y~​/T​=∥x~∥∥y~​∥x~⊤y~​​=cosθ,(8.9)
where θ is the angle between the centred vectors. Hence −1≤ρ≤1, with ρ=±1 exactly when y~​=ax~ for a number a=0.

The factors T cancel in (8.9), and so would any other divisor, T−1 included. Annualization and units cancel too, because multiplying a vector by a positive number does not change its direction. The bound ∣ρ∣≤1 is (8.6), with its equality case.

Two series in their own plane. Two vectors span a plane, even in 2,363 dimensions, and a drawing in that plane needs only their lengths and their angle. Exhibit 6 draws the centred 0050 and 00679B vectors of LM7, scaled so that each length is an annualized volatility sA​. The mix 0.423x~0050​+0.577x~00679B​ is the centred return vector of the daily-rebalanced mix, and its length is that mix's volatility.

Standardized series. Dividing a centred vector by its standard deviation gives it length T​, and the squared distance between two such vectors depends only on ρ:

​sx​x~​−sy​y~​​​2=T+T−2sx​sy​x~⊤y~​​=2T(1−ρ).(8.10)

Per date, the root-mean-square gap between two standardized series is 2(1−ρ)​: 0 for identical series, 2​ for uncorrelated ones and 2 for opposite ones. LM31 groups assets by this distance.

A bound on a portfolio's risk. A daily-rebalanced portfolio's centred return vector is ∑i​wi​x~i​, so its standard deviation is a length divided by T​. For non-negative weights the triangle inequality (8.8), applied one term at a time, bounds that length:

sp​=T​​∑i​wi​x~i​​​​≤i∑​wi​T​∥x~i​∥​=i∑​wi​si​(wi​≥0).​(8.11)

Equality needs the centred vectors of all held assets to point the same way, that is, every correlation among them equal to 1. With short positions the bound becomes ∑i​∣wi​∣si​.

7Matrices as Data

organize returns in a data matrix and compute a daily-rebalanced portfolio's returns Xw and a fixed book's P&L Yh

A matrix is a rectangular table of numbers. Stack the daily return vectors of N assets over T dates, one row per date, and you get the data matrix

X=​x11​⋮xT1​​⋯⋯​x1N​⋮xTN​​​​=​R1⊤​⋮RT⊤​​​=(x1​​⋯​xN​​).​

Entry xtj​ is the return of asset j on date t: date first, asset second. Row t is the vector Rt​ written as a row, and column j is the return series xj​ of asset j, a vector with T entries. Exhibit 8 shows the 5×3 matrix of the case week.

The transpose X⊤ swaps rows and columns. It is N×T, and its entry in row j and column t is xtj​. A column vector is an N×1 matrix and its transpose a 1×N row, which is why the dot product is written x⊤y.

A portfolio's returns. Multiply X by a weight vector w. Entry t of the product is the dot product of row t with w, the portfolio's return on date t by (8.3). Read by columns, the same product is a linear combination of the assets' return series:

Xw=​R1⊤​w⋮RT⊤​w​​=w1​x1​+⋯+wN​xN​.(8.12)

In the words of LM7, a matrix times a vector is the vector of each row's dot product with it. Every row uses the same w, so Xw is the return series of a portfolio that resets its weights to w at every close: it is rebalanced daily. LM3 and LM7 assumed daily rebalancing, and (8.12) shows where the assumption sits.

A fixed book's P&L. A book that does not trade keeps its holdings h. Let Y be the T×N matrix of price changes, with row t equal to ΔPt⊤​. By (8.2), the book's daily P&Ls are

Yh=​ΔP1⊤​h⋮ΔPT⊤​h​​=​ΔW1​⋮ΔWT​​​.(8.13)

導讀資料矩陣：一列一天、一行一資產

把每天的報酬向量（vector）一列一列疊起來，就是資料矩陣（data matrix）X。第 t 列（row）是第 t 天各資產的報酬，第 j 行（column）是資產 j 的整段報酬序列。矩陣乘向量有兩種讀法：按列看，每一列與權重做內積（dot product），得到當天的組合報酬；按行看，是各資產報酬序列的線性組合（linear combination）。每一天都用同一組權重，所以 Xw 描述的是每天收盤都再平衡（rebalancing）回固定權重的組合。持股固定、不交易的帳戶要用價格變動矩陣 Y，Yh 是每天的損益，而它的曝險（exposure）會隨價格漂移。

Knowledge Check 6

Trader A rebalances to 60/40 in 0050 and 00679B at every close. Trader B buys 60/40 on 2 April and holds. Which trader's daily returns are Xw, and which daily P&Ls are Yh for some h?

Answer

8Matrix Multiplication, Transpose and Identity

multiply and transpose matrices, check their shapes, and apply associativity, non-commutativity and (MK)⊤=K⊤M⊤
implement these operations in NumPy and Polars, with dates aligned by a join and assets in a fixed order

Equation (8.12) multiplies a matrix by one vector. Multiplying by several vectors at once, stacked as the columns of a matrix, gives the general product.

DefinitionMatrix product

If M is n×N and K is N×m, their product MK is the n×m matrix with entries

(MK)ij​:=ℓ=1∑N​miℓ​kℓj​,(8.14)
the dot product of row i of M with column j of K. The inner sizes must agree; otherwise the product is not defined.

Put the weights of m portfolios side by side as the columns of an N×m matrix K. Column j of XK is X times column j of K, so each column of XK is one portfolio's daily return series. Exhibit 9 does this for three portfolios in the case week, with rows in the order (TX, 0050, 00679B):

K=​010​00.60.4​00.50.5​​.

Rules of the product. The product is associative: (MK)x=M(Kx). By (8.14), entry i of (MK)x is

j∑​(ℓ∑​miℓ​kℓj​)xj​​=ℓ∑​miℓ​(j∑​kℓj​xj​)=ℓ∑​miℓ​(Kx)ℓ​,​

which is entry i of M(Kx). Applied to each column of a third matrix, the same identity makes a product of three matrices associative. Holding the three portfolios of Exhibit 9 in proportions x therefore gives the returns of the combined weights Kx.

The product is not commutative. The product KM may not exist, and when both products exist they usually differ:

(10​11​)(11​01​)=(21​11​),(11​01​)(10​11​)=(11​12​).​
PropositionTranspose of a product

For an n×N matrix M and an N×m matrix K,

(MK)⊤=K⊤M⊤.(8.15)

Proof. The entry in row i and column j of (MK)⊤ is entry (j,i) of MK. By (8.14) it equals

ℓ∑​mjℓ​kℓi​=ℓ∑​(K⊤)iℓ​(M⊤)ℓj​,

which is entry (i,j) of K⊤M⊤. ■

For a vector, (8.15) gives (Xw)⊤=w⊤X⊤, a row.

Identity. The N×N identity matrix I has ones on its diagonal and zeros elsewhere, so MI=M and IK=K. It plays the role of 1. Dividing by a matrix means multiplying by its inverse, which exists only sometimes (LM9). The dot products of all centred columns form a square table X~⊤X~; LM10 divides it by T and calls it the sample covariance matrix.

Summing over dates. Multiplying a column by 1⊤ adds its entries. For a fixed book the sum telescopes, because the daily price changes sum to the change over the period:

1⊤Yh=t=1∑T​ΔPt⊤​h=(PT​−P0​)⊤h.

This sum is the book's exact P&L over the period. For a daily-rebalanced portfolio, 1⊤Xw is only the sum of the daily returns, and that sum is no period return, because returns compound.

In PythonShapes and products in NumPy

@ is the matrix product and .T the transpose. NumPy treats a 1-D array as a row or a column, whichever the product needs, so print shapes when they matter and use reshape(-1, 1) for an explicit column.

Python
import numpy as np

# TX settlement (points), 0050 and 00679B closes, 2 and 7-11 April 2025
P = np.array([[21296, 176.00, 29.88], [19167, 158.40, 30.42],
              [18169, 153.25, 29.33], [17184, 146.20, 28.10],
              [18902, 160.80, 28.91], [19543, 164.55, 27.88]])
X = P[1:] / P[:-1] - 1                      # 5 x 3 simple returns
K = np.array([[0, 0, 0], [1, 0.6, 0.5], [0, 0.4, 0.5]])
print(X.shape, K.shape, (X @ K).shape)      # (5, 3) (3, 3) (5, 3)
w = K[:, 1]                                 # 60/40 weights, a 1-D array
print(w.shape, w.T.shape)                   # (3,) (3,): no orientation
print(np.allclose((X @ K).T, K.T @ X.T))    # True: (MK)^T = K^T M^T
h = np.array([-1, 24_000, 100_000])
Y = np.diff(P * [200, 1, 1], axis=0)        # price changes, NT$ per unit
print((Y @ h).round(), (Y @ h).sum().round())
# [  57400.  -33000.  -95200.   87800. -141200.] -124200.0
