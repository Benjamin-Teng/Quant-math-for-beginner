An optimizer treats every estimate as exact: measure which errors break the plug-in portfolio, and test the repairs against equal weights.

Learning Module Overview
  • With the first window's estimates as the truth, the plug-in tangency portfolio keeps a mean true Sharpe ratio of 0.423 of the possible 0.880; with the means known it would keep 0.87686.
  • Errors in the means alone give the exposure to 00646 a standard deviation of 65.7 points; errors in the covariances alone, 4.73 points.
  • A mean's standard error falls only with years: two years of sessions leave 8.2 points for 00646, however many days they hold.
  • Shrinkage toward λˉI keeps the eigenvectors and moves the eigenvalues. On 60 returns of 28 sectors it cuts the condition number from 438 to 68.1 and the realized volatility from 13.85% to 12.04%.
  • With 504 days for five funds shrinkage hardly matters: out of sample, R5 realized 9.97% against R3's 9.96%.
  • The long-only minimum-variance portfolio is the unconstrained one of an adjusted matrix, (30.4). On U28's first half 19 constraints bind, and the second half's volatility rose from 11.78% to 12.14%.
  • Black–Litterman starts from R2's implied returns; one view at c=0.5 moves only 0050 and 00646, to 22.5% and 37.5%.
  • Resampled weights hold every fund, from 3.8% to 35.2%, although each fund is excluded in 18% to 72% of the resamples.

1Introduction

LM28 and LM29 solved for weights as if the means μ and the covariance matrix Σ were known. In practice both are estimated. This module measures the error in the inputs, finds which error matters, and tests the repairs.

Motivating CaseFive funds, two years, and positions nobody should hold

On 27 February 2019 the through case reads its first window: 504 daily returns of five funds. Fed the window's means and covariances, the tangency formula of LM28 puts 62.6% in 00646 and shorts 14.7% of 00642U. Its in-sample Sharpe ratio is 0.88, against 0.33 for equal weights.

Now suppose those estimates were the truth, and draw another 504 days from them, 10,000 times. Re-estimated on the new days, the position in 00646 has a standard deviation of 67.1 points. The portfolio's true Sharpe ratio averages 0.423. If only the covariances had to be estimated, it would average 0.87686, close to the possible 0.880. The simulation's standard errors are 0.5 points, 0.003 and 0.00002.

Out of sample the through case agrees that the covariances are estimated well enough. From March 2019 to October 2026, minimum variance, which uses no means, realized 9.96% volatility net of costs, against 12.48% for equal weights. Shrinking its covariance matrix changed that by 0.01 points. Which errors matter, and which repairs work?

Section 2 measures the errors, Section 3 sets the benchmark, Sections 4 to 7 test the repairs and Section 8 collects the Python.

2How Far Plug-in Weights Fall from the Truth

simulate plug-in portfolios from a known model and measure the loss due to estimated means

A simulation can separate the errors, because it knows the truth. Take the first window's estimates as the true μ and Σ of iid normal daily returns. Their annual means are 7.5, 10.0, 2.6, 0.8 and −1.4%, the volatilities 13.5, 11.9, 8.5, 9.4 and 23.2%, and the correlations run from −0.30 to 0.54 (LM28). Samples colour standard normals with Σ's Cholesky factor (LM12).

Chopra and Ziemba (1993) found errors in means far more damaging to optimal portfolios than errors in variances or covariances, as LM7 reported. Experiment 1 measures the split.

With a risk-free asset, (28.8) gives the best exposures Σ−1e/δ. At δ=1⊤Σ−1e=11.27 they are the tangency weights of Exhibit 1, with SR∗=0.880. A sample gives three plug-ins at that fixed δ:

f^=δΣ^−1e^​ (both estimated),δΣ−1e^​ (means only),δΣ^−1e​ (covariances only).​

Each is judged by its true Sharpe ratio, with the model's e and Σ:

SR(f)=f⊤Σf​A​f⊤e​.

By (16.4), f⊤Σf is the true variance of a portfolio built from estimates. A Sharpe ratio estimated from returns is itself noisy ((20.9)); LM33 tests the rules' Sharpe ratios, while here the model knows the true one.

The comparison uses exposures, not weights that sum to 1. In 2,115 of 10,000 samples (21%, SE 0.4 points) the sum 1⊤f^ is zero or negative, so normalized weights would flip sign. Exhibit 2 shows the first 1,000 samples.

With Σ known, (28.9) puts the means-only exposures at wtan+Σ−1(e^−e)/δ, and e^−e∼N5​(0,Σ/T). So by (16.4) the means-only exposures have

Cov(δΣ−1e^​)=δ2TΣ−1ΣΣ−1​=δ2TΣ−1​.

For 00646 this covariance gives an SD of 65.7 points, almost all of the spread in Exhibit 2.

Example 1Experiment 1: which errors cost

Draw 10,000 samples of 504 days from the model (default_rng([1, 20261011]), one standard_normal((504, 5)) call per sample). Compare the three plug-ins. SEs of medians and SDs come from 20 batches of 500 samples.

Solution

The mean true Sharpe ratio is 0.423 (SE 0.003) with both inputs estimated and 0.425 (SE 0.003) with the means alone. With the covariances alone it is 0.87686 (SE 0.00002). With both estimated the median is 0.489 (batch SE 0.004): a long left tail lowers the mean.

Exposure SDs run from 28.8 to 80.7 points with both estimated and from 1.75 to 4.79 with the covariances alone (batch SEs 0.2 to 0.6 and 0.01 to 0.04). The ratio runs from 14.2 (00646: 67.1 against 4.73) to 18.0 (00635U: 72.0 against 3.99), batch SEs 0.1.

The same draws measure the error in LM27's risk shares. R1's true shares, 1.1% to 50.2%, are estimated with SDs of 1.031 to 1.67 points (batch SEs 0.007 and 0.01).

Example 2Why the means

Compare the error of the first window's annual means with that of its covariance matrix.

Solution

By (20.2) an annual mean's SE is A​s/τ​, with τ=T/A=2.0745 years of sessions. The volatilities A​s, 13.50, 11.87, 8.54, 9.39 and 23.17%, give SEs of 9.4, 8.2, 5.9, 6.5 and 16.1 points. So four of the five means lie within one SE of zero.

By (18.4), for normal returns with a known mean, the root-mean-square error of Σ^ is 8.5% of ∥Σ∥F​. More days over the same span shrink this error, not the means': (20.2) depends on years, (18.4) on days.

The plug-in minimum variance needs no mean. It predicts 4.990% (SE 0.002) and delivers 5.0353% (SE 0.0001), against the possible 5.0152%. By (28.3) its true variance exceeds σmin2​ by its weight error's quadratic form.

PitfallA fixed model measures sampling error alone

The model's truth is an in-sample estimate: SR∗=0.880 is the first window's own optimum. Its returns are iid normal, and its means stay fixed, while real means move (LM24: the sector means of a later half spread almost twice as widely). The model is a best case; R1's and R3's realized volatilities are the evidence.

導讀平均報酬的誤差最傷

把估計值（estimate）當成真值直接代入最佳化（optimization）公式，就是代入原則（plug-in principle）的做法。實驗一假設第一個視窗的估計值就是真值，再從這個模型抽出 504 天：同時估計平均與共變異數（covariance）時，真實夏普比率（Sharpe ratio）平均只剩 0.423；只估計共變異數時則有 0.87686，幾乎沒有損失。原因在於平均超額報酬（excess return）的估計誤差（estimation error）只隨年數下降：兩年資料讓 00646 年化平均的標準誤（standard error）達 8.2 個百分點，共變異數矩陣（covariance matrix）的誤差卻隨天數下降。這個模擬只量到抽樣誤差（sampling error）；真實的平均報酬還會移動，所以實際情況只會更糟。

Knowledge Check 1

How does the SE of 00646's annual mean change if the same two years held twice as many days? If the span doubled instead?

Answer

By (20.2) the SE is A​s/τ​, and A​s does not depend on how often returns are sampled. Twice the days over the same span leave the SE at 8.2 points; twice the span cuts it to 8.2/2​=5.8 points.

3Equal Weights as a Benchmark

explain why equal weights are hard to beat, and state the conditions of the 1/N evidence

Equal weights estimate nothing. By (10.6) the variance of the 1/N portfolio is var/N+(1−1/N)cov, so its risk depends only on the average variance and covariance. Its weights carry no estimation error, but they ignore every difference between the assets.

DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (2009) compared 14 models with 1/N on seven datasets. None was consistently better in Sharpe ratio, certainty-equivalent return or turnover (their abstract). They name three conditions under which optimizing wins: a long estimation window, an ex ante best Sharpe ratio well above 1/N's, and few assets (p. 1919).

Experiment 2 asks how long a window the plug-in needs. It draws 2,000 samples at each of seven lengths from 126 to 8,064 days (default_rng([2, 20261011])), plotted in Exhibit 3.

Example 3The critical window

When does the plug-in beat 1/N's true Sharpe ratio of 0.327 on average, and how does that compare with the US calibration?

Solution

With both inputs estimated the mean true Sharpe ratio is 0.230, 0.322, 0.422, 0.546, 0.672, 0.759 and 0.816 at 0.52 to 33.2 years of sessions (SEs 0.008 to 0.001). With the covariances alone it is already 0.8660 (SE 0.0002) at 0.52 years of sessions. The plug-in is about equal to 1/N at 1.04 years of sessions (0.322, SE 0.008) and well above at 2.07 (0.422, SE 0.007).

DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal judge the sample-based rule in their §4 by its certainty-equivalent return instead. In their Figure 1's panel E, calibrated to US stocks, 1/N's monthly Sharpe ratio is 0.12 against the tangency's 0.15. The rule then needs more than 3,000 months with 25 assets to beat 1/N (pp. 1939–1941).

In their calibration 1/N reaches 0.8 of the best; in this model only 0.37 (0.327 against 0.880). The plug-in has much to gain, and the means do not move: a best case.

Kan and Zhou (2007) add that an optimal mix of the risk-free asset, the sample tangency portfolio and the sample minimum-variance portfolio dominates a mix of the first two alone.

On E5, out of sample and net of costs, R1 realized 12.48% and R3 9.96% on the 1,851 daily returns from 5 March 2019 to 8 October 2026.

R3's windows had predicted 8.84% on average, within LM28's range of 4.97% to 10.42%. An optimized in-sample variance is biased down, but in experiment 1 the bias is small: 4.990% predicted against 5.0353% delivered (Example 2). Most of the 1.1-point gap comes from inputs that moved after each window, as in LM7.

Costs, turnover and net growth of the rules are LM34's numbers, and the standard errors of their statistics LM33's; this module reports only volatility. Equal weights are also the c=0 end of Section 4's shrinkage.

Knowledge Check 2

In experiment 1, 1/N has a true Sharpe ratio of 0.33 against the best 0.88, yet it beats the plug-in in 31.4% of samples (SE 0.5 points). Why can the worse portfolio win so often?

Answer

1/N's ratio is fixed, while the plug-in's varies. Errors in the means often point the exposures in a poor direction, so its ratio has a long left tail (mean 0.423, median 0.489). It falls below 0.327 in 31.4% of the samples.

4Shrinking the Covariance Matrix

derive the oracle shrinkage weight as a bias–variance trade-off
compute Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage by hand and with scikit-learn, and diagnose its effect on eigenvalues and weights
compare out-of-sample volatilities of sample, shrunk and constrained portfolios, labelling each result

Minimum-variance weights divide by the small eigenvalues of Σ^, (11.8), and the smallest is too small on average. With a known mean, (11.4) gives λN​(Σ^)≤q⊤Σ^q for a unit eigenvector q of Σ's smallest eigenvalue, and E[q⊤Σ^q]=λN​(Σ), so EλN​(Σ^)≤λN​(Σ).

DefinitionShrinkage toward the scaled identity

For a weight 0≤c≤1 on the data, with λˉ=trΣ^/N,

Σ^c​:=cΣ^+(1−c)λˉI,Σ^c​qk​=(cλk​+(1−c)λˉ)qk​,κc​=cλN​+(1−c)λˉcλ1​+(1−c)λˉ​.​(30.1)
The target Σ^tgt​=λˉI has the trace of Σ^.

By (11.3), shrinkage keeps the eigenvectors and moves every eigenvalue toward λˉ, the small ones up and the large ones down, so κ falls. For c<1 each eigenvalue is at least (1−c)λˉ>0: Σ^c​ is positive definite even when Σ^ is singular. At c=0 the minimum-variance portfolio is 1/N. Unstable eigenvectors of close eigenvalues, (11.7), stay unrepaired.

TheoremThe oracle weight

Let Σtgt​ be a nonrandom matrix, and let Σ^ satisfy two conditions:

  1. it is unbiased, EΣ^=Σ (a known mean);
  2. its noise σΣ^2​:=E∥Σ^−Σ∥F2​ is finite (finite fourth moments).

If ∥Σ−Σtgt​∥F2​+σΣ^2​>0, then over nonrandom c the expected squared Frobenius distance of cΣ^+(1−c)Σtgt​ from Σ is smallest at

c∗=∥Σ−Σtgt​∥F2​+σΣ^2​∥Σ−Σtgt​∥F2​​.(30.2)
For iid normal returns with a known mean, (18.4) gives σΣ^2​=(∥Σ∥F2​+(trΣ)2)/T.

Proof. The error is c(Σ^−Σ)+(1−c)(Σtgt​−Σ), and its squared Frobenius norm is a sum of squared entries. The cross term has expectation zero, because E(Σ^−Σ)=0 and the second matrix is nonrandom. Hence

​E​cΣ^+(1−c)Σtgt​−Σ​F2​=c2σΣ^2​+(1−c)2∥Σ−Σtgt​∥F2​,​

variance plus squared bias, (20.1)'s split entry by entry. This parabola in c is smallest where its derivative 2cσΣ^2​−2(1−c)∥Σ−Σtgt​∥F2​ is zero, at (30.2).

The oracle has the shape of LM24's (24.3), with the target's squared distance from the truth in the prior variance's place.

The oracle needs Σ. Ledoit and Wolf (2004a) estimate its parts:

c^=1−∥Σ^−λˉI∥F2​min(σ^Σ^2​, ∥Σ^−λˉI∥F2​)​,σ^Σ^2​=T21​t=1∑T​​(Rt​−Rˉ)(Rt​−Rˉ)⊤−Σ^​F2​.​(30.3)

The denominator estimates the oracle's, and 1−c^ estimates the noise's share 1−c∗ (Deep Dive). As N and T grow with N/T bounded, c^ is consistent and the shrunk matrix attains the oracle's loss asymptotically, under moment conditions. This is stated without proof (Theorems 3.2 and 3.4 of their 2001 working paper). Here N/T runs from 5/504 on E5 to 28/20 on U28.

Forecasting the matrix day by day, with the recursion (26.2) applied to Rt​Rt⊤​, is LM31's method.

Example 4Ledoit–Wolf by hand on the first window

Compute c^ for the first window by hand, check it with scikit-learn, and find where the noise comes from.

Solution

The mean eigenvalue is λˉ=0.0204 a year, a volatility of 14.3%. With ∥Σ^−λˉI∥F2​=1.885×10−3 and σ^Σ^2​=6.12×10−5,

c^=1−1.885×10−36.12×10−5​=0.9675.
In scikit-learn shrinkage_ is 0.0325, equal to 1−c^ to rounding error (below 10−15).

With (18.4)'s normal value as the noise, the oracle is c∗=0.9851; the noise estimate is 2.14 times that value. Fat tails cause the excess.

Entry by entry, (18.4)'s normal value is (σ^ij2​+σ^ii​σ^jj​)/T, with σ^ij​ the entries of Σ^. A fund's share of the excess is its row's sum over the total. 0050's tails are the fattest (raw kurtosis 13.3), and its row carries 36%. The milder tails of 00642U (raw kurtosis 4.5) meet the largest variance (volatility 23.2%), so its row carries the most, 39%.

Example 5Few returns for many sectors

Solution

On 30 returns the sample matrix has κ=116,097. Its minimum-variance portfolio predicts 1.34% and realizes 43.12%, with gross exposure ∥w∥1​=13.48. Shrunk with c^=0.823, it realizes 12.46%.

Example 6R5 against R3

R5 is R3 with the Ledoit–Wolf matrix. How far apart are their weights, and why did c^ fall in 2026?

Solution

The largest gap between R5's and R3's weights is 13.1 points, at 2026-03-31, where c^ was lowest (00679B 55.1% → 42.0%). Over the 92 signals, the median of each signal's largest gap is 1.6 points.

The target pulls each variance toward their average, a volatility of 26.5%: up for the bond fund (13.4%), down for 00642U (43.0%). So weight leaves the bond fund. Out of sample R5 realized 9.97% and R3 9.96%.

Deep DiveDeep Dive: why Ledoit and Wolf's noise estimate worksoptional · click to expand

With a known mean, Σ^ is the average of T iid matrices Mt​=(Rt​−μ)(Rt​−μ)⊤ with mean Σ. Entry by entry, (18.2) gives E∥Σ^−Σ∥F2​=E∥Mt​−Σ∥F2​/T. In (30.3) the sum over T is the sample spread of the Mt​, and the second 1/T is (18.2)'s.

The expansion in the proof of (30.2), applied to (Σ^−Σ)+(Σ−Σtgt​), gives the Pythagorean identity of the 2001 working paper's §2,

E∥Σ^−Σtgt​∥F2​=∥Σ−Σtgt​∥F2​+σΣ^2​.
So ∥Σ^−λˉI∥F2​ estimates the oracle's denominator, apart from the error in λˉ; the minimum in (30.3) keeps c^≥0.

Stated without proof, the consistency result assumes (§3 of the working paper):

  1. the ratio N/T stays bounded as both grow;
  2. the returns have bounded eighth moments;
  3. a condition holds on products of the returns' entries.
PitfallLedoit and Wolf's estimate reacts to tails

TR23's Student t fit has 3.065 degrees of freedom, so its fourth moment is infinite (LM15). Such returns make the noise of (30.2) infinite and fail Ledoit and Wolf's eighth-moment condition. In E5's window to 2026-03-31, c^ fell to 0.781 against 0.983 with the normal noise. Say what c^ responds to; never call it the right amount of shrinkage.

導讀收縮：用一點偏誤換掉大量雜訊

平均已知時，樣本共變異數矩陣（sample covariance matrix）不偏（unbiased），但雜訊（noise）大；目標 λˉI 沒有雜訊，卻有偏誤（bias）。收縮估計量（shrinkage estimator）取兩者的加權平均（weighted average），權重（weight）的選擇是偏誤及變異數之抵換（bias–variance tradeoff）：使 Frobenius 範數（Frobenius norm）下的均方誤差（mean squared error）最小。收縮（shrinkage）保留特徵向量（eigenvector），把每個特徵值（eigenvalue）往平均值拉，小的變大、大的變小，所以條件數（condition number）下降。28 個產業只用 60 筆報酬時，條件數從 438 降到 68.1，樣本外波動率從 13.85% 降到 12.04%；E5 用 504 天估 5 檔基金，收縮幾乎沒有影響。Ledoit–Wolf 的估計值會對厚尾（heavy tail）起反應，不能把它當成「正確的收縮量」。

5Constraints as Implicit Shrinkage

derive the long-only minimum-variance portfolio as an unconstrained one on an adjusted covariance matrix

R3 forbids short positions. Jagannathan and Ma (2003) showed that this acts on the covariance matrix. The problem is

wmin​ w⊤Σ^wsubject to1⊤w=1,  w≥0.

The feasible set is closed and bounded, so a minimizer exists. With Σ^ positive definite the objective is strictly convex, so by (29.3) the minimizer wlo is unique. LM29's KKT conditions (29.5), halved, read

Σ^wlo=ν0​1+ν,ν≥0,νi​wilo​=0.

Each multiplier is half of (29.5)'s, as Jagannathan and Ma scale them. Multiplying by wlo⊤, with 1⊤wlo=1 and ν⊤wlo=0, gives ν0​=sp2​. By (27.5), a held fund's sample covariance with the portfolio is sp2​, an excluded one's sp2​+νi​.

PropositionThe constraint as an adjusted matrix

Let wlo with 1⊤wlo=1, ν0​ and ν satisfy the halved KKT conditions above, and define

Σ^ν​:=Σ^−(ν1⊤+1ν⊤).(30.4)
Then Σ^ν​ is positive semidefinite and wlo is one of its unconstrained minimum-variance portfolios (Jagannathan and Ma 2003, Proposition 1). If Σ^ is positive definite, so is Σ^ν​, and wlo is its only one.

Derivation. For any x put y=x−(1⊤x)wlo. Expanding y⊤Σ^y with the KKT conditions and ν⊤wlo=0 gives

x⊤Σ^ν​x=y⊤Σ^y+ν0​(1⊤x)2≥0.

On the budget 1⊤x=1 the quadratic form is at least ν0​, the value at wlo, where y=0. With Σ^ positive definite and ν0​>0, the sum is zero only if y=0 and 1⊤x=0, that is x=0. On the budget, equality needs y=0, that is x=wlo. Jagannathan, Ma and Zhang (2019) correct the 2003 paper's proof of Proposition 2, not of Proposition 1.

The adjusted matrix lowers an excluded fund's covariances by νi​+νj​ and its variance by 2νi​: the constraint shrinks only the entries of the funds it excludes.

6Starting from a Reference Portfolio: Black–Litterman

derive the Black–Litterman posterior for one view and compute the weights it implies

Means are the weak input. The model of Black and Litterman (1992) starts from a reference portfolio and lets views move it. If an investor with risk aversion δ holds wref​ optimally, (28.8) gives wref​=Σ^−1e/δ. Reversed, it gives the implied excess returns

eeq​:=δΣ^wref​.

The reference here is R2, LM27's constructed mix of 40% 0050, 20% 00646, 30% 00679B and 10% 00635U. The book has no market weights for these funds. The risk aversion

δ=wref⊤​Σ^wref​wref⊤​e^​

makes R2's implied mean excess return equal its window mean.

The prior is e∼N5​(eeq​,Σ^/n0​): the reference is worth n0​ observations, as in LM24. The literature's scalar is 1/n0​, with no consensus value. Idzorek (2007) reports values near 0, from 0.01 to 0.05, 1, and about one over the number of observations, each from other authors.

A view is a linear statement about excess returns, with an error: Ke=b+ε, where ε∼N(0,Ξ) is independent of e. Here K=(−1,1,0,0,0), so the view says that 00646 beats 0050 by b.

PropositionThe Black–Litterman posterior for one view

Under this prior and view, the posterior mean of e and its exposures fBL​=Σ^−1eBL​/δ are

eBL​=eeq​+Σ^K⊤KΣ^K⊤c(b−Keeq​)​,c=KΣ^K⊤/n0​+ΞKΣ^K⊤/n0​​,fBL​=wref​+K⊤δKΣ^K⊤c(b−Keeq​)​.​(30.5)
A data view, Ξ=KΣ^K⊤/T, gives c=T/(T+n0​), (24.3)'s weight.

Derivation. By (16.4) and the independence of ε, each ei​ and the observed view Ke+ε form a normal pair, with covariance (Σ^K⊤)i​/n0​ and view variance KΣ^K⊤/n0​+Ξ. By (16.8), the mean of ei​ given that the view equals b is the prior mean plus the covariance over the view variance times b−Keeq​: (30.5)'s first line. Multiplying by Σ^−1/δ, with Σ^−1eeq​/δ=wref​, gives the last.

The exposures move along K⊤ only. He and Litterman (1999) state the general case: the unconstrained optimum is the scaled reference plus a weighted sum of view portfolios. The posterior mean for several views is stated without proof, in an equivalent form of Idzorek's (2007) formula (3). With one row of K per view and Ξ their error covariance,

eBL​​=eeq​+Σ^K⊤(KΣ^K⊤+n0​Ξ)−1(b−Keeq​).​

The exposures here use Σ^, not the posterior covariance of e. Exhibit 7 traces them as c rises.

PitfallThe reference carries the prior

Black–Litterman moves away from wref​ only as far as the views push, so a poor reference is a poor prior. R2 is constructed, and δ fits R2's own window mean; report both beside every such portfolio.

導讀從參考投資組合出發

Black–Litterman 不直接採用樣本平均，而是先假設參考投資組合（reference portfolio）是最適的，反推出隱含超額報酬（implied excess returns）eeq​=δΣ^wref​，作為事前分布（prior distribution）的中心。觀點（view）是對超額報酬的線性陳述，帶有誤差；事後分布（posterior distribution）的平均把兩者依精確度（precision）加權，與 LM24 的 (24.3) 同形。只有一個觀點時，部位只沿著觀點的方向移動，本例只有 0050 與 00646 改變。參考投資組合 R2 是建構的，事前分布的好壞取決於它。

7Resampling, Factor Structure and Repair

choose a repair (shrinkage, constraints, a reference portfolio, a factor model or resampling) for a given ratio of observations to assets
compare out-of-sample volatilities of sample, shrunk and constrained portfolios, labelling each result

Michaud (1989) argued that mean–variance optimization tends to maximize the effects of errors in its inputs, and that unconstrained optimization can do worse than equal weights. Resampled efficiency, first proposed by Michaud (1998), averages instead, as Michaud and Michaud (2008) describe it. It draws means and covariances around the estimates, computes each draw's frontier, and averages the weights of matched portfolios.

Experiment 4 is a variant (default_rng([4, 20261011])). It averages R4's long-only maximum-Sharpe portfolio (29.6) over 1,000 iid bootstrap resamples of the first window's rows (LM22). In the 12 where no fund's mean exceeds rf​, R3's minimum variance is used instead. The average holds every fund, from 3.8% (00642U) to 35.2% (00646), SEs 0.4 and 0.9 points.

Yet each fund is excluded in 18% (00646) to 72% (00642U) of the resamples, SEs 1 to 2 points: averaging spreads weights that each resample concentrates.

A factor structure cuts the parameters. The single-index matrix of Sharpe (1963) is LM27's (27.8) with uncorrelated residuals,

Σ^F​=σmkt2​β^​β^​⊤+diag(σ^res,12​,…,σ^res,N2​),

with (23.1)'s betas on the TAIEX price index and residual variances with divisor T. For U28 the single-index matrix has 57 parameters against the sample's 406, yet its portfolio realized 12.67% in the second half, against the sample's 11.78% (Exhibit 8): a wrong structure costs. Ledoit and Wolf shrink toward this matrix (2003) or toward constant correlations (2004b); nonlinear shrinkage is outside this book.

Example 9Repairing LM10's pairwise matrix

LM10's constructed Cpw​ has entries 0.982, 0.199 and −0.444 and detCpw​=−0.374592. Repair it three ways.

Solution

Its eigenvalues are 2.0149, 1.1471 and −0.1621. Shrinking toward I gives eigenvalues cλk​+1−c, the smallest nonnegative up to c=1/(1−λ3​)=0.8605, with off-diagonal entries 0.845, 0.171 and −0.382. Clipping λ3​ to 0 and rescaling to a unit diagonal gives 0.852, 0.154 and −0.385.

The nearest correlation matrix (Higham 2002b) gives 0.864, 0.139 and −0.379, PSD to rounding; a second solver, cvxpy's CLARABEL, agrees to 1.4×10−7. The Frobenius distances to Cpw​ are 0.216, 0.211 and 0.209.

The nearest matrix minimizes ∥M−Cpw​∥F2​ over PSD matrices M with unit diagonal. That set is closed, bounded and convex, and the objective strictly convex, so the minimizer exists and is unique. Rousseeuw and Molenberghs (1993) compare methods based on shrinking, on eigenvalues and on geometry. Constructed (LM10's).

The Summary's table chooses a repair by the ratio T/N of observations to assets. Picking the best of several estimators judged on one split is a search, which LM34 corrects.

導讀重抽樣、因子結構與修補

重抽樣（resampling）把資料重抽很多次，每次求最適權重再取平均；平均後每檔基金都有部位，但每次重抽都圍繞同一組樣本平均。單一指標模型（single index model）用市場的 beta 描述共變異數（covariance），28 個產業只要 57 個參數（parameter），但結構不對時樣本外波動率反而較高。成對估計的相關矩陣（correlation matrix）可能不是半正定（positive semidefinite）：可以往單位矩陣（identity matrix）收縮、把負的特徵值（eigenvalue）截為零，或找最近的相關矩陣。

8Estimation-Robust Portfolios in Python

compute Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage by hand and with scikit-learn, and diagnose its effect on eigenvalues and weights
Python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np
import polars as pl
from sklearn.covariance import LedoitWolf

est = pl.read_csv("data/e5_estimation.csv", schema_overrides={"fund": pl.Utf8})
wide = est.pivot(on="fund", index="date", values="R").sort("date")
funds = ["0050", "00646", "00679B", "00635U", "00642U"]
first = wide.filter(pl.col("date") <= pl.lit("2019-02-27")).tail(504)
x = first.select(funds).to_numpy()              # 504 x 5 daily returns
lw = LedoitWolf(assume_centered=False).fit(x)
c_hat = 1 - lw.shrinkage_                       # 0.9675
A = 242.951422                                  # E5's estimation frame
S = A * np.cov(x, rowvar=False, ddof=0)         # annual inputs for solvers
w = cp.Variable(5)
cons = [cp.sum(w) == 1, w >= 0]
prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(cp.quad_form(w, cp.psd_wrap(S))), cons)
prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, tol_gap_abs=1e-12, tol_gap_rel=1e-12,
           tol_feas=1e-12)
assert prob.status == "optimal"
nu = cons[1].dual_value / (2 * A)               # Jagannathan and Ma's scale

LedoitWolf(assume_centered=False) removes the mean, divides by T and shrinks toward λˉI only; shrinkage_ is the target's weight 1−c^. OAS is another estimator, after Chen et al. (2010); its source says it omits their 2/p factor. The dual values of w >= 0 belong to w⊤(AΣ^)w, so dividing by 2A gives the daily ν. psd_wrap asserts positive semidefiniteness without testing it.

On the first window no constraint binds, so every νi​ is 0 to solver tolerance; Example 7 checks the scale on U28.

Glossary

Term 中文 Meaning
Plug-in portfolio 代入投資組合 Weights computed from estimates as if they were the truth, such as Σ^−1e^/δ.
Estimation error 估計誤差 The difference between an estimate and the quantity it estimates.
Sampling error 抽樣誤差 The part of the estimation error that comes from a finite sample of a fixed model.
Shrinkage estimator 收縮估計量 A weighted average of an estimator and a target, such as Σ^c​ of (30.1).
Oracle weight — c∗ of (30.2), the best nonrandom weight on the data, which needs the truth.
Ledoit–Wolf estimator — The shrunk matrix with c^ of (30.3).
Frobenius norm Frobenius 範數 ∥M∥F​, the square root of the sum of squared entries (LM18).
Condition number 條件數 κ=λ1​/λN​ of a positive definite matrix; κc​ after shrinkage.
Adjusted covariance matrix — Σ^ν​ of (30.4), whose unconstrained minimum-variance portfolio is the long-only one.
Reference portfolio 參考投資組合 The weights wref​ that Black–Litterman's prior starts from.
Implied excess returns 隱含超額報酬 eeq​=δΣ^wref​, the means under which the reference is optimal.
View 觀點 A linear statement Ke=b+ε about excess returns, with an error.
Resampled efficiency — Averaging optimal weights over draws of the inputs (Michaud and Michaud 2008).
Single index model 單一指標模型 The covariance matrix Σ^F​ of market betas and uncorrelated residuals.
Nearest correlation matrix — The PSD matrix with unit diagonal closest to a given one in the Frobenius norm.
