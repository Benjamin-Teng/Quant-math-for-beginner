## Introduction

[[LM28]] solved mean–variance problems with equality constraints, in closed form. Real mandates add inequalities: no short sales, caps, limits on leverage and on trading. This module adds those limits, a solver that handles them, and the checks that show the solver is right.

> [!case] Why does the long-only portfolio drop two funds?
> On 30 September 2026 the through case re-estimates its inputs from its last 504 joint trading days, 23 August 2024 to 30 September 2026. Fed those means and that covariance matrix, [[LM28]]'s tangency formula puts 114.24% in 0050. It sells short 27.75% of 00646 and 48.48% of 00679B, and it buys 31.07% of 00635U and 30.92% of 00642U. Its gross exposure is 252%, for an in-sample Sharpe ratio of 2.18.
>

>
> Three questions follow. Why is 00646's weight zero, when it earns 16.44 points a year above cash? What does each limit cost? And when a solver prints these weights, how can the reader tell they are right?

## Convex Sets and Convex Functions {lo=1}

A solver walks downhill, and on a bowl only the bottom can stop it. Convexity makes both the feasible set and the objective bowl-shaped.

> [!definition] Convex set and convex function
> A set $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathbb R^N$ is **convex** if it contains the segment between any two of its points. A function $h$ on a convex set $\mathcal C$ is **convex** if every chord lies on or above its graph. For all $\mathbf u,\mathbf v\in\mathcal C$ and $\omega\in[0,1]$:
> $$\begin{gathered}\omega\mathbf u+(1-\omega)\mathbf v\in\mathcal C,\\ h\big(\omega\mathbf u+(1-\omega)\mathbf v\big)\\ \le\omega h(\mathbf u)+(1-\omega)h(\mathbf v).\end{gathered} \tag{29.1}$$
> It is **strictly convex** if the inequality is strict whenever $\mathbf u\ne\mathbf v$ and $0<\omega<1$.

[[LM4]]'s definition is the case $N=1$, and [[LM10]]'s (10.10) shows that a quadratic with a PSD matrix satisfies (29.1). The budget hyperplane, the long-only orthant $\mathbf w\ge\mathbf 0$, a cap's half-space $w_i\le b_i$ and the gross ball $\lVert\mathbf w\rVert_1\le1.5$ are convex, and so is any intersection of convex sets.

> [!theorem] Hessian test and gradient inequality
> Let $h$ be twice differentiable on a convex open set $\mathcal C$. Then $h$ is convex exactly when its Hessian $H(\mathbf u)$ is PSD at every $\mathbf u\in\mathcal C$, and strictly convex if $H(\mathbf u)$ is PD everywhere. A differentiable convex $h$ satisfies, for all $\mathbf u,\mathbf v\in\mathcal C$,
> $$h(\mathbf v)\ge h(\mathbf u)+\nabla h(\mathbf u)^{\top}(\mathbf v-\mathbf u). \tag{29.2}$$

*Proof.* Inequality (29.1) involves only the points of one segment, so $h$ is convex exactly when each restriction $\omega\mapsto h(\mathbf u+\omega(\mathbf v-\mathbf u))$ is. By the chain rule its second derivative is $(\mathbf v-\mathbf u)^{\top}H(\cdot)(\mathbf v-\mathbf u)$. [[LM4]]'s one-variable test, stated there without proof, makes the restriction convex exactly when that derivative is $\ge0$. Since $\mathcal C$ is open, $\mathbf v-\mathbf u$ takes every direction, so the condition is $H$ PSD everywhere; PD gives strict convexity. $\blacksquare$

> [!deepdive]- The gradient inequality
> Let $h$ be differentiable and convex, and apply (29.1) at $\mathbf u+\omega(\mathbf v-\mathbf u)$, $0<\omega\le1$:
> $$\begin{gathered}\frac{h\big(\mathbf u+\omega(\mathbf v-\mathbf u)\big)-h(\mathbf u)}{\omega}\\ \le h(\mathbf v)-h(\mathbf u).\end{gathered}$$
> As $\omega\to0$ the left side tends to $\nabla h(\mathbf u)^{\top}(\mathbf v-\mathbf u)$, which gives (29.2) (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, §3.1.3, p. 69). For a quadratic it is (10.9) with $\boldsymbol\Delta^{\top}M\boldsymbol\Delta\ge0$.

Sums with non-negative weights and pointwise maxima of convex functions are convex: the maximum of chords lies above the maximum of the functions (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, §3.2.3, p. 80). So $\lvert x\rvert=\max(x,-x)$, $\max(0,-x)$ and $\lVert\mathbf w\rVert_1$ are convex, though none has a derivative at 0.

> [!example] Which portfolio sets are convex? {#sets}
> On the last window, show that long-only portfolios with a 40% cap, gross exposure at most 150% and volatility at most 25% form a convex set. Show that a volatility floor and "at most three funds" do not.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The budget, the orthant and the caps are linear, and the gross ball is convex by the triangle inequality. The variance has the PD Hessian $2\Sigma$, so it is strictly convex by (29.2), and the ellipsoid $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w\le(25\%)^2$ and the intersection are convex.
> >
> > A floor $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w\ge0.01$ alone holds $\mathbf w$ and $-\mathbf w$ but not their midpoint $\mathbf 0$. A third each in 0050, 00646 and 00679B, and a third each in 00679B, 00635U and 00642U, average to a portfolio of all five funds.

> [!theorem] Convex problems have no false minima
> Let $h$ and every $\psi_i$ be convex on $\mathbb R^N$, and consider
> $$\begin{gathered}\min_{\mathbf w}\ h(\mathbf w)\quad\text{subject to}\\ \psi_i(\mathbf w)\le b_i\ \text{for each }i,\quad K\mathbf w=\mathbf b.\end{gathered} \tag{29.3}$$
> Its feasible set is convex, every local minimum is a global minimum (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, §4.2.2, p. 138), and a strictly convex $h$ has at most one minimizer.

*Proof.* Each set $\{\psi_i\le b_i\}$ is convex by (29.1). If a local minimum $\mathbf w^*$ had a feasible $\mathbf v$ with $h(\mathbf v)<h(\mathbf w^*)$, the feasible points $\mathbf w^*+\omega(\mathbf v-\mathbf w^*)$, $0<\omega\le1$, would have lower values by (29.1), arbitrarily close to $\mathbf w^*$. Two minimizers of a strictly convex $h$ would have a midpoint of lower value. $\blacksquare$

By (29.2), a point where $\nabla h=\mathbf 0$ is also a global minimum. Theorem (29.3) does not say that a minimum exists: each optimum here is proved by exhibiting a KKT point (Section 4).

> [!zh] 凸性讓局部最小就是全域最小
> 凸集（convex set）包含任兩點之間的線段；凸函數（convex function）的弦永遠在圖形上方，也就是凸組合（convex combination）的函數值不超過兩端函數值的同樣加權平均。檢查方法有三種：海森矩陣（Hessian）處處半正定（positive semidefinite）、凸函數以非負權重相加、凸函數取最大值，例如絕對值與交易成本。[[LM10]] 證明二次函數是凸函數若且唯若矩陣半正定，本節把同樣的結論推廣到一般函數。凸問題的局部最小一定是全域最小，嚴格凸函數（strictly convex function）最多只有一個最小解，所以往下走的 solver 不會被假的谷底困住。

> Show that [[LM27]]'s cost of a trade, $\sum_i\big[0.1425\%\,\lvert\Delta_i\rvert+\text{tax}_i\max(0,-\Delta_i)\big]$ with $\text{tax}_i\ge0$, is convex, using only sums and maxima. Why is "at most three funds" not convex?

## Quadratic and Linear Programs {lo=2}

Most portfolio limits are linear, and the variance is a convex quadratic.

> [!definition] Quadratic and linear programs
> A **quadratic program** (QP) minimizes $\mathbf w^{\top}M\mathbf w$ plus a linear term, with $M$ PSD, subject to linear equalities $K\mathbf w=\mathbf b$ and linear inequalities $\psi_i(\mathbf w)\le b_i$. A **linear program** (LP) has $M=0$ (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, (4.34), p. 152).

Each limit is a row: the budget a row of $K$, long-only $-w_i\le0$, a 40% cap $w_i\le0.40$, and "at least 10% in 00679B" $-w_3\le-0.10$. R3 with a cap is the QP

$$\begin{gathered}\min_{\mathbf w}\ \mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w\quad\text{subject to}\\ \mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w=1,\quad -w_i\le0,\quad w_i\le0.40.\end{gathered}$$

A gross limit $\lVert\mathbf w\rVert_1\le1.5$ becomes linear after a split ([[LM8]]'s gross exposure): $\mathbf w=\mathbf w^{+}-\mathbf w^{-}$, $\mathbf w^{\pm}\ge\mathbf 0$ and $\mathbf 1^{\top}(\mathbf w^{+}+\mathbf w^{-})\le1.5$. The positive and negative parts of $\mathbf w$ are one split, and any split has $\mathbf 1^{\top}(\mathbf w^{+}+\mathbf w^{-})\ge\lVert\mathbf w\rVert_1$. A turnover limit splits the same way.

A limit on volatility, such as $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w\le(25\%)^2$, is a second-order cone constraint. cvxpy sends such problems to a conic solver, and this module does not develop cone programming.

> [!example] A linear program: the highest mean under caps {#lp}
> On the last window, maximize $\boldsymbol\mu^{\top}\mathbf w$ subject to $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w=1$ and $0\le w_i\le40\%$. The three highest means are 48.775% (0050), 36.096% (00642U) and 20.678% (00635U).
>
> > [!solution]
> > The candidate $\mathbf w^*$ holds 40% in 0050 and 00642U and 20% in 00635U, a mean of $0.4\times48.775+0.4\times36.096+0.2\times20.678=38.08\%$. Both portfolios sum to 1, so
> > $$\begin{gathered}\boldsymbol\mu^{\top}\mathbf w-\boldsymbol\mu^{\top}\mathbf w^*\\ =\sum_i(w_i-w^*_i)(\mu_i-20.678\%).\end{gathered}$$
> > Every term is $\le0$: the top two funds have $w_i\le40\%=w^*_i$ and higher means, the third fund's term is 0, and the other two have $w_i\ge0=w^*_i$ and lower means. The means differ, so equality needs $\mathbf w^*$'s weights in four funds, and the budget fixes the fifth: the optimum is unique. A linear objective concentrates; only a risk term diversifies.

> [!example] Infeasible, unbounded and not convex {#status}
> On the last window, give cvxpy three problems: minimum variance with a 15% cap on each fund; the highest mean with only the budget; and a volatility floor $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w\ge0.01$ alone.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The caps sum to 75%: `infeasible`, with `prob.value` `inf` and `w.value` `None`. With only the budget, buying 0050 against a short in 00679B raises the mean without limit: `unbounded`, again with `prob.value` `inf` and `w.value` `None`. The floor alone is not convex: cvxpy raises a `DCPError`.
> >
> > With the budget added, the floor would exclude nothing: every fully invested portfolio has a variance of at least R3's 0.0102, because R3's bounds do not bind (Section 4).

> [!pitfall] The status is part of the answer
> `infeasible`, `unbounded` and `optimal_inaccurate` are answers, and `Variable.value` is `None` when there is no solution; a `DCPError` means the problem as written is not convex. Read the status before the weights.

> [!kc]
> Write "at most 40% in any fund, at least 10% in 00679B, gross exposure at most 150%" as linear rows. Why is a 15% cap on each of five funds infeasible?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > With $\mathbf w=\mathbf w^{+}-\mathbf w^{-}$ and $\mathbf w^{\pm}\ge\mathbf 0$, the rows are $w_i\le0.40$, $-w_3\le-0.10$ and $\mathbf 1^{\top}(\mathbf w^{+}+\mathbf w^{-})\le1.5$. Five caps of 15% allow 75% in all, so the budget cannot hold.

## KKT Conditions for Long-Only Portfolios {lo=3,8}

[[LM7]]'s (7.13) states the KKT conditions for a maximization, with an equality in the Lagrangian as $-\lambda(\psi-b)$ and a multiplier $\nu_i\ge0$ for each inequality. Applied to $-h$, it gives the form for a minimization.

> [!theorem] KKT conditions for a minimization
> Let $h$ and every $\psi_i$ be differentiable. Minimize $h(\mathbf w)$ subject to $\psi_i(\mathbf w)\le b_i$ for each $i$ and $K\mathbf w=\mathbf b$. A point $\mathbf w^*$ with multipliers $\boldsymbol\lambda$ and $\nu_i$ is a **KKT point** if
> $$\begin{gathered}\nabla h(\mathbf w^*)=K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda-\sum_i\nu_i\nabla\psi_i(\mathbf w^*),\\ K\mathbf w^*=\mathbf b,\qquad\psi_i(\mathbf w^*)\le b_i,\\ \nu_i\ge0,\qquad\nu_i\big(b_i-\psi_i(\mathbf w^*)\big)=0.\end{gathered} \tag{29.4}$$
> If $h$ is convex and every $\psi_i$ is linear, a KKT point is a global minimum.

A bound $w_i\ge0$, written $-w_i\le0$, enters stationarity as $+\nu_i$, and a cap $w_i\le b_i$ as $-\nu^{\mathrm{cap}}_i$.

*Proof of sufficiency.* Let $\mathbf w$ be feasible. By (29.2) and stationarity,
$$\begin{aligned}h(\mathbf w)&\ge h(\mathbf w^*)+\boldsymbol\lambda^{\top}K(\mathbf w-\mathbf w^*)\\&\quad-\sum_i\nu_i\nabla\psi_i(\mathbf w^*)^{\top}(\mathbf w-\mathbf w^*).\end{aligned}$$
Here $K(\mathbf w-\mathbf w^*)=\mathbf 0$, and for linear $\psi_i$ the last product is $\psi_i(\mathbf w)-\psi_i(\mathbf w^*)$. Complementary slackness turns $\nu_i\psi_i(\mathbf w^*)$ into $\nu_ib_i$, so each term is $\nu_i(\psi_i(\mathbf w)-b_i)\le0$, and $h(\mathbf w)\ge h(\mathbf w^*)$. $\blacksquare$

Boyd and Vandenberghe (2004, §5.5.3, p. 244) state sufficiency for every convex problem (29.3). The converse, that every minimum is a KKT point, needs a **constraint qualification**. For convex problems they prove it under Slater's condition, which for linear constraints is feasibility alone (p. 227), stated here without proof. [[LM7]]'s independent binding gradients are another qualification; in a non-convex problem a KKT point need not be a minimum.

So with linear constraints the optima are exactly the KKT points. In [[eg:lp]], as $\min-\boldsymbol\mu^{\top}\mathbf w$, the budget's multiplier is $\lambda=-20.678\%$; the caps' multipliers $\mu_i-20.678\%$ are 28.097 and 15.418 points, and the bounds' $20.678\%-\mu_i$ are 2.741 and 26.454, all non-negative.

**Long-only minimum variance.** Minimize $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w$ subject to $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w=1$ and $\mathbf w\ge\mathbf 0$. Stationarity is $2\Sigma\mathbf w^*=\lambda\mathbf 1+\boldsymbol\nu$. Multiplying by $\mathbf w^{*\top}$, the budget leaves $\lambda$ and complementary slackness removes $\boldsymbol\nu$, so $\lambda=2\sigma_p^2$. With (27.5), $(\Sigma\mathbf w^*)_i=\Cov(R_i,R_p)$:

$$\begin{gathered}2\Sigma\mathbf w^*=\lambda\mathbf 1+\boldsymbol\nu,\quad\boldsymbol\nu\ge\mathbf 0,\quad\nu_iw^*_i=0,\\ \lambda=2\sigma_p^2,\qquad\nu_i=2\big[\Cov(R_i,R_p)-\sigma_p^2\big],\\ \beta_{i,p}=1+\nu_i/\lambda.\end{gathered} \tag{29.5}$$

Every held fund has $\beta_{i,p}=1$ and every excluded fund $\beta_{i,p}\ge1$. A little more of a held fund leaves the variance unchanged to first order; an excluded fund would raise it. Constraining weights also acts as a shrinkage of $\hat\Sigma$, which [[LM30]] studies (Jagannathan and Ma 2003).

On E5, R3's bounds never bind: over the 92 signals its smallest weight is 0.13% (0050, 2024-11-29), so R3 equals [[LM28]]'s $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}$ at every signal. An active set needs a larger universe.

> [!zh] KKT 條件：誰被排除、為什麼
> 不可放空（long-only）的最小變異數問題，每個權重下限 $w_i\ge0$ 只有兩種狀態：鬆弛，乘數為 0；緊，乘數 $\nu_i\ge0$，也就是有效限制（active constraint）。KKT 條件說，被持有的基金對投資組合的 beta 都等於 1，被排除的都至少是 1；互補鬆弛性（complementary slackness）保證乘數與權重至少一個是 0。乘數就是影子價格（shadow price）：放寬那條限制時，變異數下降的速率。對凸問題，KKT 條件是充分條件；限制條件都是線性時，約束規範（constraint qualification）自動成立，所以也是必要條件。"active set" 本書保留英文。

## R3 and R4 in the Through Case {lo=4,5}

R3 is (29.5) on each window's $\Sigma$, solved exactly by `vol5_rules.py` (a Cholesky factor and `nnls`) and checked by its KKT conditions. R4 maximizes a ratio, which a change of variables turns into a QP.

The Sharpe ratio $\mathbf e^{\top}\mathbf w/\sqrt{\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w}$ does not change when $\mathbf w$ is scaled by a positive number. Suppose some $e_i>0$, so that the best ratio is positive. For a long-only $\mathbf w$ with $\mathbf e^{\top}\mathbf w>0$, set $\mathbf y=\mathbf w/\mathbf e^{\top}\mathbf w$; then $\mathbf e^{\top}\mathbf y=1$ and the ratio is $1/\sqrt{\mathbf y^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf y}$. Maximizing it is minimizing $\mathbf y^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf y$, by Boyd and Vandenberghe's change of variables (2004, §4.3.2, p. 151):

$$\begin{gathered}\mathbf y^*\ \text{minimizes}\ \mathbf y^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf y\\ \text{subject to}\ \mathbf e^{\top}\mathbf y=1,\ \mathbf y\ge\mathbf 0,\\ \mathbf w^*=\mathbf y^*/\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf y^*,\\ \mathrm{SR}^*=\big(\mathbf y^{*\top}\Sigma\mathbf y^*\big)^{-1/2},\qquad\lambda=2/\mathrm{SR}^{*2}.\end{gathered} \tag{29.6}$$

Stationarity is $2\Sigma\mathbf y^*=\lambda\mathbf e+\boldsymbol\nu$, and multiplying by $\mathbf y^{*\top}$ gives $\lambda=2\mathbf y^{*\top}\Sigma\mathbf y^*$. Substituting $\mathbf y^*=\mathbf w^*/(\mu_p-r_f)$, since $\mathbf e^{\top}\mathbf w^*=\mu_p-r_f$, and dividing by $\lambda$,

$$\begin{gathered}\frac{\nu_i}{\lambda}=\beta_{i,p}(\mu_p-r_f)-e_i\ge0,\\ \text{with equality for held funds}.\end{gathered} \tag{29.7}$$

A held fund earns exactly its beta to the portfolio times the portfolio's excess return; an excluded fund earns less, by $\nu_i/\lambda$. A positive excess return is not enough.

A multiplier also prices its limit. Let $J(b_i)$ be the optimal value as one level $b_i$ moves.

> [!result] Multipliers are shadow prices
> For a convex problem (29.3) with linear constraints and KKT multipliers $\nu_i$,
> $$\begin{gathered}\frac{\partial J}{\partial b_i}=-\nu_i\ \text{ where }J\text{ is}\\ \text{differentiable, and}\\ J(b_i+\Delta)\ge J(b_i)-\nu_i\Delta\ \text{ for every }\Delta.\end{gathered} \tag{29.8}$$
> Stated without proof. The derivative is Boyd and Vandenberghe's local sensitivity (2004, §5.6.3, p. 251); the bound is their global inequality (5.57), p. 250, valid by the refined Slater condition (p. 227).

## Costs in the Objective {lo=6}

[[LM27]]'s cost of a trade $\boldsymbol\Delta$, a fraction of pre-trade wealth, is 0.1425% of $\lVert\boldsymbol\Delta\rVert_1$ plus a 0.1% tax on sales, none for 00679B ((27.2); [[LM34]] prints the sources). In the objective:

$$\begin{gathered}\min_{\mathbf w}\ \mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w+\theta\operatorname{cost}\big(\mathbf w-\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t\big)\\ \text{subject to}\quad\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w=1,\quad\mathbf w\ge\mathbf 0.\end{gathered} \tag{29.9}$$

The cost is a sum of maxima (Section 2), so for $\theta\ge0$ the problem is convex. The weight $\theta$ is annual variance per unit of wealth paid once. In [[LM28]]'s exposure form, a holding period of $\tau$ years trades $\tfrac{\delta\tau}{2}\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w$ against a cost paid once, so $\theta=2/(\delta\tau)$; $\delta=4$ with monthly trading, $\tau=1/12$, gives $\theta=6$.

Splitting each trade into buys and sells, $\mathbf w=\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t+\boldsymbol\Delta^{+}-\boldsymbol\Delta^{-}$, makes the cost linear (Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004, p. 156). The problem is then a QP, and (29.4) gives, for a fund still held after the trade,

$$\begin{gathered}\text{bought: }(2\Sigma\mathbf w)_i=\lambda-\theta\,0.1425\%,\\ \text{sold: }(2\Sigma\mathbf w)_i=\lambda+\theta\,(0.1425\%+\text{tax}_i).\end{gathered}$$

An untouched fund lies in the **no-trade band** between the two. cvxpy receives the cost as written, with `norm1` and `pos`.

> Let every drifted weight be positive. Then $\mathbf w=\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t$ satisfies (29.9)'s KKT conditions exactly when one $\lambda$ fits every band:
> $$\begin{gathered}(2\Sigma\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t)_i-\theta(0.1425\%+\text{tax}_i)\\ \le\lambda\le(2\Sigma\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t)_j+\theta\,0.1425\%\\ \text{for all }i,j.\end{gathered}$$

## Solvers and How to Check Them {lo=7,8}

cvxpy is a modelling language for convex problems, embedded in Python (Diamond and Boyd 2016). A problem is built from convex atoms such as `quad_form`, `norm1` and `pos`, and cvxpy refuses one whose convexity its rules cannot verify. It canonicalizes the others into the standard form of a solver it selects (Agrawal et al. 2018).

In the installed cvxpy 1.9.3 the default solver is OSQP 1.1.3 for a QP, such as R3 or (29.9), and CLARABEL 0.11.1 for an LP or a volatility limit. OSQP solves a quadratic program by operator splitting and CLARABEL by an interior-point method; neither method is derived here (Stellato et al. 2020; Goulart and Chen 2024).

**Scaling.** A solver stops when its residuals and gaps fall below tolerances, some of them absolute. On daily inputs R3's optimal variance at the first signal is $1.035\times10^{-5}$, so CLARABEL's default absolute gap, $10^{-8}$, is about 0.1% of it. On annual inputs the variance is $2.515\times10^{-3}$, $A$ times larger, and the gap is 0.0004% of it.

The condition number, 9.72 to 20.46 over the 92 windows, does not change with the units, so by (11.9) an exact solve is as accurate in either. The exact solver uses the Cholesky factor, whose condition number is $\sqrt{\kappa(\Sigma)}$ by (12.6).

**Tolerances.** The book sets OSQP's `eps_abs` and `eps_rel` to $10^{-10}$ with polishing, and CLARABEL's `tol_gap_abs`, `tol_gap_rel` and `tol_feas` to $10^{-12}$ for QPs and LPs, $10^{-10}$ for cone programs (Problem 11). cvxpy's defaults are OSQP's $10^{-5}$ with 10,000 iterations and polishing, and CLARABEL's $10^{-8}$ with 200. Every printed solution comes from a fresh `Problem`, ends `optimal` and passes [[eg:check]]'s checks.

> On daily inputs CLARABEL reports `optimal` for R3 and still misses its weights by up to $2.1\times10^{-3}$: the residuals met the tolerances, but the printed digits are wrong. Give solvers annual inputs, set the tolerances and check the KKT residuals.

> [!zh] 先看狀態，再看權重
> solver 回報的 "optimal" 只表示它的殘差（residual）小於容許誤差（tolerance），而有些容許誤差是絕對值：用日資料時，R3 的目標函數（objective function）只有 $1.035\times10^{-5}$，預設的 $10^{-8}$ 就大約是它的千分之一，權重可能偏離 $2.1\times10^{-3}$。改用年化資料，同一個容許誤差相對小得多。檢查的順序是：先看狀態，再看每條限制條件（constraint）是否滿足、KKT 殘差、第二個 solver，最後與已知的公式解（closed form）比對。cvxpy 回報的對偶變量（dual variable）就是 KKT 條件的乘數，但等式的符號要先用公式解核對。OSQP 用 operator splitting，CLARABEL 用內點法（interior-point method），本書都不推導。

## Convex Optimization in Python {lo=7}

```python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import cholesky, solve_triangular
from scipy.optimize import nnls

BOOK = {"tol_gap_abs": 1e-12, "tol_gap_rel": 1e-12, "tol_feas": 1e-12}


def qp(S, a):                       # min y'Sy, a'y = 1, y >= 0 in cvxpy
    y = cp.Variable(len(a))
    con = a @ y == 1
    prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(cp.quad_form(y, cp.psd_wrap(S))),
                      [con, y >= 0])
    prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, **BOOK)    # a fresh Problem
    assert prob.status == "optimal" and y.value is not None
    return y.value, -float(con.dual_value)    # cvxpy's dual is -lambda


def exact(S, a):                    # the same problem by nnls
    R = cholesky(S)                 # S = R'R, R upper triangular
    z, _ = nnls(R, solve_triangular(R, a, trans="T"))
    return z / (a @ z)


def kkt(S, a, y):                   # residuals of (29.5) and (29.6)
    lam = 2 * y @ S @ y
    nu = 2 * S @ y - lam * a
    held = y > 1e-6
    return np.abs(nu[held]).max(), nu.min(), np.abs(nu * y).max()

# R3: a = np.ones(5); R4: a = e; the weights are y / y.sum().
# nnls minimizes |R z - R^-T a| over z >= 0, which is z'Sz - 2a'z plus a
# constant; then y = z / a'z. psd_wrap declares S PSD without testing it,
# so pass only a matrix known to be positive definite.
```

## Summary {-}

- A convex problem (29.3) has no false minima; test convexity by the Hessian (29.2), by sums and by maxima.
- With linear constraints the KKT conditions (29.4) are necessary and sufficient; long-only minimum variance holds funds of beta 1, (29.5).
- R4 is the QP (29.6); a fund is held only if its excess return pays for its beta, (29.7).
- A multiplier is a shadow price (29.8), whose linear prediction is the most a relaxation gains.
- Costs in the objective (29.9) create a no-trade band; $\theta=2/(\delta\tau)$.
- Give solvers annual inputs and set tolerances; read the status, then the KKT residuals.

1. For a positive definite $\Sigma$, the set $\{\mathbf w\in\mathbb R^N:\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w\ge0.01\}$ is:
   - **A.** convex, because $\Sigma$ is positive definite

   (b) Stationarity is $2\Sigma\mathbf w=\lambda\mathbf 1+\boldsymbol\nu-\boldsymbol\nu^{\mathrm{cap}}$. ## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Convex set | 凸集 | A set containing the segment between any two of its points, (29.1). |
| Convex function | 凸函數 | A function whose chords lie on or above its graph, (29.1). |
| Strictly convex function | 嚴格凸函數 | A convex function whose chords lie strictly above the graph between distinct points. |
| Convex combination | 凸組合 | $\omega\mathbf u+(1-\omega)\mathbf v$ with $\omega\in[0,1]$; more generally, a mix with non-negative weights summing to 1. |
| Convex program | 凸規劃 | Minimizing a convex function subject to convex $\psi_i\le b_i$ and $K\mathbf w=\mathbf b$, (29.3). |
| Quadratic program | 二次規劃 | A convex quadratic objective with linear constraints. |
| Linear program | 線性規劃 | A linear objective with linear constraints. |
| Feasible set | 可行解區域 | The points that satisfy every constraint. |
| KKT conditions | KKT 條件 | Stationarity, feasibility, non-negative multipliers and complementary slackness, (29.4). |
| Complementary slackness | 互補鬆弛性 | Each inequality binds or has a zero multiplier. |
| Constraint qualification | 約束規範 | A condition on the constraints under which every minimum is a KKT point; linear constraints satisfy one. |
| Active constraint | 有效限制 | An inequality that holds with equality at the optimum; the active set lists them. |
| Shadow price | 影子價格 | The rate $\nu_i=-\partial J/\partial b_i$ at which relaxing a limit improves the optimal value, (29.8). |
| Homogenized QP | — | R4 as $\min\mathbf y^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf y$ subject to $\mathbf e^{\top}\mathbf y=1$, $\mathbf y\ge\mathbf 0$, (29.6). |
| Penalty function | 懲罰函數 | A term added to the objective in place of a limit, such as $\theta\operatorname{cost}$ in (29.9). |
| No-trade band | — | The range of a fund's variance gradient in which a cost-penalized trade leaves the fund untouched. |
| Dual variable | 對偶變量 | A solver's multiplier of a constraint; cvxpy's `dual_value`. |
| Interior-point method | 內點法 | A method that approaches the optimum from inside the feasible set (CLARABEL). |
| Solver status | — | The solver's verdict (`optimal`, `optimal_inaccurate`, `infeasible`, `unbounded`), read before its answer. |
