## Introduction

[[LM20]] gave each estimate a standard error by formula, for independent days and a smooth statistic. The **bootstrap** reads the error from the data instead. It recomputes the estimate on [[LM18]]'s resamples. This module turns their spread into intervals and tests, and shows where resampling single days fails.

> [!case] How wide is a Kelly fraction?
> From 3 January 2003 to 8 October 2026 the TAIEX total-return index gave 5,849 daily returns, with mean 0.062505% and standard deviation 1.2084%. With no risk-free rate its Sharpe ratio is 0.81 a year and its volatility 18.96% a year, both by [[LM19]]'s $\sqrt A$ convention. Its Kelly fraction $\hat f=\bar R/s^2$ is 4.28 ([[LM20]]).
>
> Draw the days again at random, 10,000 times, and recompute each number. Of the resampled Kelly fractions, 95% lie between 2.2 and 6.5, like [[LM4]]'s 2.7 to 8.6 for 0050's $\hat\mu/s^2$. Blocks of about 145 days, which keep calm and turbulent stretches together, widen the Kelly range only to 1.8 to 7.1.
>
> Yet the volatility's range, 18.3% to 19.6% from single days, becomes 16.5% to 21.5% with blocks, almost four times as wide ([[ex:case]]). Which range is an interval for TAIEX, and why do clusters matter so much more for one number?

## The Bootstrap Principle {lo=1}

An estimate $\hat\theta=h(x_1,\dots,x_T)$ varies from sample to sample. Its **sampling distribution** is its law when the $T$ observations come from the unknown distribution $F$, and its standard error is that law's standard deviation. One sample shows one draw, and [[LM20]] approximated the law by formulas such as (20.8).

The **plug-in principle** replaces $F$ by $\hat F$, mass $1/T$ on each observation, (13.12) (Efron and Tibshirani 1993, ch. 4). A **resample** is $T$ iid draws from $\hat F$ ([[LM18]]), and $\hat\theta_{\text{boot}}$ is the statistic on it. The **bootstrap principle**: $\hat\theta_{\text{boot}}$ varies under $\hat F$ roughly as $\hat\theta$ varies under $F$.

The $T^T$ ordered resamples are equally likely, and the **ideal bootstrap** averages over all of them. For $T=5{,}849$ that is impossible, so $B$ random resamples approximate it, with [[LM18]]'s Monte Carlo error.

> [!definition] Bootstrap standard error
> Let $\hat\theta_{\text{boot},1},\dots,\hat\theta_{\text{boot},B}$ be the statistic on $B$ resamples from the resampling model (iid days here, blocks from Section 5), and $\bar\theta_{\text{boot}}$ their mean. The **bootstrap standard error** is
> $$\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}_{\text{boot}}=\Big(\frac1B\sum_{i=1}^B\big(\hat\theta_{\text{boot},i}-\bar\theta_{\text{boot}}\big)^2\Big)^{1/2}. \tag{22.1}$$
> As $B\to\infty$ it tends to the ideal bootstrap's standard error, by the strong law ([[LM18]]).

Efron and Tibshirani (1993, §6.2) divide by $B-1$ in their equation 6.6; the factor $\sqrt{(B-1)/B}$ is 0.99995 at $B=10{,}000$.

> [!example] The ideal bootstrap of five returns {#five}
> Take [[LM18]]'s returns +1.2%, −0.8%, +0.3%, −2.5% and +0.6% ($T=5$). Find the ideal bootstrap standard errors of their mean and median, then estimate both from $B=1{,}000$ resamples of `default_rng([1, 20261011])`.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The mean is −0.24% and $s=1.3032\%$. The $5^5=3{,}125$ ordered resamples are equally likely, so by (18.8) the resample mean has standard deviation $s/\sqrt5=0.583\%$ exactly.
> >
> > The resample median $\hat F^{-1}(\tfrac12)$ is the third smallest draw. It equals −2.5% or +1.2% with probability 181/3,125 each, −0.8% or +0.6% with 811/3,125 each, and +0.3% with 1,141/3,125, so its ideal standard error is 0.859%. Experiment 1 gives $0.59\%\pm0.01\%$ and $0.87\%\pm0.02\%$ (batch standard errors).

**Intervals.** Suppose that the error $\hat\theta-\theta$ under $F$ has about the law of $\hat\theta_{\text{boot}}-\hat\theta$ under $\hat F$. Then $\hat\theta-\theta$ lies between the $\tfrac\alpha2$ and $1-\tfrac\alpha2$ quantiles of $\hat\theta_{\text{boot}}-\hat\theta$ with probability about $1-\alpha$. Solving for $\theta$ gives the basic interval.

> [!definition] Percentile and basic intervals
> Let $\hat F_{\text{boot}}^{-1}(u)=\hat\theta_{\text{boot},(\lceil uB\rceil)}$ be the quantile function of the $B$ resampled values ([[LM14]]). The $1-\alpha$ **percentile interval** and **basic interval** are
> $$\begin{gathered}\big[\hat F_{\text{boot}}^{-1}(\tfrac\alpha2),\ \hat F_{\text{boot}}^{-1}(1-\tfrac\alpha2)\big],\\ \big[2\hat\theta-\hat F_{\text{boot}}^{-1}(1-\tfrac\alpha2),\ 2\hat\theta-\hat F_{\text{boot}}^{-1}(\tfrac\alpha2)\big].\end{gathered} \tag{22.2}$$
> At $B=10{,}000$ and $\alpha=5\%$ the ends are the 250th and 9,750th resampled values.

Both intervals are approximate (Efron and Tibshirani 1993, ch. 13, for the percentile interval). Stated without proof: their coverage tends to $1-\alpha$ for iid data and a smooth function of sample means with finite variances, such as $\bar R/s$. For $\bar R/s$ one mean is of $R_t^2$, whose variance needs a finite fourth moment; [[LM15]]'s $t_{3.065}$ fit lacks it, so TR23's intervals rest on practice. Deep Dive (a) shows when the percentile interval is exact.

**BCa** adjusts the percentile interval for bias, measured by the share of resamples below $\hat\theta$, and for skewness, measured by a jackknife. A jackknife recomputes the statistic with each day left out once. BCa's adjustments are stated here without proof (Efron 1987). This book does not use the parametric bootstrap (Wasserman 2004, §9.11), subsampling (Lahiri 2003, §7.3), bootstrap-t intervals or the wild bootstrap.

> [!example] An interval for TAIEX's Sharpe ratio {#sharpe}
> Resample TR23's days iid, $B=10{,}000$ times with `default_rng([2, 20261011])`. Give the annual Sharpe ratio's bootstrap standard error and 95% intervals, and compare them with [[LM20]]'s delta method.
>
> > [!solution]
> > Experiment 2 draws `integers(0, T, size=(500, T))` twenty times. The standard error is 0.205 a year. The percentile and basic intervals both run from 0.41 to 1.21, since the values are nearly symmetric, and SciPy's BCa interval is 0.42 to 1.22.
> >
> > [[LM20]]'s delta method gives 0.207 with the sample's skewness and kurtosis, 0.205 for normal returns, and the interval 0.40 to 1.22. All agree because the daily ratio, 0.0517, is small: its standard error is close to $1/\sqrt T=0.0131$ a day.

> [!deepdive]- (a) When the percentile interval is exact
> Here $\psi$ is a strictly increasing function and $\sigma_\psi>0$ a constant. Suppose that $\psi(\hat\theta)-\psi(\theta)$ under $F$ and $\psi(\hat\theta_{\text{boot}})-\psi(\hat\theta)$ under $\hat F$ both follow $\mathcal N(0,\sigma_\psi^2)$. Since $\psi$ is increasing, the $u$ quantile of $\hat\theta_{\text{boot}}$ is $\psi^{-1}\big(\psi(\hat\theta)+\sigma_\psi\Phi^{-1}(u)\big)$. The ideal percentile interval therefore runs from
> $$\begin{gathered}\psi^{-1}\big(\psi(\hat\theta)-\Phi^{-1}(1-\tfrac\alpha2)\,\sigma_\psi\big)\quad\text{to}\\ \psi^{-1}\big(\psi(\hat\theta)+\Phi^{-1}(1-\tfrac\alpha2)\,\sigma_\psi\big).\end{gathered}$$
> Also, $\psi(\theta)$ lies within $\Phi^{-1}(1-\tfrac\alpha2)\,\sigma_\psi$ of $\psi(\hat\theta)$ with probability exactly $1-\alpha$. Applying $\psi^{-1}$ gives an exact interval for $\theta$, the same one, found without knowing $\psi$ or $\sigma_\psi$. The basic interval is exact instead when $\hat\theta_{\text{boot}}-\hat\theta$ has the law of $\hat\theta-\theta$. $\blacksquare$

> [!zh] 自助重抽法：用樣本代替母體
> 估計量（estimator）的抽樣分布（sampling distribution）要靠許多組樣本才看得到，我們卻只有一組。自助重抽法（bootstrap）用重抽樣（resampling）解決這件事：把經驗分布（empirical distribution）$\hat F$ 當成母體（population），從中放回抽取 $T$ 天，重算統計量（statistic）$B$ 次，以這些值的分布代替抽樣分布。它們的標準差（standard deviation）就是標準誤（standard error）的估計；$B=10{,}000$ 時取第 250 小與第 9,750 小的值，就得到 95% 的百分位數信賴區間（percentile confidence interval），以 $2\hat\theta$ 減去這兩端，則是基本信賴區間（basic interval）。偏誤加速校正之自助重抽信賴區間（BCa）再修正偏誤（bias）與偏態（skewness），是 SciPy 的預設。要記得：$B$ 越大只是模擬誤差（Monte Carlo error）越小，信賴區間（confidence interval）的寬度由樣本長度 $T$ 與第 5 節的相依性（dependence）決定，與 $B$ 無關。

> [!kc]
> Why can more resamples not bring [[eg:five]]'s bootstrap standard error of the mean below 0.583%?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > The number of resamples governs the Monte Carlo error only. As $B\to\infty$, (22.1) tends to the ideal value $s/\sqrt5=0.583\%$, the sampling error of a mean of five returns under $\hat F$. Only a longer sample shrinks it.

## Intervals for the Sharpe Ratio and the Kelly Fraction {lo=2}

The bootstrap needs no derivative. It recomputes the Kelly fraction $\hat f=\bar R/s^2$ ([[LM20]]) on each resample, and the exact optimum $\hat f^*$ by solving [[LM4]]'s (4.5) on each one.

>
> > [!solution]
> > For $\hat f=4.28$ the iid resamples give a standard error of 1.10, the percentile interval 2.2 to 6.5, the basic interval 2.1 to 6.4 and SciPy's BCa interval 2.2 to 6.5. [[LM20]]'s delta method gives 1.111 with plug-in skewness and kurtosis (1.085 for normal returns, 1.082 from the mean alone) and the interval 2.10 to 6.46.

**The relative error.** Most of the error of $\hat f$ is the error of $\bar R$. By (20.2) its relative error is $(s/\sqrt T)/\bar R=1/(\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}\sqrt T)$, 25.3% here, and 1.96 such errors make about ±50%. The annual Sharpe ratio's interval is the daily one times $\sqrt A$, a convention on simple returns, exact only for log returns on uncorrelated days ([[LM19]]; Lo 2002).

[[LM7]] used an illustrative $r_f=1.5\%$ for 0050, while Volume IV sets $r_f=0$: FinMind has no Taiwan rate series. Standard errors for dependent returns, the Probabilistic and Deflated Sharpe ratios and Ledoit and Wolf's (2008) test of two Sharpe ratios are [[LM33]]'s.

> [!pitfall] An interval for the Kelly fraction is not a sizing rule
> The interval 2.2 to 6.5 says how far 4.28 could lie from the Kelly fraction behind TR23, if days were independent; it does not say which leverage to hold. Sizing from this distribution is [[LM45]]'s subject, and a walk-forward test is [[LM34]]'s.

> [!kc]
> TR23's iid percentile interval for $\hat f$ has a half-width of 50% of 4.28, and [[LM4]]'s band for 0050's $\hat\mu/s^2$ one of 52% of 5.68. Why are the relative errors so close?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > The relative error of $\bar R/s^2$ is about that of $\bar R$, $1/(\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}\sqrt T)$: 25.3% here and 26% for 0050 ([[LM4]]). Both samples have $\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}\sqrt T$ near 4 (3.96 and 3.79), and 1.96 relative errors of about 25% make about ±50%.

## When Resampling Days Fails {lo=3}

An iid resample's days are independent by construction, so a statistic whose error depends on the order of the days gets the wrong error. The variance of a mean shows which statistics are affected.

Let $x_t$ be a weakly stationary daily series, the condition of (19.7). By (19.7) with $m=T$, $\sum_tx_t$ has variance $T\gamma_0+2\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}(T-k)\gamma_k$. Dividing by $T^2$ gives

$$\Var\bar x=\frac{\gamma_0}T\Big(1+2\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}\big(1-\tfrac kT\big)\rho_k\Big).$$

An iid resample makes every $\rho_k$ zero in expectation, so it reports about $\gamma_0/T$. For the mean of $R$ the autocorrelations are small ([[LM19]]), and the iid error is close. But $s^2$ is the mean of $(R_t-\bar R)^2$, and squares are strongly autocorrelated: volatility clusters. So the iid bootstrap understates the volatility's error and cannot measure a statistic of dependence at all.

> [!example] What resampling days cannot see {#destroy}
> TR23's lag-1 autocorrelation of $\lvert r_t\rvert$ is $\hat\rho_1(\lvert r\rvert)=0.161$ ([[LM19]]; [[LM17]]'s pair correlation prints the same). TR23's log returns have the variance ratio $\operatorname{VR}(20)=1.036$ ([[LM19]]). Recompute both on experiments 2 and 3.
>
> > [!solution]
> > On the iid resamples $\hat\rho_1(\lvert r\rvert)$ averages −0.0001, and 95% of the values lie between −0.025 and 0.026: none of 10,000 reaches 0.161 ([[ex:destroyhist]]). On the block resamples it averages 0.156, with percentile interval 0.10 to 0.21.
> >
> > For $\operatorname{VR}(20)$ the iid resamples average 0.997, with 95% of them between 0.88 and 1.13, centred on independent days instead of on 1.036. The blocks average 1.037, with 95% between 0.92 and 1.16. The standard error barely moves, 0.065 against 0.061, but only the blocks centre the interval on the data. Monte Carlo standard errors of the interval ends are at most 0.0007 ($\hat\rho_1$) and 0.002 ($\operatorname{VR}(20)$).

> [!kc]
> Why do iid resamples give $\operatorname{VR}(20)$ near 1, whatever the data?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Resampled days are independent draws from $\hat F$, so their autocorrelations vanish in expectation, and by (19.7) a 20-day sum has variance close to $20s_r^2$. The resamples average 0.997, near [[LM19]]'s iid factor of (19.8), $(1-20/T)/(1-1/T)=0.997$.

## Block Bootstraps {lo=4}

A block bootstrap resamples stretches of consecutive days, so the dependence inside each stretch survives. **Moving blocks** (Künsch 1989) draw $\lceil T/\ell\rceil$ of the $T-\ell+1$ stretches of $\ell$ days with replacement, join them and cut the result to $T$ days. Days near the sample's ends lie in fewer blocks and are drawn less often (Deep Dive (b)).

> [!definition] Stationary bootstrap
> The **stationary bootstrap** with mean block length $\ell$ (Politis and Romano 1994) fills a resample position by position. Its first day is uniform on the $T$ days. At each later position, with probability $1/\ell$ a new block starts on a uniform day; otherwise the next day follows, with day $T$ followed by day 1. Hence
> $$\begin{gathered}\Pr(\text{a block lasts at least }j\text{ days})\\=\Big(1-\frac1\ell\Big)^{j-1},\end{gathered} \tag{22.3}$$
> the mean block length is $\ell$, and every position is uniform on the $T$ days (Deep Dive (b)).

**Circular blocks** are moving blocks on a circle: day $T$ is followed by day 1, so every day is equally likely (Lahiri 2003, §2.7.1). Stationarity is defined in [[LM25]], and volatility that depends on yesterday's is modelled in [[LM26]].

Stated without proof (Lahiri 2003, Theorem 3.1): let a stationary series have finite moments of some order above 2, and let its dependence die out fast enough (strong mixing). Then moving and circular blocks estimate the variance of its mean consistently if $\ell\to\infty$ and $\ell/T\to0$. For the stationary bootstrap, Theorem 3.3 asks more: moments of some order above 4, faster-dying dependence, and $\ell/\sqrt T\to0$.

[[LM15]]'s Student $t$ fit to TR23 has 3.065 degrees of freedom, so its moments exist only below order 3.065. That meets Theorem 3.1's moment condition for the mean of $R_t$ but not Theorem 3.3's, so the stationary bootstrap of the mean ([[eg:btest]]) rests on practice too.

The volatility, Sharpe ratio and Kelly fraction, $\operatorname{VR}(20)$, $\hat\rho_1(\lvert r\rvert)$, the windows' spread and Section 7's correlations and component shares average squares or products of returns. For these, Theorem 3.1 needs moments above order 4, so their intervals rest on the method's practice; for the first three, the three block schemes give similar widths ([[ex:methods]]).

In [[ex:paths]] the iid path turns TR23's calm and turbulent stretches into an even 12% to 28% a year, while the block path copies whole stretches, extremes included.

The printed intervals use the stationary bootstrap, whose resampled series is stationary given the data (Politis and Romano 1994).

> [!deepdive]- (b) Every day is equally likely at every position
> In a stationary or a circular resample, suppose that the day at one position is uniform on the $T$ days. The next position holds a new uniform draw or the day after (day $T$ followed by day 1), chosen independently of the day. A uniform day shifted one place around the circle is still uniform, so the next position is uniform too.
>
> By induction from the first, every position is uniform, with mean $\bar R$ under $\hat F$, so $\E_{\hat F}\bar R_{\text{boot}}=\bar R$ for every $\ell$.
>
> Moving blocks have no wrap. Day $t$ lies in $\min(t,\ell,T-\ell+1,T-t+1)$ of the $T-\ell+1$ blocks, so the first and last days are drawn less often. $\blacksquare$

> [!pitfall] A block keeps no dependence longer than itself
> Blocks of mean length 145 days keep clustering that fades within months. Dependence over years, such as [[LM19]]'s variance ratios below 1 beyond a year, is cut at every block's end. So the volatility's standard error still rises at 400 days (Section 6), and almost no block (about 1 in 5,000) holds a five-year window (Section 7).

> [!zh] 區塊自助重抽法：保留波動群聚
> 一天一天地重抽，會把波動群聚現象（volatility clustering）完全打散：資料中 $\lvert r_t\rvert$ 的一階自我相關（autocorrelation）是 0.161，獨立同分布（iid）重抽的結果卻都在 0 附近。區塊自助重抽法（block bootstrap）改為整段抽取連續的交易日：移動區塊（moving block）從所有長度為 $\ell$ 的區段中抽取，循環區塊（circular block）把樣本頭尾相接，平穩自助重抽法（stationary bootstrap）則讓區塊長度（block length）隨機，平均為 $\ell$。整段抽取保留了平靜與動盪的期間，所以波動率（volatility）的信賴區間（confidence interval）寬度變成將近原來的四倍，凱利比例（Kelly fraction）的信賴區間卻只寬一點，因為它的誤差主要來自平均報酬（mean return）。比區塊更長的相依性（dependence），仍然會在區塊的接縫處被切斷。

## Choosing the Block Length {lo=5}

No block length suits every statistic. Politis and White (2004), as corrected by Patton, Politis and White (2009), estimate the length that makes the block bootstrap's variance of a **mean** most accurate. Their target is the mean of the series they are given (stated without proof). arch's `optimal_block_length` computes it for stationary and circular blocks, capped at $\lceil\min(3\sqrt T,T/3)\rceil$.

> [!example] A block length for TR23 {#ellrule}
> Apply `optimal_block_length` to $R$, to $(R-\bar R)^2$ and to $(r-\bar r)^2$, and choose one length for TR23.
>
> > [!solution]
> > On $R$ the estimate is 3.07 days for the stationary bootstrap (3.51 for circular blocks), since returns are nearly uncorrelated. On $(R-\bar R)^2$ it is 144.59 (165.52), and on $(r-\bar r)^2$ 144.02; the cap is 230.
> >
> > The **book's rule** takes one length per sample and family of statistics: the largest stationary estimate among the squared demeaned simple-return series the statistic involves, rounded. For TR23 that is 145, for every statistic on $R$ or on $r$, $\operatorname{VR}(20)$ and $\hat\rho_1(\lvert r\rvert)$ included. It is a rule, not the right length.

The rule is conservative for statistics driven by the mean. In [[ex:ell]] the standard errors of the Sharpe ratio and of the Kelly fraction grow by only 8% and 14% from $\ell=3$ to 145. Their ratios to the iid error peak at 1.15 and 1.24, between $\ell=50$ and 100.

The volatility's ratio is 1.18 at $\ell=2$, 3.91 at 145 and 4.63 at 400, still rising. Dependence in the squares, from clustering or from shifts between volatility regimes, may last longer than any block a 23-year sample allows. For $\operatorname{VR}(20)$ the ratio is 0.93 at 145: blocks move its centre, not its width ([[eg:destroy]]).

## Bootstrap Tests {lo=7}

A test needs the statistic's law when the null hypothesis holds ([[LM21]]). A bootstrap test builds that law by resampling a version of the data in which the null holds: for $\mu=0$, the centred returns $R_t-\bar R$.

> [!definition] Bootstrap p-value of a mean
> Draw $B$ resamples of the centred returns $R_t-\bar R$ under a stated scheme, and compute each one's t-stat (21.1), $\text{t-stat}_{\text{boot},i}$. The two-sided **bootstrap p-value** is
> $$\frac1B\,\#\big\{i:\lvert\text{t-stat}_{\text{boot},i}\rvert\ge\lvert\text{t-stat}\rvert\big\}. \tag{22.4}$$
> Its Monte Carlo standard error is $\sqrt{\text{p-value}\times(1-\text{p-value})/B}$.

> [!example] A bootstrap test of the mean {#btest}
> In TR23's 7% regime (3,077 returns), [[LM21]]'s t-stat is 2.05, with a two-sided normal p-value of 0.041. Test $\mu=0$ at the 5% level with (22.4), iid and with stationary blocks; then test the full sample.
>
> > [!solution]
> > Resampling $R_t-\bar R$ iid (experiment 16), 408 of 10,000 resampled $\lvert\text{t-stat}\rvert$ reach 2.05: a bootstrap p-value of $0.041\pm0.002$, as [[LM21]] found. With stationary blocks of mean length 108, the regime's own rule (experiment 17), 477 reach it: $0.048\pm0.002$. The blocks barely widen the null law, whose standard deviation is 1.03 against 1.01 for iid days.
> >
> > Both p-values are below 5%, so both reject, though the block one is close (Problem 7). For the full sample [[LM21]] gives the t-stat 3.96 and the normal p-value $7.6\times10^{-5}$. With blocks of 145 (experiment 18) none of 10,000 resampled $\lvert\text{t-stat}\rvert$ reaches 3.96. That makes (22.4) zero, but zero hits only bound the p-value: below about $3/B=0.0003$ at the 95% level. The decision at the 5% level stands.

**Independence as a null.** [[eg:destroy]]'s iid resamples are draws under the null that days are independent. None reaches 0.161, which lies 12 standard errors (0.013) above 0, so independence of $\lvert r_t\rvert$ is rejected. [[LM21]] built its calendar nulls by shuffling the dates, a resampling without replacement that imposes "no calendar effect" (*Model*); here centring imposes "no mean".

**Tests and intervals.** A two-sided test at level $\alpha$ rejects $\theta=\theta_0$ about when the $1-\alpha$ interval (22.2) excludes $\theta_0$, as in [[eg:crash]]. They agree only approximately: the test resamples under the null, the interval under the data.

**How many tests.** This module ran four tests: three on TR23 (the mean in the 7% regime and in the full sample, and independence) and one on U28. Each null counts once, whatever the scheme, and the U28 test is the crash gap's interval read by duality. The 7% regime is one of two, and [[eg:pc1]] compared years chosen after looking ([[LM21]]). Testing many strategies with the bootstrap (the reality check) is [[LM34]]'s.

> [!zh] 自助重抽檢定：先造一個虛無假設成立的世界
> 假設檢定（hypothesis testing）需要統計量（statistic）在虛無假設（null hypothesis）成立時的分布（distribution）。自助重抽檢定（bootstrap test）先把資料改成虛無假設成立的樣子，例如把報酬減去平均數（mean），使平均報酬（mean return）為零，再重抽樣（resampling）、重算 t 統計量（t-statistic）。p值（p-value）就是重抽樣的 $\lvert\text{t-stat}\rvert$ 至少和實際值一樣大的比例。若直接重抽原始報酬，得到的是對立假設（alternative hypothesis）下的分布，t 統計量的中心在 3.96 附近，不能當檢定的基準。漲跌幅限制（price limit）為 7% 的時期以區塊自助重抽法（block bootstrap）算出的 p值是 0.048，在 5% 的顯著水準（significance level）下仍拒絕虛無假設，但很接近界線。

> [!kc]
> Why does [[eg:btest]]'s full-sample test resample $R_t-\bar R$ and not $R_t$?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > To impose the null $\mu=0$. iid resamples of $R_t$ itself (experiment 19) give t-stats with mean 3.96, and half of them lie above the observed 3.96. That is the law under the alternative, useless as a null.

## Resampling in Python {lo=8}

The function below draws the stationary bootstrap in arch's order, $T$ candidate start days and then $T$ uniforms, and checks itself against arch (experiment 20). Here `R` holds TR23's daily simple returns:

```python
import numpy as np
from arch.bootstrap import StationaryBootstrap


def stationary_idx(rng, T, ell):
    """Day indices of one stationary resample, drawn in arch's order."""
    start = rng.integers(T, size=T)        # a candidate start day per position
    new = rng.random(T) <= 1 / ell         # a new block starts here
    new[0] = True
    first = np.flatnonzero(new)            # positions where blocks start
    blk = np.cumsum(new) - 1               # the block of each position
    step = np.arange(T) - first[blk]       # steps since its block started
    return (start[first][blk] + step) % T  # day T is followed by day 1


g1 = np.random.default_rng([20, 20261011])      # experiment 20: twins
g2 = np.random.default_rng([20, 20261011])
bs = StationaryBootstrap(145, R, seed=g1)
for _ in bs.bootstrap(3):
    print((bs.index == stationary_idx(g2, len(R), 145)).all())   # True
```

arch's `CircularBlockBootstrap` and `MovingBlockBootstrap` match their NumPy twins too. `optimal_block_length(x)` returns a pandas DataFrame with the columns `stationary` and `circular`; its docstring calls them `b_sb` and `b_cb`. Resampled portfolios are [[LM30]]'s.

`scipy.stats.bootstrap` resamples days only and differs from this book in three places. Its interval ends interpolate (`stats.quantile`, linear): 0.4157 against the book's 0.4147 for [[eg:sharpe]]. Its `standard_error` divides by $B-1$: 0.20539 against 0.20538. Its default is BCa with 9,999 resamples. To draw this book's resamples, pass `batch=500` and the generator as the keyword `rng`; `method="percentile"` selects the percentile interval:

```python
    return np.sqrt(A) * x.mean(axis=axis) / x.std(axis=axis)


res = stats.bootstrap((R,), sharpe, n_resamples=10_000, batch=500,
                      method="percentile",
                      rng=np.random.default_rng([2, 20261011]))
print(res.confidence_interval)             # low=0.4157... high=1.2142...
```

## Summary {-}

- The bootstrap replaces $F$ by $\hat F$: (22.1) and (22.2) come from resamples, and more resamples shrink only the Monte Carlo error.
- The iid bootstrap agrees with [[LM20]]'s delta method: 0.41 to 1.21 for the Sharpe ratio, 2.2 to 6.5 for the Kelly fraction.
- Resampling days destroys dependence and understates the error of statistics of squares.
- Blocks (22.3) keep dependence shorter than a block: the volatility's interval widens 3.9 times, the Kelly fraction's 1.2 times.
- The book's block length, 145 for TR23, is the largest Politis–White estimate on the squared series.
- The crash gap's interval excludes 0. Near a bound the percentile interval stays in range, and the basic interval corrects a bias.
- A bootstrap test resamples under the null, (22.4).
- arch draws NumPy's block indices from the same generator; SciPy resamples days only.

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Sampling distribution | 抽樣分布 | The law of an estimate across samples drawn from $F$. |
| Bootstrap | 自助重抽法 | Reading an estimate's sampling distribution from resamples drawn from $\hat F$ (or from blocks). |
| Resampling | 重抽樣 | Drawing a new sample from the data. |
| Ideal bootstrap | — | The exact law of the resampled statistic, over all $T^T$ equally likely resamples. |
| Bootstrap standard error | 標準誤 | The standard deviation of $B$ resampled values, divisor $B$, (22.1). |
| Percentile interval | 百分位數信賴區間 | The $\tfrac\alpha2$ and $1-\tfrac\alpha2$ quantiles of the resampled values, (22.2). |
| Basic interval | 基本信賴區間 | $2\hat\theta$ minus the percentile interval's ends, reversed, (22.2). |
| BCa interval | 偏誤加速校正之自助重抽信賴區間 | The percentile interval adjusted for bias and skewness (Efron 1987). |
| Block bootstrap | 區塊自助重抽法 | Resampling stretches of consecutive days. |
| Moving blocks | 移動區塊 | Blocks of $\ell$ consecutive days drawn from the $T-\ell+1$ in the sample. |
| Circular blocks | 循環區塊 | Moving blocks on a circle, day $T$ followed by day 1. |
| Stationary bootstrap | 平穩自助重抽法 | Blocks of random length with mean $\ell$, (22.3). |
| Block length | 區塊長度 | $\ell$; for TR23 the book's rule gives 145. |
| Bootstrap test | 自助重抽檢定 | A test whose null law comes from resamples of data altered so that the null hypothesis holds. |
| Bootstrap p-value | p值 | The share of resamples under the null at least as extreme as the data, (22.4). |
