## Introduction

[[LM27]] took the weights $\mathbf w$ as given and computed a portfolio's mean, variance and risk contributions. This module chooses $\mathbf w$: the least variance for a target mean, and the best Sharpe ratio beside a riskless asset. It also states when each answer exists and how far it moves with its inputs.

> [!case] Five funds, two years, one optimizer
> At the end of February 2019 a TWD investor holds five Taiwan-listed funds. They track Taiwan stocks (0050), US stocks (00646), long US Treasuries (00679B), gold futures (00635U) and crude-oil futures (00642U). The investor has 504 days of joint returns, from 13 February 2017 to 27 February 2019. Which mix has the least variance, and which the best Sharpe ratio?
>
> On those days the calculus is exact. The minimum-variance mix holds every fund, 41.4% in the bond fund, and predicts 5.02% volatility a year. At a 1.5% riskless rate the best Sharpe ratio is 0.88, at 7.81% volatility, with oil sold short ([[ex:case]]).
>
> Hold both mixes from 4 March 2019 to 8 October 2026. The first realizes 10.05%, twice its prediction; the second 14.15%. And one more point of expected return on gold, under a sixth of its standard error, would lift gold's weight from 2.6% to 12.8%. Was the calculus wrong, were the weights wrong, or did the inputs change?

## The Minimum-Variance Portfolio {lo=1}

[[LM10]] showed that a portfolio's variance $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w$ is a convex function of the weights, and stopped at its gradient. Minimizing it under linear constraints takes one Lagrangian, as in [[LM7]], now with $N$ assets and $k$ constraints at once.

Let $\Sigma$ be an $N\times N$ positive definite matrix, $K$ a $k\times N$ matrix of full row rank $k$, and $\mathbf b$ a vector of length $k$. The feasible set $\{\mathbf w:K\mathbf w=\mathbf b\}$ is not empty, because $K$ has independent rows. The budget alone is $K=\mathbf 1^{\top}$ with $\mathbf b=1$.

With [[LM7]]'s signs the Lagrangian is $\mathcal L=\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w-\boldsymbol\lambda^{\top}(K\mathbf w-\mathbf b)$. By (10.8) its gradient in $\mathbf w$ is $2\Sigma\mathbf w-K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda$, so a critical point has $\mathbf w=\tfrac12\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda$, and the constraint then requires $\tfrac12K\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda=\mathbf b$. The matrix $K\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}$ is positive definite. By (11.8) the eigenvalues of $\Sigma^{-1}$ are the reciprocals of $\Sigma$'s, all positive, and $K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda\ne\mathbf 0$ whenever $\boldsymbol\lambda\ne\mathbf 0$, because $K^{\top}$ has independent columns. So the multipliers are unique.

> [!theorem] Minimum variance under linear constraints
> Let $\Sigma$ be positive definite and $K$ ($k\times N$) of full row rank. Then the problem $\min\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w$ subject to $K\mathbf w=\mathbf b$ has the unique solution $\mathbf w^*$, with value and multipliers
> $$\begin{gathered}\mathbf w^*=\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}\big(K\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf b,\\ \mathbf w^{*\top}\Sigma\mathbf w^*=\mathbf b^{\top}\big(K\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf b,\\ \boldsymbol\lambda=2\big(K\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf b.\end{gathered} \tag{28.1}$$
> Every feasible $\mathbf w$ satisfies $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w=\mathbf w^{*\top}\Sigma\mathbf w^*+(\mathbf w-\mathbf w^*)^{\top}\Sigma(\mathbf w-\mathbf w^*)$.

*Proof.* Let $\mathbf w$ be feasible and $\boldsymbol\Delta:=\mathbf w-\mathbf w^*$, so that $K\boldsymbol\Delta=\mathbf 0$. By (10.9),

$$\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w=\mathbf w^{*\top}\Sigma\mathbf w^*+2\boldsymbol\Delta^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w^*+\boldsymbol\Delta^{\top}\Sigma\boldsymbol\Delta .$$

The middle term is $\boldsymbol\Delta^{\top}K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda=(K\boldsymbol\Delta)^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda=0$, because $2\Sigma\mathbf w^*=K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda$. The last term is positive unless $\boldsymbol\Delta=\mathbf 0$, since $\Sigma$ is positive definite. So $\mathbf w^*$ is the unique minimum, and substituting it gives the value. $\blacksquare$

The identity proves existence and uniqueness by itself, so no KKT theory is needed. The result is also [[LM10]]'s theorem at work: a positive definite $\Sigma$ makes the variance strictly convex. Long-only weights add inequality constraints, which need the KKT conditions of [[LM7]]'s (7.13); [[LM29]] applies them.

With the budget alone, $K\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}=\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1$ is a number, and (28.1) becomes the following result.

> [!result] The global minimum-variance portfolio
> For $\Sigma$ positive definite, the fully invested portfolio of least variance is
> $$\begin{gathered}\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1},\qquad \sigma^2_{\min}=\frac{1}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1},\\ \lambda^*=2\sigma^2_{\min},\qquad \Sigma\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}=\sigma^2_{\min}\mathbf 1.\end{gathered} \tag{28.2}$$
> For every $\mathbf w$ with $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w=1$,
> $$\begin{aligned}\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w&=\sigma^2_{\min}\\&\quad+(\mathbf w-\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}})^{\top}\Sigma(\mathbf w-\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}).\end{aligned} \tag{28.3}$$

Identity (28.3) is [[LM7]]'s (7.9) for $N$ assets, and $\lambda^*$ is its shadow price of the budget. The last part of (28.2) says that every fund has covariance $\sigma^2_{\min}$ with $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}$. So does every fully invested portfolio, since $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}=\sigma^2_{\min}\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w$. By (27.5) every fund's beta to $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}$ is 1, and by (27.6) every risk share equals its weight. [[LM11]] solved $\hat\Sigma\mathbf x=\mathbf 1$; rescaled to sum to 1, its solution is (28.2).

> [!pitfall] Solve, never invert, and test positive definiteness first
> Compute $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1$ with `np.linalg.solve(S, ones)`, which factorizes once and rounds less than forming the inverse. Before that, `np.linalg.cholesky(S)` tests positive definiteness: a symmetric matrix is positive definite exactly when it has a Cholesky factor ([[LM12]]). If the factor fails, some $\boldsymbol\Delta\ne\mathbf 0$ has $\boldsymbol\Delta^{\top}\Sigma\boldsymbol\Delta\le0$, the proof of (28.1) breaks, and a unique minimum need not exist.

> [!zh] 最小變異數：一個線性方程組
> 在權重總和為 1 的條件下讓變異數（variance）最小，用的就是 [[LM7]] 的拉格朗日乘數（Lagrange multiplier）法，只是資產從兩檔變成 $N$ 檔。共變異數矩陣（covariance matrix）$\Sigma$ 正定（positive definite）時，變異數是嚴格凸函數（convex function），所以解存在而且唯一，這就是 [[LM10]] 所說最佳化的基礎。最小變異數投資組合（minimum-variance portfolio）是把 $\Sigma\mathbf x=\mathbf 1$ 的解縮放成總和為 1，每一檔基金與它的共變異數都等於 $\sigma^2_{\min}$。恆等式 (28.3) 直接證明它是全域最小值（global minimum），不需要 KKT 理論。E5 第一個視窗的最小變異數組合五檔都持有，預測波動 5.02%，低於任何一檔基金。

## The Frontier and Two-Fund Separation {lo=2}

Now add a target mean. A frontier portfolio has the least variance among fully invested portfolios with mean $\mu_p$: (28.1) with $K=(\mathbf 1\ \boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}$ and $\mathbf b=(1,\mu_p)^{\top}$. The two rows are independent exactly when $\boldsymbol\mu$ is not proportional to $\mathbf 1$.

A shorter route starts from $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}$. Write $\mathbf w=\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}+\boldsymbol\Delta$ with $\mathbf 1^{\top}\boldsymbol\Delta=0$. By (28.3) the variance is $\sigma^2_{\min}+\boldsymbol\Delta^{\top}\Sigma\boldsymbol\Delta$, and the mean is $\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}+(\boldsymbol\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\mathbf 1)^{\top}\boldsymbol\Delta$. So $\boldsymbol\Delta$ solves (28.1) with the rows $\mathbf 1^{\top}$ and $(\boldsymbol\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\mathbf 1)^{\top}$ and the right-hand side $(0,\mu_p-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}})^{\top}$. Its $2\times2$ matrix is diagonal, because (28.2) gives $\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}/\sigma^2_{\min}$ and $\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1=1/\sigma^2_{\min}$:

$$\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\mathbf 1)=\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}}{\sigma^2_{\min}}-\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}}{\sigma^2_{\min}}=0 .$$

The inverse of that diagonal matrix follows from (9.5) at once, and (28.1) gives the frontier.

> [!result] The mean–variance frontier
> Let $\Sigma$ be positive definite and $\boldsymbol\mu$ not proportional to $\mathbf 1$, and let $\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2:=(\boldsymbol\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\mathbf 1)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\mathbf 1)>0$. The fully invested portfolio of least variance with mean $\mu_p$ is
> $$\begin{aligned}\mathbf w(\mu_p)&=\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}\\&\quad+\frac{\mu_p-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}}{\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2}\,\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\mathbf 1),\end{aligned} \tag{28.4}$$
> and its variance is
> $$\sigma_p^2=\sigma^2_{\min}+\frac{(\mu_p-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}})^2}{\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2}. \tag{28.5}$$

Equation (28.5) is a parabola in $(\sigma_p^2,\mu_p)$ and a hyperbola in $(\sigma_p,\mu_p)$ with vertex $(\sigma_{\min},\mu_{\mathrm{mv}})$. As $\sigma_p$ grows, it approaches the asymptotes $\mu_p=\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\pm\mathrm{SR}_\infty\sigma_p$ (Problem 8). The upper half, $\mu_p\ge\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}$, is the **efficient frontier**: for each variance it holds the higher mean.

The multiplier of the target mean is a shadow price, (7.6): by (28.5), $\lambda_\mu=d\sigma_p^2/d\mu_p=2(\mu_p-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}})/\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2$. Deep Dive 1 computes both multipliers from the $2\times2$ system.

By (28.4) the frontier weights are affine in $\mu_p$. So any two distinct frontier portfolios $\mathbf w_a$ and $\mathbf w_b$, with means $\mu_a$ and $\mu_b$, rebuild every other:

$$\mathbf w(\mu_p)=\mathbf w_a+\frac{\mu_p-\mu_a}{\mu_b-\mu_a}\,(\mathbf w_b-\mathbf w_a).$$

The rebuilding is **two-fund separation**: an investor who wants a frontier portfolio needs only two funds. Merton (1972) derived the frontier in closed form and proved this separation as a mutual-fund theorem.

> [!pitfall] Nearly equal means make a flat frontier
> In (28.4) the weights move by $(\mu_p-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}})/\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2$ times a fixed direction. When the means are nearly equal, $\mathrm{SR}_\infty$ is small, and a modest target mean demands huge long and short positions.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive 1: the frontier's multipliers from the $2\times2$ system
> With $K=(\mathbf 1\ \boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}$, expanding the definition of $\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2$ gives $\boldsymbol\mu^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol\mu=\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2+\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}^2/\sigma^2_{\min}$, so the matrix of (28.1) is
> $$\begin{aligned}&K\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}\\&\quad=\frac{1}{\sigma^2_{\min}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\\ \mu_{\mathrm{mv}}&\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}^2+\sigma^2_{\min}\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2\end{pmatrix}.\end{aligned}$$
> Its determinant is $\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2/\sigma^2_{\min}$, positive exactly when $\boldsymbol\mu$ is not proportional to $\mathbf 1$. Cramer's rule (9.4) solves $\tfrac12K\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda=(1,\mu_p)^{\top}$:
> $$\begin{gathered}\lambda_\mu=\frac{2(\mu_p-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}})}{\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2},\\ \lambda=2\sigma^2_{\min}-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\lambda_\mu .\end{gathered}$$
> Then $\mathbf w=\tfrac12\Sigma^{-1}(\lambda\mathbf 1+\lambda_\mu\boldsymbol\mu)$ is (28.4), and the value $\tfrac12(\lambda+\lambda_\mu\mu_p)$ is (28.5).

> [!zh] 效率前緣是一條拋物線
> 指定目標平均報酬之後，變異數（variance）最小的投資組合構成效率前緣（efficient frontier）。在（變異數，平均數）平面上它是一條拋物線（parabola），在（波動率，平均數）平面上則是雙曲線（hyperbola），頂點是最小變異數投資組合，兩條漸近線（asymptote）的斜率是 $\pm\mathrm{SR}_\infty$。前緣上的權重是目標平均數的一次函數，所以任選兩個前緣投資組合，就能組合出其他所有的前緣投資組合：這就是兩基金分離（two-fund separation），是分離定理（separation theorem）的一種。上半支才是效率投資組合（efficient portfolio）。第一個視窗的等權重組合落在前緣內側，平均數比最小變異數組合低，波動卻更高。

## The Tangency Portfolio and the Capital Market Line {lo=3,4}

Now add a riskless asset that earns $r_f$. A book holds exposures $\mathbf f$ to the funds and cash $1-L$ at $r_f$, where $L=\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf f$, so cash is borrowing when $L>1$. By (8.4) its return is $r_f+\mathbf f^{\top}(\mathbf R-r_f\mathbf 1)$, with mean $r_f+\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf e$ and variance $\mathbf f^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf f$. Borrowing without limit at the riskless rate is an idealization; [[LM29]] adds a financing spread and limits.

The least-variance book with mean $\mu_p\ne r_f$ solves (28.1) with $K=\mathbf e^{\top}$ and $\mathbf b=\mu_p-r_f$, for any $\mathbf e\ne\mathbf 0$:

$$\begin{gathered}\mathbf f^*=\frac{(\mu_p-r_f)\,\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e}{\mathbf e^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e},\qquad \mathrm{SR}^{*2}:=\mathbf e^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e,\\ \mathbf f^{*\top}\Sigma\mathbf f^*=\frac{(\mu_p-r_f)^2}{\mathrm{SR}^{*2}}.\end{gathered}$$

So every efficient book points along $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$, and every book has $\lvert\mu_p-r_f\rvert\le\mathrm{SR}^*\sigma_p$. The efficient books with $\mu_p>r_f$ lie on the **capital market line** $\mu_p=r_f+\mathrm{SR}^*\sigma_p$.

A fully invested book on that direction divides $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$ by $\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$. By (28.2), that sum is $(\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f)/\sigma^2_{\min}$, so its sign is the sign of $\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f$.

> [!result] The tangency portfolio and the capital market line
> Let $\Sigma$ be positive definite, $\boldsymbol\mu$ not proportional to $\mathbf 1$, and $r_f<\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}$. The fully invested portfolio with the highest Sharpe ratio is
> $$\begin{gathered}\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e},\\ \mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e=\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f}{\sigma^2_{\min}}>0,\\ \mathrm{SR}^{*2}=\mathbf e^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e,\end{gathered} \tag{28.6}$$
> with mean and volatility
> $$\begin{gathered}\mu_{\tan}-r_f=\frac{\mathrm{SR}^{*2}\sigma^2_{\min}}{\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f},\\ \sigma_{\tan}=\frac{\mathrm{SR}^*\sigma^2_{\min}}{\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f}.\end{gathered}$$
> Its Sharpe ratio decomposes, and the capital market line passes through it:
> $$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{SR}^{*2}=\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2+\frac{(\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f)^2}{\sigma^2_{\min}},\\ \mu_p=r_f+\mathrm{SR}^*\sigma_p.\end{gathered} \tag{28.7}$$

The portfolio $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}$ is the book on the direction $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$ with $L=1$, and its excess mean $\mathrm{SR}^{*2}/(\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e)$ is positive. So it lies on the capital market line and attains $\mathrm{SR}^*$, and its mean and volatility follow by substitution. Problem 8 derives (28.7) by splitting $\mathbf e$.

Every efficient book is a multiple of $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$, so the mix of risky funds does not depend on how much of the book they take. Tobin (1958, read in Cowles Foundation Discussion Paper No. 14, 1956, §3.6) found this separation with riskless cash at zero yield, no borrowing, and non-negativity assumed but not binding. Equation (28.6) allows any $r_f<\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}$ and borrowing at $r_f$, and on the first window it shorts oil, which his non-negativity excludes.

By (28.6), $\Sigma\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}=\mathbf e/(\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e)$. So by (27.5) every fund's beta to the tangency portfolio is proportional to its excess return:

$$\begin{gathered}\beta_{i,\tan}=\frac{e_i}{\mu_{\tan}-r_f},\quad\text{that is,}\\ \mu_i-r_f=\beta_{i,\tan}\,(\mu_{\tan}-r_f).\end{gathered}$$

The second form is the CAPM's equation of [[LM23]], $\E[R_i]-r_f=\beta_i(\E[R_{\text{mkt}}]-r_f)$, with $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}$ in the market's place. Conversely, a fully invested portfolio with $\mu_p\ne r_f$ whose betas satisfy it for every fund is the normalized $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$. The identity holds on any data, so it is no evidence for the CAPM. Why the market should be the tangency portfolio is a question of equilibrium theory, outside this book.

A risk-averse investor ranks books by their mean excess return minus a penalty on variance.

> [!result] Risk aversion with a riskless asset
> Let $\delta>0$ and $\Sigma$ be positive definite, with borrowing and lending at $r_f$ without limit. Then
> $$\begin{gathered}\max_{\mathbf f}\ \mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf e-\frac{\delta}{2}\,\mathbf f^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf f\\ \text{has the unique solution}\quad \mathbf f^*=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e}{\delta},\end{gathered} \tag{28.8}$$
> with volatility $\mathrm{SR}^*/\delta$. When $r_f<\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}$, the solution is $\mathbf f^*=L\,\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}$ with $L=\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e/\delta>0$.

The objective's Hessian is $-\delta\Sigma$, so the objective is strictly concave for any $r_f$ ([[LM10]]), and its one critical point is the maximum. By (10.8) that point solves $\mathbf e-\delta\Sigma\mathbf f=\mathbf 0$, and its variance is $\mathbf e^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e/\delta^2$. The investor therefore holds the tangency mix, levered when $L>1$ and diluted with cash when $L<1$, and $\delta$ chooses only how much. [[LM7]] found the same structure for two assets: its Kelly fractions are $\mathbf f^*=L^*\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}$. The Kelly fractions of $N$ assets and growth-optimal leverage are [[LM43]]'s.

> [!zh] 切點投資組合與資本市場線
> 加入無風險資產（risk-free asset）之後，所有效率投資組合（efficient portfolio）的風險部位都和 $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$ 同方向，只差規模大小。把這個方向縮放成權重總和為 1，就是切點投資組合（tangency portfolio），它的夏普比率（Sharpe ratio）最高。從無風險利率（risk-free rate）出發、穿過切點的直線，就是資本市場線（capital market line）。前提是最小變異數組合的平均報酬高於 $r_f$，否則同一個公式算出的是夏普比率最低的組合。風險規避（risk aversion）係數 $\delta$ 只決定持有多少切點投資組合，$\delta$ 越大，現金越多。每檔基金對切點投資組合的 beta 都與它的超額報酬成正比，這在任何資料上都成立，所以不能拿來驗證 CAPM。

## How Tangency Weights React to the Means {lo=5,8}

The means in (28.6) are estimates. Exposures respond to them linearly: by (28.8), a change $\Delta\mathbf e$ moves the exposures by exactly $\Sigma^{-1}\Delta\mathbf e/\delta$. By (11.8), $\Sigma^{-1}$ divides each eigenvector's part of $\Delta\mathbf e$ by its eigenvalue, so a change along a direction of small variance moves the book the most.

The weights also divide by $\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$, which moves too. A Jacobian matrix stacks as rows the gradients of several functions, each as in (10.8). Differentiating (28.6) by the quotient rule gives the weights' Jacobian, with entry $(i,j)$ equal to $\partial w^{\mathrm{tan}}_i/\partial e_j$:

$$\begin{gathered}\Delta\mathbf f^*=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\Delta\mathbf e}{\delta}\ \text{exactly},\\ \frac{\partial\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}}{\partial\mathbf e^{\top}}=\frac{(I-\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}\mathbf 1^{\top})\,\Sigma^{-1}}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e}.\end{gathered} \tag{28.9}$$

The Jacobian gives only a first-order approximation of a weight change. [[ex:sensitivity]] shows the exact changes on the first window: its diagonal runs from +1.9 points (oil) through +4.7 (00646), +6.6 (0050) and +7.6 (00679B) to +10.3 (gold).

> [!zh] 平均數差一點，權重差很多
> 曝險部位 $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e/\delta$ 對平均報酬是線性的，但切點投資組合的權重還要除以 $\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$，所以是非線性的。敏感度（sensitivity）由 $\Sigma^{-1}$ 決定：沿著變異數小的方向，平均數的一點變動會被放大很多。第一個視窗中，黃金的平均報酬只要提高 1 個百分點，也就是標準誤（standard error）的 0.15 倍，它的權重就從 2.6% 升到 12.8%。兩年資料估出的平均數，估計誤差（estimation error）遠大於這個變動，所以樣本內（in-sample）算出的切點權重不能當成建議。[[LM30]] 討論修正的方法。

## Mean–Variance in Python {lo=7}

The KKT system of (28.1) stacks stationarity, $2\Sigma\mathbf w=K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda$, and feasibility into one linear system:

$$\begin{pmatrix}2\Sigma&K^{\top}\\K&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf w\\-\boldsymbol\lambda\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf 0\\\mathbf b\end{pmatrix}.$$

```python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np

# X: the first window's 504 x 5 daily returns; A = 242.95, rf = 0.015;
# mu_p: a target mean. Keep mu, S and rf in the same (annual) units:
# w_tan depends on them, w_mv does not.
mu = A * X.mean(axis=0)
S = A * np.cov(X, rowvar=False, ddof=0)
np.linalg.cholesky(S)                  # raises LinAlgError if not PD
one = np.ones(5)
x = np.linalg.solve(S, one)
w_mv = x / x.sum()                     # (28.2)
y = np.linalg.solve(S, mu - rf * one)
assert y.sum() > 0                     # r_f < mu_mv, (28.6)
w_tan = y / y.sum()

K = np.vstack([one, mu])               # a frontier point, (28.1)
b = np.array([1.0, mu_p])
KKT = np.block([[2 * S, K.T], [K, np.zeros((2, 2))]])
w, neg_lam = np.split(np.linalg.solve(KKT, np.r_[np.zeros(5), b]), [5])

v = cp.Variable(5)
cons = [one @ v == 1, mu @ v == mu_p]
prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(cp.quad_form(v, cp.psd_wrap(S))), cons)
prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, tol_gap_abs=1e-12, tol_gap_rel=1e-12,
           tol_feas=1e-12)
print(prob.status, np.abs(v.value - w).max(), cons[0].dual_value)
```

## Summary {-}

- For a positive definite $\Sigma$ and linear constraints, minimum variance has one closed-form solution, (28.1), whose identity proves uniqueness.
- The minimum-variance portfolio (28.2) has covariance $\sigma^2_{\min}$ with every fund; on E5's first window it predicts 5.02%.
- The frontier (28.4)–(28.5) is a parabola in variance and mean, and any two frontier portfolios span it.
- Beside a riskless asset every efficient book is a multiple of $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$. The tangency portfolio (28.6) exists only when $r_f<\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}$, and $\delta$ in (28.8) chooses how much of it to hold.
- Tangency weights are fragile: 0.15 standard errors on gold's mean move its weight by 10.3 points.
- Held to 2026, the minimum-variance portfolio missed mostly because the minimum itself moved, from 5.02% to 9.63%; the tangency portfolio's weight error added 4.51 points more.
- Unconstrained mean–variance weights are in-sample estimates, not recommendations; [[LM29]] adds constraints, and [[LM30]] repairs the inputs.

## Solutions {-}

1. **B** is correct: with uncorrelated assets $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1=(100, 25, 25)$, so the weights are inverse variances, $100/150=66.7\%$, and $\sigma_{\min}=\sqrt{1/150}=8.16\%$. A weights by inverse volatilities, and C ignores the volatilities.

2. **A** is correct: by (7.7) the weight is $(0.01-0.012)/(0.04+0.01-0.024)=-7.7\%$. It shorts the riskier asset because $\rho>\sigma_2/\sigma_1=0.5$ ([[LM7]]). B assumes a long-only answer, and C uses inverse variances, ignoring the correlation.

3. **C** is correct: by (28.5), $\sigma_p^2=0.05^2+0.04^2/0.5^2=0.0089$, so $\sigma_p=9.43\%$. A adds volatilities, $5\%+0.04/0.5$, and B drops $\sigma_{\min}$.

4. **B** is correct: by (28.7), $\mathrm{SR}^{*2}=0.5^2+(0.025/0.05)^2=0.5$, so $\mathrm{SR}^*=0.707$, and by (28.6) $\mu_{\tan}=1.5\%+0.5\times0.0025/0.025=6.50\%$. A is the asymptote's slope, and C adds the two terms unsquared.

5. **A** is correct: by (28.8) the volatility is $\mathrm{SR}^*/\delta=20\%$ and $L=8/4=2.0$. B inverts the ratio, $\delta/8$, and C multiplies $\mathrm{SR}^*$ by $\delta$.

6. **C** is correct: the denominator is negative, so the portfolio lies on $-\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e$ with Sharpe ratio $-\mathrm{SR}^*$, the lowest possible; on 30 April 2024 it was −1.20. A needs a positive denominator; B is unrelated.

7. Section 2 gives the derivation: the gradient (10.8) vanishes at $\mathbf w=\tfrac12\Sigma^{-1}K^{\top}\boldsymbol\lambda$, the constraint fixes $\boldsymbol\lambda$, and (10.9) gives the identity. With $K=\mathbf 1^{\top}$ and $\mathbf b=1$, the multiplier is $2/(\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1)=2\sigma^2_{\min}$ and $\mathbf w^*=\sigma^2_{\min}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1$, which is (28.2).

8. (a) In (28.4), $\boldsymbol\Delta=c\,\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\mathbf 1)$ with $c=(\mu_p-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}})/\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2$, and $\mathbf 1^{\top}\boldsymbol\Delta=0$ (Section 3). By (28.3) the variance is $\sigma^2_{\min}+c^2\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2$, which is (28.5).
   (b) By (28.5), $\mu_p-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}=\pm\mathrm{SR}_\infty\sqrt{\sigma_p^2-\sigma^2_{\min}}$, whose distance from $\pm\mathrm{SR}_\infty\sigma_p$ is $\mathrm{SR}_\infty\sigma^2_{\min}/(\sigma_p+\sqrt{\sigma_p^2-\sigma^2_{\min}})$. That distance tends to 0 as $\sigma_p$ grows.
   (c) The split gives $\mathbf e^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e=\mathrm{SR}_\infty^2+2(\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f)\,\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\mathbf 1)+(\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f)^2\,\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1$. The cross term is zero by Section 3, and $\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1=1/\sigma^2_{\min}$.
   (d) The same split, divided by $\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e=(\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f)/\sigma^2_{\min}$, gives
   $$\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}=\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}+\frac{\sigma^2_{\min}}{\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}-r_f}\,\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol\mu-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}\mathbf 1),$$
   the frontier portfolio (28.4) at $\mu_{\tan}$. So (28.4) reads
   $$\mathbf w(\mu_p)=\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}+\frac{\mu_p-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}}{\mu_{\tan}-\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}}\,(\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}-\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}),$$
   with the coefficient 0.905 in [[eg:tan5]].

9. By (9.5), $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1=(\sigma_2^2-\sigma_{12},\ \sigma_1^2-\sigma_{12})^{\top}/\det\Sigma$, whose entries sum to $(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2-2\sigma_{12})/\det\Sigma$. So the first weight is (7.7), and $\sigma^2_{\min}=\det\Sigma/(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2-2\sigma_{12})$ is (7.8). With LM7's inputs, $0.023261/0.054969=42.3\%$, $\sigma_{\min}=9.22\%$ and $\lambda^*=0.0170$, as [[LM7]] printed.

10. (a) The changes are +6.6 points in 0050, −6.4 in 00646, and +0.8, −1.6 and +0.5 in the others ([[ex:sensitivity]]'s first column).
    (b) By (28.9) the exposures move along column 1 of $\Sigma^{-1}$, $(82.8, -48.1, 25.8, -17.3, -0.7)$ in annual units: up in 0050 and down in 00646, its closest substitute at correlation 0.54.
    (c) One point is only 0.11 of 0050's standard error, 9.4 points, yet it moves two weights by more than 6 points. The data support the window's minimum-variance mix far better than any tangency weight, which they cannot fix to within 10 points.

11. The lab reproduces [[eg:mv5]], [[eg:tan5]], [[eg:inside]] and [[eg:kkt]]: both solvers return `optimal`, and the duals +0.00193 and −0.161 are the negatives of LM7's multipliers.

12. The lab gives 10.05% with standard error 0.40, and the split into $\sigma_{\min}=9.63\%$ and 0.41 points of weight error ([[eg:realized]], [[eg:held]]).

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Minimum-variance portfolio | 最小變異數投資組合 | The fully invested portfolio of least variance, $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}$, (28.2). |
| Efficient frontier | 效率前緣 | The upper half of the least-variance portfolios for each target mean, (28.4)–(28.5). |
| Efficient portfolio | 效率投資組合 | A portfolio on the efficient frontier, or, beside a riskless asset, on the capital market line. |
| Two-fund separation | 兩基金分離 | Any two frontier portfolios rebuild the whole frontier, because its weights are affine in the target mean. |
| Tangency portfolio | 切點投資組合 | The fully invested portfolio with the highest Sharpe ratio, $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e/(\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e)$ when $r_f<\mu_{\mathrm{mv}}$, (28.6). |
| Capital market line | 資本市場線 | The line $\mu_p=r_f+\mathrm{SR}^*\sigma_p$ of efficient books beside a riskless asset, (28.7). |
| Risk-free asset | 無風險資產 | An asset with the certain return $r_f$, here an illustrative 1.5% a year. |
| Risk aversion | 風險規避 | The weight $\delta$ of the variance penalty in (28.8). |
| Shadow price | 影子價格 | A multiplier read as the rate at which the optimal value changes with its constraint, (7.6). |
| Sensitivity | 敏感度 | The change of an optimal weight for a change of an input, exact or by the Jacobian (28.9). |
| Jacobian matrix | Jacobi 矩陣 | The matrix whose rows are the gradients of several functions. |
| In-sample / out-of-sample | 樣本內／樣本外 | Data used to estimate a portfolio versus later data used to test it. |
