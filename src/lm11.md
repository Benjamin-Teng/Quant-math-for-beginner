## Introduction

A covariance matrix of 28 sectors has 406 distinct entries ([[LM10]]). Its structure lies in the perpendicular directions that it only stretches, its eigenvectors, and in the stretch factors, its eigenvalues. They rank the data's independent movements by variance and explain why the inverse matrix magnifies small changes.

> [!case] Twenty-eight sectors, how many movements?
> Take U28, the 28 leaf sector indices of the Taiwan Stock Exchange: the 30 that FinMind serves daily, less the aggregates Electronic and ChemicalBiotechnologyMedicalCare. From 2 December 2008 to 8 October 2026 their daily returns are all positively correlated, from 0.217 (BiotechnologyMedicalCare and OilGasElectricity) to 0.755 (ComputerPeripheralEquipment and ElectronicPartsComponents).
>
> One fixed mix of the 28 standardized returns, with weights from 0.130 to 0.224, carries 52.6% of their total variance. Yet 12 such directions are needed to pass 80%, and the first one's share was 66.6% in 2020 but 34.5% in 2017. A second direction is positive on the eight electronics sub-industries and on BiotechnologyMedicalCare, Chemical and ElectricMachinery, and negative on the other 17.
>
> On 7 April 2025 all 28 fell, by 5.90% (CommunicationsInternet) to 9.95% (Semiconductor). Yet over the sample all 28 moved the same way on only 300 of 4,382 days (6.8%).
>
> Solved on each half of the sample, the linear system $\hat\Sigma\mathbf x=\mathbf 1$ moves Semiconductor's entry from 430.84 to −587.60 and FinancialInsurance's from −2,547.30 to 2,216.20 ([[ex:case]]). How many independent movements are there, do they hold over time, and why does the solution move so much more than the matrix? Sections 5, 7 and 8 answer.

> [!note] Research hygiene
> The universe (U28) and the sample were fixed before any result was computed. Full-sample eigenvalues, shares, loadings and condition numbers are **descriptive**: they summarize the past, and nobody inside the sample could have known them. ## Directions a Matrix Only Stretches {lo=1}

Write $M:=10^4\hat\Sigma$ for the daily returns of Semiconductor and FinancialInsurance on the U28 sample:

$$M=\begin{pmatrix}2.3234&1.0666\\1.0666&1.4817\end{pmatrix}.$$

Multiply every unit vector $\mathbf u$ by $M$. The unit circle becomes an ellipse ([[ex:stretch]]), and most vectors also turn: $\mathbf e_1$ goes to $(2.3234,\,1.0666)^{\top}$, which points 24.7° away. Two perpendicular directions do not turn. Along them $M$ only stretches, by 3.0492 along one and by 0.7559 along the other.

> [!definition] Eigenvalue and eigenvector
> Let $M$ be an $N\times N$ matrix. A number $\lambda_k$ and a vector $\mathbf x\neq\mathbf 0$ with $M\mathbf x=\lambda_k\mathbf x$ are an **eigenvalue** and an **eigenvector** of $M$, together an **eigenpair**. The eigenvector keeps its direction under $M$ and is stretched by the factor $\lambda_k$, reversed if $\lambda_k<0$.

Rewrite the definition as $(M-\lambda_kI)\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$. A non-zero solution exists exactly when $M-\lambda_kI$ is singular ([[LM9]], (9.6)), so

$$\begin{aligned}&M\mathbf x=\lambda_k\mathbf x\ \text{ for some }\mathbf x\neq\mathbf 0\\ \Longleftrightarrow\ &\det(M-\lambda_kI)=0.\end{aligned} \tag{11.1}$$

The second line is the **characteristic equation**. For a $2\times2$ matrix it is a quadratic in $\lambda_k$, with the **trace** $\operatorname{tr}M=m_{11}+m_{22}$:

$$\begin{aligned}0&=\det(M-\lambda_kI)\\&=(m_{11}-\lambda_k)(m_{22}-\lambda_k)-m_{12}m_{21}\\&=\lambda_k^2-(\operatorname{tr}M)\,\lambda_k+\det M .\end{aligned}$$

The quadratic formula gives both roots:

$$\lambda_{1,2}=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}M\pm\sqrt{\tfrac14(\operatorname{tr}M)^2-\det M}. \tag{11.2}$$

Factored, the quadratic is $(\lambda_k-\lambda_1)(\lambda_k-\lambda_2)$. So **the eigenvalues add up to the trace and multiply to the determinant**: $\lambda_1+\lambda_2=\operatorname{tr}M$ and $\lambda_1\lambda_2=\det M$. Section 3 extends both facts to symmetric matrices of any size.

A rotation by an angle other than 0° or 180° turns every vector of the plane, so it has no real eigenvector (Strang 2016, §6.1, Example 5). Symmetry excludes such matrices.

> [!example] The eigenpairs of a two-sector covariance matrix {#semifin}
> Find the eigenvalues and unit eigenvectors of $M=10^4\hat\Sigma$ for Semiconductor and FinancialInsurance, with $m_{11}=2.3234$, $m_{22}=1.4817$ and $m_{12}=1.0666$.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The trace is 3.8051, so $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}M=1.90255$. For a symmetric matrix the term under the root of (11.2) equals $\tfrac14(m_{11}-m_{22})^2+m_{12}^2$ (Section 3), here $0.17711+1.13764=1.31475$, with root 1.14663. So $\lambda_1=3.0492$ and $\lambda_2=0.7559$. They add up to the trace, and their product 2.3049 is the determinant $2.3234\times1.4817-1.0666^2$.
> >
> > For $\lambda_1$, the first row of $(M-\lambda_1I)\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$ reads $-0.7258\,x_1+1.0666\,x_2=0$. So $x_2/x_1=0.6805$, an angle of 34.2° from the Semiconductor axis, and the unit eigenvector is $\mathbf q_1=(0.827,\,0.563)^{\top}$. The second is perpendicular to it (Section 3), $\mathbf q_2=(-0.563,\,0.827)^{\top}$.

> [!pitfall] An eigenvalue is not a sector's variance
> Here $\lambda_1=3.0492$ exceeds both variances, 2.3234 and 1.4817, and $\lambda_2=0.7559$ is below both. Each eigenvalue is the variance of a mix along its eigenvector (Section 4). The mix 0.827 Semiconductor plus 0.563 FinancialInsurance has 1.31 times the variance of Semiconductor alone.

>
> > [!answer]-
> > They add up to 5 and multiply to 4, so they are 4 and 1, the roots of $\lambda_k^2-5\lambda_k+4=0$.

## Symmetric Matrices: the Spectral Theorem {lo=2}

Covariance and correlation matrices are symmetric, $m_{ij}=m_{ji}$, and symmetry buys three properties.

> [!theorem] Spectral theorem
> Let $M$ be a real symmetric $N\times N$ matrix. Then (i) all its eigenvalues are real; (ii) eigenvectors of different eigenvalues are orthogonal; (iii) one can choose mutually orthogonal unit eigenvectors $\mathbf q_1,\dots,\mathbf q_N$, so that $Q^{\top}Q=I$ and
> $$M=Q\Lambda Q^{\top}=\sum_{k=1}^N\lambda_k\,\mathbf q_k\mathbf q_k^{\top}. \tag{11.3}$$

*Proof for $N=2$.* (i) The term under the root of (11.2) is

$$\begin{aligned}&\tfrac14(m_{11}+m_{22})^2-(m_{11}m_{22}-m_{12}^2)\\&\quad=\tfrac14(m_{11}-m_{22})^2+m_{12}^2\ \ge0,\end{aligned}$$

so both roots are real. They are equal only when $m_{11}=m_{22}$ and $m_{12}=0$, that is, when $M$ is a multiple of $I$.

(ii) Let $M\mathbf x_1=\lambda_1\mathbf x_1$ and $M\mathbf x_2=\lambda_2\mathbf x_2$ with $\lambda_1\neq\lambda_2$, and read $\mathbf x_1^{\top}M\mathbf x_2$ two ways. Multiplying $M\mathbf x_2$ first gives $\lambda_2\,\mathbf x_1^{\top}\mathbf x_2$. By symmetry $\mathbf x_1^{\top}M=(M\mathbf x_1)^{\top}$, which gives $\lambda_1\,\mathbf x_1^{\top}\mathbf x_2$. So $(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)\,\mathbf x_1^{\top}\mathbf x_2=0$ and $\mathbf x_1^{\top}\mathbf x_2=0$, for any $N$.

(iii) Scale the eigenvectors to unit length; if $\lambda_1=\lambda_2$, $M$ is a multiple of $I$ and any orthonormal pair will do. The columns of $MQ$ are $\lambda_1\mathbf q_1$ and $\lambda_2\mathbf q_2$, so $MQ=Q\Lambda$. Orthonormal columns give $Q^{\top}Q=I$, hence $Q^{-1}=Q^{\top}$ and $M=Q\Lambda Q^{\top}$. Written as columns times rows, as in [[LM10]]'s (10.2), this product is the sum in (11.3). $\blacksquare$

Section 5 proves (i) and (iii) for every $N$. Three consequences follow from (11.3).

**Trace and determinant.** The diagonal of $\mathbf q_k\mathbf q_k^{\top}$ sums to $\mathbf q_k^{\top}\mathbf q_k=1$, so $\operatorname{tr}M=\sum_k\lambda_k$. Since $Q^{-1}=Q^{\top}$, also $QQ^{\top}=I$, and the product rule (9.3) gives $\det M=\prod_k\lambda_k$.

**Definiteness.** In the coordinates $\mathbf y=Q^{\top}\mathbf x$, (11.3) gives $\mathbf x^{\top}M\mathbf x=\sum_k\lambda_ky_k^2$. So $M$ is positive semidefinite exactly when every $\lambda_k\ge0$ (take $\mathbf x=\mathbf q_k$ for the converse), and positive definite exactly when every $\lambda_k>0$. This is [[LM10]]'s promised eigenvalue test, and a sample covariance matrix, positive semidefinite by (10.5), never has a negative eigenvalue.

**Signs and ties.** If $\mathbf q_k$ is a unit eigenvector, so is $-\mathbf q_k$. The book's sign rule makes the entry largest in absolute value positive, the first of them on a tie; a $2\times2$ correlation matrix thus has $\mathbf q_2=(1,-1)^{\top}/\sqrt2$. When two eigenvalues are equal, every unit vector in the plane of their eigenvectors is an eigenvector. Orthonormal eigenvectors can then be chosen, but only the plane, the **eigenspace**, is determined.

> [!pitfall] Comparing eigenvectors entry by entry
> An eigenvector's sign is a convention, and when two eigenvalues are close the vector itself can turn. Compare two periods' eigenvectors by $\lvert\cos\theta\rvert$, the absolute value of their dot product (Section 7), never entry by entry.

> [!zh] 對稱矩陣只會「拉伸」
> 矩陣把單位圓變成橢圓。大多數向量會被轉向，只有特徵向量（eigenvector）的方向不變，只被拉長或壓短，倍數就是特徵值（eigenvalue）。共變異數矩陣是對稱的，所以譜定理（spectral theorem）保證特徵值都是實數、特徵向量互相垂直，矩陣可以寫成幾個秩一方向的加總，這就是譜分解（spectral decomposition）。特徵值的和等於各變數變異數的總和；三個產業的例子中，第一個方向就占了 70.0%。特徵向量的正負號沒有意義，本書規定絕對值最大的元素為正；兩個特徵值相等時，只有它們張成的平面是確定的。

## Variance in a Direction: the Rayleigh Quotient and the Condition Number {lo=3,7}

Read $\mathbf u^{\top}\mathbf R_t$, for a unit vector $\mathbf u$, as a mix of the $N$ returns, rebalanced daily. Its sample variance is $\mathbf u^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf u$ ([[LM10]], (10.4)), the variance of the data in the direction $\mathbf u$. For any symmetric $M$ and any $\mathbf u\neq\mathbf 0$, the ratio

$$h(\mathbf u):=\frac{\mathbf u^{\top}M\mathbf u}{\mathbf u^{\top}\mathbf u}$$

is the **Rayleigh quotient**. Rescaling $\mathbf u$ does not change it, so unit vectors suffice.

> [!proposition] Bounds on the variance in a direction
> Let $M$ be symmetric with eigenvalues $\lambda_1\ge\dots\ge\lambda_N$. For every unit vector $\mathbf u$,
> $$\lambda_N\le\mathbf u^{\top}M\mathbf u\le\lambda_1, \tag{11.4}$$
> with equality on the right at $\mathbf u=\mathbf q_1$ and on the left at $\mathbf u=\mathbf q_N$.

*Proof.* Put $\mathbf y=Q^{\top}\mathbf u$. Since $QQ^{\top}=I$, $\lVert\mathbf y\rVert^2=\mathbf u^{\top}QQ^{\top}\mathbf u=1$. By (11.3), $\mathbf u^{\top}M\mathbf u=\mathbf y^{\top}\Lambda\mathbf y=\sum_k\lambda_ky_k^2$: an average of the eigenvalues with weights $y_k^2$ that sum to 1. It lies between the smallest and the largest eigenvalue, and $\mathbf u=\mathbf q_1$ puts all the weight on $\lambda_1$. $\blacksquare$

[[LM7]] used these bounds without proof: its smallest and largest curvatures of $\Sigma$ are $\lambda_2$ and $\lambda_1$ of [[eg:lm7sigma]]. [[ex:rayleigh]] traces $h(\mathbf u)$ for Semiconductor and FinancialInsurance.

**The condition number.** For a positive definite $M$, the **condition number** is $\kappa:=\lambda_1/\lambda_N$, the largest variance in any direction over the smallest. It is 1 for a multiple of $I$ and grows as the ellipse of [[ex:stretch]] flattens; a singular $M$ has an infinite $\kappa$.

A $2\times2$ correlation matrix has eigenvalues $1+\rho$ and $1-\rho$, with eigenvectors $(1,1)^{\top}/\sqrt2$ and $(1,-1)^{\top}/\sqrt2$, so

$$\kappa(C)=\frac{1+\lvert\rho\rvert}{1-\lvert\rho\rvert}.$$

This measures [[LM7]]'s warning about correlations near ±1. On [[LM8]]'s sample, 0050 and 00631L have $\rho=0.974365$, so $\kappa(C)=77.0$: a correlation that looks merely high makes the variance in one direction 77 times that in another ([[ex:ladder]]).

> [!example] Equal correlations {#equicorr}
> Let $N$ assets have the same correlation $\rho>0$, so $C=(1-\rho)I+\rho\,\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}$. Find its eigenvalues and $\kappa$. Then use $\mathbf u=\mathbf 1/\sqrt N$ in (11.4) to bound $\lambda_1$ of U28's correlation matrix, whose 756 off-diagonal entries average $\bar\rho=0.50227$.
>
> > [!solution]
> > Since $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf 1=N$, $C\mathbf 1=(1+(N-1)\rho)\mathbf 1$, so $1+(N-1)\rho$ is an eigenvalue. Every $\mathbf x$ in the space of dimension $N-1$ orthogonal to $\mathbf 1$ has $C\mathbf x=(1-\rho)\mathbf x$, so $1-\rho$ is an eigenvalue $N-1$ times, and $\kappa=(1+(N-1)\rho)/(1-\rho)$.
> >
> > For U28, $h(\mathbf 1/\sqrt N)=\mathbf 1^{\top}C\mathbf 1/N=1+27\times0.50227=14.561$, which by (11.4) is at most $\lambda_1$; indeed $\lambda_1=14.721$.

> [!deepdive]- Why LM7's gradient ascent contracts by (κ − 1)/(κ + 1)
> In [[LM7]]'s notation, $\mathbf f$ are exposures, $g$ is the quadratic growth model with Hessian $-\Sigma$ and optimum $\mathbf f^*$, and $\alpha$ is the step. Since $\nabla g(\mathbf f)=-\Sigma(\mathbf f-\mathbf f^*)$, a step to $\mathbf f+\alpha\nabla g(\mathbf f)$ turns the error $\mathbf f-\mathbf f^*$ into $(I-\alpha\Sigma)(\mathbf f-\mathbf f^*)$, which multiplies its component along $\mathbf q_k$ by $1-\alpha\lambda_k$. With $\alpha=2/(\lambda_1+\lambda_N)$ the extreme factors are $\pm(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$ and the others are smaller in size, so each step shrinks the distance to $\mathbf f^*$ at least by that factor. [[eg:lm7sigma]]'s eigenvalues give $(0.040789-0.017850)/(0.040789+0.017850)=0.391$ at $\alpha=34.1$, LM7's 0.39.

> [!pitfall] Say which matrix a condition number belongs to
> Rescaling the variables changes the eigenvalues, so $\kappa(C)\neq\kappa(\hat\Sigma)$: for U28 they are 70.6 and 108.1. A daily and an annualized $\hat\Sigma$ share one $\kappa$, because multiplying a matrix by $A$ multiplies every eigenvalue by $A$.

> [!zh] 方向上的變異數與條件數
> 把投資組合看成單位向量 $\mathbf u$，它的樣本變異數就是 $\mathbf u^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf u$。換到特徵向量（eigenvector）座標後，這個數是各特徵值（eigenvalue）的加權平均，權重加總為 1，所以一定落在最小與最大特徵值之間，最大值就在第一個特徵向量上。最大特徵值除以最小特徵值是條件數（condition number），代表橢圓有多扁。0050 與 00631L 的相關係數 0.974 看似只是「很高」，條件數卻是 77：某個方向的變異數是另一個方向的 77 倍。相關矩陣與共變異數矩陣的條件數不同，引用時要說清楚是哪一個。

> [!kc]
> Find the condition number of a $2\times2$ correlation matrix with $\rho=0.9$.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > The eigenvalues are $1\pm0.9$, so the condition number is $1.9/0.1=19$.

## Principal Component Analysis {lo=3,4}

Principal component analysis (PCA) asks which mix of the $N$ returns varies most, then which varies most among the mixes uncorrelated with it, and so on. A mix is a vector $\mathbf u$; its centred return on date $t$ is $\mathbf u^{\top}(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R})$, with sample variance $\mathbf u^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf u$.

The question needs a constraint, and portfolio weights, $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf u=1$, do not work. The vector $(a,1-a,0,\dots,0)^{\top}$ satisfies them for every number $a$, and its variance grows like $a^2$ times the variance of $R_1-R_2$. PCA fixes the length instead, $\mathbf u^{\top}\mathbf u=1$, and compares directions.

The Lagrangian is $\mathcal L=\mathbf u^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf u-\lambda(\mathbf u^{\top}\mathbf u-1)$, with gradient $2\hat\Sigma\mathbf u-2\lambda\mathbf u$ by [[LM10]]'s (10.8). Setting it to zero gives $\hat\Sigma\mathbf u=\lambda\mathbf u$. So **every stationary point is a unit eigenvector, and its multiplier is the eigenvalue**, which is also the variance there: $\mathbf u^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf u=\lambda\,\mathbf u^{\top}\mathbf u=\lambda$.

A continuous function on a closed and bounded set, such as the unit sphere, attains a largest value. This extreme value theorem is stated without proof; Strang and Herman (2016, §4.7, Theorem 4.18) give the case of two variables. So the maximum is the stationary point with the largest value:

$$\max_{\mathbf u^{\top}\mathbf u=1}\mathbf u^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf u=\lambda_1,\quad\text{attained at }\mathbf u=\mathbf q_1 . \tag{11.5}$$

Maximizing again over unit vectors orthogonal to $\mathbf q_1$ gives $\mathbf q_2$, with variance $\lambda_2$, and so on. The directions $\mathbf q_k$ are the **principal components**, and their entries are **loadings**.

> [!deepdive]- PCA proves the spectral theorem for every N
> Let $M$ be real symmetric, with Lagrange multipliers $\lambda$ and $\eta$. The maximum of $\mathbf u^{\top}M\mathbf u$ on the unit sphere exists, and the Lagrange condition makes it a real eigenpair $(\lambda_1,\mathbf q_1)$. Next maximize over unit vectors with $\mathbf q_1^{\top}\mathbf u=0$. The gradient of $\mathbf u^{\top}M\mathbf u-\lambda(\mathbf u^{\top}\mathbf u-1)-\eta\,\mathbf q_1^{\top}\mathbf u$ is $2M\mathbf u-2\lambda\mathbf u-\eta\mathbf q_1$, and its dot product with $\mathbf q_1$ is $-\eta$, since $\mathbf q_1^{\top}M\mathbf u=\lambda_1\mathbf q_1^{\top}\mathbf u=0$. So $\eta=0$, and the maximizer is a real eigenpair $(\lambda_2,\mathbf q_2)$ with $\mathbf q_2\perp\mathbf q_1$. Repeating $N$ times gives (11.3), with no complex numbers and no characteristic polynomial.

**Shares.** The eigenvalues add up to the trace, the sum of the $N$ variances, so component $k$ carries the **explained share**

$$\frac{\lambda_k}{\operatorname{tr}\hat\Sigma}=\frac{\lambda_k}{\sum_m\lambda_m}\qquad\Big(=\frac{\lambda_k}{N}\ \text{ for }C\Big) \tag{11.6}$$

of the total variance. A **scree plot** shows the shares against $k$ ([[ex:scree]]).

> [!example] How many components does U28 need? {#shares}
> The correlation matrix of U28 has $\lambda_1=14.7214$ and $\lambda_2=1.4837$. Find their shares and the number of components needed to pass 80% of the total variance.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The trace is $N=28$, so the shares are $14.7214/28=52.58\%$ and $1.4837/28=5.30\%$. The later shares decline slowly: 11 components carry 79.31% and 12 carry 80.93%, so 12 are needed.

**Scores.** The score of component $k$ on date $t$ in correlation PCA is

$$\xi_{k,t}=\mathbf q_k^{\top}\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf s)^{-1}(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R}),$$

the day's standardized returns read along $\mathbf q_k$. PCA turns $N$ correlated series into $N$ uncorrelated ones, ranked by variance.

## Overlapping Indices and Near-Zero Eigenvalues {lo=4,7}

If $\lambda_N$ is close to zero, the mix $\mathbf q_N^{\top}(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R})$ has a mean square of only $\lambda_N$ and stays near zero, so the series obey a near-exact linear relation. When $\lambda_N=0$ the relation is exact and $\hat\Sigma$ is singular, the perfect collinearity of [[LM9]]. How small counts as zero in floating point is a question of numerical rank ([[LM12]]).

> [!example] An aggregate and its members {#overlap}
> Electronic is a market-value-weighted price index of the stocks in its eight electronics sub-industries. Read the smallest eigenpair of the correlation matrix of Electronic and the eight.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The smallest eigenvalue is $\lambda_9=0.0073$, against $\lambda_1=6.104$, so $\kappa(C)=836$. Its eigenvector has entries 0.828 for Electronic and −0.529 for Semiconductor, and the other seven are at most 0.104 in size. With almost no variance along $\mathbf q_9$, the standardized Electronic return stays close to 0.64 times the standardized Semiconductor return, plus smaller multiples of the others.

> > [!answer]-

## Eigenvalue Gaps and the Stability of Components {lo=5,6}

How far does an eigenvector turn when the matrix changes a little? Rotate the axes of a symmetric $2\times2$ matrix by an angle $\theta$. The unit vectors $\mathbf q_1=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^{\top}$ and $\mathbf q_2=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)^{\top}$ are its eigenvectors exactly when $M\mathbf q_1$ has no component along $\mathbf q_2$:

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf q_2^{\top}M\mathbf q_1={}&m_{12}\cos2\theta\\&-\tfrac12(m_{11}-m_{22})\sin2\theta=0 .\end{aligned}$$

Start from a diagonal matrix with neighbouring eigenvalues $\lambda_k>\lambda_{k+1}$, and add a small $\varepsilon$ to both off-diagonal entries. The eigenvectors turn by

$$\begin{gathered}\theta=\tfrac12\arctan\frac{2\varepsilon}{\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1}}\approx\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1}},\\ \text{from}\quad\tan2\theta=\frac{2m_{12}}{m_{11}-m_{22}}.\end{gathered} \tag{11.9}$$

The arctangent is exact; the approximation drops terms of third order in $\varepsilon$ over the gap. **The rotation is the perturbation divided by the gap**, so a gap no larger than $\varepsilon$ can turn the eigenvectors by tens of degrees, up to 45°. Equal correlations are the limit, with every gap below $\lambda_1$ zero ([[eg:equicorr]]).

> [!example] How far noise can turn a component {#gap}
> U28's correlation matrix has $\lambda_2=1.48370$, $\lambda_3=1.14694$, $\lambda_7=0.58028$ and $\lambda_8=0.54445$. Let noise add $\varepsilon=0.05$ to the entry linking two neighbouring eigenvectors. Find the rotation by (11.9), exactly and to first order.
>
> > [!solution]
> > For components 2 and 3 the gap is 0.33676, and $\varepsilon$ over the gap is 0.148. The exact angle is $\tfrac12\arctan(0.1/0.33676)=8.27^\circ$; to first order it is 0.148 radians, $8.51^\circ$.
> >
> > For components 7 and 8 the gap is 0.03583, smaller than $\varepsilon$. The exact angle is $35.1^\circ$, but the first-order formula gives 1.40 radians, $80.0^\circ$. It fails once $\varepsilon$ is not small next to the gap, and components 7 and 8 are barely determined.

**Calendar years.** Each year from 2009 to 2026 (2026 to 8 October, 185 returns) gives its own correlation matrix. December 2008's 22 returns enter only the full sample and the halves. The first component's share moves from 34.5% (2017) to 66.6% (2020) ([[ex:yshare]]). Its direction hardly moves: its cosine with the full sample's $\mathbf q_1$ is at least 0.981 every year ([[ex:ycos]]).

The second component's cosine runs from 0.119 (2016) to 0.946 (2025). When $\lambda_2$ and $\lambda_3$ are close, one period's $\mathbf q_2$ can be a mix of another period's $\mathbf q_2$ and $\mathbf q_3$. With the first half as the reference, the second half's $\mathbf q_2$ has the **plane measure**

$$\sqrt{\big(\mathbf q_2^{(1)\top}\mathbf q_2^{(2)}\big)^2+\big(\mathbf q_3^{(1)\top}\mathbf q_2^{(2)}\big)^2}.$$

Since $\mathbf q_2^{(1)}$ and $\mathbf q_3^{(1)}$ are orthonormal, this measure is the cosine of the angle between $\mathbf q_2^{(2)}$ and their plane. For a year, against the full sample, it runs from 0.690 (2020) to 0.959 (2024).

> Comparing the halves shows how much the estimates move; it tests nothing. ## The Inverse of $\hat\Sigma$ and Error Amplification {lo=7}

Rescaled to sum to 1, the solution of $\hat\Sigma\mathbf x=\mathbf 1$ is the minimum-variance portfolio of [[LM28]]; here it is only a linear system. When every $\lambda_k>0$, the inverse of $Q\Lambda Q^{\top}$ is $Q\Lambda^{-1}Q^{\top}$, because their product is $QQ^{\top}=I$. Written as a sum,

$$\hat\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf b=\sum_{k=1}^N\frac{\mathbf q_k^{\top}\mathbf b}{\lambda_k}\,\mathbf q_k. \tag{11.7}$$

Each direction enters with the weight $1/\lambda_k$. **The smallest eigenvalues get the largest weights**, and Section 7 showed that their eigenvectors are the least stable.

**The spectral norm.** The **spectral norm** $\lVert M\rVert_2$ is the largest length of $M\mathbf u$ over unit vectors $\mathbf u$. For symmetric $M$, $\lVert M\mathbf u\rVert^2=\mathbf u^{\top}M^2\mathbf u$ and $M^2=Q\Lambda^2Q^{\top}$, so by (11.4) $\lVert M\rVert_2$ is the largest $\lvert\lambda_k\rvert$. A positive definite $M$ thus has $\lVert M\rVert_2=\lambda_1$, $\lVert M^{-1}\rVert_2=1/\lambda_N$ and $\kappa=\lVert M\rVert_2\lVert M^{-1}\rVert_2$.

> [!proposition] How far a solution can move
> Let $M$ be positive definite with $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, and let a change $\Delta M$ move the solution to $\mathbf x+\Delta\mathbf x$, so that $(M+\Delta M)(\mathbf x+\Delta\mathbf x)=\mathbf b$. Then
> $$\frac{\lVert\Delta\mathbf x\rVert}{\lVert\mathbf x+\Delta\mathbf x\rVert}\le\kappa\,\frac{\lVert\Delta M\rVert_2}{\lVert M\rVert_2}. \tag{11.8}$$

*Proof.* Subtracting $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ leaves $M\Delta\mathbf x=-\Delta M(\mathbf x+\Delta\mathbf x)$, so $\Delta\mathbf x=-M^{-1}\Delta M(\mathbf x+\Delta\mathbf x)$. Since $\lVert K\mathbf y\rVert\le\lVert K\rVert_2\lVert\mathbf y\rVert$ for any matrix $K$ and vector $\mathbf y$, $\lVert\Delta\mathbf x\rVert\le\lVert M^{-1}\rVert_2\lVert\Delta M\rVert_2\lVert\mathbf x+\Delta\mathbf x\rVert$. Finally $\lVert M^{-1}\rVert_2=1/\lambda_N=\kappa/\lVert M\rVert_2$. $\blacksquare$

The change $\Delta M$ is usually indefinite, so $\lVert\Delta M\rVert_2$ is its largest eigenvalue in absolute value, not its $\lambda_1$. The bound is a worst case, nearly reached only when the change pushes the solution along the eigenvector of $\lambda_N$.

> [!example] A bound nearly reached {#tight}
> Let $C$ be the correlation matrix of two assets with $\rho=0.95$, and solve $C\mathbf x=\mathbf e_1$. Then raise $\rho$ to 0.96, and compare the relative change of the solution with the bound (11.8) for $M=C$.
>
> > [!solution]
> > By [[LM9]]'s (9.5), $\mathbf x=(1,-\rho)^{\top}/(1-\rho^2)$. At $\rho=0.95$, $\mathbf x=(10.256,\,-9.744)^{\top}$; at 0.96, $\mathbf x+\Delta\mathbf x=(12.755,\,-12.245)^{\top}$. So $\Delta\mathbf x=(2.499,\,-2.501)^{\top}$, and the relative change is 0.19996.
> >
> > Here $\kappa=1.95/0.05=39$, and $\Delta M$ has 0.01 off the diagonal, with eigenvalues ±0.01, so $\lVert\Delta M\rVert_2/\lVert M\rVert_2=0.01/1.95=0.005128$. The bound $39\times0.005128=0.2000$ is almost reached, because $\Delta\mathbf x$ lies along $(1,-1)^{\top}/\sqrt2$, the eigenvector of the small eigenvalue $1-\rho$.

> A large $\kappa$ does not mean that a computed solution is wrong; it says how far the solution could move for a given change in the matrix. Nor does it rank realized changes. S5 has $\kappa=10.9$ and its solution moved by 0.910, while U28 has $\kappa=126$ and moved by 0.729 ([[ex:solve]]).

> [!zh] 反矩陣為什麼會放大誤差
> 由譜分解（spectral decomposition），$\hat\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf b$ 把每個特徵向量（eigenvector）方向乘上 $1/\lambda_k$，特徵值（eigenvalue）越小的方向權重越大。偏偏最小的那些特徵值彼此接近、方向最不穩定，所以解 $\hat\Sigma\mathbf x=\mathbf 1$ 時，矩陣只動了 24.5%，解卻動了 72.9%。條件數（condition number）$\kappa$ 給的是最壞情況：相對誤差最多放大 $\kappa$ 倍，28 個產業的上界是 30.93，實際是 0.729。條件數大不代表算錯，也不能用來排序實際變動的大小。把小特徵值往上拉可以降低條件數，這是 [[LM30]] 的主題。

> [!kc]
> Where does most of $\hat\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1$ come from, the first component or the last ones? Why?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > From the last ones. By (11.7) component $k$ enters with the weight $1/\lambda_k$, so small eigenvalues dominate unless $\mathbf q_k^{\top}\mathbf 1$ is tiny. For U28 the 14 smallest components give 72.0% and 80.5% of $\lVert\mathbf x\rVert^2$.

## Eigen-decomposition in Python {lo=1,6}

For symmetric matrices, NumPy's `eigh` returns the eigenvalues in ascending order, so reverse them, and signs the eigenvectors arbitrarily, so apply the sign rule. It uses an iterative method, not the characteristic polynomial (stated without proof; Golub and Van Loan 2013, ch. 8). The same components also come from the singular value decomposition of $\tilde X$, the route software prefers ([[LM12]]).

`np.linalg.cond` uses the spectral norm, so it equals $\lambda_1/\lambda_N$ only for symmetric positive definite input. Solve systems with `solve`, never with `inv` ([[LM9]]).

```python
import numpy as np

def eig_desc(M):
    """Eigenpairs of a symmetric M, largest first, signed by the book's rule."""
    lam, Q = np.linalg.eigh(M)              # ascending order
    lam, Q = lam[::-1], Q[:, ::-1].copy()   # largest first
    for k in range(Q.shape[1]):
        a = np.abs(Q[:, k])
        i = np.flatnonzero(a >= a.max() * (1 - 1e-12))[0]   # first of ties
        if Q[i, k] < 0:
            Q[:, k] = -Q[:, k]
    return lam, Q

# X: the T x N array of daily returns (lab, Part 1)
S = np.cov(X, rowvar=False, ddof=0)
lam, Q = eig_desc(np.corrcoef(X, rowvar=False))
print(lam[:2] / lam.sum())                    # explained shares (11.6)
lamS, QS = eig_desc(S)
print(np.linalg.cond(S), lamS[0] / lamS[-1])  # equal: S is SPD
x = np.linalg.solve(S, np.ones(X.shape[1]))   # never inv(S) @ ones
```

## Summary {-}

- An eigenvector is a direction that a matrix only stretches, $M\mathbf x=\lambda_k\mathbf x$; the eigenvalues solve $\det(M-\lambda_kI)=0$ (11.1)–(11.2) and add up to the trace.
- A real symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors (11.3); it is positive semidefinite exactly when every $\lambda_k\ge0$.
- Every unit direction has a variance between $\lambda_N$ and $\lambda_1$ (11.4). The condition number $\kappa=\lambda_1/\lambda_N$ is 70.6 for U28's correlation matrix and 77.0 for 0050 and 00631L.
- The first principal component maximizes variance over unit vectors, with the eigenvalue as multiplier (11.5). In U28 it carries 52.6% of the standardized variance; 12 components reach 80%.
- Eigenvectors turn by about the perturbation divided by the gap (11.9): U28's first component is stable (cosine at least 0.981), its second is not (0.297).
- The inverse weights direction $k$ by $1/\lambda_k$ (11.7), so solutions come mostly from the least stable directions. The bound (11.8) is a worst case: 30.93 for U28's halves, against a realized 0.729.

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Eigenvalue, eigenvector | 特徵值、特徵向量 | $M\mathbf x=\lambda_k\mathbf x$ with $\mathbf x\neq\mathbf 0$: a direction that $M$ only stretches, and the factor. |
| Characteristic equation | 特徵方程式 | $\det(M-\lambda_kI)=0$, (11.1); for $2\times2$, (11.2). |
| Trace | 跡 | Sum of the diagonal entries; the sum of the eigenvalues. |
| Spectral theorem | 譜定理 | A real symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors. |
| Spectral decomposition | 譜分解 | $M=Q\Lambda Q^{\top}=\sum_k\lambda_k\mathbf q_k\mathbf q_k^{\top}$, (11.3). |
| Eigenspace | 特徵空間 | All eigenvectors of one eigenvalue, with $\mathbf 0$. |
| Sign rule | 符號規則 | The book's convention: an eigenvector's entry largest in absolute value is positive. |
| Rayleigh quotient | Rayleigh 商 | $h(\mathbf u)=\mathbf u^{\top}M\mathbf u/\mathbf u^{\top}\mathbf u$; between $\lambda_N$ and $\lambda_1$, (11.4). |
| Condition number | 條件數 | $\kappa=\lambda_1/\lambda_N$ for a positive definite matrix. |
| Spectral norm | 譜範數 | $\lVert M\rVert_2$, the largest length of $M\mathbf u$ over unit vectors. |
| Principal component analysis (PCA) | 主成分分析 | Directions of largest variance, one after another, (11.5). |
| Explained share | 解釋比例 | $\lambda_k$ divided by the trace, (11.6). |
| Loading | 負荷量 | An entry of a principal component $\mathbf q_k$. |
| Score | 主成分分數 | $\xi_{k,t}$, a day's standardized returns read along $\mathbf q_k$. |
| Plane measure | 平面度量 | Cosine of the angle between one period's $\mathbf q_2$ and the plane of another period's $\mathbf q_2$ and $\mathbf q_3$. |
| Stability check | 穩定性檢查 | The same calculation on two periods, compared; not an out-of-sample test. |
