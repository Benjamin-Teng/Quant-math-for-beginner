## Introduction

A backtest is an experiment with hidden choices: dates, universe, costs, and the versions tried. [[LM27]], [[LM28]], [[LM29]], [[LM30]] and [[LM31]] built eight rules, [[LM33]] measures their Sharpe ratios, and this module tests the procedure behind them.

> [!case] The grid's winner
> From March 2019 to October 2026, the through case's eight rules grew between 8.70% (R3) and 14.49% (R2) a year after Taiwan's commission and tax. Now vary each rule's window (126, 252 or 504 joint dates) and how often it trades (every one, three or six month-ends). R1 and R2 use no window. That gives 60 configurations. The best, maximum Sharpe on a 252-date window traded every six month-ends, grew 18.62% a year.
>
> The median configuration grew 10.96%, and maximum Sharpe as fixed in advance (R4) grew 10.51%. Would you trade the winner? Three questions come first. Did the test use only what was known at each decision? Did its universe include the funds that later vanished?
>
> Of the six gold, oil and VIX futures funds trading in January 2017, two were delisted. Equal amounts in all six lost 27.7%, while the four survivors gained 5.4%. And could the search alone have produced 18.62%?

## The Walk-Forward Protocol {lo=1}

[[LM11]] defined an out-of-sample test: fit on earlier data, and judge on later data that the fit never used. A walk-forward test repeats that step at every decision.

> [!definition] Walk-forward protocol
> A walk-forward test fixes in advance its decision dates, estimation windows, trade timing, costs, valuation and criteria. At each decision it estimates from data known by then and trades at the first close it can reach.

The through case's protocol is [[LM27]]'s:

1. **Signal.** Each month's last session, from the trailing 504 joint dates of the estimation frame. Its close is known only after 13:30, when matching ends on TWSE (Operating Rules, Art. 3) and on TPEx, 00679B's market (Business Rules, Art. 40; ETF rules, Art. 4 ¶2).
2. **Trade.** At the close of the next session on which every fund trades. The first signal, 27 February 2019, traded on 4 March, and the last, 30 September 2026, on 1 October.
3. **Start and end.** Every rule starts in cash, so its first purchase pays costs. Valuation on 8 October 2026 sells nothing, so no final sale is charged.

The window rolls: each signal uses only the latest 504 joint dates, where an expanding window keeps all earlier dates. Choosing a backtested strategy's leverage is [[LM45]]'s subject, and each rule's exposure is taken as given.

A model choice is tested the same way. [[LM26]] scored six forecasts out of sample by QLIKE (26.8), but named the best on the same 4,609 days. A later test would score that choice on new days.

> [!result] Gross against net wealth
> Let the gross path trade to the net path's targets on the same dates, without commission and tax. Suppose that
> 1. costs are scale-free, $\operatorname{cost}(a\mathbf x)=a\operatorname{cost}(\mathbf x)$ for $a>0$, as (27.2)'s are;
> 2. cash interest and borrowing accrue in proportion to wealth, at rates set by the book's exposure.
>
> Then
> $$\begin{aligned}\ln\frac{W^{\text{gross}}_{\text{end}}}{W^{\text{net}}_{\text{end}}}&=-\sum_{\text{trades}}\ln\Big(1-\frac{\operatorname{cost}}{W^{\mathrm{pre}}}\Big)\\&\ge\sum_{\text{trades}}\frac{\operatorname{cost}}{W^{\mathrm{pre}}}.\end{aligned} \tag{34.1}$$

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: the log gap between gross and net wealth
> After a trade both paths hold $W_t\mathbf f_t$ in the funds and the rest in cash. Their ratio then stays fixed, and both reach the next trade with the same $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t$. Write $W_t=(1-x_t)W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$ for the net path. Dividing (27.2) by $W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$ gives, for scale-free costs,
> $$x_t=\operatorname{cost}\big((1-x_t)\mathbf f_t-\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t\big),$$
> so $x_t=\operatorname{cost}/W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$ depends on $\mathbf f_t$ and $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t$ alone, while the gross path keeps $W_t=W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$. Each trade multiplies $W^{\text{gross}}/W^{\text{net}}$ by $1/(1-x_t)$, which gives the equality. The bound follows from $\ln(1-x)\le-x$ for $0\le x<1$.

For R1, (34.1) gives a log gap of 0.0080768 against the cost sum 0.0080755, and on every rule it holds to below $2\times10^{-14}$. [[ex:grossnet]] splits gross growth into net growth and costs.

> [!pitfall] One constant subtracted at the end
> An average cost subtracted from final growth charges every rule alike, yet R4's costs are 4.22% of wealth and R2's 0.64%. Costs belong in (27.2), at each trade.

> [!zh] 成本要放進每一筆交易
> 交易成本（transaction cost）包括買賣兩邊各 0.1425% 的手續費（commission），以及賣出時的證券交易稅（securities transaction tax）：一般 ETF 與期貨 ETF 是 0.1%，債券 ETF 在 2026 年底前免徵。0.1425% 是參考費率，券商收得比它高時必須事先告知客戶，所以它不是上限。成本在每次交易時從財富扣除，週轉率（turnover）高的規則付得多，不能在最後一次扣掉一個平均值。每筆交易的成本除以交易前的財富，稱為成本比例（cost share）。若成本與交易金額成正比、利息與財富成正比，且扣除成本前後的兩條財富路徑交易到相同的目標，兩者財富的對數差距（log gap）就等於各次交易 −ln(1 − 成本比例) 的總和（式 (34.1)），比成本比例直接相加略大。

> [!kc]

>
> > [!answer]-

## Look-Ahead Bias {lo=3}

> [!definition] Look-ahead bias
> A backtest has look-ahead bias when a decision uses information not known when it acted, such as a later price, a revised series or a parameter fitted on later data. The bias is measured by rerunning the test with only what each decision knew.

Adjusted prices are a common case. A series adjusted for distributions and splits is built backward from its last day $t'$:

$$\begin{gathered}P^{\mathrm{adj}}_{t\mid t'}=\frac{P_{t'}}{G_{t,t'}},\\ G_{t,t'}=(1+R_{t+1})(1+R_{t+2})\cdots(1+R_{t'}).\end{gathered} \tag{34.2}$$

Each $R$ is Volume I's total return: the close plus any distribution, over the previous close, restated per current share on a split day. So $P^{\mathrm{adj}}_{t\mid t'}/P^{\mathrm{adj}}_{t-1\mid t'}=1+R_t$ for every build date $t'$: returns do not depend on $t'$, but levels do. A level is look-ahead for any decision before its build date.

> [!example] One close, three build dates (descriptive) {#builddate}
> 0050 closed at 72.30 on 17 January 2017. What does that day read in adjusted series built later?
>
> > [!solution]
> > It reads 65.12 in a series built on 2 January 2020 and 55.20 in one built on 31 December 2024. Built on 8 October 2026, after 20 distributions and the 4-for-1 split of 18 June 2025, it reads 13.25. February 2017's NT\$1.70 yielded 2.32% of the previous close but 12.65% in 2026's series ([[ex:adjusted]]).

## Survivorship Bias {lo=4}

A list of today's funds omits those that vanished. FinMind's `TaiwanStockDelisting` names the delisted ETFs, and price snapshots give the funds that traded on a date.

> >

The six are futures-trust ETFs on futures indices, all listed on TWSE. Three carry a daily multiple: 00672L +2× crude oil, 00673R −1× crude oil and 00674R −1× gold. A multiple holds for one day only, so under daily compounding their long-run returns depart from it (TWSE's ETF overview, §四), and the gap mixes product types. The repair is a data policy set before any result: fix the universe from a dated list of the funds listed on TWSE or TPEx (Research hygiene). A delisted fund's last close is an approximation, since FinMind does not record its holders' payout. Funds that disappear tend to do so after poor performance (Elton, Gruber and Blake 1996, abstract).

> [!zh] 只看今天還在的基金會高估過去
> 前視偏誤（look-ahead bias）是決策用到當時還不知道的資訊，例如用日後的配息回頭調整過的價格，或用全部資料估計的參數。存活者偏誤（survivorship bias）是只從今天還在交易的基金挑選，看不到已經下市（delisting）的基金。2017 年 1 月的六檔黃金、石油與 VIX 期貨 ETF 有兩檔後來下市：等金額持有六檔虧損 27.7%，只看存活的四檔卻賺 5.4%。其中三檔是單日正向兩倍或反向一倍，長期報酬會偏離倍數；期貨 ETF 遇到正價差（contango）時，轉倉（rollover）可能產生損失。

>
> > [!answer]-
> > 00672L, the +2× crude-oil fund. It last traded on 12 November 2020 and was delisted the next day, with growth factor $G=0.038$.

## Correcting a Search on the Same Data {lo=5,8}

[[LM21]]'s Experiment 10 judged the best of 10,000 random rules on TR23, the TAIEX total-return index, by (21.9) for independent rules. White's (2000) reality check corrects this data snooping while keeping the rules' dependence.

His null is that no rule beats the benchmark: every true mean difference is at most 0. He resamples blocks of days with the stationary bootstrap (22.3), so the rules' correlations survive, and subtracts from each resampled mean the sample mean $\bar R_k$. The reality-check p-value is the share of the $B$ resamples in which

$$\max_k\sqrt T\big(\bar R_{k,\text{boot}}-\bar R_k\big)\ \ge\ \max_k\sqrt T\,\bar R_k. \tag{34.3}$$

Dividing each rule's terms by its $s_{\mathrm u,k}$ gives the studentized form, which compares largest t-stats (Romano and Wolf 2005, §4.1; Hansen 2005).

> [!result] White's reality check (White 2000, Corollary 2.4; stated without proof)
> Let the rules be fixed in advance, with no estimated parameters, and let their number stay fixed as $T$ grows. Suppose that
> 1. each difference from the benchmark has a finite moment of order $6+\varepsilon$, for some $\varepsilon>0$;
> 2. the differences form a strictly stationary series whose dependence, measured by α-mixing coefficients, falls with the lag at least as fast as a power whose exponent exceeds $3(6+\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$;
> 3. their long-run covariance matrix is positive definite (White notes that a singular one also works);
> 4. the mean block length grows more slowly than $T$: $\ell\to\infty$ and $\ell/T\to0$.
>
> Then, as $T\to\infty$, the law of the resampled maximum in (34.3), given the data, approaches that of the observed maximum when every true mean difference is 0.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: why the resamples are recentred
> A p-value must hold at every point of the null, and the hardest point is where every mean difference is 0. A resample's means scatter around the sample means $\bar R_k$, so subtracting them makes the resampled maxima behave as at that point. White's Corollary 2.4 gives their law for large $T$.

Large $T$ here means 5,849 days for Experiment 10, whose 10,000 rules outnumber them, outside White's fixed number of rules. For the grid's 59 differences it means 1,851, closer to his setting. The grid's estimates are fixed functions of past data, so this book reads its rules as fixed in advance.

Romano and Wolf (2005) ask which rules beat the benchmark. Their stepdown (Algorithm 3.1) rejects each rule above the bootstrap 95% point of the largest centred statistic among the rules left, and repeats. Its first step is [[eg:rc10]]'s 95% point, with the sample's $s_{\mathrm u}$ (their footnote 22) and centred at the sample means (their Remark 3.4).

Hansen's (2005) test for superior predictive ability (SPA) floors the largest t-stat at 0. It recentres only the rules whose t-stat is at least $-\sqrt{2\ln\ln T}$, the version with a consistent p-value (his Corollary 2). On Experiment 10 that threshold is −2.079, so it recentres 9,814 rules and gives 0.277 (MC SE 0.0045). The code's iid $s_{\mathrm u}$ replaces his kernel estimate, and his strict count gives the same value.

Both tests rest on large-$T$ bootstrap theory beyond this book and are stated without proof. Other data-snooping tests are not covered, and Harvey, Liu and Zhu's hurdle of 3 is [[LM21]]'s. Sullivan, Timmermann and White (1999) applied the reality check to technical trading rules on 100 years of daily Dow Jones data (their abstract).

> [!pitfall] Best, not which
> The reality check asks whether the best rule beats the benchmark. A large p-value does not show that the rules are equal, and a small one does not say which rules win: that is the stepdown's question.

> [!zh] 在同一份資料上挑出最好的
> 在同一份資料上試很多規則、只留下最好的一個，就是資料偵伺（data snooping）：試得愈多，最好的那個愈可能只是運氣。Bonferroni 校正（Bonferroni correction）控制族系誤差率（familywise error rate），卻不利用規則之間的相關（correlation）。White 的 reality check 用平穩自助重抽法（stationary bootstrap）整段重抽日期，保留規則之間的相關，再從每個規則重抽的平均數（mean）扣掉它的樣本值，看最好的統計量（statistic）是否超出偶然能解釋的範圍。它只回答最好的規則是否勝過基準（benchmark），不回答哪些規則勝過基準。

> [!definition] Combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV)
> Take $n$ strategies' daily returns on the same $T$ days (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu 2017, Algorithm 2.3, in this book's words).
> 1. Cut its rows into $S$ equal blocks, $S$ even, dropping the earliest rows that do not fill a block.
> 2. For each choice of $S/2$ blocks, call those blocks, in time order, the in-sample set and the others the out-of-sample set.
> 3. On the in-sample set find the best strategy $k^*$ by Sharpe ratio.
> 4. On the out-of-sample set rank $k^*$ among the $n$, with $j=1$ the worst, and set $\omega=j/(n+1)$.
>
> The probability of backtest overfitting (PBO) is the share of splits in which $k^*$ falls in the lower half out of sample:
> $$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{PBO}=\frac{\#\{\text{splits with logit}\le0\}}{\binom{S}{S/2}},\\ \text{logit}=\ln\frac{\omega}{1-\omega}.\end{gathered} \tag{34.4}$$

With $S=16$ there are $\binom{16}{8}=12{,}870$ splits (the manuscript prints 12,780). Dropping the earliest rows is this book's convention. Daily returns do not overlap, so cross-validation that removes overlaps is not needed.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: counting the splits
> A split chooses 8 of 16 blocks, and sorting the choices by whether block 1 is among them gives Pascal's rule,
> $$\begin{aligned}\binom{16}{8}&=\binom{15}{7}+\binom{15}{8}\\&=6{,}435+6{,}435=12{,}870,\end{aligned}$$
> the terms equal by symmetry. Each split's out-of-sample set is itself a choice of 8, so every split appears with its halves swapped. A strategy's out-of-sample sum is then its full-sample sum minus the in-sample one.

## Backtesting in Python {lo=1,5,6}

`vol5_rules.wealth_path(fr, sigs, rule, cost_mult)` books (27.2) every session, with `cost_mult=0` for the gross path. The reality check uses [[LM22]]'s `stationary_idx`, which draws in arch's `StationaryBootstrap` order (the lab checks it), and CSCV needs only block sums.

```python
import numpy as np
from itertools import combinations

def reality_check(d, ell, B, rng):    # d: T days x rules, minus benchmark
    T = len(d)
    dm, se = d.mean(0), d.std(0, ddof=1) / np.sqrt(T)
    mx = np.empty(B)
    for b in range(B):                 # stationary_idx: LM22's
        cnt = np.bincount(stationary_idx(rng, T, ell), minlength=T)
        mx[b] = ((cnt @ d / T - dm) / se).max()
    return (mx >= (dm / se).max() - 1e-12).mean()   # (34.3), studentized

def cscv(ret, S=16):                  # ret: T x n daily excess returns
    T, n = ret.shape
    blen = T // S                      # block length; the earliest rows go
    blk = ret[T - blen * S:].reshape(S, blen, n)
    s1, s2 = blk.sum(1), (blk**2).sum(1)            # S x n block sums
    cmb = list(combinations(range(S), S // 2))
    mask = np.zeros((len(cmb), S))
    for r, chosen in enumerate(cmb):
        mask[r, list(chosen)] = 1.0
    half = blen * (S // 2)             # days in each half
    i1, i2 = mask @ s1, mask @ s2      # in-sample sums, splits x n
    o1, o2 = s1.sum(0) - i1, s2.sum(0) - i2
    sr_i = i1 / half / np.sqrt(i2 / half - (i1 / half) ** 2)
    sr_o = o1 / half / np.sqrt(o2 / half - (o1 / half) ** 2)
    best = sr_i.argmax(1)              # k*, the first on a tie
    so = sr_o[np.arange(len(cmb)), best]
    j = 1 + (sr_o < so[:, None]).sum(1)             # rank, 1 = worst
    omega = j / (n + 1)
    return np.log(omega / (1 - omega))  # PBO = (logits <= 0).mean()
```

On the grid's 59 differences, `ell=75`, `B=10000` and `default_rng([4, 20261011])` give [[eg:rcgrid]]'s 0.375.

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Backtest | 回測 | A test of a rule on past data, run as the rule would have run. |
| Walk-forward test | 滾動前進測試 | A backtest that, at each decision, estimates from data known by then and trades at the next close it can reach. |
| Fixed in advance | — | Settled, with its dates, windows, costs and criteria, before any result is computed. |
| Out of sample | 樣本外 | Judged on later data that the fit never used. |
| Transaction cost | 交易成本 | Commission on both sides and tax on sales, paid at the trade, (27.2). |
| Commission | 手續費 | The broker's fee, 0.1425% of the value traded on each side in this book. |
| Securities transaction tax | 證券交易稅 | The tax on a sale: 0.1% for ETFs and futures ETFs, 0 for bond ETFs to 2026. |
| Turnover | 週轉率 | One-way $\mathrm{TO}_t$, (27.3), summed over the trades after the first purchase, per year. |
| Gross wealth | — | A rule's wealth without commission and tax, financing kept; (34.1) links it to the net. |
| Look-ahead bias | 前視偏誤 | The use of information not known when a decision was made. |
| Adjusted price | — | A price restated for later distributions and splits; its level depends on the build date, (34.2). |
| Survivorship bias | 存活者偏誤 | The bias of a universe drawn only from what still exists. |
| Delisting | 下市 | The end of a security's listing. |
| Contango | 正價差 | Far futures priced above near ones, TWSE's word. |
| Data snooping | 資料偵伺 | Searching many rules on the same data and keeping the best. |
| Familywise error rate | 族系誤差率 | The probability of at least one false rejection among several tests ([[LM21]]). |
| Stationary bootstrap | 平穩自助重抽法 | Resampling blocks of geometric length, (22.3). |
| Reality check | — | White's test of whether the best of a search beats a benchmark, (34.3). |
| Benchmark | 基準 | The rule that the others are compared with: R1 monthly for the grid, a zero return in Experiment 10. |
| Overfitting | 過度配適 | Fitting the noise of a sample rather than what persists. |
| Cross-validation | 交叉確認 | Judging a choice on data left out of it. |
| CSCV | — | Combinatorially symmetric cross-validation over $\binom{S}{S/2}$ splits of $S$ blocks. |
| Rank | 秩 | A strategy's place in an ordering, 1 the worst; the 導讀 calls it 名次. |
| Logit | 對數勝算 | $\ln(\omega/(1-\omega))$ of the relative rank $\omega$. |
| PBO | — | The share of splits whose in-sample best falls in the lower half out of sample, (34.4). |
| Performance degradation | — | The fall of the in-sample best's ratio out of sample, measured by an OLS slope. |
