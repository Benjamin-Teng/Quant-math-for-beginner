## Introduction

Volumes I and II measured co-movement in samples: [[LM7]]'s covariance, [[LM8]]'s correlation, [[LM10]]'s covariance matrix and [[LM12]]'s whitened returns. This module builds the probability model behind them, the joint distribution of several random returns. Under [[LM13]]'s empirical distribution, each population formula gives back its sample formula exactly.

> [!case] Do correlations rise in a crash?
> Semiconductor and FinancialInsurance are two of U28's sector indices. Over 4,382 daily returns, from 2 December 2008 to 8 October 2026, their correlation is 0.5749. Over 7–9 April 2025, the three sessions after the Qingming holiday, [[LM10]] found 0.851. On 7 April both fell nearly 10%, the pair's most extreme day of the sample.
>
> [[LM12]] whitened the pair. In those coordinates 177 days lie outside the circle of radius 3. If the two returns were jointly normal with the sample's covariance matrix, about 49 would lie there.
>
> So did the correlation rise in the crash, or would any sell-off look like this? What should a crash correlation be compared with, and how many extreme days does a normal model allow?

## Joint, Marginal and Conditional Distributions {lo=1}

Start with counts. Pick one of the 4,382 days at random, each with probability 1/4,382: the empirical distribution of [[LM13]]. Let $X$ be Semiconductor's bin that day: −1 for a return at or below −1%, 1 at or above 1%, 0 between. Let $Y$ be FinancialInsurance's bin, defined the same way ([[ex:pmf]]).

> [!definition] Joint, marginal and conditional pmf
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be discrete random variables on one sample space. Their **joint pmf** is $p_{X,Y}(x,y):=\Pr(X=x,Y=y)$. The **marginal** pmfs are its sums over the other variable, $p_X(x)=\sum_yp_{X,Y}(x,y)$ and $p_Y(y)=\sum_xp_{X,Y}(x,y)$. For $p_X(x)>0$, the **conditional pmf** of $Y$ given $X=x$ is
> $$p_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x):=\frac{p_{X,Y}(x,y)}{p_X(x)}.$$

The marginal pmfs are the table's totals. The conditional pmf is (13.4) for the events $\{X=x\}$ and $\{Y=y\}$: one row divided by its total. Read backwards, $p_{X,Y}(x,y)=p_X(x)\,p_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)$ is the multiplication rule (13.5).

> [!example] Reading the joint pmf {#joint}
> From [[ex:pmf]], find the probability that both sectors fall 1% or more, the two marginal probabilities and their product. Then find FinancialInsurance's probability of such a fall given a Semiconductor fall of 1% or more, and given a rise.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The joint probability is $p_{X,Y}(-1,-1)=347/4{,}382=0.0792$. The marginals are $p_X(-1)=859/4{,}382=0.1960$ and $p_Y(-1)=538/4{,}382=0.1228$, with product 0.0241, which independence would make the joint probability (Section 3). The joint frequency is 3.29 times the product: a descriptive ratio, not evidence ([[LM21]]).
> >
> > Given a Semiconductor fall, $p_{Y\mid X}(-1\mid-1)=347/859=0.404$, against the marginal 0.1228. Given a rise, it is $20/1{,}004=0.0199$. Knowing one bin changes the probabilities of the other.

For continuous returns, probability spreads over the plane with a density, as it spread along a line in [[LM5]].

> [!definition] Joint and conditional density
> A **joint density** of $X$ and $Y$ is a function $f_{X,Y}\ge0$ with
> $$\begin{aligned}&\Pr(x_1<X\le x_2,\ y_1<Y\le y_2)\\&\quad=\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\!\!\int_{y_1}^{y_2}f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dy\,dx\end{aligned}$$
> for all $x_1<x_2$ and $y_1<y_2$. The marginal density is $f_X(x)=\int f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,dy$, and for $f_X(x)>0$ the **conditional density** is $f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x):=f_{X,Y}(x,y)/f_X(x)$. The **joint distribution function** is $F_{X,Y}(x,y):=\Pr(X\le x,Y\le y)$.

By Fubini's theorem ([[LM5]]), a double integral equals an iterated integral in either order when the integrand is non-negative or absolutely integrable (stated without proof; Durrett 2019, Theorem 1.7.2). A marginal density integrates the other variable out.

> [!example] A density on the unit square {#density}
> Let $f_{X,Y}(x,y)=x+y$ for $0\le x\le1$ and $0\le y\le1$, and 0 elsewhere. Check that it is a density, and find $f_X$, $\Pr(X\le\tfrac12,Y\le\tfrac12)$ and $f_{Y\mid X}$. Are $X$ and $Y$ independent?
>
> > [!solution]
> > The function is non-negative, and
> > $$\begin{aligned}\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1(x+y)\,dy\,dx&=\int_0^1\big(x+\tfrac12\big)\,dx\\&=1.\end{aligned}$$
> > The inner integral is the marginal, $f_X(x)=x+\tfrac12$ on $[0,1]$; by symmetry $f_Y(y)=y+\tfrac12$. The corner square has probability $\int_0^{1/2}\big(\tfrac x2+\tfrac18\big)\,dx=\tfrac1{16}+\tfrac1{16}=\tfrac18$.
> >
> > The conditional density $f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)=(x+y)/(x+\tfrac12)$ changes with $x$: it is $2y$ at $x=0$ and $\tfrac23(1+y)$ at $x=1$. Knowing $X$ changes the law of $Y$, so the two are dependent (Section 3).

> [!pitfall] The marginals do not determine the joint law
> The totals of [[ex:pmf]] also fit the table of independent bins, each cell the product of its marginals. That table puts 0.0241 in the corner where the data put 0.0792 ([[eg:joint]]). Risk models with the same marginals can disagree completely about joint losses.

> [!zh] 聯合分布：一張表看兩個資產
> 只看每個資產自己的分布（distribution），看不出它們會不會一起跌。聯合分布（joint distribution）把「兩件事同時發生」的機率排成一張表，把列（row）或行（column）加總，就得到邊際分布（marginal distribution）。條件分布（conditional distribution）是把某一列除以該列的總和：半導體跌 1% 以上的 859 天裡，金融保險也跌 1% 以上的有 347 天，比例 0.404，遠高於不分情況時的 0.1228。連續型的情形把表格換成聯合機率密度函數（joint probability density function），把加總換成積分（integral）。同樣的邊際分布可以搭配完全不同的聯合分布，所以多資產的風險模型必須描述聯合分布本身。

> [!kc]
> From [[ex:pmf]], what is the probability that Semiconductor rose 1% or more, given that FinancialInsurance fell 1% or more?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Condition on the column: $20/538=0.0372$, dividing by FinancialInsurance's falls, not by a row total.

## Independence, Covariance and Correlation {lo=1,2}

[[LM13]] called discrete random variables independent when their joint pmf is the product of their pmfs, (13.13). The general definition uses distribution functions, which every random variable has.

> [!definition] Independence and the product rule
> Random variables $X_1,\dots,X_n$ are **independent** when the first line below holds for all numbers $x_1,\dots,x_n$. Then the **product rule**, the second line, holds for all functions $h_i$ with $\E\lvert h_i(X_i)\rvert<\infty$:
> $$\begin{aligned}&\Pr(X_1\le x_1,\dots,X_n\le x_n)\\&\quad=\prod_{i=1}^nF_{X_i}(x_i),\\&\E\Big[\prod_{i=1}^nh_i(X_i)\Big]=\prod_{i=1}^n\E[h_i(X_i)].\end{aligned} \tag{16.1}$$
> Independence holds exactly when the joint pmf, or the joint density when there is one, is the product of the marginals. The proof below covers pmfs; for densities the equivalence is stated without proof (Grinstead and Snell 1997, §4.2, Definition 4.7 and Theorem 4.2).

*Proof of the equivalence for pmfs.* Summing the product of the pmfs over the values up to each $x_i$ gives the first line of (16.1). Conversely, write the first line with $x<x_1$ in place of $x_1$, and let $x$ rise to $x_1$. By continuity ([[LM13]]), the limit is the same equation with $X_1<x_1$ in place of $X_1\le x_1$ on both sides. Subtracting it from the first line leaves $X_1=x_1$ on both sides. Repeating the step for $x_2,\dots,x_n$ gives (13.13). $\blacksquare$

*Proof of the product rule for two discrete variables.* The joint pmf factorizes, so the double sum splits:

$$\begin{aligned}&\E[h_1(X_1)h_2(X_2)]\\&\quad=\sum_{x_1}h_1(x_1)\,p_{X_1}(x_1)\sum_{x_2}h_2(x_2)\,p_{X_2}(x_2)\\&\quad=\E[h_1(X_1)]\,\E[h_2(X_2)].\end{aligned}$$

The finite expectations allow the sums in any order. A joint density splits the same way, as in [[LM5]]'s Gaussian integral, and $n$ variables repeat the step. $\blacksquare$

> [!definition] Covariance and correlation
> Let $X$ and $Y$ have finite variances. Their **covariance** is
> $$\begin{aligned}\Cov(X,Y)&:=\E[(X-\mu_X)(Y-\mu_Y)]\\&=\E[XY]-\mu_X\mu_Y,\end{aligned} \tag{16.2}$$
> written $\sigma_{XY}$. If also $\sigma_X,\sigma_Y>0$, their **correlation** is $\rho_{XY}:=\sigma_{XY}/(\sigma_X\sigma_Y)$. It satisfies $\lvert\rho_{XY}\rvert\le1$, with equality exactly when $\Pr(Y=\eta X+\zeta)=1$ for constants $\eta\ne0$ and $\zeta$.

The second form follows from linearity (14.3). The bound copies [[LM8]]'s proof of (8.6), with an expectation in place of the sum over dates (Deep Dive below).

> [!deepdive]- Why $\lvert\rho_{XY}\rvert\le1$, and when it equals 1
> Here $\delta>0$ and $W$ is a random variable. For every real $\eta$,
> $$\begin{aligned}0&\le\E\big[(Y-\mu_Y-\eta(X-\mu_X))^2\big]\\&=\sigma_Y^2-2\eta\,\sigma_{XY}+\eta^2\sigma_X^2 .\end{aligned}$$
> The choice $\eta=\sigma_{XY}/\sigma_X^2$, [[LM8]]'s closest multiple, gives $0\le\sigma_Y^2-\sigma_{XY}^2/\sigma_X^2$, so $\lvert\rho_{XY}\rvert\le1$.
>
> Equality holds exactly when this expectation is 0. A variable $W\ge0$ with $\E W=0$ is 0 with probability one: Markov's inequality (14.7) gives $\Pr(W\ge\delta)\le\E W/\delta=0$, and these events increase to $\{W>0\}$ as $\delta$ falls ([[LM13]]). So $Y=\eta X+\zeta$ with probability one, where $\zeta=\mu_Y-\eta\mu_X$ and $\eta\ne0$ since $\sigma_Y>0$. Conversely, $Y=\eta X+\zeta$ gives $\rho_{XY}=\eta\sigma_X^2/(\sigma_X\lvert\eta\rvert\sigma_X)=\pm1$.

Independence implies zero covariance: (16.1) with $h_1$ and $h_2$ the identity gives $\E[XY]=\mu_X\mu_Y$. Variables with zero covariance are **uncorrelated**. The converse fails.

> [!example] Uncorrelated but dependent {#xsquared}
> Let $X$ take the values −1, 0 and 1 with probability 1/3 each, and let $Y=X^2$. Show that $\Cov(X,Y)=0$ although $Y$ is a function of $X$.
>
> > [!solution]
> > By (16.2), $\Cov(X,Y)=\E[X^3]-\E X\,\E[X^2]=0-0\times\tfrac23=0$, since $X^3=X$ has mean 0. Yet $\Pr(Y=0\mid X=0)=1$ while $\Pr(Y=0)=\tfrac13$, so the joint pmf is not the product of the marginals. Covariance measures only the linear part of a dependence, and this symmetric dependence has none.

Market data show the same pattern. Let $R_t$ be the TAIEX price index's return (no dividends) on day $t$ of the U28 dates.

> [!example] Today's move and tomorrow's {#lagcorr}
> For the 4,381 pairs $(R_t,R_{t+1})$, compute $\hat\rho(R_t,R_{t+1})$ and $\hat\rho(\lvert R_t\rvert,\lvert R_{t+1}\rvert)$, and the mean of $\lvert R_{t+1}\rvert$ after the days with $\lvert R_t\rvert\ge2\%$ and after the others.

> > Under independence, both population correlations would be zero and the two conditional means equal. Large moves cluster in time, of either sign; [[LM26]] models how tomorrow's spread depends on today's move.

Under the empirical distribution an expectation is an average with divisor $T$, so (16.2) is [[LM7]]'s average product and $\rho_{XY}$ is [[LM8]]'s (8.9). The sample's 0.5749 is exactly the empirical model's correlation; as an estimate of another model's correlation, its error is [[LM20]]'s subject. Orthogonal centred return vectors ([[LM8]]) are uncorrelated under this model, not independent.

> [!pitfall] A correlation near zero does not prevent a joint loss
> In 2022 0050 and 00679B both fell about 21–22% while their daily correlation was −0.06 ([[LM7]]). Correlation concerns deviations from each asset's own mean; when both means turn negative, both assets lose, whatever the correlation.

> [!zh] 不相關不等於獨立
> 兩個隨機變數（random variable）獨立（independent），是指聯合分布（joint distribution）等於邊際分布（marginal distribution）的乘積；這時乘積的期望值（expectation）等於期望值的乘積，所以共變異數（covariance）為 0。反過來不成立：$X$ 取 −1、0、1，$Y=X^2$，兩者互不相關（uncorrelated），$Y$ 卻完全由 $X$ 決定。相關係數（correlation coefficient）只量線性的部分。加權股價指數今天與明天報酬的相關係數只有 0.040，報酬大小的相關係數卻是 0.183：大波動之後常跟著大波動，方向卻難說。樣本相關係數就是經驗分布（empirical distribution）這個母體（population）的相關係數，不是另一套定義。

> [!kc]
> For constants $\eta\ne0$ and $\zeta$, how do $\Cov(X,\eta Y+\zeta)$ and the correlation of $X$ with $\eta Y+\zeta$ compare with $\Cov(X,Y)$ and $\rho_{XY}$?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > By (16.2), $\Cov(X,\eta Y+\zeta)=\E[(X-\mu_X)\,\eta(Y-\mu_Y)]=\eta\Cov(X,Y)$, since $\zeta$ cancels with the mean. The standard deviation becomes $\lvert\eta\rvert\sigma_Y$ (14.4), so the correlation is $\rho_{XY}$ for $\eta>0$ and $-\rho_{XY}$ for $\eta<0$.

## Sums, Portfolios and Covariance Matrices {lo=3}

[[LM7]] expanded the variance of a two-asset portfolio's sample returns. The same expansion works for random variables.

> [!theorem] Variance of a sum
> Let $X_1,\dots,X_n$ have $\E X_i^2<\infty$ and covariances $\sigma_{ij}:=\Cov(X_i,X_j)$, so that $\sigma_{ii}=\Var X_i$, and let $a_1,\dots,a_n$ be constants. Then
> $$\Var\Big(\sum_ia_iX_i\Big)=\sum_i\sum_ja_ia_j\sigma_{ij}. \tag{16.3}$$
> If the $X_i$ are uncorrelated, in particular if they are independent, then $\Var(\sum_iX_i)=\sum_i\Var X_i$.

*Proof.* By linearity (14.3), the sum deviates from its mean by $\sum_ia_i(X_i-\mu_{X_i})$. Square it, and take expectations term by term. For uncorrelated variables only the terms with $i=j$ remain. $\blacksquare$

With two assets, (16.3) gives [[LM7]]'s identities in population form: the portfolio variance $\sigma_p^2=w_1^2\sigma_1^2+2w_1w_2\sigma_{12}+w_2^2\sigma_2^2$ and $\Var(R_1-R_2)=\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2-2\sigma_{12}$. Then (16.2) and linearity (14.3) give $\Cov(R_1,R_p)=w_1\sigma_1^2+w_2\sigma_{12}$. The theorem's last sentence, $\Var(\sum_iX_i)=\sum_i\Var X_i$, needs only zero covariances, not independence, as [[LM3]]'s sum of squared increments did.

> [!example] A two-asset model {#twoasset}
> Take a model whose annualized covariance matrix for Semiconductor and FinancialInsurance is [[LM10]]'s: $\sigma_1^2=570.34$, $\sigma_{12}=261.83$ and $\sigma_2^2=363.73$, in %². Find the variance and volatility of the 50/50 portfolio, $\Cov(R_1,R_p)$ and the volatility of $R_1-R_2$.
>
> > [!solution]
> > By (16.3),
> > $$\begin{aligned}\sigma_p^2&=0.25(570.34)\\&\quad+2(0.25)(261.83)\\&\quad+0.25(363.73)=364.43,\end{aligned}$$
> > so $\sigma_p=19.09\%$. Next, by (16.2) and linearity, $\Cov(R_1,R_p)=0.5(570.34)+0.5(261.83)=416.085$, exactly. Finally, $\Var(R_1-R_2)=570.34+363.73-2(261.83)=410.41$, a volatility of 20.26%, as [[LM10]] found for $(1,-1,0)^{\top}$. Under the empirical distribution each value is also the sample value.

Sums over days use log returns, which add ([[LM1]], (1.12)). Model the $m$ daily log returns of a year as uncorrelated, each with mean $g$ and standard deviation $\sigma_r$. Their sum has mean $mg$ by (14.3) and variance $m\sigma_r^2$ by (16.3): the assumption behind [[LM5]]'s annualization.

For the TAIEX price index on U28 dates, take $g=\bar r=0.05454\%$ and $\sigma_r=s_r=1.1392\%$ (divisor $T$), and $m=245$, the rounded $A$. The model year's log return has mean 245 × 0.05454% = 13.36% and standard deviation √245 × 1.1392% = 17.83% (a model fitted to the same days). If the days are iid, not only uncorrelated, a sum of many of them is nearly normal: the central limit theorem of [[LM17]].

The same rule counts events. An indicator $\mathbf 1_{\mathcal A}$ with $\Pr(\mathcal A)=p$ has mean $p$ by (14.1) and, being its own square, variance $p-p^2$ by (14.4). A count of $n$ independent such indicators has mean $np$ and variance $np(1-p)$: [[LM15]]'s binomial moments (15.2) in one line.

> [!definition] Mean vector and covariance matrix
> Let $\mathbf X=(X_1,\dots,X_N)^{\top}$ be a random vector with $\E\lVert\mathbf X\rVert^2<\infty$ and mean $\boldsymbol\mu:=\E\mathbf X$. Its **covariance matrix** $\Sigma$ has entries $\sigma_{ij}=\Cov(X_i,X_j)$. For a $J\times N$ matrix $M$ and vectors $\mathbf b$ and $\mathbf a$ of lengths $J$ and $N$,
> $$\begin{gathered}\Sigma:=\E\big[(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}\big],\\ \Cov(M\mathbf X+\mathbf b)=M\Sigma M^{\top},\\ \mathbf a^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf a=\Var(\mathbf a^{\top}\mathbf X)\ge0,\end{gathered} \tag{16.4}$$
> where $\Cov$ of a vector means its covariance matrix. So $\Sigma$ is positive semidefinite.

*Proof.* The vector $M\mathbf X+\mathbf b$ deviates from its mean $M\boldsymbol\mu+\mathbf b$ by $M(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)$. By linearity, entry by entry, its covariance matrix is $\E[M(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}M^{\top}]=M\Sigma M^{\top}$. With $M=\mathbf a^{\top}$, a single row, this is a variance, never negative. $\blacksquare$

The correlation matrix $\operatorname{Corr}(\mathbf X)$ is the covariance matrix of the entries $X_i/\sigma_i$, so it is positive semidefinite too. A portfolio return $R_p=\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf R$ has variance $\sigma_p^2=\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w$. Under the empirical distribution, (16.4) gives [[LM10]]'s (10.2), (10.3) and (10.5). Splitting $\sigma_p^2$ among assets is the work of [[LM27]] and [[LM31]]; how far $\hat\Sigma$ lies from $\Sigma$ is [[LM30]]'s.

> [!pitfall] Standard deviations do not add
> Only variances and covariances combine by (16.3). The 20.26% of [[eg:twoasset]] is neither 23.88 − 19.07 = 4.81 points nor √(570.34 + 363.73) = 30.56%. The first holds only when $\rho=1$, the second only when $\rho=0$.

> [!kc]
> Let $X$ and $Y$ be independent with variances 4 and 9. Find $\Var(X-Y)$ and the correlation of $X$ with $X+Y$.
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Independent variables are uncorrelated, so (16.3) gives $\Var(X-Y)=4+9=13$: a difference adds variances too. Next, $\Cov(X,X+Y)=\Var X=4$ and $\Var(X+Y)=13$, so the correlation is $4/(2\sqrt{13})=0.555$.

## Conditional Expectation {lo=4}

The conditional density of [[eg:density]] changes with $x$, so the mean of $Y$ given $X=x$ changes too.

> [!definition] Conditional expectation
> Let $\E\lvert Y\rvert<\infty$. For $p_X(x)>0$, the **conditional expectation** of $Y$ given $X=x$ is $h(x)=\E[Y\mid X=x]:=\sum_yy\,p_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)$; with densities, it is $\int y\,f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\,dy$. The random variable $\E[Y\mid X]:=h(X)$ is the conditional expectation of $Y$ given $X$. The **conditional variance** $\Var(Y\mid X=x)$ is $\E[(Y-h(x))^2\mid X=x]$, and $\Var(Y\mid X)$ is that function evaluated at $X$.

For [[eg:density]],

$$\begin{aligned}\E[Y\mid X=x]&=\int_0^1y\,\frac{x+y}{x+\tfrac12}\,dy\\&=\frac{x/2+1/3}{x+1/2}=\frac{3x+2}{6x+3}.\end{aligned}$$

The conditional mean falls from 2/3 at $x=0$ to 5/9 at $x=1$.

On an event $\{X\in\mathcal S\}$ with $\Pr(X\in\mathcal S)>0$, the conditional mean is the average over the event:

$$\E[X\mid X\in\mathcal S]=\frac{\E\big[X\,\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal S\}\big]}{\Pr(X\in\mathcal S)}.$$

[[LM5]]'s tail mean (5.11) is this formula for a normal $X$ and the values below a threshold.

> [!theorem] Tower property and law of total variance
> If $\E\lvert Y\rvert<\infty$, then
> $$\E\big[\E[Y\mid X]\big]=\E Y. \tag{16.5}$$
> If $\E Y^2<\infty$, then
> $$\begin{aligned}\Var Y&=\E\big[\Var(Y\mid X)\big]\\&\quad+\Var\big(\E[Y\mid X]\big).\end{aligned} \tag{16.6}$$

*Proof for discrete variables.* The events $\{X=x\}$ partition the sample space, and total probability (13.8) gives $p_Y(y)=\sum_xp_X(x)\,p_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)$. Hence

$$\begin{aligned}\E Y&=\sum_yy\sum_xp_X(x)\,p_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\\&=\sum_xp_X(x)\,h(x),\end{aligned}$$

which is (16.5); the finite $\E\lvert Y\rvert$ allows the exchange of sums. For (16.6), square $Y-\mu_Y=(Y-h(X))+(h(X)-\mu_Y)$. Given $X=x$, the cross term has conditional mean $(h(x)-\mu_Y)(h(x)-h(x))=0$. Taking expectations with (16.5) leaves $\E[\Var(Y\mid X)]$ and $\Var(h(X))$. With densities, sums become integrals. $\blacksquare$

So (16.6) splits a variance into the average variance **within** the groups $\{X=x\}$ and the variance of the group means **between** them. On the empirical distribution both parts are exact sums over days.

> [!zh] 條件期望值與全變異數法則
> 條件期望值（conditional expectation）$\E[Y\mid X]$ 是「知道 $X$ 之後對 $Y$ 的平均」，它是 $X$ 的函數，所以本身也是隨機變數（random variable）。雙重期望值定理（tower property）說，把各組的條件平均按各組的機率加權平均，就回到 $\E Y$。權重一定要用機率，等權平均會得到錯的答案。全變異數法則（law of total variance）把變異數（variance）拆成「組內變異數的平均」加上「組平均數（mean）之間的變異數」。以半導體的報酬分三組時，金融保險的變異數 1.482 中有 23.1% 落在組間；在經驗分布（empirical distribution）上，這兩條公式都是精確的恆等式。

## The Multivariate Normal Distribution {lo=5,6}

[[LM12]] coloured standard normal draws with a Cholesky factor to simulate returns. This section defines the law of such draws.

> [!definition] Multivariate normal
> A random vector $\mathbf X$ of length $N$ is **multivariate normal** when every combination $\mathbf a^{\top}\mathbf X$ is normal, a constant counting as normal with variance 0. Its law is written $\mathcal N_N(\boldsymbol\mu,\Sigma)$, with $\boldsymbol\mu$ and $\Sigma$ as in (16.4).

Two facts are stated without proof: independent normal variables are jointly normal, and $\boldsymbol\mu$ and $\Sigma$ fix a normal vector's law. Durrett (2019, §3.10 and Exercise 3.10.8) gives both for every positive semidefinite $\Sigma$, through characteristic functions.

> [!theorem] Linear maps, colouring and the density
> Let $\mathbf X\sim\mathcal N_N(\boldsymbol\mu,\Sigma)$ with $\Sigma=LL^{\top}$, and let $M$ be a $J\times N$ matrix and $\mathbf b$ a vector of length $J$. Then
> $$\begin{gathered}M\mathbf X+\mathbf b\sim\mathcal N_J(M\boldsymbol\mu+\mathbf b,\,M\Sigma M^{\top}),\\ \boldsymbol\mu+L\mathbf Z\sim\mathcal N_N(\boldsymbol\mu,\Sigma)\ \text{ for }\ \mathbf Z\sim\mathcal N_N(\mathbf 0,I).\end{gathered} \tag{16.7}$$
> If $\Sigma$ is positive definite, $\mathbf X$ has the density
> $$\begin{gathered}f_{\mathbf X}(\mathbf x)=(2\pi)^{-N/2}(\det\Sigma)^{-1/2}e^{-d^2/2},\\ d^2:=(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu),\end{gathered}$$
> where $d^2$ is the squared **Mahalanobis distance** (stated without proof; Wasserman 2004, §2.10, equation (2.10) and Theorem 2.43).

*Proof of the two lines.* For any $\mathbf a$, the variable $\mathbf a^{\top}(M\mathbf X+\mathbf b)=(M^{\top}\mathbf a)^{\top}\mathbf X+\mathbf a^{\top}\mathbf b$ is normal, so $M\mathbf X+\mathbf b$ is multivariate normal, with the moments of (16.4). Independent standard normals form $\mathbf Z\sim\mathcal N_N(\mathbf 0,I)$ by the first stated fact; take $M=L$ and $\mathbf b=\boldsymbol\mu$. $\blacksquare$

The second line is **colouring** in population form, and $L^{-1}(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)\sim\mathcal N_N(\mathbf 0,I)$ is **whitening**. [[LM12]]'s whitened returns are only uncorrelated with variance 1, in the sample; the model's $\mathbf Z$ has independent normal entries. Drawing $\mathbf Z$ on a computer is [[LM18]]'s subject.

With $M$ a row of the identity, (16.7) makes each $X_i$ normal; with several rows, any sub-vector is multivariate normal. For an orthogonal matrix such as [[LM11]]'s $Q$, (16.7) gives $Q\mathbf Z\sim\mathcal N_N(\mathbf 0,I)$: a standard normal vector looks the same in every rotated frame.

If $\Sigma$ is diagonal with every $\sigma_i>0$, $d^2=\sum_i(x_i-\mu_i)^2/\sigma_i^2$ and the density factorizes, so uncorrelated entries of a normal vector are independent. An entry of variance 0 is constant with probability one (Section 3's Deep Dive), so it is independent of the others too.

> [!deepdive]- The density for $N=2$
> Here $\mathbf X=\boldsymbol\mu+L\mathbf Z$ by colouring, and $\mathbf x=\boldsymbol\mu+L\mathbf z$ is the image of a point $\mathbf z$ of the plane. The vector $\mathbf Z$ has the density $\varphi(z_1)\varphi(z_2)=e^{-\lVert\mathbf z\rVert^2/2}/(2\pi)$. The map sends a small square at $\mathbf z$ to a parallelogram spanned by $L$'s columns, so areas grow by $\lvert\det L\rvert=l_{11}l_{22}$ ([[LM9]], (9.2)). Both small regions have the same probability, so
> $$f_{\mathbf X}(\mathbf x)\,l_{11}l_{22}=\frac{e^{-\lVert\mathbf z\rVert^2/2}}{2\pi}.$$
> Since $\det\Sigma=(l_{11}l_{22})^2$ and $\lVert\mathbf z\rVert^2=d^2$ by (12.9), this is (16.7)'s density for $N=2$.

> [!theorem] The conditional law in a normal pair
> Let $(X,Y)$ be a normal pair with $\sigma_X,\sigma_Y>0$ and correlation $\rho$, and let $\beta:=\sigma_{XY}/\sigma_X^2$. Then
> $$\begin{gathered}Y=\mu_Y+\beta(X-\mu_X)+\varepsilon,\\ \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N\big(0,\sigma_Y^2(1-\rho^2)\big),\end{gathered} \tag{16.8}$$
> with $\varepsilon$ independent of $X$. Given $X=x$, $Y$ is normal with mean $\mu_Y+\beta(x-\mu_X)$ and variance $\sigma_Y^2(1-\rho^2)$.

*Proof.* Define $\varepsilon$ by (16.8). The pair $(X,\varepsilon)$ is a linear map of $(X,Y)$ plus a constant, so it is jointly normal, as in the proof of (16.7). Since $\Cov(X,\varepsilon)=\sigma_{XY}-\beta\sigma_X^2=0$, $X$ and $\varepsilon$ are independent. By (16.3),

$$\begin{aligned}\Var\varepsilon&=\sigma_Y^2-2\beta\sigma_{XY}+\beta^2\sigma_X^2\\&=\sigma_Y^2-\frac{\sigma_{XY}^2}{\sigma_X^2}=\sigma_Y^2(1-\rho^2).\end{aligned}$$

Fixing $X=x$ leaves the law of the independent $\varepsilon$ unchanged. $\blacksquare$

The slope $\beta$ is the population slope that [[LM12]]'s least squares estimate; its standard error is [[LM23]]'s. Since $\Var(\E[Y\mid X])=\beta^2\sigma_X^2=\rho^2\sigma_Y^2$, the share between in (16.6) is $\rho^2$.

> [!definition] Chi-square law
> Let $Z_1,\dots,Z_N$ be independent standard normal variables. The law of $Z_1^2+\dots+Z_N^2$ is the **chi-square distribution** with $N$ **degrees of freedom**, $\chi^2_N$. Its mean is $N$, since $\E Z_i^2=1$ ([[LM5]]). Its variance is $2N$, since $\Var(Z_i^2)=2$ by (14.6) and the squares are uncorrelated by (16.1), so (16.3) applies.

> [!theorem] Mahalanobis distance of a normal vector
> If $\mathbf X\sim\mathcal N_N(\boldsymbol\mu,\Sigma)$ with $\Sigma$ positive definite, the squared Mahalanobis distance of $\mathbf X$ has the law $\chi^2_N$. For $N=2$,
> $$\Pr(d^2>k^2)=e^{-k^2/2}. \tag{16.9}$$

*Proof.* Whitening gives $\mathbf Z=L^{-1}(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)\sim\mathcal N_N(\mathbf 0,I)$, and $d^2=\lVert\mathbf Z\rVert^2$ because $\Sigma^{-1}=L^{-\top}L^{-1}$, as in (12.9). For $N=2$, [[LM5]]'s disc integral gives $\Pr(Z_1^2+Z_2^2\le k^2)=1-e^{-k^2/2}$. $\blacksquare$

For $N>2$, SciPy's `stats.chi2` gives the tails of $\chi^2_N$, and [[LM21]] builds tests on it.

The density (16.7) is constant where $d^2$ is. With $\Sigma$'s eigenpairs, (11.3) gives $\Sigma^{-1}=\sum_i\mathbf q_i\mathbf q_i^{\top}/\lambda_i$, so the contour $d^2=k^2$ is an ellipse (for $N>2$, an ellipsoid) with semi-axes $k\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ along the eigenvectors $\mathbf q_i$ of (11.1). It holds the probability $\Pr(\chi^2_N\le k^2)$. For the pair, $\hat\Sigma$ has entries 2.323354, 1.066603 and 1.481729 in %² ([[LM12]]), and (11.2) gives $\lambda_1=3.049$ and $\lambda_2=0.756$. [[LM11]]'s ellipse on [[LM7]]'s sample was a description; under a normal model it becomes a probability contour ([[ex:contours]]).

> [!pitfall] Normal marginals do not make a normal vector
> A pair can have two standard normal marginals and zero covariance and still be dependent. The rule "uncorrelated implies independent" needs the pair to be jointly normal, not only each marginal.

> [!zh] 多變量常態分布與 Mahalanobis 距離
> 多變量常態分布（multivariate normal distribution）的定義是：任何線性組合（linear combination）都是常態分布（normal distribution）。把獨立（independent）的標準常態（standard normal）變數乘上 Cholesky 因子 $L$ 再加上 $\boldsymbol\mu$，就得到共變異數矩陣（covariance matrix）為 $\Sigma$ 的常態向量，這是著色（colouring）；反過來乘上 $L^{-1}$ 就是白化（whitening）。[[LM12]] 的樣本白化後只保證互不相關（uncorrelated）且變異數（variance）為 1，模型裡的 $\mathbf Z$ 卻是獨立的常態，兩者差很多。常態向量的 Mahalanobis 距離（Mahalanobis distance）平方服從自由度（degrees of freedom）為 $N$ 的卡方分布（chi-square distribution），二維時落在半徑 $k$ 之外的機率是 $e^{-k^2/2}$。半導體與金融保險這一對，模型預期約 49 天落在半徑 3 之外，實際有 177 天；這個差距是否可能出於偶然，留待 [[LM21]]。

## Correlation in a Crash {lo=7}

The case's three days gave a correlation of 0.851, against 0.5749 for the whole sample. All 28 sectors fell together on 156 days ([[LM11]]). A higher correlation on bad days looks like contagion. Yet a normal pair with one fixed $\rho$ also shows another correlation on selected days.

> [!theorem] Correlation given an event of one variable
> Let $(X,Y)$ be a normal pair with $\sigma_X,\sigma_Y>0$ and correlation $\rho$. Let $\{X\in\mathcal S\}$ be an event with $\Pr(X\in\mathcal S)>0$ on which $X$ has a positive conditional variance $\sigma_{\mathcal S}^2$. Then
> $$\begin{aligned}&\operatorname{Corr}(X,Y\mid X\in\mathcal S)\\&\quad=\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{\rho^2+(1-\rho^2)\,\sigma_X^2/\sigma_{\mathcal S}^2}}.\end{aligned} \tag{16.10}$$
> (Boyer, Gibson and Loretan 1999, Theorem 1.)

*Proof.* By (16.8), $Y=\mu_Y+\beta(X-\mu_X)+\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ independent of $X$, hence of the event, on which it keeps its mean 0 and variance $\sigma_Y^2(1-\rho^2)$. Conditionally, $\Cov(X,Y)=\beta\sigma_{\mathcal S}^2$ and $\Var Y=\beta^2\sigma_{\mathcal S}^2+\sigma_Y^2(1-\rho^2)$ by (16.3). Divide by the two standard deviations, put $\beta=\rho\sigma_Y/\sigma_X$, and cancel $\sigma_Y\sigma_{\mathcal S}/\sigma_X$. $\blacksquare$

The correlation rises when the event widens the spread of $X$ and falls when it narrows it. Moves of $k$ standard deviations either way widen it; a one-sided tail, whose values lie close together, narrows it. Forbes and Rigobon (2002) adjust crisis correlations this way before reading them as contagion.

> [!deepdive]- Conditional variances of a standard normal
> Here $\ell:=\varphi(k)/\Phi(-k)$ for $k\ge0$. Integration by parts, as [[LM5]] found $\E Z^2=1$, gives
> $$\begin{aligned}\int_{-\infty}^{-k}z^2\varphi(z)\,dz&=\big[-z\varphi(z)\big]_{-\infty}^{-k}\\&\quad+\int_{-\infty}^{-k}\varphi(z)\,dz\\&=k\varphi(k)+\Phi(-k).\end{aligned}$$
> Dividing by $\Phi(-k)$ gives $\E[Z^2\mid Z\le-k]=1+k\ell$, and (5.11) gives $\E[Z\mid Z\le-k]=-\ell$. Hence
> $$\begin{gathered}\Var(Z\mid Z\le-k)=1+k\ell-\ell^2,\\ \Var(Z\mid\lvert Z\rvert\ge k)=1+k\ell,\end{gathered}$$
> the second because the two-sided event has mean 0 and equal tails. At $k=2$, $\ell=2.3732$ ([[LM5]], Problem 14), and the variances are 0.1143 and 5.7464, which enter (16.10) with $\sigma_X^2=1$.

> [!pitfall] Compare a conditional correlation with the model's, not with the full sample's
> The two-sided 0.788 exceeds the full-sample 0.5749, yet a normal pair with the constant correlation 0.5749 gives 0.860 for the same event. Compare a conditional correlation with the model under the same event; whether a gap could be chance is for [[LM21]] and [[LM22]].

> [!zh] 崩盤時的相關係數：和同一事件下的模型比
> 只挑大波動的日子算相關係數（correlation coefficient），常態模型本身就會給出不同的數字：條件相關係數（conditional correlation）取決於條件事件（conditioning event）下 $X$ 的變異數（variance）。雙邊的大波動讓 $X$ 的變異數變大，相關係數跟著上升；單邊的尾部讓變異數變小，相關係數反而下降。所以崩盤時的相關係數要和「同一事件下的常態模型」比，不能和全樣本的 0.5749 比。半導體大漲或大跌 2 個標準差（standard deviation）時，資料是 0.788，模型是 0.860；兩者同時下跌 2 個標準差時，資料是 0.669，模型只有 0.156。在 2 個標準差時，超越相關係數（exceedance correlation）的下跌端（0.669）高於上漲端（0.541），方向與 Longin 和 Solnik（2001）一致，但這些天數很少。

## Joint Distributions in Python {lo=8}

Counts at an edge use a guard, because index levels have two decimals and a return can equal −1% exactly ([[LM5]]). `np.cov` divides by $T-1$ unless `ddof=0` (or `bias=True`) is passed, while (10.2) divides by $T$. Mahalanobis distances come from a Cholesky factor and a triangular solve, never from an inverse.

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats
from scipy.linalg import solve_triangular

# x1, x2: the pair's T daily returns (Semiconductor, FinancialInsurance)
EPS = 1e-12                               # guard for edges such as -1%
both = (x1 <= -0.01 + EPS) & (x2 <= -0.01 + EPS)
print(both.sum())                         # 347 days, the corner cell
XP = np.column_stack([x1, x2])
S = np.cov(XP, rowvar=False, ddof=0)      # divisor T, as (10.2)
L = np.linalg.cholesky(S)                 # lower factor, S = L L'
Z = solve_triangular(L, (XP - XP.mean(axis=0)).T, lower=True)
d2 = (Z**2).sum(axis=0)                   # d_t^2 of every day
print((d2 > 9 + EPS).sum())               # 177
print(len(d2) * stats.chi2.sf(9, 2))      # 48.68 expected
print(1 - stats.chi2.cdf(89.30, 5))       # 0.0: the tail is lost
print(stats.chi2.sf(89.30, 5))            # 9.4e-18
```

> [!pitfall] A tail probability from `1 - cdf` can vanish
> `stats.chi2.cdf(89.30, 5)` differs from 1 by less than $10^{-17}$. Double precision has no number between $1-1.1\times10^{-16}$ and 1, so the result is stored as 1 and `1 - cdf` returns 0.0. `stats.chi2.sf` computes the upper tail directly: $9.4\times10^{-18}$.

## Summary {-}

- A conditional law divides a slice of the joint law by its marginal: FinancialInsurance fell 1% or more on 0.404 of Semiconductor's falls, against 0.1228 of all days.
- Independence makes covariance zero, (16.1), but not conversely: the TAIEX price index's returns correlate 0.040 day to day, their sizes 0.183.
- Covariance and correlation (16.2) are the population versions of [[LM7]]'s and [[LM8]]'s statistics.
- Variances of sums follow (16.3); covariance matrices are positive semidefinite, (16.4).
- By (16.5) and (16.6), Semiconductor's bin explains 23.1% of FinancialInsurance's variance.
- By (16.9), a normal model expects 48.68 days outside radius 3 for the pair (standard deviation 6.94); the data hold 177.
- Conditioning changes a normal pair's correlation, (16.10): 0.860 for moves of 2 either way and 0.156 for joint falls of 2, against the pair's 0.788 and 0.669.

1. **B** is correct: $347/859=0.404$. A is the joint probability $347/4{,}382=0.079$. C conditions the wrong way, $347/538=0.645$.

2. **A** is correct. Here $\E X=\int_0^1x(x+\tfrac12)\,dx=\tfrac7{12}$ and $\E[XY]=\int_0^1\!\int_0^1xy(x+y)\,dy\,dx=\tfrac13$, so $\Cov(X,Y)=\tfrac13-\tfrac{49}{144}=-\tfrac1{144}=-0.0069$. With $\Var X=\tfrac{11}{144}$ the correlation is $-\tfrac1{11}=-0.0909$. B omits $\mu_X\mu_Y$; C assumes independence, which [[eg:density]] excluded.

3. **C** is correct. By symmetry, $\Pr(Y\le y)=\tfrac12\Phi(y)+\tfrac12\Pr(-X\le y)=\Phi(y)$. By (16.1), $\Cov(X,Y)=\E[S]\,\E[X^2]=0$, yet $\lvert Y\rvert=\lvert X\rvert$. A needs joint normality. B fails: $X+Y=0$ with probability 1/2, while for a normal pair with $\rho=0$, $X+Y\sim\mathcal N(0,2)$ gives each single value probability 0.

4. By (16.3), $\Var(R_1-R_2)=0.04+0.0225-2(0.5)(0.20)(0.15)=0.0325$, a volatility of 18.03%. Next, $\Cov(R_1,R_p)=0.6(0.04)+0.4(0.015)=0.0300$, and $\sigma_p^2=0.36(0.04)+2(0.24)(0.015)+0.16(0.0225)=0.0252$, so $\sigma_p=15.87\%$. The correlation is $0.0300/(0.20\times0.1587)=0.945$. The value 5% is $\lvert\sigma_1-\sigma_2\rvert$, right only for $\rho=1$; 25% is $\sqrt{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2}$, right only for $\rho=0$.

5. By (16.3), the variance is $ms_r^2$ plus twice the $m-1$ adjacent covariances $0.04s_r^2$. The factor is $1+2(0.04)(244/245)=1.0797$, and the standard deviation is $17.83\%\times\sqrt{1.0797}=18.53\%$. Wrong routes: 245 × 1.1392% = 279.1% adds standard deviations, and 17.83% × 1.0797 = 19.25% applies a variance factor to a standard deviation. This is a model; [[LM19]] examines the data.

6. By (16.5), $(306\times0.2251+4{,}075\times0.0496)/4{,}381=0.0619\%$. By (16.6), within 1.288 plus between 0.002 gives the total 1.290 (%²), a share between of 0.2% (0.155%). The between part is tiny (0.002 of 1.290), but the spreads differ by a factor of 1.8889/1.0570 = 1.79, which a correlation of returns cannot see. The equal-weight mean, (0.2251 + 0.0496)/2 = 0.13735%, ignores that big days are rare.

7. By (16.8), the mean is $0.0494+0.4591\times(-3-0.0947)=-1.37\%$, and the standard deviation stays 0.996%. Then $(-2+1.37)/0.996=-0.6325$ and $\Phi(-0.6325)=0.264$. The unconditional standard deviation would give $\Phi(-0.63/1.2173)=0.302$, and the slope $\rho$ in place of $\beta$ a mean of −1.73%.

   $$\begin{aligned}z_1&=(-9.48-0.0947)/1.5243=-6.281,\\z_2&=(-6.93-0.0494-0.6998z_1)/0.9960\\&=-2.594,\end{aligned}$$

9. (a) By (16.3), $\Var(X/\sigma_X\pm Y/\sigma_Y)=2\pm2\rho_{XY}\ge0$, so $-1\le\rho_{XY}\le1$. (b) The deviation $M(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)$ gives $M\Sigma M^{\top}$ by linearity. ## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Joint distribution | 聯合分布 | The law of several random variables together: a joint pmf, density or distribution function. |
| Marginal distribution | 邊際分布 | The law of one variable, obtained from the joint law by summing or integrating the others out. |
| Conditional distribution | 條件分布 | The law of $Y$ given $X=x$: the joint pmf or density divided by the marginal of $X$. |
| Independent | 獨立 | Random variables whose joint distribution function factorizes, (16.1). |
| Covariance | 共變異數 | $\E[(X-\mu_X)(Y-\mu_Y)]$, (16.2). |
| Correlation | 相關係數 | $\sigma_{XY}/(\sigma_X\sigma_Y)$, between −1 and 1. |
| Uncorrelated | 互不相關 | With covariance zero; implied by independence, not conversely. |
| Covariance matrix | 共變異數矩陣 | $\Sigma=\E[(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}]$, positive semidefinite, (16.4). |
| Conditional expectation | 條件期望值 | $\E[Y\mid X=x]$, the mean of the conditional law; $\E[Y\mid X]$ is that function of $X$. |
| Tower property | 雙重期望值定理 | $\E[\E[Y\mid X]]=\E Y$, (16.5). |
| Law of total variance | 全變異數法則 | Variance = mean within-group variance + variance of group means, (16.6). |
| Multivariate normal distribution | 多變量常態分布 | A random vector all of whose combinations are normal, $\mathcal N_N(\boldsymbol\mu,\Sigma)$, (16.7). |
| Colouring, whitening | 著色、白化 | $\boldsymbol\mu+L\mathbf Z$ and $L^{-1}(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)$, with $\Sigma=LL^{\top}$. |
| Mahalanobis distance | Mahalanobis 距離 | $d$, with $d^2=(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)$. |
| Chi-square distribution | 卡方分布 | The law $\chi^2_N$ of a sum of $N$ independent squared standard normals: mean $N$, variance $2N$. |
| Degrees of freedom | 自由度 | The number $N$ of squared standard normals in $\chi^2_N$. |
| Conditional correlation | 條件相關係數 | The correlation given an event, (16.10) for a normal pair. |
| Exceedance correlation | 超越相關係數 | The correlation on the days when both variables lie beyond $k$ standard deviations on the same side. |
