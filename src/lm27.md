## Introduction

[[LM7]] priced two funds at fixed weights, and [[LM8]], [[LM10]], [[LM16]] and [[LM23]] gave a book's return, its variance and the market model. This module adds time and attribution. Weights drift between trades, trades cost money out of wealth, five funds need two sets of dates, and the risk splits among them in one meaningful way.

> [!case] Which fund carries the risk?
> A reader holds [[LM7]]'s two funds, 0050 and 00679B, and adds three: 00646 (the S&P 500), 00635U (gold futures) and 00642U (crude-oil futures). She puts a fifth of her money in each. On the 2,362 joint dates from January 2017 to October 2026, when all five funds traded, their volatilities ran from 13.49% to 37.43% a year. Yet the mix, reset to a fifth each day, had a volatility of 11.49% on the same dates.
>
> Which fund carried that risk? 00642U held a fifth of the money and 45.9% of the risk; 00679B held a fifth and 2.6%. Left alone, the weights drift. Bought on 4 March 2019 and held, 0050 grew to 48.6% of the book by 8 October 2026.
>
> Resetting the weights each month costs money. The largest of 91 monthly resets, on 1 April 2026, traded 8.06% of the book and cost 0.031% of its value. How should the risk be split, and what does each trade cost?

## The Return of a Book {lo=1}

A book's return uses the weights held at the previous close, as an index's does ([[LM12]]). [[LM8]]'s (8.3) and [[LM1]]'s (1.13) are that sum for weights adding up to 1. A book may also hold cash, or borrow.

> [!definition] A book's return and the drift of its weights
> Let $\mathbf w_{t-1}$ hold each fund's value over wealth after the close of date $t-1$, and let cash earn $r_f$ a year. The book's simple return on date $t$, and its weights after that day's returns and before any trade, are
> $$\begin{gathered}R_{p,t}=\mathbf w_{t-1}^{\top}\mathbf R_t+\big(1-\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w_{t-1}\big)\,\frac{r_f}{A_{\mathrm w}},\\ w^{\mathrm{drift}}_{t,i}=\frac{w_{t-1,i}\,(1+R_{i,t})}{1+R_{p,t}}.\end{gathered} \tag{27.1}$$
> A negative cash balance, $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w_{t-1}>1$, pays [[LM31]]'s financing rate in place of $r_f$. The drift needs $1+R_{p,t}>0$; otherwise the book is ruined on date $t$.

Fund $i$'s holding grows from $W_{t-1}w_{t-1,i}$ to $W_{t-1}w_{t-1,i}(1+R_{i,t})$, and cash by the factor $1+r_f/A_{\mathrm w}$. Their sum is the new wealth, $W_{t-1}(1+R_{p,t})$, and each holding over it is the drifted weight. For weights summing to 1 the cash term vanishes, and (27.1) is (8.3).

Between trades (27.1) applies daily, so a fund that beats the book gains weight. A book reset at every close is [[LM8]]'s $X\mathbf w$, (8.12). A book that never trades keeps its holdings, (8.13), and drifts, as does a cap-weighted index between changes of its members or share counts ([[LM12]]). [[LM9]]'s buy-and-hold sector mix drifted from 0.50 to 0.858.

[[ex:drift]] shows the drift of an E5 book bought at a fifth each at the close of 4 March 2019. On 8 October 2026 it had 48.619%, 19.734%, 5.422%, 14.513% and 11.712% in the five funds. 0050's weight ran from 19.1% (15 August 2019) to 49.1% (1 July 2026). 00642U's fell from 21.3% (23 April 2019) to 7.0% (2 November 2020), and 00679B's peaked at 28.0% on 23 March 2020.

> [!example] Six sessions across 0050's halt {#span}
> 0050 stopped trading after 10 June 2025 for its 4-for-1 split and resumed on 18 June. Over those six sessions the five funds returned +0.864%, −1.689%, −0.792%, +2.226% and +12.816%. What did a book earn that held a fifth of each from the close of 10 June?
>
> > [!solution]
> > By (27.1) applied once to the span, it earns their average, 2.685%: across funds, simple returns add ([[LM3]]). The average log return, 0.02524, gives $e^{0.02524}-1=2.56\%$, too low, as (1.14) says of a long-only book.
> >
> > Across time, log returns add. R1 itself, with its weights drifted since its trade of 2 June, earned 2.73%; its log return is the sum of its six daily log returns, (1.12).

The drift needs $1+R_{p,t}>0$, the domain of $\ln(1+R_{p,t})$. R1 and R2, long-only and fully invested, never leave it, since every fund's return exceeds −100%; [[LM35]] shows a book that can.

> [!pitfall] Averaging log returns across funds
> For a long-only book the average of its funds' log returns understates its log return, (1.14). Average simple returns across funds and add log returns across days.

> [!zh] 權重會漂移，報酬要分兩個方向加
> 帳戶當天的報酬，是前一天收盤時的權重（weights）乘上各基金當天的報酬。不交易的時候，漲得比整個帳戶多的基金權重變大，落後的變小，這就是權重漂移（drift）。每天收盤都調回固定權重叫做再平衡（rebalancing），完全不交易叫做買進持有（buy-and-hold）；按市值加權的指數在成分股與股數不變時就是買進持有。跨資產彙總要用簡單報酬（simple return），跨時間彙總要用對數報酬（log return）。把各基金的對數報酬平均，會低估只做多帳戶的報酬。

## Trades, Turnover and Costs {lo=2}

The through case, fixed on 24 October 2026 before any result was computed, runs eight rules on E5. **R1** holds a fifth of each fund; **R2** holds a *constructed* mix of 40% 0050, 20% 00646, 30% 00679B and 10% 00635U. R3 to R8 are defined in [[LM28]] to [[LM31]], and every rule trades by (27.2).

A rule reads its window at a month's last close, known only after 13:30 ([Research hygiene](../research-hygiene.html)). It trades at the next close on which every fund trades, from $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t$ to target exposures $\mathbf f_t$. The costs come out of wealth, so the book is **self-financing**.

> [!definition] The wealth recursion
> With $W_{t-1}$ the wealth after date $t-1$'s trade, the wealth before and after date $t$'s trade and the cost of trades $\mathbf x$ in money are
> $$\begin{aligned}W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t&=W_{t-1}(1+R_{p,t}),\\ W_t&=W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t\\&\quad-\operatorname{cost}\big(W_t\mathbf f_t-W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t\big),\\ \operatorname{cost}(\mathbf x)&=0.1425\%\,\lVert\mathbf x\rVert_1\\&\quad+\textstyle\sum_i\text{tax}_i\max(0,-x_i).\end{aligned} \tag{27.2}$$
> After a trade $\mathbf w_t=\mathbf f_t$. On a date without a trade, $W_t=W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$ and $\mathbf w_t=\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t$.

The trade depends on the post-trade wealth, so $W_t$ sits on both sides. The commission is 0.1425% of the value traded on each side, and the tax 0.1% of every sale except 00679B's, a bond ETF exempt through 2026. These are Taiwan's posted commission and transaction tax; [[LM34]] gives their official sources. No slippage is charged.

> [!result] One post-trade wealth
> If $(0.1425\%+\max_i\text{tax}_i)\,\lVert\mathbf f_t\rVert_1<1$, then (27.2) has exactly one solution $W_t$. If selling the whole book would also cost less than $W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$, then $0<W_t\le W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$ (Deep Dive 1).

For E5's rules the factor is below 0.4%. To size a trade, replace $W_t$ by $W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$ inside the cost:

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{TO}_t&=\tfrac12\lVert\mathbf f_t-\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t\rVert_1,\\ \frac{\operatorname{cost}}{W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t}&\approx0.285\%\,\mathrm{TO}_t\\&\quad+\textstyle\sum_i\text{tax}_i\max\big(0,\,w^{\mathrm{drift}}_{t,i}-f_{t,i}\big),\\ \lvert\text{error}\rvert&\le\frac{\operatorname{cost}}{W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t}\big(0.1425\%+\max_i\text{tax}_i\big)\lVert\mathbf f_t\rVert_1.\end{aligned} \tag{27.3}$$

The one-way turnover $\mathrm{TO}_t$ is half [[LM8]]'s 1-norm, since for weights summing to 1 the buys equal the sales. A trade then costs 0.285% of its turnover with no taxed sale, and up to 0.385% when every sale is taxed. Problem 8 derives the bound.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive 1: one post-trade wealth
> Here $h(W)=W+\operatorname{cost}(W\mathbf f_t-W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t)$, so (27.2) reads $h(W_t)=W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$. The cost is Lipschitz in $\mathbf x$ with constant $0.1425\%+\max_i\text{tax}_i$, so a move of $W$ moves it by at most the result's factor times as much. Hence $h$ is continuous, with slope at least 1 minus that factor, and takes the value $W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$ once.
>
> The root is at most $W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$, since a cost is never negative. It is positive when $h(0)$, the cost of selling the whole book, is below $W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t$, as for every E5 rule. The iteration $W\mapsto W^{\mathrm{pre}}_t-\operatorname{cost}(\cdot)$ contracts by the same factor, so it converges.

> [!example] R1's first purchase {#first}
> R1 starts with cash of 1 and buys a fifth of each fund at the close of 4 March 2019. Find the wealth after the trade, the cost and the turnover.
>
> > [!solution]
> > Every trade is a buy, so no tax is due, and the trades sum to $W_t$ in money. By (27.2), $W_t=1-0.001425\,W_t$, so $W_t=1/1.001425=0.998577$. The cost is 0.1423% of the pre-trade wealth of 1, and $\mathrm{TO}_t=\tfrac12\lVert\mathbf f_t\rVert_1=0.5$. R2's first purchase is the same.

> [!example] R1's trade of 1 April 2026 {#rebalance}
> After the signal of 31 March 2026, R1's drifted weights at the close of 1 April were 17.954%, 18.611%, 18.697%, 16.680% and 28.058%. It traded back to a fifth each. Find the turnover and the cost, by (27.3) and exactly.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The turnover is half the sum of the five gaps to 20%: $\mathrm{TO}_t=8.058\%$. Only 00642U is sold, by 8.058 points, so (27.3) gives $0.285\%\times8.058\%+0.1\%\times8.058\%=0.03102\%$ of the pre-trade wealth. The exact root of (27.2) gives 0.03100%: NT\$3,100 on a book of NT\$10,000,000 ([[ex:trade]]). It was the largest of R1's 91 rebalances by turnover.

> [!pitfall] Turnover counted on both sides
> Adding the buys and the sales doubles the one-way turnover, which is half the 1-norm. The commission falls on both sides, so (27.3)'s rate is $2\times0.1425\%$ of $\mathrm{TO}_t$; the tax falls on sales only.

> [!zh] 交易成本從財富裡扣
> 再平衡要買賣，每一筆都付手續費（commission），賣出還要付證券交易稅（securities transaction tax）。這些交易成本（transaction cost）直接從帳戶財富扣掉，不另外放錢進來，所以叫做自我融資（self-financing）。麻煩在於交易後的財富決定要買多少，買多少又決定成本，所以它同時出現在等式兩邊；只要成本率乘上總曝險（gross exposure）小於 1，就只有一個解，可以反覆代入求出。週轉率（turnover）是單邊的，等於權重差的 1-範數（1-norm）的一半；把買進和賣出加總會算成兩倍。

## E5: Two Frames and Asynchronous Closes {lo=3}

E5's funds track the indices of [[ex:case]]; those of 00635U and 00642U follow COMEX gold and NYMEX WTI crude-oil futures. Returns are total returns in New Taiwan dollars, $R_{i,t}=(P_t+D_t)/P_{t-1}-1$, and on 0050's 4-for-1 split of 18 June 2025 the return is $(P_t+D_t)/(P_{t-1}/4)-1$. The sample runs from the base close of 17 January 2017, 00679B's first, to 8 October 2026: 3,551 days, or 9.7221 years.

The funds were chosen in 2026 among funds still listed; [[LM34]] shows what such a choice can hide. Joint statistics need every fund's return over the same session, but wealth must cover every session. The **estimation frame** holds the dates on which every fund has a one-session return: $T=2{,}362$, and $A=2{,}362\times365.25/3{,}551=242.951$ as [[LM10]] counts a year. The **wealth frame** holds all 2,368 sessions, $A_{\mathrm w}=243.569$; a halted fund earns 0 there until it trades again.

Every covariance, correlation, mean and risk share uses the estimation frame, "on the 2,362 joint dates"; every book's return, drift, trade and wealth uses the wealth frame.

> [!example] Two frames, one halt {#frames}
> Why do E5's frames differ by six dates, and what does that do to 00642U's growth factor?
>
> > [!solution]
> > The six dates 11 to 18 June 2025 are in the wealth frame only. 0050 earns 0 on the first five and $4\times47.57/188.65-1=0.864\%$ on 18 June, from its close of 10 June. Lacking 0050's one-session returns, the estimation frame drops all six. So 00642U's growth factor is 1.593 over the wealth frame and 1.412 over the estimation frame, which misses its +12.816% of [[eg:span]].

All five funds trade in Taipei, 00679B on TPEx and the others on TWSE. Both markets match orders from 09:00 to 13:30 with a closing call auction from 13:25 (TWSE's Operating Rules, Art. 3; TPEx's Business Rules, Art. 40, which its ETF rules apply). New York's core session ends at 4:00 p.m. (NYSE): 04:00 Taipei time in US summer time, 05:00 otherwise.

So the 13:30 closes of 00646 and 00679B follow the previous New York session, and 0050 reacts to it too. TWSE's ETF overview page adds that a futures-trust ETF on a foreign futures index is valued on European and US futures, so the time difference is a risk. Across markets that close at different times, correlations are understated (Burns, Engle and Mezrich 1998). Here the closes coincide but reflect different sessions, so daily correlations can mislead either way.

[[ex:freq]] compounds the daily returns within ISO weeks and calendar months, weeks annualized as [[LM19]] counts a year. The 500 weeks with a session span 9.6975 years, from the close of 20 January 2017 to that of 2 October 2026, so $A=500\times365.25/3{,}542=51.56$; months use $A=12$.

> [!zh] 同一個收盤時間，不同的海外交易時段
> 五檔指數股票型基金（ETF）都在台北 13:30 收盤，但 00646 和 00679B 的價格反映前一晚的紐約交易時段，0050 在同一個收盤也受那個時段影響。期貨型 ETF 依歐美期貨評價，時差本身就是風險。這種非同步（asynchronous）收盤會讓日報酬的相關係數（correlation）和週、月報酬的不同，而且方向不一定。比較不同頻率時要附上標準誤（standard error）：兩種標準誤都假設各期獨立，plug-in 標準誤還需要四階動差（fourth moment）有限，所以要同時看各基金的峰態（kurtosis）。

## Portfolio Mean and Variance {lo=4}

Let $\mathbf R$ have mean $\boldsymbol\mu$ and covariance matrix $\Sigma$, and hold constant weights $\mathbf w$. The return $R_p=\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf R$ is linear in $\mathbf R$, so its mean is $\mathbf w^{\top}\boldsymbol\mu$, and (16.4) with $M=\mathbf w^{\top}$ gives its variance:

$$\begin{gathered}\mu_p=\mathbf w^{\top}\boldsymbol\mu,\qquad \sigma_p^2=\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w,\\ \text{a year: }A\mu_p\ \text{and}\ \sqrt A\,\sigma_p.\end{gathered} \tag{27.4}$$

In sample the same steps give (10.4), $s_p^2=\mathbf w^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf w$, exact for a book reset at every close. The annual forms are conventions on simple returns, since variances add only for log returns on uncorrelated periods, (19.7). Every matrix here is a plug-in estimate with an error this module does not measure; [[LM30]] measures the error of $\hat\Sigma$ and of R1's risk shares.

## Which Fund Carries the Risk {lo=5,6}

By (16.2) and the bilinearity of covariance, $\Cov(R_i,R_p)=\sum_j\sigma_{ij}w_j$, entry $i$ of $\Sigma\mathbf w$, as [[LM7]] found for two assets. Dividing by $\sigma_p^2$ gives fund $i$'s beta to the book, the slope of its line on $R_p$ by (12.3):

$$\begin{gathered}\Cov(R_i,R_p)=(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i,\\ \beta_{i,p}=\frac{(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i}{\sigma_p^2},\qquad \sum_iw_i\beta_{i,p}=1.\end{gathered} \tag{27.5}$$

The last identity holds because $\sum_iw_i(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i=\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w=\sigma_p^2$. Both ratios need $\sigma_p>0$.

The **marginal risk contribution** $\mathrm{MRC}_i$ is the rate at which the book's volatility changes with fund $i$'s weight, the other weights fixed. By (10.8), $\nabla\sigma_p^2=2\Sigma\mathbf w$, and for $\sigma_p>0$ the chain rule gives $\partial\sigma_p/\partial w_i=(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i/\sigma_p$. Multiplying by $w_i$ gives the **risk contribution** $\mathrm{RC}_i$:

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{MRC}_i=\frac{\partial\sigma_p}{\partial w_i}=\frac{(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i}{\sigma_p},\\ \mathrm{RC}_i=w_i\,\mathrm{MRC}_i,\qquad \textstyle\sum_i\mathrm{RC}_i=\sigma_p,\\ \mathrm{RC}_i/\sigma_p=w_i\beta_{i,p}.\end{gathered} \tag{27.6}$$

The sum is **Euler's identity**, which holds because $\sigma_p$ is homogeneous of degree 1 in $\mathbf w$: doubling every weight doubles the volatility (Deep Dive 2). Maillard, Roncalli and Teïletche (2010) define the contributions this way. The **risk share** $w_i\beta_{i,p}$ is $\mathrm{RC}_i$ over $\sigma_p$. [[LM10]] warned that entry $i$ of $2\hat\Sigma\mathbf w$ is a rate of change, not a share: divided by $2s_p$ it is $\mathrm{MRC}_i$, and the share needs $w_i$ times it.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive 2: Euler's theorem
> Here $h$ is differentiable at $\mathbf x$, with $h(a\mathbf x)=a\,h(\mathbf x)$ for every $a>0$. Differentiating in $a$ at $a=1$ gives $\sum_ix_i\,\partial h/\partial x_i=h(\mathbf x)$. The volatility $\sqrt{\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w}$ is such a function wherever $\sigma_p>0$, and not differentiable at a book of zero variance. The variance, homogeneous of degree 2, gives $\sum_iw_i\,\partial\sigma_p^2/\partial w_i=2\sigma_p^2$, which by (10.8) reads $\sum_iw_i\Cov(R_i,R_p)=\sigma_p^2$. The working paper of Maillard et al. states the split this way (§2.1, p. 4, and its footnote).

Euler's split is the meaningful one, for five reasons. It adds up to the book's volatility. Fund $i$'s share of the variance is its variance term plus half of each covariance term it enters (Problem 7). ## Negative and Moving Contributions {lo=6}

A risk contribution is negative exactly when $w_i\Cov(R_i,R_p)<0$, as for a long position in a fund that moves against the book. It lowers the book's volatility at the margin, not at any size: with the other weights fixed,

$$\begin{aligned}\frac{\partial\,\mathrm{MRC}_i}{\partial w_i}&=\frac{\sigma_i^2-\beta_{i,p}^2\sigma_p^2}{\sigma_p}\\&=\frac{\sigma_i^2\,(1-\rho_{i,p}^2)}{\sigma_p}\ge0\end{aligned}$$

(Problem 5). So $\mathrm{MRC}_i$ never falls as $w_i$ grows, and a hedge at the margin stops hedging at a large enough weight.

## Systematic and Residual Risk {lo=7}

[[LM23]]'s market model, fitted by (23.1), splits each sector's return into a part that moves with the market and a residual:

$$R_{i,t}=\alpha_i+\beta_iR_{\text{mkt},t}+\varepsilon_{i,t}.$$

A book with weights $\mathbf w$ then has the beta $\mathbf w^{\top}\boldsymbol\beta$ and the residual $\sum_iw_i\varepsilon_{i,t}$. If the errors are uncorrelated with $R_{\text{mkt},t}$, (16.4) gives

$$\begin{gathered}\sigma_p^2=(\mathbf w^{\top}\boldsymbol\beta)^2\sigma^2_{\text{mkt}}+\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{res}}\mathbf w,\\ \mathrm{RC}_i=\frac{w_i\beta_i(\mathbf w^{\top}\boldsymbol\beta)\,\sigma^2_{\text{mkt}}+w_i(\Sigma_{\mathrm{res}}\mathbf w)_i}{\sigma_p}.\end{gathered} \tag{27.8}$$

In sample (27.8) is exact when each $\beta_i$ is the OLS slope with an intercept, because the normal equations make each residual series' sample covariance with $R_{\text{mkt},t}$ zero (Problem 9). The first term is the **systematic** part, the second the **residual** part, and both split by fund.

U28 holds price indices, without dividends, and only their covariances are used. Under (23.8)'s assumption that dividends accrue evenly, the dividend gap moves the intercepts, not the covariances.

> [!zh] 系統性風險與殘差風險
> 市場模型（market model）把每個類股的報酬拆成跟著大盤動的部分和自己的殘差（residual）。帳戶的 beta 是各類股 beta 的加權平均，帳戶變異數於是分成兩塊：beta 平方乘上大盤變異數是系統性風險（systematic risk），其餘來自殘差的共變異數矩陣（covariance matrix）。用最小平方法加截距估計時，殘差和大盤的樣本共變異數剛好是 0，所以這個變異數分解（variance decomposition）在樣本內完全成立。單一指標模型（single index model）假設殘差彼此不相關，但等權重類股帳戶的殘差平均正相關，略去它們會低估波動率：14.73%，實際是 15.75%。

## Portfolio Accounts in Python {lo=8}

The lab's files hold E5's frames in long form, one row per date and fund, and the block below works from them.

```python
import numpy as np
import polars as pl

funds = ["0050", "00646", "00679B", "00635U", "00642U"]
est = pl.read_csv("data/e5_estimation.csv",
                  schema_overrides={"fund": pl.String})
X = (est.pivot(on="fund", index="date", values="R")
     .sort("date").select(funds).to_numpy())
A = 2362 * 365.25 / 3551                       # 242.951
S = A * np.cov(X, rowvar=False, ddof=0)        # divisor T
w = np.full(5, 0.2)
sp = np.sqrt(w @ S @ w)                        # 0.11493
rc = w * (S @ w) / sp                          # (27.6)
assert abs(rc.sum() - sp) < 1e-15              # Euler's identity
d = pl.col("date").str.to_date()
wk = (est.group_by("fund", d.dt.iso_year().alias("y"),
                   d.dt.week().alias("k"))
      .agg((pl.col("R") + 1).product() - 1)   # weekly returns
      .sort("fund", "y", "k"))

def post_trade(W_pre, drift, f, tax, k=0.001425):
    W = W_pre                                  # (27.2) by iteration
    while True:
        x = W * f - W_pre * drift
        new = W_pre - k * np.abs(x).sum() - tax @ np.clip(-x, 0, None)
        if abs(new - W) < 1e-15 * W_pre:
            return new
        W = new
```

`np.cov` divides by $T-1$ unless given `ddof=0`. `select(funds)` fixes the column order, and the sort fixes the order of the groups, which `group_by` does not keep.

## Summary {-}

- A book's return uses the previous close's weights, (27.1); between trades the weights drift.
- Costs come out of wealth, so the post-trade wealth solves (27.2), which has one root.
- One-way turnover is half the 1-norm; a trade costs about 0.285% of it plus taxes, (27.3).
- Joint statistics use E5's 2,362 joint dates, wealth its 2,368 sessions; daily correlations can mislead either way.
- A book's mean and variance are $\mathbf w^{\top}\boldsymbol\mu$ and $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w$, (27.4); the ratio (27.7) is at least 1 for a long-only book.
- Euler's contributions (27.5)–(27.6) add up to $\sigma_p$ and price a switch; a negative one hedges only at the margin.
- With market betas the variance splits exactly in sample into systematic and residual parts, (27.8).

1. **B** is correct: per unit of start wealth the funds grow to 0.55 and 0.475, so fund 1's weight is $0.55/1.025=0.537$. A forgets the book's growth; C is the rebalanced weight.

2. **C** is correct: $\mathrm{TO}_t=\tfrac12(0.10+0+0.05+0.05+0)=0.10$, and only 0050's sale of 0.10 is taxed, so the cost is $0.285\%\times0.10+0.1\%\times0.10=0.0385\%$; the exact root also gives 0.0385%. A leaves out the tax, and B charges commission on twice the turnover.

3. **A** is correct: by (1.13) the growth factor is $0.5\times1.40+0.5\times0.80=1.10$, a return of 10.00%. B averages the log returns, $\sqrt{1.40\times0.80}-1=5.83\%$, which understates a long-only book by (1.14). C reads that average log return, 0.0567, as a simple return.

4. **C** is correct: with $\sigma_{ij}=\rho_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j$, $(\Sigma\mathbf w)_1=0.016+0.0016+0.0072=0.0248$ and $\sigma_p^2=0.4\times0.0248+0.4\times0.0044+0.2\times0.03=0.01768$. So the share is $0.4\times0.0248/0.01768=56.1\%$. A is the variance term, $0.4^2\times0.04/0.01768=36.2\%$, and B the stand-alone split, $0.08/0.18=44.4\%$.

5. **A** is correct: $w_i>0$ and $\mathrm{RC}_i<0$ give $\mathrm{MRC}_i<0$, so moving a small weight from fund $j$ changes $\sigma_p$ by about that weight times $\mathrm{MRC}_i-\mathrm{MRC}_j<0$. B fails, because $(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i/\sigma_p=\rho_{i,p}\sigma_i$ gives
   $$\begin{aligned}\frac{\partial\,\mathrm{MRC}_i}{\partial w_i}&=\frac{\sigma_i^2}{\sigma_p}-\frac{(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i^2}{\sigma_p^3}\\&=\frac{\sigma_i^2(1-\rho_{i,p}^2)}{\sigma_p}\ge0,\end{aligned}$$
   so $\mathrm{MRC}_i$ turns positive at a large enough weight. C fails: selling a small weight into cash changes $\sigma_p$ by about minus that weight times $\mathrm{MRC}_i$, a rise.

6. **B** is correct: $(1-0.5521^2)/\sqrt{116}=0.6952/10.770=0.0645$. A divides by $T$ instead of $\sqrt T$, and C leaves out $1-\rho^2$. The plug-in standard error of [[LM20]]'s Problem 6 is 0.081.

7. By (10.8), $\partial\sigma_p^2/\partial w_i=2(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i$, and the chain rule on $\sigma_p=\sqrt{\sigma_p^2}$ gives $(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i/\sigma_p$. Then $\sum_iw_i\,\mathrm{MRC}_i=\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w/\sigma_p=\sigma_p$, and $\mathrm{RC}_i/\sigma_p=w_i(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i/\sigma_p^2=w_i\beta_{i,p}$, whose sum is 1. Finally, $w_i(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i=w_i^2\sigma_i^2+\sum_{j\ne i}w_iw_j\sigma_{ij}$, and each $w_iw_j\sigma_{ij}$ is half of the term $2w_iw_j\sigma_{ij}$ of $\sigma_p^2$.

8. Sum separately over the funds bought (b) and sold (s), write $k=0.1425\%$, drop the date and write $w_i$ for $w^{\mathrm{drift}}_i$. Then
   $$\begin{aligned}W&=W^{\mathrm{pre}}-k\textstyle\sum_{\mathrm b}(Wf_i-W^{\mathrm{pre}}w_i)\\&\quad-\textstyle\sum_{\mathrm s}(k+\text{tax}_i)(W^{\mathrm{pre}}w_i-Wf_i)\end{aligned}$$
   is linear in $W$, so
   $$\frac{W}{W^{\mathrm{pre}}}=\frac{1+k\sum_{\mathrm b}w_i-\sum_{\mathrm s}(k+\text{tax}_i)\,w_i}{1+k\sum_{\mathrm b}f_i-\sum_{\mathrm s}(k+\text{tax}_i)\,f_i}.$$
   Replacing $W$ by $W^{\mathrm{pre}}$ inside the cost gives (27.3). The two trade vectors differ by $(W^{\mathrm{pre}}-W)\mathbf f$, of 1-norm $\operatorname{cost}\times\lVert\mathbf f\rVert_1$, and the cost is Lipschitz with constant $k+\max_i\text{tax}_i$: that is the bound. On [[eg:rebalance]] the gap, 0.0000203% of pre-trade wealth, is within the bound $0.0310028\%\times0.2425\%=0.0000752\%$.

9. With an intercept, the normal equations give each residual series a zero mean and a zero sample covariance with $R_{\text{mkt},t}$. So $\hat\Sigma=\sigma^2_{\text{mkt}}\boldsymbol\beta\boldsymbol\beta^{\top}+\Sigma_{\mathrm{res}}$ exactly, and (27.8) follows from $\mathbf w^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf w$ and $(\hat\Sigma\mathbf w)_i$. With equal weights the dropped part is $N^{-2}$ times the sum of the off-diagonal residual covariances, positive when they average positive. Dropping them understates the variance: 14.73% against 15.75% in [[eg:u28book]].

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Drift of weights | 權重漂移 | The change of a book's weights between trades, (27.1). |
| Buy-and-hold | 買進持有 | A book that keeps its holdings, so its weights drift with prices. |
| Rebalancing | 再平衡 | A trade back to target weights. |
| Self-financing | 自我融資 | Costs are paid out of wealth; no money comes in or goes out, (27.2). |
| Wealth recursion | — | (27.2): the pre-trade wealth, the trade and the post-trade wealth. |
| One-way turnover | 週轉率 | $\mathrm{TO}_t=\tfrac12\lVert\mathbf f_t-\mathbf w^{\mathrm{drift}}_t\rVert_1$, (27.3). |
| Transaction cost | 交易成本 | Commission on both sides and tax on sales, paid at the trade. |
| Estimation frame | — | E5's 2,362 dates on which every fund has a one-session return. |
| Wealth frame | — | E5's 2,368 sessions; a halted fund earns 0 until it trades again. |
| Asynchronous closes | 非同步收盤 | Prices that reflect different sessions abroad at the same close. |
| Diversification ratio | — | $\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf s/s_p$, at least 1 for a long-only book, (27.7). |
| Beta to the portfolio | — | $\beta_{i,p}=\Cov(R_i,R_p)/\sigma_p^2$, (27.5). |
| Marginal risk contribution | 邊際風險貢獻 | $\mathrm{MRC}_i=\partial\sigma_p/\partial w_i=(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i/\sigma_p$, (27.6). |
| Risk contribution | 風險貢獻 | $\mathrm{RC}_i=w_i\,\mathrm{MRC}_i$; the contributions sum to $\sigma_p$. |
| Risk share | — | $\mathrm{RC}_i/\sigma_p=w_i\beta_{i,p}$. |
| Euler's identity | Euler 定理 | $\sum_iw_i\,\partial\sigma_p/\partial w_i=\sigma_p$, since $\sigma_p$ is homogeneous of degree 1. |
| Systematic risk | 系統性風險 | $(\mathbf w^{\top}\boldsymbol\beta)^2\sigma^2_{\text{mkt}}$, the market part of the variance, (27.8). |
| Residual risk | 殘差 | $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma_{\mathrm{res}}\mathbf w$, the rest of the variance, (27.8). |
| Single index model | 單一指標模型 | A market model whose residuals are taken as uncorrelated. |
