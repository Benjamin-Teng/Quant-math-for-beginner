## Introduction

[[LM13]] to [[LM18]] found that large moves come in clusters, and [[LM19]] measured it: TR23's squared log returns correlate 0.194 with the previous day's. This module forecasts tomorrow's variance from that dependence, measures a day's variance from intraday index data, and judges the forecasts against that noisy measurement.

> [!case] The day after the worst day
> On Wednesday 2 April 2025, the last session before the Qingming holiday, a GARCH(1,1) model refitted on the previous five years forecast a daily standard deviation of 1.72%. That is 27.0% a year. An exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) said 1.56%. On Monday 7 April the TAIEX total-return index fell 9.70%, a log return of −10.20%: 5.9 of GARCH's standard deviations, and TR23's largest fall ([[LM15]]).
>
> On the evening of 7 April the forecasts for 8 April diverged: GARCH 3.60%, EWMA 2.92%, and 7 April's own realized volatility, read as a forecast, 10.09%. The day's realized measure, $\sqrt{V_t}$ from intraday TAIEX data, then put 8 April at 3.39% ([[ex:case]]). Which forecast should a risk manager trust, how fast should it fall back, and how can a forecast be judged when a day's true variance is never observed?

## Volatility Clustering and the ARCH Test {lo=1}

The evidence so far concerns the sizes of returns, not their signs. After a fall of 3% or more TR23 fell again 12.04% of the time, against 1.85% overall ([[LM13]]). Next-day log returns correlate only 0.0393, but their sizes correlate 0.161 ([[LM19]]). [[LM25]]'s Ljung–Box statistic for $\lvert r_t\rvert$, (25.5), is 1,820 on ten lags.

**Volatility clustering** is this dependence between sizes. A model for it describes the **conditional variance** $\sigma_t^2$, the variance of day $t$ given the returns before it:

$$r_t=g+\varepsilon_t,\qquad \varepsilon_t=\sigma_t\xi_t.$$

Here $\sigma_t>0$ depends only on earlier returns, and the $\xi_t$ are iid with mean 0 and variance 1, each independent of the past. By the tower property (16.5), $\E[\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t-k}]=\E\big[\E[\varepsilon_t\mid\text{past}]\,\varepsilon_{t-k}\big]=0$ for $k\ge1$. So the innovations are uncorrelated, yet $\varepsilon_t^2$ and $\varepsilon_{t-1}^2$ correlate whenever $\sigma_t$ grows with $\lvert\varepsilon_{t-1}\rvert$ and fourth moments are finite.

> [!result] The ARCH test
> Let $\hat\varepsilon_t=r_t-\bar r$. Regress $\hat\varepsilon_t^2$ by OLS, (23.1), on a constant and $\hat\varepsilon_{t-1}^2,\dots,\hat\varepsilon_{t-K}^2$, with coefficients $a,b_1,\dots,b_K$, over the $T-K$ days that have $K$ lags. Under $H_0$ the $\varepsilon_t$ are iid with a finite fourth moment, and then, for large $T$, the statistic
> $$\begin{gathered}(T-K)\times\text{R-squared}\\ \text{is approximately }\chi^2_K.\end{gathered} \tag{26.1}$$
> The test uses the upper tail. Stated without proof (Engle 1982).

> [!example] The ARCH test on TR23 {#archtest}
> Test TR23's log returns, in percent, with $K=1$ and with $K=5$.
>
> > [!solution]
> > With $K=1$ the intercept is $a=1.1779$ and the slope $b_1=0.196$. The slope is close to [[LM19]]'s $\hat\rho_1$ of $r_t^2$, 0.194, because regressing a stationary series on its lag gives about its lag-1 autocorrelation. R-squared is 0.03844, so the statistic is $5{,}848\times0.03844=224.8$, with a p-value of $8.2\times10^{-51}$ from $\chi^2_1$.
> >
> > With $K=5$ the statistic is 696.3, far above the 5% point 11.07 of $\chi^2_5$; its p-value is below $10^{-100}$. These $\chi^2$ p-values are nominal: (26.1) needs a finite fourth moment, which [[LM15]]'s fit ($\nu=3.065$) lacks ([[LM19]]). The five slopes sum to 0.529.
> >
> > Experiment 3 shuffles TR23's days 20,000 times (`default_rng([3, 20261011])`), as [[LM21]]'s experiment 8 shuffled dates; no shuffle reaches 224.8 or 696.3. A constant variance is rejected beyond doubt.

> [!pitfall] The ARCH test names no model
> Any change in variance makes the test reject. After a calm decade and a turbulent one, squared residuals correlate with their lags, because a day and its lag usually share a decade. A rejection says only that the variance is not constant.

> [!zh] 大小會群聚，方向不會
> 報酬的正負號幾乎無法預測，報酬的大小卻會成群出現，這就是波動群聚現象（volatility clustering）。模型描述的是條件變異數（conditional variance）：在已知昨天以前所有報酬的條件下，今天報酬的變異數。新息（innovation）的條件期望值（conditional expectation）是零，所以互不相關（uncorrelated）；但只要今天的變異數取決於昨天的意外有多大，它們的平方就會相關。Engle 的自我迴歸條件變異數非齊一檢定（ARCH test）把殘差（residual）平方對自己前幾期的值（lagged values）做迴歸（regression），對立假設（alternative hypothesis）是自我迴歸條件變異數非齊一模型（ARCH model）；在虛無假設（null hypothesis）下，若四階動差（fourth moment）有限，統計量（statistic）近似卡方分布（chi-square distribution）。檢定拒絕只代表變異數不是常數，並沒有指出是哪一種模型。

>

## The Exponentially Weighted Moving Average {lo=2}

The simplest variance forecast is the mean of the last 60 squared returns, in which a crash counts fully for 60 days and then drops out at once. An exponentially weighted average lets old days fade instead.

> [!definition] EWMA variance
> With a decay $0<\lambda<1$ and returns of mean zero, the **exponentially weighted moving average** forecast of tomorrow's variance is
> $$\begin{aligned}\sigma^2_{t+1\mid t}&=\lambda\,\sigma^2_{t\mid t-1}+(1-\lambda)\,r_t^2\\&=(1-\lambda)\sum_{j\ge0}\lambda^jr_{t-j}^2.\end{aligned} \tag{26.2}$$

The second form follows by substituting the recursion into itself (Problem 7). The weights $(1-\lambda)\lambda^j$ sum to 1 and halve every $\ln\frac12/\ln\lambda$ days. The **effective window** $1/(1-\lambda)$ is the number of equal weights that would give today the same weight.

RiskMetrics (1996, §5.3.2.2) fixed $\lambda=0.94$ for daily data. That is a weighted average, over 480 series, of the $\lambda$ that minimizes each series' root mean squared error of variance forecasts, weighted by forecast accuracy. Its §5.3.1.2 counts the effective days as those that leave out a tolerance level of the weight: at 1%, $\ln0.01/\ln0.94=74.4$, which its Table 5.7 lists as 74 days. RiskMetrics sets the mean return to zero (its Table 5.9), as in (26.2).

> [!example] EWMA across 7 April 2025 {#ewmahand}
> The EWMA forecast for 7 April was 2.4351 %², or 1.5605%. Update it with 7 April's log return, −10.2029%, and describe the weights at $\lambda=0.94$.
>
> > [!solution]
> > In %², $r_t^2=104.0999$. By (26.2),
> > $$\begin{aligned}\sigma^2_{t+1\mid t}&=0.94\times2.4351+0.06\times104.0999\\&=2.2890+6.2460,\end{aligned}$$
> > so the forecast for 8 April is $\sqrt{8.5350}=2.9215\%$, nearly double.
> >
> > The effective window is 16.7 days, the weights halve every 11.2 days, and the last 75 days carry 99.0% of the weight. The recursion started at 1.84 %², the mean of $r_t^2$ over the first 246 returns. In the forecast for 2 January 2008, Section 7's first test day, that start carries a weight of $0.94^{1{,}240}=5\times10^{-34}$.

> [!pitfall] A decay chosen on the test days
> The best $\lambda$ of [[eg:lambda]] beats 0.94 on the days it was chosen on, by construction. Only a value fixed in advance, as RiskMetrics' 0.94 was, gives an out-of-sample score.

> [!zh] 記憶逐漸淡去，而不是一刀切
> 60 天移動平均（moving average）把每一天都當成同等重要，滿 60 天就一次丟掉，所以預測值（forecast）會突然跳動。指數加權移動平均模型（EWMA）讓每一天的權重（weight）以 $\lambda$ 的比例逐日遞減：昨天的平方報酬占 $1-\lambda$，越早的日子越不重要，但不會一下子歸零。RiskMetrics 對日資料用 $\lambda=0.94$，有效視窗（effective window）約 16.7 天，權重每 11.2 天減半。崩盤隔天，波動率（volatility）預測立刻上升，之後慢慢回落；回落到哪裡，取決於最近的平靜程度，因為 EWMA 沒有長期水準。

## GARCH(1,1): Model, Forecasts and Half-Life {lo=3}

EWMA has no long-run level, so its forecast for every later day equals tomorrow's (Deep Dive 1). GARCH, generalized ARCH (Bollerslev 1986), adds a constant that pulls the variance back toward a level.

> [!definition] GARCH(1,1)
> Let $r_t=g+\varepsilon_t$ and $\varepsilon_t=\sigma_t\xi_t$, with
> $$\sigma_t^2=\omega+\alpha\,\varepsilon_{t-1}^2+\beta\,\sigma_{t-1}^2. \tag{26.3}$$
> The model's conditions are:
> 1. the $\xi_t$ are iid with mean 0 and variance 1, each independent of the returns before day $t$;
> 2. a positive constant, $\omega>0$;
> 3. non-negative coefficients, $\alpha\ge0$ and $\beta\ge0$;
> 4. a persistence below 1, $\alpha+\beta<1$.

Tomorrow's variance is a constant plus a share of today's squared surprise and a share of today's variance. The $\xi_t$ may be normal, or Student $t$ scaled to variance 1 by (15.11).

Given the returns up to day $t$, $\sigma_{t+1}^2$ is known, so $\sigma^2_{t+1\mid t}=\sigma_{t+1}^2$. For $j\ge1$, condition 1 and the tower property (16.5) give $\E[\varepsilon_{t+j}^2\mid\text{day }t]=\sigma^2_{t+j\mid t}$. Taking that expectation of (26.3) for day $t+j+1$ gives

$$\sigma^2_{t+j+1\mid t}=\omega+(\alpha+\beta)\,\sigma^2_{t+j\mid t}.$$

A stationary model has a constant unconditional variance $\sigma^2=\E\varepsilon_t^2$, and the same step on unconditional expectations gives $\sigma^2=\omega+(\alpha+\beta)\sigma^2$. Subtracting, each step multiplies the forecast's distance from $\sigma^2$ by $\alpha+\beta$.

> [!result] Unconditional variance, forecasts and half-life
> Under the conditions of (26.3), with $\varepsilon_t$ stationary,
> $$\begin{gathered}\sigma^2=\frac{\omega}{1-\alpha-\beta},\\ \sigma^2_{t+m\mid t}=\sigma^2+(\alpha+\beta)^{m-1}\big(\sigma^2_{t+1\mid t}-\sigma^2\big),\\ \text{half-life}=\frac{\ln\frac12}{\ln(\alpha+\beta)}.\end{gathered} \tag{26.4}$$
> The half-life is (25.2)'s: the number of sessions in which a shock's effect on the forecast halves. That a stationary solution exists when $\alpha+\beta<1$ is stated without proof (Tsay 2010, ch. 3).

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive 1: the GARCH variance unrolled
> Here $\eta_t$ is [[LM25]]'s white noise. Apply (26.3) $k$ times:
> $$\sigma_t^2=\omega\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta^j+\alpha\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\beta^j\varepsilon_{t-1-j}^2+\beta^k\sigma_{t-k}^2.$$
> For $\beta<1$ and bounded $\E\sigma_{t-k}^2$ the last term vanishes as $k\to\infty$. So $\sigma_t^2=\omega/(1-\beta)+\alpha\sum_{j\ge0}\beta^j\varepsilon_{t-1-j}^2$: past surprises weighted with decay $\beta$, plus a floor. EWMA is the case $\omega=0$, $\alpha=1-\lambda$, $\beta=\lambda$, which breaks conditions 2 and 4. With $\omega=0$ and $\alpha+\beta=1$ the forecast recursion gives $\sigma^2_{t+m\mid t}=\sigma^2_{t+1\mid t}$ for every $m$; with $\omega>0$ it rises by $\omega$ a step.
>
> Next, let $\eta_t:=\varepsilon_t^2-\sigma_t^2$, which has mean 0 given the past and is white noise when $\E\varepsilon_t^4<\infty$. Replacing $\sigma_t^2$ and $\sigma_{t-1}^2$ in (26.3) by $\varepsilon_t^2-\eta_t$ and $\varepsilon_{t-1}^2-\eta_{t-1}$ gives
> $$\varepsilon_t^2=\omega+(\alpha+\beta)\,\varepsilon_{t-1}^2+\eta_t-\beta\,\eta_{t-1},$$
> an ARMA(1,1) in the squares. For $k\ge2$, $\eta_t$ and $\eta_{t-1}$ are uncorrelated with $\varepsilon_{t-k}^2$. So from lag 2 on, each autocovariance of $\varepsilon_t^2$ is $\alpha+\beta$ times the one before, like (25.1)'s AR(1).

> [!example] How fast the forecast falls back {#term}
> Take the full-sample normal fit of [[ex:garchfit]], with $\alpha+\beta=0.98443$ and $\sigma^2=1.5971$ %². On the evening of 7 April 2025 it forecast $\sigma_{t+1\mid t}=3.517\%$ (12.3695 %²). Trace the forecasts.
>
> > [!solution]
> > By (26.4) the forecasts fall to 3.42%, 3.10%, 2.66% and 1.35% at $m$ = 5, 20, 44 and 250 sessions, toward $\sigma=1.264\%$ a day. The half-life is $\ln\frac12/\ln0.98443=44.2$ sessions. The average forecast variance over the next 20 days is a volatility of 51.8% a year, and over 250 days 32.6%.
> >
> > On 8 October 2026, the last day, the forecast was 1.092% (17.13% a year), below 19.83%, so it rises toward that level. arch's `forecast` gives the same values to $10^{-12}$.

Starting from [[eg:term]], the widget draws (26.4) for any $\alpha+\beta<1$ at the fit's unconditional variance. [[LM31]] scales a position by such a forecast, and [[LM32]] turns it into a value-at-risk. In continuous time a variance that moves at random is [[LM39]]'s stochastic volatility.

> [!pitfall] $\sqrt A$ times a one-day forecast is not a year's forecast
> Annualizing tomorrow's forecast by $\sqrt A$ assumes that every later day has the same variance. On the evening of 7 April 2025 that gave 55.18% a year, while the average forecast over the next 250 sessions was 32.57%. Average the forecasts over the horizon instead, as the widget does.

> [!zh] 衝擊會衰退，回到長期水準
> 廣義自我迴歸條件變異數非齊一模型（GARCH）說：明天的變異數（variance）等於一個常數，加上今天意外的平方的一部分，再加上今天變異數的一部分。只要 $\alpha+\beta<1$，就有一個長期的無條件變異數（unconditional variance），預測值（forecast）與它的差距每天乘上 $\alpha+\beta$。差距減半所需的天數就是半衰期（half-life）。加權股價報酬指數的 $\alpha+\beta=0.98443$，半衰期約 44 個交易日。把明天的預測乘上 $\sqrt A$ 當成年化波動率（annualized volatility），等於假設往後每天都一樣，在崩盤之後會高估很多。

>
> > [!answer]-
> > By (26.4), $\ln\frac12/\ln0.98=34.3$ sessions, and $\ln\frac12/\ln0.99098=76.5$ sessions.

## Fitting GARCH by Maximum Likelihood {lo=4}

The parameters $\boldsymbol\theta=(g,\omega,\alpha,\beta)^{\top}$ are fitted by maximum likelihood ([[LM20]]). Given a start value $\sigma_1^2$, the recursion (26.3) turns each candidate $\boldsymbol\theta$ into a path $\sigma_1^2,\dots,\sigma_T^2$. For normal $\xi_t$ the log-likelihood is [[LM20]]'s normal one, with a variance that changes each day:

$$\ell(\boldsymbol\theta)=-\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T\Big[\ln2\pi+\ln\sigma_t^2+\frac{(r_t-g)^2}{\sigma_t^2}\Big]. \tag{26.5}$$

The arch package starts from its backcast, a mean of the first 75 squared residuals about the sample mean with weights proportional to $0.94^j$, and sets $\sigma_1^2=\omega+(\alpha+\beta)\times\text{backcast}$. It maximizes (26.5) numerically. With Student $t$ innovations the density becomes (15.9)'s, scaled by (15.11), and $\nu$ joins $\boldsymbol\theta$.

Classical standard errors invert $-\ell''$ as in [[LM20]]'s (20.6). Robust ones use a sandwich that does not assume the density is right (arch's `cov_type` options `"classic"` and `"robust"`, its default). Their validity for large $T$ under the dependence of (26.3) is stated without proof; [[LM20]] gives the iid theory.

> [!example] GARCH on TR23 {#fit}
> Fit (26.3) to TR23's 5,849 log returns, in percent, with normal and with $t$ innovations, and give the half-life with an interval.
>
> > [!solution]
> > [[ex:garchfit]] lists both fits. For the normal fit, the NumPy likelihood (26.5), started from arch's backcast of 2.78 %², equals arch's $\ell=-8{,}647.25$ to $10^{-6}$. Its $\alpha+\beta=0.98443$ gives $\sigma^2=1.597$ %², 19.83% a year (robust delta-method standard error 2.3 points) against the sample's 18.99% ([[LM19]]).
> >
> > The half-life is 44.2 sessions. The delta method (20.8) with robust errors gives it a standard error of 16.1, so a 95% interval runs from 13 to 76 sessions. Mapping instead the robust interval for $\alpha+\beta$, $0.98443\pm1.96\times0.0056$ (0.97342 to 0.99544 unrounded), gives 26 to 152 sessions: near 1 the half-life is poorly determined.
> >
> > With $t$ innovations, $\hat\nu=5.54$, $\ell$ rises by 183.35, and the half-life is 76.5 sessions; mapped from $\alpha+\beta$ (0.98343 to 0.99853), its interval runs from 41 to 471 sessions.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive 2: the kurtosis that GARCH implies
> Let (26.3)'s conditions hold, with $\varepsilon_t$ stationary and $\E\varepsilon_t^4<\infty$. Square (26.3) and take expectations, using $\E\varepsilon_{t-1}^4=\E\xi_t^4\,\E\sigma_{t-1}^4$ and $\E[\varepsilon_{t-1}^2\sigma_{t-1}^2]=\E\sigma_{t-1}^4$ (condition 1), $\E\sigma_t^4=\E\sigma_{t-1}^4$ and $\omega=\sigma^2(1-\alpha-\beta)$:
> $$\begin{aligned}&\E\sigma_t^4\,\big(1-(\alpha+\beta)^2-(\E\xi_t^4-1)\alpha^2\big)\\&\qquad=\sigma^4\big(1-(\alpha+\beta)^2\big).\end{aligned}$$
> Since $\E\varepsilon_t^4=\E\xi_t^4\,\E\sigma_t^4$, when the bracket on the left is positive,
> $$\operatorname{Kurt}(\varepsilon_t)=\frac{\E\xi_t^4\,\big(1-(\alpha+\beta)^2\big)}{1-(\alpha+\beta)^2-(\E\xi_t^4-1)\alpha^2}.$$
> For normal $\xi_t$, $\E\xi_t^4=3$ by (14.6). At $\hat\alpha=0.091254$ and $\hat\alpha+\hat\beta=0.984432$ the denominator is 0.014239, so the kurtosis is 6.51, an excess of 3.51. For the $t$ fit, $\E\xi_t^4=3+6/(\nu-4)=6.89$ by (15.10) at $\hat\nu=5.544$, and $(\alpha+\beta)^2+(\E\xi_t^4-1)\alpha^2=1.008>1$: that model has no finite fourth moment.

> [!example] What GARCH explains {#explains}
> How much of the clustering that [[LM13]], [[LM15]] and [[LM16]] found does the normal fit reproduce?

> [!pitfall] A robust standard error does not repair a wrong model
> The sandwich keeps the standard errors honest when $\xi_t$ is not normal, but the estimates still belong to the fitted model. If the variance reacts more to falls than to rises, both kinds of error describe the wrong parameters.

> [!zh] 用最大概度估計配適 GARCH
> 給定起始值之後，GARCH 的遞迴（recursion）把每一組參數（parameter）變成一條條件變異數（conditional variance）的路徑，概度函數（likelihood function）就是每天一個常態密度（normal density）的乘積，只是每天的變異數不同。最大概度估計（maximum likelihood estimation）找出讓這個乘積最大的參數。標準誤（standard error）有兩種：古典標準誤（classical standard error）假設模型的分布（distribution）正確；穩健標準誤（robust standard error）用夾層估計量（sandwich estimator），不需要這個假設。檢查配適（fit）時看標準化殘差（standardized residual）：加權股價報酬指數的標準化殘差還有超額峰態（excess kurtosis）2.57，GARCH 解釋了部分厚尾（heavy tail），但不是全部。

> [!kc]
> Why is the robust standard error of $\hat\alpha$ 1.80 times the classical one?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > The classical error assumes that the normal density is right. The sandwich does not, and the standardized residuals of [[eg:resid]] have excess kurtosis 2.57, not 0.

## Realized Variance and the Signature Plot {lo=5}

A day's variance is never observed, but intraday prices measure it. Take a grid of interval $h$ from 09:30:00 to 13:30:00, with $x_i$ the log index at point $i$. The **realized variance** of day $t$ is [[LM3]]'s $\mathrm{RV}$ on these intraday log returns. A return over $Jh$ sums $J$ returns over $h$, so for returns with mean zero and autocorrelations $\rho_k$ that hold all day, (19.7) gives, ignoring the day's edges,

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{RV}_t(h)=\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-x_{i-1})^2,\quad n=\frac{14{,}400\text{ s}}{h},\\ \frac{\E\,\mathrm{RV}(Jh)}{\E\,\mathrm{RV}(h)}=1+2\sum_{k=1}^{J-1}\Big(1-\frac kJ\Big)\rho_k.\end{gathered} \tag{26.6}$$

For $J=2$ the ratio is $1+\rho_1$.

The grid does not start at 09:00:00 because FinMind prints the previous close there, and the index needs about a minute to absorb the opening gap. It starts at 09:30:00 because the 240 minutes from there to 13:30:00 divide evenly into every interval used here, 5 seconds to 30 minutes.

The **opening return** $r_{\mathrm o,t}$ runs from the previous close to 09:30:00, and the daily target $V_t:=r_{\mathrm o,t}^2+\mathrm{RV}_t(15\text{ min})$, in %², splits the day into 17 squared pieces. A day has 2,880 five-second returns, or 16 fifteen-minute ones. Dividends change TR23's squared returns by less than 0.01% in total on these days.

Without noise, a finer grid only helps: if day $t$'s $n$ returns were iid normal with variance $\sigma_t^2/n$ each, $\mathrm{RV}_t(h)$ would have mean $\sigma_t^2$ and variance $2\sigma_t^4/n$ by (14.6). [[ex:signature]] plots the **volatility signature**, a period's mean $\mathrm{RV}(h)$ over its mean $\mathrm{RV}(5\text{ min})$. On the 5-second days of 2015–2026, $\mathrm{RV}(5\text{ s})$ averaged 11.7 times $\mathrm{RV}(5\text{ min})$, and in 2025, 24.3 times. In 2005–2010 $\mathrm{RV}(1\text{ min})$ averaged only 0.483 of it.

One model of the reversal separates the observed price from an efficient one.

> [!result] A noise model (a hypothesis)
> Let $x_i=x_i^{\mathrm e}+u_i$, where the efficient returns $x_i^{\mathrm e}-x_{i-1}^{\mathrm e}$ are iid with variance $\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2$, the noise $u_i$ is iid with variance $\sigma_u^2$, and the two are independent. Then the log index is [[LM25]]'s persistent part plus noise, (25.9), with a random walk as the persistent part, and its returns form an MA(1), (25.3). Their lag-1 autocorrelation and mean realized variance are
> $$\begin{gathered}\rho_1=-\frac{\sigma_u^2}{\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2+2\sigma_u^2}\in\big[-\tfrac12,0\big],\\ \E\,\mathrm{RV}(h)=n\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2+2n\sigma_u^2.\end{gathered} \tag{26.7}$$
> Here $n\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2$ is the window's efficient variance, the same for every $h$, while the noise term grows with $n$.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive 3: noise and the variance ratio
> Each observed return is $(x_i^{\mathrm e}-x_{i-1}^{\mathrm e})+u_i-u_{i-1}$, with variance $\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2+2\sigma_u^2$. Adjacent returns share $u_i$ with opposite signs, so their covariance is $-\sigma_u^2$; returns further apart share nothing. That gives $\rho_1$ of (26.7), the MA(1) shape of (25.3), and $\E\,\mathrm{RV}(h)$ as a sum of $n$ variances. The noise share of a return's variance is $2\sigma_u^2/(\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2+2\sigma_u^2)=2\lvert\rho_1\rvert$.
>
> If (26.7) held, the mean RV would rise by the same $2\sigma_u^2$ for every extra return a day. On the 5-second days, between neighbouring intervals from 5 seconds to 5 minutes, the rise per extra return runs from $3.1\times10^{-3}$ to $4.9\times10^{-3}$ of $\mathrm{RV}(5\text{ min})$. Each has a Newey–West standard error across days of about a tenth of itself. Day by day the two extremes move together (correlation 0.93), and the Newey–West t-stat of their per-day difference is 10.6.
>
> The Newey–West errors need a finite fourth moment of returns ([[LM19]]), so they are nominal. Yet the largest rise exceeds the smallest on 88% of the days.
>
> So (26.7), with iid efficient returns and iid noise, does not fit. In the 30 Dow Jones stocks, Hansen and Lunde (2006) find noise that is serially dependent and correlated with the efficient returns.

## Judging Forecasts Out of Sample {lo=6}

Six forecasts are fixed in advance and made each evening for the next session. GARCH with normal and with $t$ innovations is refitted at each month's first session on the previous 1,231 returns, five years by [[LM17]]'s rule (226 refits). The others are EWMA, the 60-day mean of squared returns, yesterday's $V$, and an EWMA of $V$, both EWMAs with $\lambda=0.94$. Forecasts run from 2 January 2008 to 8 October 2026: 4,609 days.

> [!definition] Losses for a variance forecast
> For a target $V_t>0$ and a forecast $\sigma^2_{t\mid t-1}>0$, the losses of day $t$ are
> $$\begin{gathered}\text{squared error}=\big(V_t-\sigma^2_{t\mid t-1}\big)^2,\\ \text{QLIKE}=\frac{V_t}{\sigma^2_{t\mid t-1}}-\ln\frac{V_t}{\sigma^2_{t\mid t-1}}-1\ \ge0.\end{gathered} \tag{26.8}$$
> A forecast's MSE is the mean of its squared errors over the test days, in %⁴, and its QLIKE score the mean of its daily QLIKE. QLIKE has no unit and is zero for a perfect forecast.

The target $V_t$ is only a noisy measurement of the variance. MSE and QLIKE still rank forecasts as the true variance would if $\E[V_t\mid\text{day }t-1]=\sigma_t^2$, a conditionally unbiased target. That result is stated without proof (Patton 2011; his 2006 working paper, Section 3). QLIKE depends only on the ratio of target to forecast, so a calm day counts as much as a crash.

> [!definition] Mincer–Zarnowitz regression
> Regress the target on a constant and the forecast by OLS, (23.1):
> $$\E\big[V_t\mid\text{day }t-1\big]=a+b\,\sigma^2_{t\mid t-1}. \tag{26.9}$$
> A conditionally unbiased forecast has $a=0$ and $b=1$. The target's errors grow with the forecast, so the standard errors are White's HC0, (23.6).

> [!pitfall] A noisy, unbiased target lowers R-squared, not the ranking
> GARCH's R-squared of 0.119 against $r_t^2$ does not make it a poor forecast: a single squared return is a noisy target, and its noise caps R-squared. Given the past, $r_t^2$ is unbiased up to the tiny squared mean, so a robust loss still ranks forecasts as the true variance would.

> [!zh] 用有雜訊的目標值評估預測
> 真正的每日變異數（variance）看不到，只能拿實現變異數（realized variance）這類有雜訊（noise）的量來比，所以損失函數（loss function）的選擇很重要。Patton 證明，只要目標值（target）條件不偏（conditionally unbiased），均方誤差（mean squared error，MSE）與 QLIKE 的排序會和用真實變異數排序相同。QLIKE 只看目標值與預測值（forecast）的比率，對預測偏低的懲罰比偏高重。在 4,609 天的樣本外預測（out-of-sample forecasting）中，GARCH 的 QLIKE 最低，但只比 EWMA 好 1.83 個標準誤（standard error）。條件不偏的目標值，其雜訊會壓低判定係數（R-squared），卻不會改變排序。

> [!kc]
> The target is 1 %². What QLIKE does a forecast of half the target score, and one of double the target? What squared error does each score?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Half: $2-\ln2-1=0.3069$. Double: $0.5+\ln2-1=0.1931$. The squared errors are 0.25 and 1. For errors of the same ratio, QLIKE punishes the forecast that is too low more, and the squared error the one that is too high. The squared error itself is symmetric in $V_t-\sigma^2_{t\mid t-1}$.

## Volatility Models in Python {lo=7}

```python
import numpy as np
import polars as pl
from arch import arch_model
from statsmodels.stats.diagnostic import acorr_ljungbox, het_arch

tr = pl.read_csv("data/taiex_tr.csv")["price"].to_numpy()
y = 100 * np.log1p(tr[1:] / tr[:-1] - 1)      # log returns in percent
res = arch_model(y, mean="Constant", vol="GARCH", p=1, q=1).fit(
    disp="off", cov_type="classic")
g, omega, alpha, beta = res.params.to_numpy()  # a pandas Series in arch
fc = res.forecast(horizon=250, reindex=False).variance.to_numpy()[0]
xi = (y - g) / res.conditional_volatility      # an array for array input
arch5 = het_arch(y - y.mean(), nlags=5, result_object=True)
print(arch5.lm)                                # 696.3
print(acorr_ljungbox(np.abs(xi), lags=[10]).to_numpy())   # 27.6
rv = pl.read_csv("data/vol4_rv.csv", comment_prefix="#")
V = 1e4 * (rv["o2"] + rv["rv_15m"]).to_numpy()  # the target, %^2
```

`het_arch` squares the residuals it is given, so pass $r_t-\bar r$; without `result_object=True` it returns a tuple and warns. `acorr_ljungbox` returns a DataFrame. `comment_prefix="#"` skips the derived file's first line, a comment.

## Summary {-}

- Returns are nearly uncorrelated, their sizes are not; the ARCH test (26.1) detects that but names no model.
- EWMA (26.2) weights squared returns by $(1-\lambda)\lambda^j$; RiskMetrics uses $\lambda=0.94$.
- GARCH(1,1) (26.3) pulls forecasts toward $\sigma^2$ with half-life $\ln\frac12/\ln(\alpha+\beta)$, (26.4): 44.2 sessions on TR23.
- The likelihood (26.5) follows from the recursion; for $\omega$, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of the normal fit, robust errors are 1.7 to 1.8 times the classical ones.
- Standardized residuals keep fat tails; GARCH(1,1) misses the asymmetry after falls.
- Realized variance (26.6) depends on the interval; under the noise model (26.7) fine returns reverse.
- MSE and QLIKE (26.8) rank forecasts as the true variance would if the target is conditionally unbiased; GARCH leads, but not every year.

## Solutions {-}

1. **B** is correct: $1/(1-0.97)=33.3$ days. A is the weights' half-life, $\ln\frac12/\ln0.97$. C is the mean lag $\lambda/(1-\lambda)$, one day shorter.

2. **A** is correct: $\sigma^2=0.025/(1-0.98)=1.25$ %², and $\sqrt{246.12\times1.25}=17.5\%$. B forgets $\alpha$: $0.025/(1-0.89)=0.2273$ %², 7.5% a year. C is the daily $\sqrt{1.25}=1.12\%$, not annualized.

3. **C** is correct: $\ln\frac12/\ln0.98=34.3$ sessions by (26.4). A assumes a linear decay, $0.5/(1-0.98)$. B is $1/(1-0.98)$, the decay's effective window (Section 3), not its half-life.

4. **A** is correct: given the past, $r_t^2$ has mean $\sigma_t^2$ plus the tiny squared mean, so Patton's result keeps the ranking, while the noise lowers R-squared. B confuses noise with bias. C reads R-squared as accuracy, although the target's noise caps it.

5. **B** is correct: by (26.6) with $J=2$, the ratio is $1+\rho_1=0.60$. A reverses the sign. C counts the pair twice, $1+2\rho_1$.

6. Given day $t$, condition 1 and (16.5) give $\E[\varepsilon_{t+j}^2\mid\text{day }t]=\sigma^2_{t+j\mid t}$ for $j\ge1$. So (26.3) gives $\sigma^2_{t+j+1\mid t}-\sigma^2=(\alpha+\beta)(\sigma^2_{t+j\mid t}-\sigma^2)$, and $m-1$ steps give (26.4). Then
   $$\begin{aligned}\sigma^2_{t+20\mid t}&=1.5971+0.984432^{19}\\&\quad\times(12.3695-1.5971)\\&=9.5925,\end{aligned}$$
   so $\sigma_{t+20\mid t}=3.097\%$. The average over $m=1,\dots,20$ is
   $$\begin{aligned}&1.5971+10.7724\times\frac{1-0.984432^{20}}{20\times0.015568}\\&\quad=10.9157\text{ \%²},\end{aligned}$$
   and $\sqrt{246.12\times10.9157}=51.8\%$ a year.

7. The weights form a geometric series: $(1-\lambda)\sum_{j\ge0}\lambda^j=(1-\lambda)/(1-\lambda)=1$. Equal weights over $1/(1-\lambda)$ days also give today $1-\lambda$. With $\omega=0$, $\alpha=1-\lambda$ and $\beta=\lambda$, (26.3) is (26.2) for returns with mean zero. Then $\alpha+\beta=1$, so the step of Problem 6 gives $\sigma^2_{t+j+1\mid t}=\sigma^2_{t+j\mid t}$: every horizon has tomorrow's forecast.

8. (a) A return over $2h$ sums two adjacent returns over $h$, so by (19.7) its variance is $2\gamma_0(1+\rho_1)$. A day has $n/2$ such returns, so $\E\,\mathrm{RV}(2h)=n\gamma_0(1+\rho_1)=(1+\rho_1)\,\E\,\mathrm{RV}(h)$.
   (b) By Deep Dive 3, $\rho_1=-\sigma_u^2/(\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2+2\sigma_u^2)$. Since $\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2\ge0$, the denominator is at least $2\sigma_u^2$, so $\rho_1\ge-\frac12$. The noise part of a return, $u_i-u_{i-1}$, has variance $2\sigma_u^2$, a share $2\sigma_u^2/(\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2+2\sigma_u^2)=2\lvert\rho_1\rvert$.
   (c) Here $2\times0.386=0.772$: if (26.7) held, noise would supply 77% of a 5-second return's variance in 2025.

9. Deep Dive 2 gives the derivation. At the fit the denominator is $1-0.984432^2-2\times0.091254^2=0.014239$, and $\operatorname{Kurt}=3\times(1-0.984432^2)/0.014239=6.51$, an excess of 3.51. That is a population value. With so small a denominator, rare stretches of very high variance carry it, and 5,849 days usually miss them: the median path shows 1.63.

10. By hand $\ell=-8{,}647.25$, equal to arch's to $10^{-6}$, and $\hat\sigma_t=1.737\%$ on 7 April 2025 ([[eg:fit]]; the lab).

11. The lab gives QLIKE 0.5153 for EWMA and 0.5032 for GARCH normal, and a Newey–West t-stat of 1.83 for the difference ([[eg:oos]]).

12. The lab rebuilds the line of [[ex:signature]], from 11.71 at 5 seconds to 0.930 at 30 minutes, and gives $\hat\rho_1\approx-0.381$ ([[eg:vrback]]).

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Volatility clustering | 波動群聚現象 | Dependence between the sizes of returns: large moves follow large moves. |
| Conditional variance | 條件變異數 | $\sigma_t^2$, the variance of day $t$ given the returns before it. |
| ARCH test | 自我迴歸條件變異數非齊一檢定 | $(T-K)\times\text{R-squared}$ of squared residuals on $K$ lags, approximately $\chi^2_K$ for iid returns with a finite fourth moment, (26.1). |
| EWMA | 指數加權移動平均模型 | The forecast $\lambda\sigma^2_{t\mid t-1}+(1-\lambda)r_t^2$, (26.2). |
| Effective window | 有效視窗 | $1/(1-\lambda)$, the number of equal weights that give today EWMA's weight. |
| GARCH(1,1) | 廣義自我迴歸條件變異數非齊一模型 | $\sigma_t^2=\omega+\alpha\varepsilon_{t-1}^2+\beta\sigma_{t-1}^2$ with (26.3)'s conditions. |
| Unconditional variance | 無條件變異數 | $\sigma^2=\omega/(1-\alpha-\beta)$, the level GARCH forecasts return to, (26.4). |
| Half-life | 半衰期 | $\ln\frac12/\ln(\alpha+\beta)$ sessions, in which a shock's effect on the forecast halves. |
| Standardized residual | 標準化殘差 | $\hat\xi_t=(r_t-\hat g)/\hat\sigma_t$; close to iid if the model is right. |
| Realized variance | 實現變異數 | $\mathrm{RV}_t(h)$, the sum of squared intraday log returns at interval $h$, (26.6). |
| Realized volatility | 實現波動率 | The square root of a realized variance. |
| Volatility signature | — | Mean realized variance against the sampling interval, as a ratio to 5 minutes. |
| Market microstructure noise | 市場微結構雜訊 | The gap between the observed and an efficient price in (26.7), a hypothesis. |
| Opening return | 開盤報酬 | $r_{\mathrm o,t}$, the log change from the previous close to 09:30:00. |
| Loss function | 損失函數 | The squared error or QLIKE of a variance forecast against a target, (26.8). |
| Mincer–Zarnowitz regression | — | OLS of the target on a constant and the forecast, (26.9). |
| Out-of-sample forecast | 樣本外預測 | A forecast made only from data before the day it forecasts. |
