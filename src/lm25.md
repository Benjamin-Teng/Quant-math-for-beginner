## Introduction

A price index has no level it must return to, while a futures price is tied to its index by carry and dividends. This module turns that difference into models and tests, applied to the TX near-month future and the TAIEX.

> [!case] A basis that comes back
> From 2 January 2003 to 8 October 2026 the TAIEX price index rose from 4,524.87 to 49,313.44, 10.9 times its start, and its log never settled around a level. The near-month TX future followed it. Their log basis, $b_t=\ln F_t-\ln S_t$, averaged −25.4 bp with a standard deviation of 58.2 bp ([[ex:case]]). It reached −793 bp on 27 October 2008, when TX settled 7.0% lower and the index 4.6% lower, and +248 bp on 5 May 2009.
>
> The future settled below the index on 4,051 of 5,850 days. A day's change in the basis had a standard deviation of 38.9 bp, yet the basis did not wander off. Next-day values correlate 0.776, and a gap halves in about 2.7 sessions. [[LM6]] split one season's basis into carry, dividends and a residual. Is the basis stationary while the index is not? How fast does it return, and how much of a day's move is noise?

## Stationarity, White Noise and the Random Walk {lo=1}

A **time series** is a sequence $x_1,\dots,x_T$ observed at equal steps, here trading sessions, and treated as one draw of random variables $x_t$. Its **autocovariance** at lag $k$ is $\Cov(x_t,x_{t-k})$, (16.2).

> [!definition] Weak stationarity
> A series $x_t$ is **weakly stationary** when
> 1. its mean $\E x_t$ is the same for every $t$;
> 2. its variance $\gamma_0:=\Var x_t<\infty$ is the same for every $t$;
> 3. its autocovariance $\gamma_k:=\Cov(x_t,x_{t-k})$ depends only on the lag $k$.
>
> Its **autocorrelations** $\rho_k:=\gamma_k/\gamma_0$ then depend only on $k$ as well.

> [!definition] White noise
> A series $\eta_t$ is **white noise** with variance $\sigma_\eta^2$ when $\E\eta_t=0$, $\Var\eta_t=\sigma_\eta^2<\infty$ and $\Cov(\eta_t,\eta_{t-k})=0$ for every lag $k\ge1$.

Independent draws with mean 0 and a common finite variance are white noise by (16.1). The converse fails, because white noise only needs zero correlation. TAIEX's daily log returns are close to uncorrelated, but their sizes are not: the lag-1 autocorrelation of $\lvert r_t\rvert$ is 0.161 ([[LM19]]). Volatility that changes over time is modelled in [[LM26]]; white noise here only needs a constant variance.

A **random walk** adds white noise to its last value, $x_t=x_{t-1}+\eta_t$; with a **drift** $c$ it is $x_t=c+x_{t-1}+\eta_t$. After $t$ steps $x_t-x_0=ct+\eta_1+\dots+\eta_t$, and the terms are uncorrelated, so (16.3) gives

$$\Var(x_t-x_0)=t\,\sigma_\eta^2 .$$

This variance grows without bound, so a random walk is not stationary, while its changes $\Delta x_t=c+\eta_t$ are. [[LM3]] treated $\ln S$ as such a walk.

> [!example] A level and a spread across two regimes {#regimes}
> TWSE's daily price limit widened from 7% to 10% on 1 June 2015, which splits TX23 into 3,078 and 2,772 dates. Compare the mean of $S_t$ and the mean and standard deviation of $b_t$ in the two regimes. Then give the standard deviation of a random walk after $t$ sessions.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The index averaged 7,302 before the change and 16,042 after it: a level's mean depends on the window. The basis averaged −28.6 bp and −21.8 bp, with standard deviations of 65.7 and 48.2 bp, so the spread moved much less. A random walk's standard deviation after $t$ sessions is $\sigma_\eta\sqrt t$, so it has no fixed variance at all.

> [!pitfall] A sample mean of a level is not a mean to return to
> The mean TAIEX close over 2003–2026 describes the path the index happened to take. A random walk has no mean to return to, so a level "below its long-run average" is no forecast. Only a stationary series has a mean that pulls it back.

> [!zh] 價格水準沒有回得去的平均，變動才可能有
> 時間序列（time series）是依時間排列的一串觀測值。平穩（weakly stationary）要求平均數（mean）與變異數（variance）固定，兩期之間的共變異數（covariance）只跟間隔有關。白噪音（white noise）只要求平均為零、變異數固定、互不相關（uncorrelated）；平均為零、變異數有限的獨立同分布（iid）序列一定是白噪音，反之則不然，例如報酬幾乎互不相關，大小卻會成群出現。隨機漫步（random walk）把白噪音一期一期加上去，$t$ 期後的變異數是 $t\sigma_\eta^2$，越走越散，所以價格指數沒有一個會拉它回去的平均。它的變動才是平穩的，這就是分析報酬而不分析價格水準的原因。

> > By $\sigma_\eta\sqrt t$, 6.0% after 25 sessions and 12.0% after 100, growing without bound. The second series' standard deviation settles at $1.2\%/\sqrt{0.19}=2.753\%$, however long the horizon.

## Autoregressive and Moving-Average Models {lo=2}

The basis remembers yesterday: next-day values correlate 0.776. The simplest model with such memory regresses today's value on yesterday's.

> [!result] The AR(1) model
> Let $\eta_t$ be white noise with variance $\sigma_\eta^2$, uncorrelated with $x_{t-1},x_{t-2},\dots$, and let
> $$x_t=c+\phi_1x_{t-1}+\eta_t. \tag{25.1}$$
> If $x_t$ is weakly stationary, then $\lvert\phi_1\rvert<1$, $\E x_t=c/(1-\phi_1)$, $\Var x_t=\sigma_\eta^2/(1-\phi_1^2)$ and $\rho_k=\phi_1^k$. At $\phi_1=1$ the model is a random walk with drift $c$.

Take expectations of (25.1): $\E x_t=c+\phi_1\E x_t$. Take variances: $\gamma_0=\phi_1^2\gamma_0+\sigma_\eta^2$, because $\eta_t$ is uncorrelated with $x_{t-1}$, and a positive $\gamma_0$ needs $\phi_1^2<1$. Multiplying the centred equation by $x_{t-k}$ and taking expectations gives $\gamma_k=\phi_1\gamma_{k-1}$ (Problem 7).

When the $\eta_t$ are independent of the past, today's gap from the mean is expected to shrink by the factor $\phi_1$ each session. For $0<\phi_1<1$ the gap halves after

$$\text{half-life}=\frac{\ln\frac12}{\ln\phi_1}\ \text{sessions}. \tag{25.2}$$

A **moving-average** model remembers shocks rather than values. The MA(1) is $x_t=c+\eta_t+\theta_1\eta_{t-1}$, stationary for every $\theta_1$. Its variance is $(1+\theta_1^2)\sigma_\eta^2$ and $\Cov(x_t,x_{t-1})=\theta_1\sigma_\eta^2$ by (16.3) and (16.4), while values two or more sessions apart share no $\eta$. So

$$\rho_1=\frac{\theta_1}{1+\theta_1^2},\qquad\rho_k=0\quad(k\ge2). \tag{25.3}$$

The **partial autocorrelation** at lag $k$ is the last coefficient of an OLS regression of $x_t$ on a constant and $x_{t-1},\dots,x_{t-k}$. It measures the link with $x_{t-k}$ that the nearer lags leave unexplained.

An AR(1) has autocorrelations that decay and partial autocorrelations that stop after lag 1: given $x_{t-1}$, earlier lags add nothing, because $\eta_t$ is uncorrelated with all of them. An MA(1) is the reverse: with $\rho_2=0$, the normal equations of the two-lag regression give a lag-2 coefficient of $-\rho_1^2/(1-\rho_1^2)\ne0$.

The **ARMA(1,1)** model combines the two, $x_t=c+\phi_1x_{t-1}+\eta_t+\theta_1\eta_{t-1}$ with $\lvert\phi_1\rvert<1$. Its autocorrelations fall by the factor $\phi_1$ per lag from

$$\rho_1=\frac{(1+\phi_1\theta_1)(\phi_1+\theta_1)}{1+2\phi_1\theta_1+\theta_1^2}$$

(stated without proof; Hamilton 1994, ch. 3). OLS and its classical standard error are [[LM23]]'s, (23.1)–(23.3); the AR(1) estimate is (12.1) with a constant and one lagged value.

> [!example] An AR(1) for the basis {#ar1}
> Regress $b_t$ (bp) on a constant and $b_{t-1}$ by OLS over TX23's 5,849 rolled changes. Report $\hat c$, $\hat\phi_1$ with its classical standard error, $s_\varepsilon$, the half-life and the implied mean.
>
> > [!solution]
> > OLS gives $\hat c=-5.66$ bp, $\hat\phi_1=0.7758$ (classical SE 0.00824) and $s_\varepsilon=36.7$ bp. By (25.2) the half-life is $\ln\frac12/\ln0.7758=2.73$ sessions, and the implied mean is $\hat c/(1-\hat\phi_1)=-25.25$ bp. The classical standard error assumes white-noise errors, which the partial autocorrelation of 0.230 at lag 2 denies. So the half-life gets no interval here; [[eg:arma]] gives one. Roll-free changes give 0.7748 and 2.72 sessions.

[[ex:acf]] compares the basis with two models. Its partial autocorrelations of 0.230 and 0.109 at lags 2 and 3 exclude a pure AR(1). Beyond lag 5 the sample autocorrelations decay more slowly than either model, because the mean itself moves (Section 7).

> [!pitfall] A half-life from a noisy series is too short
> One-day noise on top of a persistent series lowers $\hat\rho_1$, and with it the AR(1) half-life. The basis's AR(1) gives 2.7 sessions; Section 7 separates the noise and finds 7.0 sessions for the persistent part.

> [!zh] 自我迴歸與移動平均：記住昨天，或記住昨天的衝擊項
> 自我迴歸模型（autoregressive model, AR）把今天的值寫成昨天的值乘上 $\phi_1$ 再加白噪音（white noise），所以自我相關函數（autocorrelation function, ACF）是 $\phi_1^k$，一期期衰減。偏離平均數（mean）的缺口每期縮成 $\phi_1$ 倍，縮到一半所需的期數就是半衰期（half-life）$\ln\frac12/\ln\phi_1$。移動平均模型（moving average model, MA）記住的是昨天的衝擊項（shock），自我相關只到第一期就歸零。偏自我相關函數（partial autocorrelation function, PACF）扣掉較近各期的影響後再看第 $k$ 期，AR(1) 的 PACF 在第一期之後歸零，MA(1) 則相反。自我迴歸移動平均模型（ARMA）結合兩者，第 7 節會用它把基差（basis）拆成持續的部分與一日雜訊（noise）。

## Testing for Autocorrelation {lo=3}

Is TAIEX's lag-1 autocorrelation of log returns, 0.0393 ([[LM19]]), evidence of memory? [[LM19]]'s iid band $\pm1.96/\sqrt T$, (19.6), rests on a normal approximation that [[LM19]] states without proof, beyond [[LM17]]'s (17.5). Tests and p-values are [[LM21]]'s (21.2), and each p-value comes with the number of tests run on the same data.

The iid band assumes independent returns. Clustered returns pair large moves with large moves, so the products $\tilde r_t\tilde r_{t-k}$ vary more than under independence, and $\hat\rho_k$ with them.

> [!result] A robust standard error of $\hat\rho_k$
> Let the returns $r_t$ have conditional mean $\E r$ given the past (a null hypothesis that implies no autocorrelation) and finite fourth moments, and let $\tilde r_t:=r_t-\bar r$. Then, for large $T$,
> $$\operatorname{SE}_{\text{rob}}(\hat\rho_k)=\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{t>k}\tilde r_t^2\tilde r_{t-k}^2}}{\sum_t\tilde r_t^2}. \tag{25.4}$$
> For iid returns it is close to $\operatorname{SE}_{\text{iid}}=1/\sqrt T$.

Formula (25.4) is HC0-type, the lag-$k$ version of [[LM23]]'s White errors (23.6); the Deep Dive derives it.

> [!example] Is the lag-1 autocorrelation evidence? {#lag1}
> TR23's log returns have $\hat\rho_1=0.0393$ with $T=5{,}849$. Test $\rho_1=0$ at the 5% level (two-sided, normal reference) with $\operatorname{SE}_{\text{iid}}$ and with (25.4), whose value at lag 1 is 0.0205.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The iid standard error is $1/\sqrt{5849}=0.013076$, so the t-stat is $0.0393/0.013076=3.01$, and the test rejects. The robust t-stat is $0.0393/0.0205=1.92$, below 1.96: once clustering is allowed for, the lag-1 correlation is no evidence at the 5% level. Over lags 1 to 30, 9 values leave the iid band and 2 leave the robust one, against $0.05\times30=1.5$ expected under the null ([[ex:robust]]).
> >
> > Formula (25.4) needs a finite fourth moment, which TR23 may lack ([[LM19]]), so its t-stats are approximate. Leaving the product of 9 and 10 April 2025 out of (25.4)'s sum lowers the standard error to 0.01954 and lifts the t-stat to 2.01.

The **Ljung–Box test** combines lags 1 to $K$ in one statistic,

$$Q_{\text{LB}}(K)=T(T+2)\sum_{k=1}^K\frac{\hat\rho_k^2}{T-k}. \tag{25.5}$$

For iid data with a finite variance and large $T$ it is approximately $\chi^2_K$ (stated without proof; Ljung and Box 1978), and large values reject.

[[LM19]]'s identity column shows $\operatorname{VR}(m)-1$ close to $2\sum_{k<m}(1-k/m)\hat\rho_k$. If the $\hat\rho_k$ are uncorrelated, that sum has variance $\sum_{k<m}[2(1-k/m)]^2\operatorname{SE}(\hat\rho_k)^2$, and the **variance-ratio test** is

$$\begin{gathered}\text{t-stat}=\frac{\operatorname{VR}(m)-1}{\operatorname{SE}},\\ \operatorname{SE}_{\text{iid}}^2=\frac{2(2m-1)(m-1)}{3mT},\\ \operatorname{SE}_{\text{rob}}^2=\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\Big[2\Big(1-\frac km\Big)\Big]^2\operatorname{SE}_{\text{rob}}(\hat\rho_k)^2.\end{gathered} \tag{25.6}$$

The iid form puts $1/T$ for each $\operatorname{SE}(\hat\rho_k)^2$. The test is two-sided with a normal reference, a large-sample approximation; its robust form also assumes that the Deep Dive's cross-lag terms vanish.

> [!example] Ljung–Box and variance ratios for TAIEX {#portmanteau}
> On TR23's log returns, compute $Q_{\text{LB}}(10)$ and the t-stats (25.6) at $m$ = 2, 5, 10 and 20, each at the 5% level.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The statistic $Q_{\text{LB}}(10)$ is 37.1 against the $\chi^2_{10}$ 5% point 18.31, a p-value of $5.4\times10^{-5}$. Its $\chi^2$ law needs iid data, so clustering may cause the rejection (Problem 6).
> >
> > [[LM19]]'s variance ratios, with iid and robust t-stats, are 1.0392 (3.00, 1.91) at $m=2$, 1.0465 (1.62, 1.02) at 5, 1.0065 (0.15, 0.09) at 10 and 1.0359 (0.55, 0.37) at 20. Only the iid test at $m=2$ rejects. Keeping the cross-lag terms widens the robust standard error by up to 25% and changes no verdict (t-stats 0.90, 0.08 and 0.30 at $m$ = 5, 10 and 20). For $\lvert r_t\rvert$, $Q_{\text{LB}}(10)$ is 1,820: sizes cluster ([[LM26]]).

> [!deepdive]- Where the robust band comes from
> Here $e_t:=r_t-\E r$ is a return's deviation from its true mean. Under the null each $e_t$ has conditional mean 0 given the past. The product $e_te_{t-k}$ then has conditional mean 0 given days up to $t-1$, which fix every earlier product, so the products are uncorrelated. By (16.3),
> $$\Var\Big(\sum_{t>k}e_te_{t-k}\Big)=\sum_{t>k}\E\big[e_t^2e_{t-k}^2\big].$$
> Dividing by $(\sum_te_t^2)^2$, then replacing $\E r$ by $\bar r$ and the expectations by sample values, a large-$T$ approximation, gives (25.4). Formula (25.4) is (23.6)'s White form for a regression of $\tilde r_t$ on $\tilde r_{t-k}$. For iid returns each expectation is $(\Var r)^2$, and (25.4) is close to $1/\sqrt T$. For two lags $j\ne k$ the argument leaves cross terms $\E[e_t^2e_{t-j}e_{t-k}]$, which the robust form of (25.6) sets to zero.

> [!pitfall] An iid band on clustered data
> The iid band is too narrow for clustered returns, and Ljung–Box's $\chi^2_K$ law has the same flaw. Use (25.4).

> [!zh] 波動群聚讓獨立同分布區間太窄
> 自我相關（autocorrelation）的獨立同分布區間（iid band）$\pm1.96/\sqrt T$ 是在獨立（independent）報酬的虛無假設（null hypothesis）下算出來的。可是加權指數有波動群聚現象（volatility clustering）：大漲大跌成群出現，相乘項 $\tilde r_t\tilde r_{t-k}$ 的波動比獨立時大，樣本自我相關也跟著跳得更大。穩健標準誤（robust standard error）把這些相乘項的平方直接加總，落後一期的標準誤從 0.0131 變成 0.0205，t 統計量（t-statistic）從 3.01 降到 1.92，不再顯著（significant）。Ljung–Box 檢定（Ljung–Box test）與用 iid 標準誤的變異數比（variance ratio）檢定也假設獨立，同樣會誤判。報酬幾乎互不相關（uncorrelated），但大小彼此相關（correlated），這是 [[LM26]] 的主題。

> [!kc]
> [[LM19]] found a lag-1 autocorrelation of 0.161 for $\lvert r_t\rvert$, and (25.4) gives it a robust standard error of 0.0240. Is it significant at the 5% level?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Yes. The robust t-stat is $0.161/0.0240=6.7$, and 0.161 lies far outside the iid band $\pm0.0256$ too. Clustering is dependence of sizes, which [[LM26]] models.

## Unit Roots and the Dickey–Fuller Test {lo=4}

Is $\ln S_t$ a random walk, or an AR(1) with $\phi_1$ just below 1? Subtract $x_{t-1}$ from both sides of (25.1):

$$\Delta x_t=c+\delta x_{t-1}+\eta_t,\qquad\delta:=\phi_1-1. \tag{25.7}$$

A **unit root**, $\phi_1=1$, is $\delta=0$. The **Dickey–Fuller test** (Dickey and Fuller 1979) tests $H_0:\delta=0$ against $H_1:\delta<0$, one-sided. Its statistic, the DF t-stat, is $\hat\delta/\operatorname{SE}(\hat\delta)$ from OLS with the classical standard error, and small values reject. Under $H_0$ with no drift ($c=0$ too) it follows neither the normal nor the $t$ law of (23.4): its limit is a functional of Brownian motion ([[LM36]]), simulated here.

With a drift, the statistic of a regression with a constant only is asymptotically normal instead (stated without proof; Hamilton 1994, §17.4, Case 3). With a trend in the regression, it follows the trend case's own Dickey–Fuller law, not the one simulated here, whatever the drift (Case 4, also stated without proof). So a drifting series is tested with a trend (below).

> [!example] The Dickey–Fuller null by simulation {#dfsim}
> Experiment 1 (*Model*, seed `[1, 20261011]`) draws 10,000 random walks $x_t=z_1+\dots+z_t$ of 5,850 levels, with $z_t$ iid standard normal. Each walk gives a DF t-stat with a constant and no lags. Find the 1%, 5% and 10% points and the share below −1.645.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The points are −3.44, −2.86 (batch SE 0.014, [[LM18]]) and −2.58, and the mean is −1.54 ([[ex:dfnull]]). For 5,849 observations, statsmodels gives MacKinnon's (2010) points as −3.43, −2.86 and −2.57. A true unit root falls below the normal 5% point −1.645 in 46.7% of the draws (SE 0.5%), and below −1.96 in 30.7%.

> [!exhibit] The DF t-stat under a unit root against the standard normal {#dfnull}
> <div data-chart="lm25-dfnull"></div>
>
> Note: histogram, in bins of 0.1, of 10,000 DF t-stats with a constant and no lags, from random walks of 5,850 levels (experiment 1), and the standard normal density. Dashed: the simulated 5% point −2.86, equal to MacKinnon's to two decimals, and the normal −1.645. *Model.*

When the changes are autocorrelated, the errors of (25.7) are not white noise. The **augmented Dickey–Fuller** (ADF) test adds $J$ lagged changes, which approximate an ARMA by an autoregression (Said and Dickey 1984):

$$\Delta x_t=c+\delta x_{t-1}+\sum_{j=1}^Ja_j\,\Delta x_{t-j}+\eta_t. \tag{25.8}$$

statsmodels' `adfuller` picks $J$ by the Akaike information criterion (AIC), which trades fit against lags, from 0 to $\lceil12(T/100)^{1/4}\rceil$, 34 here. It reports MacKinnon's p-values (MacKinnon 1994, 2010). A drifting series also gets a trend term, `regression="ct"`, because its alternative is stationarity around a trend.

The **KPSS test** (Kwiatkowski et al. 1992) reverses the null: $H_0$ is stationarity around a constant, and large values reject (one-sided). Its statistic grows with the squared partial sums of the demeaned series, which wander under a unit root. statsmodels interpolates its p-value in a table of four points (0.347, 0.463, 0.574 and 0.739 at 10%, 5%, 2.5% and 1%), so p-values stop at 0.01 and 0.10.

> [!example] Unit-root tests on the index and on the basis {#adf}
> Test $\ln S_t$ and $b_t$ on TX23 at the 5% level: the DF regression by hand, then ADF, then KPSS.
>
> > [!solution]
> > For $\ln S_t$ the DF t-stat with a constant and no lags is 1.19, with $\hat\phi_1=1.000392$. The no-drift p-value, 0.996, does not apply to a drifting index, but a positive statistic rejects a unit root under neither law. ADF with a trend (32 lags by AIC) gives −0.77, p-value 0.97: no evidence against a unit root. KPSS gives 10.356, p-value below 0.01.
> >
> > For $b_t$ the DF t-stat is −27.20, and ADF with a constant (28 lags) gives −9.00, p-value below $10^{-14}$. KPSS gives 0.664, between the 2.5% and 1% points, p-value 0.017. Both tests reject for the basis: it returns within days to a mean that moves across years, from −80 bp in 2008 to +34 bp in 2026 to date.

> [!deepdive]- Why the DF t-stat shifts left
> Under $\delta=0$ and $c=0$ the regressor $x_{t-1}$ is a random walk, so $\E\sum_tx_{t-1}^2$ grows like $T^2$, not like $T$. The estimate $\hat\delta$ settles at the rate $T$, and the t-stat converges to a ratio of functionals of a standard Brownian motion $B_t$ ([[LM36]]), not to $\mathcal N(0,1)$. With a constant the limit is negative on average: experiment 1's mean is −1.54. Stated without proof (Hamilton 1994, ch. 17; MacKinnon 1996 for the tables).

> [!pitfall] Reading the DF t-stat against −1.645
> Against −1.645, a test at the nominal 5% level rejects a true unit root in 46.7% of samples. For a series without drift, use MacKinnon's point, −2.86 with a constant and no trend, or the p-value of `adfuller`.

> [!zh] 單根檢定：別拿 −1.645 當標準
> 單根（unit root）就是 $\phi_1=1$，序列成了隨機漫步（random walk）。單根檢定（unit root test）的虛無假設（null hypothesis）是 $\delta=0$，對立假設（alternative hypothesis）是 $\delta<0$，屬於單邊檢定（one-sided test）。問題在於虛無假設成立、序列又沒有漂移項（drift）時，水準的平方和以 $T^2$ 的速度成長，t 統計量（t-statistic）不再近似常態分布（normal distribution），而是整體往左移：模擬一萬條隨機漫步，有 46.7% 落在 −1.645 以下。所以要用 MacKinnon 的臨界值（critical value），5% 約為 −2.86。增廣 Dickey-Fuller 檢定（augmented Dickey–Fuller test）加入前幾期的變動（lagged changes），吸收殘差（residual）的自我相關（autocorrelation）；KPSS 檢定（KPSS test）則反過來，以平穩性（stationarity）為虛無假設。

> [!kc]
> Adding 28 lagged changes moves the basis's statistic from −27.20 to −9.00. Why does it move, and why does the verdict stay?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > The lagged changes absorb the part of the reversion that the partial autocorrelations show at lags 2 and 3 (0.230 and 0.109), so $\hat\delta$ alone carries less of it. Both statistics lie far below MacKinnon's 1% point, −3.43.

## Spurious Regression and Cointegration {lo=5}

Regress one random walk on another, independent one: OLS finds a link almost every time.

> [!example] Two unrelated random walks {#spur}
> Experiment 2 (*Model*, seed `[2, 20261011]`) draws 1,000 independent pairs of random walks of 5,850 sessions, $x_t$ then $y_t$. Regress $y_t$ on a constant and $x_t$ by OLS with the classical standard error. How often is $\lvert\text{t-stat}\rvert>1.96$?
>
> > [!solution]
> > The first pair ([[ex:spurious]]) gives a slope of 0.61, a t-stat of 23.5 and an R-squared of 0.086. Over 1,000 pairs, 96.6% (SE 0.6%) have $\lvert\text{t-stat}\rvert>1.96$; the median $\lvert\text{t-stat}\rvert$ is 36 (batch SE 0.9) and the median R-squared 0.18 (batch SE 0.007). On one year of 246 sessions (experiment 4) the share is still 81.7%. Regressing $\Delta y_t$ on $\Delta x_t$ for the first pair gives a t-stat of −0.98.

> <div data-chart="lm25-spurious"></div>

> Note: the first pair of experiment 2 (seed `[2, 20261011]`), 5,850 sessions of iid standard normal steps; the walks end at 62.0 and 98.3. Simulated, not market data. *Model.*

The classical standard error (23.2) assumes white-noise errors, but here the residual is itself close to a random walk. Such a fit is a **spurious regression** (Granger and Newbold 1974; Greene 2018, ch. 21). Differencing restores a valid test, but it discards the levels.

> [!definition] Cointegration
> Two series $x_t$ and $y_t$, each with a unit root, are **cointegrated** when some combination $y_t-\beta x_t$ is stationary. The vector $(1,-\beta)$ is their **cointegrating vector**.

The log basis is such a combination with the known vector $(1,-1)$, so a unit-root test on $b_t$ is a cointegration test, and [[eg:adf]] rejects a unit root. Whether futures and spot prices are cointegrated depends on the time-series properties of the cost of carry (Brenner and Kroner 1995). With an estimated vector, the **Engle–Granger** test applies a unit-root test to the OLS residual, with its own critical values (Engle and Granger 1987; MacKinnon 2010).

In an **error-correction model** each series' change responds to yesterday's gap $b_{t-1}$ through an **adjustment coefficient**, $\alpha_F$ for the future and $\alpha_S$ for the index (Engle and Granger 1987).

> [!example] Engle–Granger and error correction for TX and TAIEX {#englegranger}
> Regress $\ln F_t$ on a constant and $\ln S_t$ over TX23 and test the residual. Then regress the roll-free $\Delta\ln F_t$ and $\Delta\ln S_t$ on a constant and $b_{t-1}$, with White (HC0) standard errors.
>
> > [!solution]
> > OLS gives $\ln F_t=-0.0283+1.0028\ln S_t+\hat\varepsilon_t$. statsmodels' `coint` gives −9.42 against its 5% point −3.34 (one-sided), a p-value below $10^{-14}$: it rejects the null of no cointegration. The residual's lag-1 autocorrelation is 0.764, so the slope's classical SE of 0.000154 and its "t-stat" of 18 against $\beta=1$ mean nothing. Theory fixes the vector at $(1,-1)$; $\hat\beta>1$ fits a basis whose mean drifts up with the index level ([[eg:adf]]).
> >
> > The adjustment coefficients are $\hat\alpha_F=-0.1597$ (SE 0.055) and $\hat\alpha_S=+0.0655$ (SE 0.040). A high basis predicts a fall in $F$ and, less clearly, a rise in $S$: the future does most of the correcting. Their difference, −0.2252, is the $\hat\delta$ of the DF regression on roll-free changes.

With more than two series, Johansen's (1988, 1991) test counts the cointegrating vectors. Threshold models let the basis revert only outside a band (Dwyer, Locke and Yu 1996; Lin, Chen and Hwang 2003, for Taiwan). Pairs trading is not in this book.

> [!pitfall] Testing $\hat\beta=1$ with the classical standard error
> In a cointegrating regression the regressor has a unit root and the residual is autocorrelated, so OLS's classical standard error is meaningless. When theory fixes the vector, test $b_t$ for a unit root instead.

> [!zh] 虛假迴歸與共整合
> 兩條互相獨立（independent）的隨機漫步（random walk）做迴歸（regression），t 統計量（t-statistic）卻常常大得驚人：一千組模擬中有 96.6% 的 $\lvert\text{t-stat}\rvert$ 超過 1.96，這就是虛假迴歸（spurious regression）。原因是殘差（residual）本身也像隨機漫步，古典標準誤（classical standard error）假設的白噪音（white noise）根本不成立。共整合（cointegration）是例外：兩條都有單根（unit root），但某個線性組合（linear combination）是平穩（stationary）的。期貨與現貨的對數基差（basis）就是向量（vector）$(1,-1)$ 的組合，所以對基差做單根檢定（unit root test），就是在檢定共整合。誤差修正模型（error correction model）描述兩者如何把昨天的缺口拉回來，在台指期上，主要是期貨在修正。

> [!kc]
> The first pair of [[ex:spurious]] gives a t-stat of 23.5 in levels and −0.98 in differences. Why not difference $\ln F_t$ and $\ln S_t$ as well?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Differencing removes the spurious fit, but on $\ln F_t$ and $\ln S_t$ it would also remove the long-run link that the error-correction term $b_{t-1}$ keeps.

## The TX Basis as a Time Series {lo=6}

In logs, [[LM6]]'s (6.22) reads $b_t=(r_f-q)\tau$. Take the dividends that actually went ex, $q_{\text{real}}\tau$ from the total-return index ([[LM6]]). Then the **fair log basis** is $r_f\tau-q_{\text{real}}\tau$, and the basis minus it is the residual of (6.25) in log form. The basis has three slow parts and one fast one.

**Convergence at expiry.** Carry and dividends shrink with $\tau$ ([[ex:expiry]]). Twenty sessions before the last trading day the basis averaged −37.95 bp (standard deviation 69.17) against a fair −14.78; two sessions before, −6.56 bp (35.13) against −1.84. The standard deviation falls only to about 35 bp, of which one-day noise (22.2 bp, [[eg:arma]]) is a part.

Now suppose the basis is a persistent AR(1) plus one-day noise: $b_t=x_t+u_t$, with $x_t=c+\phi_1x_{t-1}+\zeta_t$ and $\zeta_t$, $u_t$ independent white noise. The quasi-difference $b_t-\phi_1b_{t-1}=c+\zeta_t+u_t-\phi_1u_{t-1}$ is correlated with its own lag only. An MA(1) $\eta_t+\theta_1\eta_{t-1}$ has the same autocovariances when

$$\begin{gathered}\sigma_u^2=-\frac{\theta_1\sigma_\eta^2}{\phi_1},\\ \sigma_\zeta^2=(1+\theta_1^2)\,\sigma_\eta^2-(1+\phi_1^2)\,\sigma_u^2\end{gathered} \tag{25.9}$$

(Problem 8). So $b_t$ has ARMA(1,1) autocorrelations with $\theta_1<0$, and a fitted ARMA(1,1) splits it into the two parts. statsmodels fits it by maximum likelihood ([[LM20]]), with standard errors from the curvature $-\ell''$, (20.6). Their large-sample law for a stationary ARMA that is invertible ($\lvert\theta_1\rvert<1$) is stated without proof.

> [!example] A persistent basis plus one-day noise {#arma}
> Fit an ARMA(1,1) to $b_t$ (bp, TX23) at the likelihood maximum and read it with (25.9). Give the persistent part's half-life with a delta-method interval.
>
> > [!solution]
> > statsmodels' default fit stops 0.006 below the maximum log-likelihood, so the fit starts from `method="innovations_mle"`, and a restart does not raise $\ell$. At the maximum the mean is −25.5 bp (SE 3.2), $\hat\phi_1=0.90620$ (SE 0.0071), $\hat\theta_1=-0.3553$ (SE 0.017) and $\hat\sigma_\eta^2=1{,}257.0$ bp².
> >
> > By (25.9), $\hat\sigma_u=22.2$ bp and $\hat\sigma_\zeta=22.8$ bp, and the persistent part has standard deviation $\hat\sigma_\zeta/\sqrt{1-\hat\phi_1^2}=53.8$ bp. The noise is 64% of the variance of a day's change and 14.5% of the level's. The model's daily-change standard deviation, 39.11 bp, matches the sample's 38.91.
> >
> > The persistent part's half-life is $\ln\frac12/\ln0.90620=7.04$ sessions. The delta method (20.8) with $\operatorname{SE}(\hat\phi_1)=0.007055$ gives a standard error of 0.556 and a 95% interval of 5.95 to 8.13 sessions.

The standard errors of [[eg:arma]] assume that the model is right, yet the basis's volatility changes across years ([[eg:regimes]]). The likelihood's sandwich, the analogue of (23.6) (stated without proof), gives $\operatorname{SE}(\hat\phi_1)=0.01462$ and an interval of 4.78 to 9.30 sessions. [[LM26]] meets it as the robust standard errors of a GARCH fit.

Stability check: before and from 1 June 2015, $\hat\phi_1$ is 0.90 and 0.91 and $\hat\sigma_u$ 25.74 and 17.45 bp (Problem 8); the DF t-stats are −20.20 and −17.88. In 2025 alone $\hat\sigma_u=23.4$ bp, about 56 index points at the year's mean close of 23,919.

Measured in points, the 242 rolled daily changes within 2025 had a standard deviation of 81.4, and the roll-free ones 76.7. [[LM6]]'s 83.4 used same-contract pairs from April to October only, with another roll rule.

At the 5% level KPSS rejects every version, consistent with a mean that moves across years. A fixed $r_f=2\%$ also leaves 23 years of rate changes in the residual, which FinMind's data cannot measure. One candidate source of the noise is timing: TAIEX closes on a 13:25–13:30 call auction, while TX settles from the last minute before 13:45 ([[LM6]]). [[LM26]] uses TX's 2025 one-minute bars. In continuous time a mean-reverting spread is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process ([[LM39]]).

> [!zh] 基差：慢的三塊加上快的雜訊
> 對數基差（basis）裡有三個慢慢變的部分：到期前逐漸收斂、轉倉（roll）時跳到存續期較長的合約、每年夏天的除息旺季；6 月的平均 −74.80 bp 裡有 −67.43 bp 是持有成本（carry）與股利。剩下的是快的部分。把基差看成「持續的 AR(1)」加上「一日白噪音（white noise）」，ARMA(1,1) 的估計顯示一天變動的變異數（variance）有 64% 來自雜訊（noise），持續部分的半衰期（half-life）約 7 個交易日，而不是直接配 AR(1) 得到的 2.7 個交易日。基差幾天內就回到平均數（mean），所以單根檢定（unit root test）拒絕單根；但這個平均數會隨年份移動，所以 KPSS 也拒絕平穩（stationary）的虛無假設（null hypothesis）。13:30 收盤與 13:45 結算的時間差，可能是雜訊的來源之一。

> [!kc]
> Rolls are grouped by the calendar month of the roll date, so the June roll moves from the June into the July contract. It lowers the near-month basis by 144.7 bp on average, the July roll by 104.95 bp and the other rolls by 19.8 bp. Why?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > The contract entered at the June roll lives through the heart of the ex-dividend season. Over 2003–2025, on average 45% of a year's dividends went ex in its life, and 39% in the July roll's contract. So its fair basis lies far below the expiring contract's, which is near zero. The other rolls add a month's carry, but the residual discount widens with time to expiry ([[ex:expiry]]) and outweighs it.

## Time Series in Python {lo=7}

Here `b` is TX23's log basis, `F` and `S` its prices and `r` TR23's log returns, all NumPy arrays; a DataFrame output ends in `.to_numpy()`.

```python
import numpy as np
from statsmodels.stats.diagnostic import acorr_ljungbox
from statsmodels.tsa.arima.model import ARIMA
from statsmodels.tsa.stattools import acf, adfuller, coint, kpss, pacf

rho = acf(r, nlags=30, fft=False)[1:]        # (19.5), divisor T
pac = pacf(b, nlags=20, method="ols")[1:]    # the default is "ywadjusted"
lb = acorr_ljungbox(r, lags=[10]).to_numpy() # [[Q_LB(10), p-value]]
a = adfuller(b, regression="c", autolag="AIC", result_object=True)
a0 = adfuller(b, maxlag=0, autolag=None, result_object=True)
k = kpss(b, regression="c", nlags="auto", result_object=True)
eg = coint(np.log(F), np.log(S), trend="c", return_results=True)
model = ARIMA(1e4 * b, order=(1, 0, 1))      # basis in bp; const = mean
f0 = model.fit(method="innovations_mle")
f = model.fit(start_params=f0.params, cov_type="approx")  # restart, -l''
print(a.statistic, a.lags, a0.statistic, f.params, f.bse)
```

## Summary {-}

- A random walk's variance grows like $t$, so a price level has no mean to return to; white noise needs zero correlation, not independence.
- An AR(1) has $\rho_k=\phi_1^k$ and half-life $\ln\frac12/\ln\phi_1$, (25.1)–(25.2); an MA(1) has $\rho_k=0$ beyond lag 1, (25.3).
- Clustering widens the band of $\hat\rho_k$, (25.4); Ljung–Box (25.5) and the iid variance-ratio test (25.6) need iid data.
- Under a unit root without drift, the DF t-stat (25.7) is shifted left: use MacKinnon's −2.86, not −1.645. ADF (25.8) adds lagged changes; KPSS reverses the null.
- Independent random walks give spurious regressions; the log basis is a cointegrating combination.
- As an AR(1) plus noise, (25.9), the basis puts 64% of the variance of a day's change in noise, and its persistent part halves in about 7 sessions.
- statsmodels matches each hand calculation; an ARIMA fit's standard errors depend on its `cov_type`.

## Solutions {-}

1. **A** is correct: $\rho_3=0.8^3=0.512$ by (25.1). B is $\rho_1$, as if an AR(1) correlated only one lag back. C is the partial autocorrelation at lag 3.

2. **B** is correct: $\ln\frac12/\ln0.9=6.58$ sessions by (25.2). A is $0.5/(1-\phi_1)$, a linear decay. C is $1/(1-\phi_1)=10$, the sum of the weights $\phi_1^k$, not a half-life.

3. **C** is correct: $0.5/(1+0.25)=0.40$ by (25.3). A is $\theta_1$ itself, and B is $\theta_1^2$.

4. **B** is correct: under a unit root without drift the DF t-stat is shifted left, and −2.50 lies above −2.86. A uses the normal one-sided point. C uses a two-sided normal test.

5. **A** is correct: the two tests have different nulls, and a mean that moves across years fails KPSS while the reversion within days rejects a unit root. B forgets that KPSS's alternative includes a moving mean. C misses that both nulls can be false.

6. By (25.5), $Q_{\text{LB}}(5)=5849\times5851\times\sum_k\hat\rho_k^2/(5849-k)=16.96$ from the printed values (16.98 unrounded), above 11.07, so Ljung–Box rejects. The robust t-stats are 1.92, 0.00, −0.14, −1.81 and −0.88, none beyond 1.96. Ljung–Box gives each $\hat\rho_k$ the iid variance $1/T$, but clustering makes the robust standard errors 0.0178 to 0.0227, against 0.013076. So $Q_{\text{LB}}$ is too large. Five tests at the 5% level expect 0.25 false rejections, and none occurs.

7. Weak stationarity fixes $\E x_t$, so $\E x_t=c+\phi_1\E x_t$ gives $c/(1-\phi_1)$, which needs $\phi_1\ne1$. Since $\eta_t$ is uncorrelated with $x_{t-1}$, $\gamma_0=\phi_1^2\gamma_0+\sigma_\eta^2$, so $\gamma_0=\sigma_\eta^2/(1-\phi_1^2)$, positive only if $\lvert\phi_1\rvert<1$. Multiplying $x_t-\E x_t=\phi_1(x_{t-1}-\E x_t)+\eta_t$ by $x_{t-k}-\E x_t$ and taking expectations gives $\gamma_k=\phi_1\gamma_{k-1}$, so $\rho_k=\phi_1^k$. The expected gap shrinks by $\phi_1^k$ in $k$ sessions, and $\phi_1^k=\tfrac12$ at $k=\ln\frac12/\ln\phi_1$.

8. (a) The quasi-difference $b_t-\phi_1b_{t-1}=c+\zeta_t+u_t-\phi_1u_{t-1}$ has variance $\sigma_\zeta^2+(1+\phi_1^2)\sigma_u^2$ and lag-1 autocovariance $-\phi_1\sigma_u^2$ by (16.3) and (16.4), and none beyond. An MA(1) has $(1+\theta_1^2)\sigma_\eta^2$ and $\theta_1\sigma_\eta^2$. Equating gives (25.9).
   (b) Before 1 June 2015: $\hat\sigma_u=25.74$ bp, persistent standard deviation 60.50 bp, noise share 65.2%. From then: 17.45 bp, 44.95 bp and 62.6%. The noise fell by about a third and the persistent spread by about a quarter, while $\hat\phi_1$ and the noise share barely moved.

9. By hand, $\Delta b_t$ on a constant and $b_{t-1}$ gives the DF t-stat −27.20, equal to `adfuller(b, maxlag=0, autolag=None)`, and $\hat\phi_1=0.7758$, a half-life of 2.73 sessions. With roll-free changes the t-stat is −29.66 and $\hat\phi_1=0.7748$, a half-life of 2.72. The roll jumps barely matter here.

10. The 5% point is −2.87 (batch SE 0.006), and MacKinnon's for 245 observations is also −2.87. The share below −1.645 is 45.7% (SE 0.16%): one year of data does not rescue the normal point.

11. The share is 81.7% (SE 1.2%), and the median R-squared is 0.17 (batch SE 0.011). A short sample gives spurious fits too.

12. The ratio is $\operatorname{VR}(5)=1.0465$. The iid standard error $\sqrt{72/(15\times5849)}=0.02865$ gives a t-stat of 1.62. The robust form with Problem 6's standard errors gives $\operatorname{SE}_{\text{rob}}=0.04573$ and a t-stat of 1.02. Neither rejects at the 5% level.

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Time series | 時間序列 | Observations of one quantity at equal time steps. |
| Weak stationarity | 平穩 | Constant mean and variance, and autocovariances that depend only on the lag. |
| White noise | 白噪音 | Mean 0, constant variance, zero correlation between dates. |
| Random walk | 隨機漫步 | $x_t=x_{t-1}+\eta_t$ (with drift, $+c$); its variance grows like $t$. |
| Autocorrelation function (ACF) | 自我相關函數 | $\rho_k$ as a function of the lag $k$. |
| Partial autocorrelation function (PACF) | 偏自我相關函數 | The last coefficient of an OLS regression on lags 1 to $k$. |
| Autoregressive model, AR(1) | 自我迴歸模型 | $x_t=c+\phi_1x_{t-1}+\eta_t$, (25.1). |
| Moving-average model, MA(1) | 移動平均模型 | $x_t=c+\eta_t+\theta_1\eta_{t-1}$, (25.3). |
| ARMA(1,1) | 自我迴歸移動平均模型 | An AR(1) with an MA(1) error. |
| Half-life | 半衰期 | $\ln\frac12/\ln\phi_1$ sessions, (25.2). |
| Robust standard error | 穩健標準誤 | (25.4): valid under clustering with finite fourth moments, for no autocorrelation. |
| Ljung–Box test | Ljung–Box 檢定 | (25.5): a joint test of lags 1 to $K$, approximately $\chi^2_K$ for iid data. |
| Variance-ratio test | 變異數比檢定 | (25.6): $\operatorname{VR}(m)-1$ over its standard error. |
| Unit root | 單根 | $\phi_1=1$ in an AR(1), a random walk. |
| Augmented Dickey–Fuller test | 增廣 Dickey-Fuller 檢定 | (25.8): (25.7) with lagged changes; one-sided, MacKinnon's points. |
| KPSS test | KPSS 檢定 | A test of stationarity against a unit root; large values reject. |
| Spurious regression | 虛假迴歸 | A significant-looking regression between unrelated nonstationary series. |
| Cointegration | 共整合 | A stationary combination of series that each have a unit root. |
| Error-correction model | 誤差修正模型 | Changes that respond to yesterday's gap from the cointegrating relation. |
