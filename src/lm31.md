## Introduction

[[LM27]] split a book's volatility into risk contributions; [[LM28]] to [[LM30]] chose weights from estimated means. This module allocates by risk alone. It gives each fund a chosen share of the risk, builds a portfolio from a tree of correlations, and scales the whole book by a volatility forecast.

> [!case] Equal risk, then a forecast
> A fund that cannot trust estimated means can still share risk evenly. On 31 March 2025 rule R6 did that for E5's five funds ([[LM27]]). It put 30.7% in the bond fund 00679B and 12.0% in the oil-futures fund 00642U, so each fund carried a fifth of the risk. Rule R8 then scales R6 by a forecast.
>
> R6's book had been calm: its EWMA forecast was 5.89% a year, against 7.84% over the 504 joint dates before it. So on 1 April R8 held 1.33 times its equity in the funds, borrowing the extra 0.33 at 3.5% a year. On 7 April, the first session after the Qingming holiday, the forecast jumped to 23.58%, an implied exposure of 0.33. R8 trades only after a month-end, so it kept its leverage until 2 May.
>
> Over that holding month its realized volatility was 5.78 times its target, the worst of all 91. Does a forecast keep risk steady? And what does an equal share of risk buy?

## Risk Contributions as Targets {lo=1,5}

On the first window, 13 February 2017 to 27 February 2019, R1 held 20% of each fund, yet 00642U carried 50.2% of its risk ([[LM27]], [[ex:shares]]). For $\sigma_p>0$, (27.5) and (27.6) give

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{MRC}_i=\frac{(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i}{\sigma_p},\qquad \mathrm{RC}_i=w_i\,\mathrm{MRC}_i,\\ \sum_i\mathrm{RC}_i=\sigma_p.\end{gathered}$$

Here $\mathrm{MRC}_i$ is [[LM7]]'s marginal variance divided by $2\sigma_p$, and $\mathrm{RC}_i/\sigma_p$ is fund $i$'s **risk share**.

> [!definition] Risk budgets
> Let $\Sigma$ be positive definite, and let budgets $b_i>0$ sum to 1. A fully invested portfolio, $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w=1$, meets them when $\mathrm{RC}_i=b_i\sigma_p$ for every $i$. Multiplying by $\sigma_p$ and using (27.6), that is
> $$w_i(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i=b_i\,\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w,\qquad i=1,\dots,N. \tag{31.1}$$
> The **risk-budget portfolio** is the long-only solution, $\mathbf w\ge\mathbf 0$. **Equal risk contributions** (ERC) take $b_i=1/N$.

> [!example] Two funds {#twofunds}
> On the first window 0050 and the bond fund 00679B have volatilities 13.50% and 8.54% and correlation −0.30 ([[LM28]]). Find their ERC weights.
>
> > [!solution]
> > With two funds, (31.1) at $b_i=\tfrac12$ reads $w_1(w_1\sigma_1^2+w_2\sigma_{12})=w_2(w_1\sigma_{12}+w_2\sigma_2^2)$. The covariance terms cancel, so $w_1^2\sigma_1^2=w_2^2\sigma_2^2$, and positive weights give $w_1\sigma_1=w_2\sigma_2$ whatever $\rho$. So $w_1=8.54/(13.50+8.54)=0.3875$ and $w_2=0.6125$, with risk shares 50/50. [[LM7]]'s minimum-variance weight (7.7) puts 0.331 in 0050, at 6.1% against ERC's 6.2%.

> [!result] When ERC is inverse volatility (Maillard, Roncalli and Teïletche 2010)
> Let $\Sigma$ be positive definite. The inverse-volatility weights
> $$w_i=\frac{1/\sigma_i}{\sum_j1/\sigma_j} \tag{31.3}$$
> are an ERC portfolio for two assets with any $\rho>-1$, and for $N$ assets whose correlations all equal $\rho$, with $-1/(N-1)<\rho<1$. By (31.2), Section 3, they are then the only long-only one.

*Proof.* Two assets are [[eg:twofunds]]; at $\rho=-1$ the book's $\sigma_p$ would be 0. With one correlation $\rho$ and the weights (31.3),

$$(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i=\sum_j\rho_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_jw_j=\frac{\sigma_i\big(1+(N-1)\rho\big)}{\sum_j1/\sigma_j},$$

so $w_i(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i$ is the same for every $i$. The correlation matrix has eigenvalues $1+(N-1)\rho$ and $1-\rho$ ([[LM11]]), so $\Sigma$ is positive definite exactly when $-1/(N-1)<\rho<1$. $\blacksquare$

By (29.5), each fund that long-only minimum variance holds has the rate $(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i=\sigma_p^2$, so its beta $\beta_{i,p}$ is 1 and its share $w_i\beta_{i,p}$ is its weight. ERC equalizes the products $w_i(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i$ instead: [[LM10]]'s gradient entry is a rate, a share a weight times that rate. Maillard, Roncalli and Teïletche (2010) order the volatilities:

$$\sigma_{\mathrm{mv}}\le\sigma_{\mathrm{erc}}\le\sigma_{1/N},$$

with $\sigma_{\mathrm{mv}}$ the long-only minimum variance (stated without proof; their 2009 working paper, Appendix A.3). Bruder and Roncalli (2012, §2.3.4, p. 10) put a budget portfolio between minimum variance and the budgets used as weights.

> [!example] Five funds on the first window {#firstshares}

>

> > Inverse-volatility weights are 0.174, 0.198, 0.275, 0.251 and 0.102. They leave 00679B only 9.8% of the risk and 00642U 24.8%, because 00679B correlates −0.30 and −0.26 with the two equity funds. R6 holds 0.1561, 0.1828, 0.3386, 0.2372 and 0.0854 (rounded separately; 1.0001 as printed), and each fund carries 20%.
> >
> > The volatilities, 5.02% for R3 ([[LM28]]), 5.45% for R6 and 7.38% for R1, follow Maillard et al.'s order; R3 equals (28.2)'s $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{mv}}$ here, since every weight is positive. With every correlation set to the mean, $\bar\rho=0.0533$, ERC equals inverse volatility. All use the window's unrounded $\hat\Sigma$.

> [!pitfall] A risk share is neither a weight nor a share of losses
> R6 holds 33.9% in 00679B, which carries 20% of its risk. A risk share splits one covariance matrix's variance before the fact; it is no share of a day's loss, and it moves afterwards (Section 5).

> [!zh] 風險預算：每檔基金分擔指定的風險
> 權重相等不代表風險相等：第一個視窗裡，等權重組合有一半的風險來自石油期貨基金。風險貢獻（risk contribution）把組合波動率拆成各資產的部分，加總剛好是組合波動率。風險預算（risk budget）事先指定每檔資產分擔的比例，每檔都是 $1/N$ 時稱為等風險貢獻（equal risk contribution，ERC），是最常見的風險平價（risk parity）組合。本模組的風險預算是分給每一檔資產的份額，[[LM7]] 的風險預算則是兩個策略合計的波動率上限，兩者不同。最小變異數投資組合（minimum-variance portfolio）讓持有資產的 $(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i$ 相等，ERC 讓權重乘上它的乘積相等，所以低波動又與股票負相關的債券基金權重很大，風險份額卻只有五分之一。

> [!kc]
> On the first window R3 holds 41.4% in 00679B, and 41.4% of its risk is there ([[LM28]]). R6 holds 33.9% in 00679B, yet that fund carries only 20% of R6's risk. Why less than its weight?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Minimum variance equalizes the rates $(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i$ of held funds, (29.5), so each share equals its weight; ERC equalizes the products $w_i(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i$. 00679B's rate is low, since it is the calmest fund (8.5% a year, [[LM28]]) and moves against the equity funds. So a large weight buys a fifth of the risk.

## Budgets: Existence, Uniqueness and Computation {lo=2,3}

A change of variables makes the $N$ nonlinear equations (31.1) the first-order conditions of a convex problem.

> [!theorem] The risk-budget portfolio (Bruder and Roncalli 2012, §2.3.3, pp. 9–10)
> Let $h(\mathbf y)=\tfrac12\mathbf y^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf y-\sum_ib_i\ln y_i$ on the open set $\mathbf y>\mathbf 0$, and suppose that
> 1. the matrix $\Sigma$ is positive definite;
> 2. every budget is positive, $b_i>0$;
> 3. the budgets sum to 1.
>
> Then $h$ has exactly one minimizer $\mathbf y^*$, and its normalized form
> $$\mathbf w^*=\frac{\mathbf y^*}{\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf y^*} \tag{31.2}$$
> is the only long-only solution of (31.1).

*Proof.* The Hessian of $h$, $\Sigma+\operatorname{diag}(b_i/y_i^2)$, is positive definite on $\mathbf y>\mathbf 0$. So $h$ is strictly convex there by (29.2) and has at most one minimizer by (29.3)'s argument; one exists (Deep Dive). At it the gradient $\Sigma\mathbf y-\mathbf b/\mathbf y$ vanishes ($\mathbf b/\mathbf y$ entry by entry), so

$$y_i(\Sigma\mathbf y)_i=b_i,\qquad \mathbf y^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf y=\sum_ib_i=1.$$

Dividing both by $(\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf y^*)^2$ shows that $\mathbf w^*$ satisfies (31.1). Conversely, let a long-only $\mathbf w$ with $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w=1$ solve (31.1). Each $w_i(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i=b_i\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w$ is then positive, so every $w_i>0$, and $\mathbf y=\mathbf w/\sqrt{\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w}$ has $y_i(\Sigma\mathbf y)_i=b_i$. So the gradient of $h$ vanishes there, and a stationary point of a convex function is its minimizer, by (29.2)'s tangent inequality. So $\mathbf y=\mathbf y^*$ and $\mathbf w=\mathbf w^*$; Bruder and Roncalli only sketch this argument. $\blacksquare$

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: why the minimizer exists
> By (11.4), $\mathbf y^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf y$ is at least $\lVert\mathbf y\rVert^2$ times the smallest eigenvalue of $\Sigma$, which is positive. Each $y_i$ is at most $\lVert\mathbf y\rVert$ and the budgets sum to 1, so $-\sum_ib_i\ln y_i\ge-\ln\lVert\mathbf y\rVert$. Hence $h\to\infty$ as $\lVert\mathbf y\rVert\to\infty$, and also as any $y_i\to0^+$ on a bounded set. So the sublevel set $\{\mathbf y>\mathbf 0:h(\mathbf y)\le h(\mathbf 1)\}$ is closed and bounded, and the continuous $h$ attains a minimum on it ([[LM11]]'s extreme value theorem).

**Newton's method**, [[LM7]]'s in five variables, solves $\big(\Sigma+\operatorname{diag}(b_i/y_i^2)\big)\boldsymbol\Delta=-(\Sigma\mathbf y-\mathbf b/\mathbf y)$ at each step. The rules' `erc()` starts at $y_i=1/\sigma_i$ rescaled to $\mathbf y^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf y=1/N$, halves a step until $\mathbf y>\mathbf 0$ and $h$ falls enough, and stops below $\max_i\lvert y_i(\Sigma\mathbf y)_i-b_i\rvert=10^{-13}$.

**Cyclical coordinate descent** (CCD; Griveau-Billion, Richard and Roncalli 2013) minimizes $h$ along one $y_i$ at a time. With the others fixed, $\partial h/\partial y_i=0$ is a quadratic whose positive root is the update:

$$\begin{gathered}\sigma_i^2y_i^2+\Big(\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}y_j\Big)\,y_i-b_i=0,\\ y_i\leftarrow\frac{\sqrt{\big(\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}y_j\big)^2+4\sigma_i^2b_i}-\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}y_j}{2\sigma_i^2}.\end{gathered} \tag{31.4}$$

The sum is $(\Sigma\mathbf y)_i-\sigma_i^2y_i$. A sweep updates $y_1$ to $y_N$ in turn, each update lowers $h$, and the sweeps converge (Tseng 2001, Theorem 5.1, as Griveau-Billion et al. cite it; stated without proof).

**A fixed point.** At the solution $w_i\beta_{i,p}=b_i$ by (27.6), so $w_i\propto b_i/\beta_{i,p}$ (Bruder and Roncalli, eq. (7), p. 9). Iterating that property is the fixed point that Griveau-Billion et al. attribute to Chaves et al. (2012); it is no descent of $h$. cvxpy minimizes $h$ as an exponential-cone program that CLARABEL solves to about $10^{-6}$, so it checks Newton to $10^{-5}$.

> [!pitfall] A fixed point is not an algorithm
> The property $w_i\propto b_i/\beta_{i,p}$ holds at the solution, but iterating it need not settle, and it can settle at negative weights. Newton's method and CCD descend a strictly convex $h$. Check an answer by its risk shares and signs, not by a solver's status.

> [!zh] 先保證唯一，再談演算法
> 風險預算條件 (31.1) 是一組非線性方程式，本身不保證只有一個解。把問題改寫成在 $\mathbf y>\mathbf 0$ 上最小化 $h(\mathbf y)$，因為 $h$ 是嚴格凸函數（strictly convex function），最小值點存在而且只有一個，正規化之後就是唯一的只做多風險預算組合，這是它的唯一性（uniqueness）。牛頓法（Newton's method）與座標下降法（coordinate descent）每一步都讓 $h$ 下降，所以會收斂到這個解。不動點（fixed point）迭代 $w_i\propto b_i/\beta_{i,p}$ 不是下降法，可能停在含負權重的解，也可能一直不收斂。檢查答案要看風險份額是否等於預算、權重是否全為正，不能只看求解器回報的狀態。

> [!kc]
> R2 holds no 00642U. Can its weights serve as risk budgets for all five funds?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > No. A zero budget removes that fund's log term from $h$, so (31.1) can have several solutions (Bruder and Roncalli 2012, §2.2, pp. 4–5), and theorem (31.2) does not apply. CCD's update (31.4) still runs, but it sets that $y_i$ to 0 whenever $(\Sigma\mathbf y)_i-\sigma_i^2y_i\ge0$, outside $\mathbf y>\mathbf 0$. Drop the fund instead ([[eg:budgets]]).

## Hierarchical Risk Parity {lo=4}

Hierarchical risk parity (HRP; López de Prado 2016) needs no optimizer and no inverse of $\Sigma$. It builds a tree of the assets from their correlations, orders the assets along it, and splits the weight from the top down. Its distance is half of [[LM8]]'s per-date distance in (8.10):

$$\begin{gathered}d_{ij}=\sqrt{\tfrac12(1-\rho_{ij})}=\tfrac12\sqrt{2(1-\rho_{ij})},\\ \text{column distance }\lVert\mathbf d_i-\mathbf d_j\rVert.\end{gathered} \tag{31.5}$$

[[LM8]]'s distance is Euclidean, so $d_{ij}$ satisfies the triangle inequality (8.8). HRP clusters on the **column distance**, the norm (8.5) of the difference between columns $i$ and $j$ of $(d_{ij})$. Assets are close when their distances to all assets are alike.

1. **Tree.** Single linkage merges the two closest clusters until one is left, the distance between clusters being the smallest distance between their members. Numbered from 1 in SciPy's order, the assets are 1 to $N$ and the clusters $N+1$, $N+2$, … as they form; each merge lists its lower-numbered member first.
2. **Order.** From the root down, replace each cluster by its two members, in that order. This quasi-diagonal order puts correlated assets side by side.
3. **Bisection.** Start with weight 1 on the ordered list. Split every list of $n>1$ items into its first $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ items and the rest, until single assets remain. Each half's inverse-variance portfolio, weights in proportion to $1/\sigma_i^2$, has variance $\sigma^2_{(1)}$ or $\sigma^2_{(2)}$.

Each split multiplies the weights of the two halves by

$$\frac{\sigma^2_{(2)}}{\sigma^2_{(1)}+\sigma^2_{(2)}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\sigma^2_{(1)}}{\sigma^2_{(1)}+\sigma^2_{(2)}}. \tag{31.6}$$

The calmer half gets more, and inverse variance is minimum variance for a diagonal covariance matrix (López de Prado, Appendix A.2). Single linkage only compares distances, so the factor 2 to [[LM8]]'s distance doubles the heights and changes no tree.

> [!pitfall] Two codes, two trees
> The author's code passes the square matrix $(d_{ij})$ to SciPy's `linkage`, which reads its rows as observations and warns. So it builds the column-distance tree, at all 92 windows and on U28. Code that passes the condensed $d_{ij}$ links on $d$ itself, and the leaf order differs at 17 of the 92 windows. The bisection cuts the list, not the tree ([[eg:hrp5]]).

> [!zh] 先分群，再由上而下分配
> 層次風險平價（hierarchical risk parity，HRP）不需要反矩陣（inverse matrix），也不需要最佳化（optimization）。第一步用相關係數（correlation coefficient）定義距離 $d_{ij}$，再算距離矩陣各行之間的歐氏距離（Euclidean distance），以單一連接叢集法（single linkage clustering）做層次叢集法（hierarchical clustering），畫成樹形圖（dendrogram）。第二步準對角化（quasi-diagonalization），讓相關高的資產排在一起。第三步遞迴二分（recursive bisection）：把排好的清單從中間切開，風險較低的一半分到較多權重。切的是清單而不是樹，所以樹上最先合併的一對也可能被切開。

> > Every distance and column distance doubles. Single linkage only compares distances, so the merges stay and their heights double, on U28 and at all 92 windows.

## Volatility Targeting: R8 {lo=6,8}

R6 fixes the shares of risk but not its level. At signal $t$, R6's new weights on the window's 504 rows give a book with uncentred mean square $\bar\sigma_t^2$. Run through the same returns from $\bar\sigma_t^2$, [[LM26]]'s EWMA (26.2), $\lambda=0.94$, gives $\sigma^2_{t+1\mid t}$. R8 scales R6 by their ratio:

$$L_t=\min\Big(1.5,\ \frac{\bar\sigma_t}{\sigma_{t+1\mid t}}\Big),\qquad \mathbf f_t=L_t\,\mathbf w_t^{\text{R6}}. \tag{31.7}$$

[[LM26]] writes (26.2) for log returns; R8 applies it to daily simple returns in decimals, uncentred. Cash, $1-L_t>0$, earns $r_f=1.5\%$ a year and borrowing costs $r_f$ plus 2 points, 3.5%, both accrued per session over $A_{\mathrm w}$. Between trades the exposures drift, and (27.1)–(27.3) book returns, interest and costs. R8 keeps $1+R_t>0$ on all 1,851 sessions.

The financing rate, 3.5% a year, is the through case's illustrative rate, since no official one exists. [[LM34]] gives the sources of the commission and the tax, and R8's growth at other spreads. The tail risk of the scaled book, its value at risk and expected shortfall, is [[LM32]]'s subject.

> [!example] R8 at the first signal {#r8first}

> > The window gives $\bar\sigma_t=0.0034991$ a day (5.45% a year) and the EWMA 0.0041204 (6.42%). So $L=0.0034991/0.0041204=0.8492$, the exposures are 0.8492 times R6's weights, 0.1326, 0.1552, 0.2875, 0.2014 and 0.0725, and the cash, 0.151, earns 1.5%. After 504 days the start value's weight is $0.94^{504}=2.9\times10^{-14}$.

> > Harvey et al. (2018) target a fixed 10% with a zero-mean EWMA of half-life 20 days, $\lambda=0.5^{1/20}=0.9659$; R8 targets $\bar\sigma_t$, with a half-life of 11.2 days ([[LM26]]).

Over the 92 trades R8's exposure averaged 1.14 ([[ex:exposure]]): at the cap 14 times, above 1 at 62 and below at 30; the uncapped ratio reached 2.14. Over the signals $\bar\sigma_t$ ran from 5.37% to 11.24% a year (mean 9.42%). The forecast ran from 3.66% (31 May 2019) to 31.48% (31 March 2020), which set the low of 0.295.

[[LM26]]'s recursion extends to a covariance matrix, and for fixed weights its quadratic form is (26.2) on the book's returns:

$$\begin{aligned}\Sigma_{t+1\mid t}&=\lambda\Sigma_{t\mid t-1}+(1-\lambda)\mathbf R_t\mathbf R_t^{\top},\\ \mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma_{t+1\mid t}\mathbf w&=\lambda\,\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma_{t\mid t-1}\mathbf w\\&\quad+(1-\lambda)(\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf R_t)^2.\end{aligned} \tag{31.8}$$

So from a start with $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma_{\text{start}}\mathbf w=\bar\sigma_t^2$, R8's forecast equals $\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma_{t+1\mid t}\mathbf w$; on E5 the two agree to rounding, below $10^{-14}$ of $\bar\sigma_t^2$. The matrix stays positive semidefinite (Problem 9). Multivariate GARCH is beyond this book.

**The evidence.** Moreira and Muir (2017) scale US factor returns each month by a constant over the previous calendar month's realized variance. The constant equates the managed volatility with buy-and-hold's over the full sample, 1926–2015 for the market, size, value and momentum. Caps of 1 and 1.5 appear only as robustness checks. Sharpe ratios rise, because expected returns do not rise in proportion to volatility (their abstract).

Harvey et al. study more than 60 assets with daily data from as early as 1926. Their Sharpe ratio improves slightly for equity indexes and credit, hardly for bonds, currencies and commodities. The vol of vol and the left tail improve for almost all their futures, forwards and credit; for US 10-year Treasury futures the left tail is similar. Neither paper tests E5's funds.

> [!pitfall] A target that moves with the book
> R8 has no fixed target. It targets R6's own volatility over the last 504 joint dates, which ran from 5.37% to 11.24%. So R8 aims low after calm years and high after a crash.

> [!zh] 用預測波動率調整曝險
> 波動率目標（volatility targeting）在預測波動率低時加碼、高時減碼。R8 的目標不是固定水準，而是 R6 自己過去 504 個共同交易日的波動率；預測來自指數加權移動平均模型（EWMA），兩者的比值就是總曝險（exposure），上限 1.5 倍。曝險超過 1 就是槓桿（leverage），借款年息 3.5%；低於 1 的部分是現金，年息 1.5%。Moreira 與 Muir 用上個月的實現變異數（realized variance）縮放，常數由全樣本決定；Harvey 等人以固定 10% 為目標，研究 60 多種資產。這些研究都沒有檢驗 E5 的基金，結果不能直接套用。

## Risk-Based Allocation in Python {lo=3,4}

```python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np
from scipy.cluster.hierarchy import leaves_list, linkage
from scipy.spatial.distance import squareform

def ccd(S, b, tol=1e-12):                 # (31.4), S annualized
    y = 1 / np.sqrt(np.diag(S))
    while np.abs(y * (S @ y) - b).max() >= tol:
        for i in range(len(b)):
            r = S[i] @ y - S[i, i] * y[i]  # the other funds' part
            y[i] = (np.sqrt(r * r + 4 * S[i, i] * b[i]) - r) / (2 * S[i, i])
    return y / y.sum()

def newton(S, b):                         # as vol5_rules.erc
    y = 1 / np.sqrt(np.diag(S))
    y *= np.sqrt(1 / (len(b) * (y @ S @ y)))
    h = lambda v: 0.5 * v @ S @ v - b @ np.log(v)
    while np.abs(y * (S @ y) - b).max() >= 1e-13:
        g = S @ y - b / y
        d = np.linalg.solve(S + np.diag(b / y**2), -g)
        t, far = 1.0, np.abs(g * y).max() > 1e-6
        while (y + t * d).min() <= 0 or (
                far and h(y + t * d) > h(y) + 1e-4 * t * (g @ d)):
            t /= 2                         # halve until h falls enough
        y = y + t * d
    return y / y.sum()

# S: the annualized covariance, b: the budgets, x: the window's returns
y = cp.Variable(len(b))                  # the check: h in cvxpy
h = 0.5 * cp.quad_form(y, cp.psd_wrap(S)) - b @ cp.log(y)  # S is PSD
prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(h))
prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, tol_gap_abs=1e-10, tol_gap_rel=1e-10,
           tol_feas=1e-10)
assert prob.status == "optimal" and y.value is not None

C = np.corrcoef(x, rowvar=False)          # x: T x N returns
C = (C + C.T) / 2                         # exactly symmetric
np.fill_diagonal(C, 1.0)
d = np.sqrt(np.clip((1 - C) / 2, 0, None))        # (31.5)
D2 = np.sqrt(((d[:, :, None] - d[:, None, :]) ** 2).sum(axis=0))
D2 = (D2 + D2.T) / 2
np.fill_diagonal(D2, 0.0)
Z = linkage(squareform(D2), method="single")      # checks pass
order = leaves_list(Z)                            # quasi-diagonal
# linkage(d, "single") reads the rows of the square d as observations,
# warns (ClusterWarning) and builds the column-distance tree as well
```

`squareform` requires exact symmetry and a zero diagonal, which `np.corrcoef` does not guarantee.

## Summary {-}

- Risk budgets (31.1) set $\mathrm{RC}_i=b_i\sigma_p$; ERC is inverse volatility (31.3) for two funds or one common correlation.
- For positive definite $\Sigma$ and positive budgets the long-only budget portfolio is unique, (31.2); Newton's method and CCD (31.4) find it, a fixed point need not.
- HRP clusters on the column distance of $d_{ij}$, (31.5), and bisects the ordered list, (31.6).
- Shares set before the fact moved afterwards; some share was negative in 29 of 91 holding months.
- R8, (31.7), scales R6 to its window volatility, capped at 1.5.
- Targeting moved R8's median ratio to 0.98 but left the spread at 0.421; the published evidence uses other designs and samples.

Its first-order condition $y_i(\Sigma\mathbf y)_i=b_i$ gives (31.1) for $\mathbf y^*/\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf y^*$, and a long-only solution rescales to a stationary point, the minimizer. The fixed point has negative weights, outside the set the theorem covers.

5. (a) With $\rho=0$, (31.1) reads $w_i^2\sigma_i^2=b_i\sigma_p^2$, so $w_i\propto\sqrt{b_i}/\sigma_i$ and $w_1=4.1833/(4.1833+5.4772)=0.4330$. ## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Risk contribution | 風險貢獻 | $\mathrm{RC}_i=w_i(\Sigma\mathbf w)_i/\sigma_p$; the contributions sum to $\sigma_p$ ([[LM27]]). |
| Risk share | 風險份額 | $\mathrm{RC}_i/\sigma_p$, a fund's share of the book's volatility on one covariance matrix. |
| Risk budget | 風險預算 | A target share $b_i>0$ for each fund, summing to 1. |
| Risk-budget portfolio | 風險預算組合 | The long-only solution of (31.1). |
| Equal risk contributions (ERC) | 等風險貢獻 | The risk-budget portfolio with $b_i=1/N$. |
| Risk parity | 風險平價 | Allocation by risk shares rather than by means. |
| Inverse-volatility weights | 反波動率權重 | $w_i\propto1/\sigma_i$, (31.3). |
| Cyclical coordinate descent (CCD) | 座標下降法 | Minimizing $h$ along one coordinate at a time, (31.4). |
| Fixed point | 不動點 | A point that an iteration maps to itself, such as $w_i\propto b_i/\beta_{i,p}$. |
| Hierarchical clustering | 層次叢集法 | Merging clusters step by step into a tree. |
| Single linkage | 單一連接叢集法 | Cluster distance as the smallest distance between members. |
| Dendrogram | 樹形圖 | A drawing of the merges and their heights. |
| Quasi-diagonalization | 準對角化 | Ordering the assets along the tree. |
| Recursive bisection | 遞迴二分 | Halving the ordered list and splitting weight by (31.6). |
| Hierarchical risk parity (HRP) | 層次風險平價 | López de Prado's tree-based allocation. |
| Volatility targeting | 波動率目標 | Scaling a book by a target over a volatility forecast, (31.7). |
| Holding month | 持有月份 | The sessions from the day after a trade through the next trade. |
| Periodic re-estimation | 定期再估計 | Re-estimating and trading on a calendar. |
| Risk trigger | 風險觸發 | Trading when a risk measure crosses a threshold. |
