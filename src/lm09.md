## Introduction

A replicating mix asks which multiples of a few assets reproduce a target's returns. Each day gives one linear equation in the multiples, so a few days give a linear system. This module solves such systems four ways and builds the inverse and the rank from them. It also shows why an exact fit on a few days says almost nothing.

> [!case] Three sectors, three days
> On 7 April 2025, after the 3–4 April holidays, TAIEX fell 9.70%, its largest daily move in data back to July 2007. It then moved −4.02%, −5.79% and +9.25% on 8, 9 and 10 April. Is there one mix of the Semiconductor, Financial and Food sector indices whose returns reproduce TAIEX's on each day ([[ex:days]])?
>
> With two days there are infinitely many such mixes. With 7–9 April there is exactly one: 0.334 of Semiconductor, 1.447 of Financial and −0.916 of Food, which sum to 0.865. With 8–10 April the mix becomes $(-0.018,\,2.069,\,-1.376)$. With all four days there is none, and the 7–9 April mix gives +10.17% on 10 April, not +9.25%. Why does a perfect fit say so little?

> A system $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ has $m$ equations and $n$ unknowns; $M$ is $m\times n$, with entries $m_{ij}$ (row $i$, column $j$) and columns $\mathbf m_j$, and $[M\mid\mathbf b]$ is the augmented matrix. ## Two Pictures of a Linear System {lo=1}

Start with two days and two unknowns. Which multiple $x_1$ of Semiconductor and $x_2$ of Financial reproduce TAIEX's returns on 8 and 9 April? Each day gives one equation:

$$\begin{aligned}-3.99\,x_1+0.53\,x_2&=-4.02 &&\text{(8 April)},\\ -4.47\,x_1-5.61\,x_2&=-5.79 &&\text{(9 April)}.\end{aligned}$$

> [!definition] Linear system
> A **linear system** of $m$ equations in $n$ unknowns is $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, with $M$ an $m\times n$ matrix, $\mathbf x$ in $\mathbb R^n$ and $\mathbf b$ in $\mathbb R^m$. Row $i$ is the equation $\sum_jm_{ij}x_j=b_i$. The **augmented matrix** $[M\mid\mathbf b]$ appends $\mathbf b$ to $M$ as a last column.

**The row picture.** With two unknowns, each equation is a line in the $(x_1,x_2)$ plane, and a solution lies on every line at once. Here the two lines meet in one point ([[ex:rowpic]]). With three unknowns each equation is a plane, and three planes usually meet in one point.

**The column picture.** Read $M\mathbf x$ by columns instead, as [[LM8]] (8.12) read $X\mathbf w$:

$$M\mathbf x=x_1\mathbf m_1+x_2\mathbf m_2+\dots+x_n\mathbf m_n. \tag{9.1}$$

The system asks which combination of the columns reaches $\mathbf b$. In the case, column $j$ holds sector $j$'s returns on the chosen days. So a solution is a mix of sectors whose returns equal TAIEX's day by day ([[ex:colpic]]).

> [!example] Two days, two sectors, two pictures {#two}
> Solve the 8–9 April system above, and check the answer in both pictures.
>
> > [!solution]
> > Eliminate one unknown at a time. Adding 5.61 times the first equation to 0.53 times the second removes $x_2$, with
> > $$\begin{aligned}-3.99(5.61)-4.47(0.53)&=-24.753,\\ -4.02(5.61)-5.79(0.53)&=-25.6209,\end{aligned}$$
> > so $-24.753\,x_1=-25.6209$ and $x_1=1.035062$. Adding 4.47 times the first equation to −3.99 times the second removes $x_1$ and gives $24.753\,x_2=5.1327$, so $x_2=0.207357$. Both divisions use 24.753, which Section 3 names the determinant.
> >
> > In the row picture the two lines cross at $(1.035062,\,0.207357)$. In the column picture,
> > $$\begin{aligned}1.035062\,\mathbf m_1+0.207357\,\mathbf m_2&=\begin{pmatrix}-4.02\\-5.79\end{pmatrix}\\&=\mathbf b.\end{aligned}$$
> > So 1.035 of Semiconductor and 0.207 of Financial reproduce TAIEX's returns on both days.

> 一個線性方程組（system of linear equations）可以按列讀，也可以按行讀。按列讀，每一天是一條直線（三個未知數時是一個平面），解就是所有直線的交點。按行讀，每一行是一個產業指數在這幾天的報酬，解就是用這幾個產業「組合」出 TAIEX 報酬的倍數，也就是一個線性組合（linear combination）。兩條直線可能交於一點、平行或重合，所以解可能剛好一個、沒有或無限多個。

## The Determinant {lo=2}

The number 24.753 that both divisions of [[eg:two]] used is $m_{11}m_{22}-m_{12}m_{21}$. It measures how far the two columns are from lying on one line.

> [!definition] Determinant of a $2\times2$ matrix
> For a $2\times2$ matrix $M$, the **determinant** is
> $$\det M:=m_{11}m_{22}-m_{12}m_{21}. \tag{9.2}$$
> The absolute value of $\det M$ is the area $\lVert\mathbf m_1\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf m_2\rVert\sin\theta$ of the parallelogram spanned by the columns. The sign is positive when $\mathbf m_2$ lies counter-clockwise from $\mathbf m_1$.

The area follows from [[LM8]]'s dot product. Expanding both sides shows

$$(\det M)^2+(\mathbf m_1^{\top}\mathbf m_2)^2=\lVert\mathbf m_1\rVert^2\,\lVert\mathbf m_2\rVert^2,$$

and $\mathbf m_1^{\top}\mathbf m_2=\lVert\mathbf m_1\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf m_2\rVert\cos\theta$, so $\lvert\det M\rvert=\lVert\mathbf m_1\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf m_2\rVert\sin\theta$. The determinant is 0 exactly when the columns lie on one line: $\theta$ is 0° or 180°, or a column is $\mathbf 0$.

> [!example] Area and nearly parallel columns {#sliver}
> Find the determinant of [[eg:two]] and the angle between its columns. Then solve the system of 7 and 9 April with Semiconductor and Food, and compare.
> $$\begin{pmatrix}-9.95&-8.66\\-4.47&-4.17\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\x_2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-9.70\\-5.79\end{pmatrix}$$
>
> > [!solution]
> > For [[eg:two]],
> > $$\begin{aligned}\det M&=(-3.99)(-5.61)\\&\quad-(0.53)(-4.47)\\&=22.3839+2.3691=24.753.\end{aligned}$$
> > The columns have lengths 5.991744 and 5.634980, so $\sin\theta=24.753/33.7634=0.7331$ and $\theta=47.15°$.
> >
> > For Semiconductor and Food, $\det M=41.4915-38.7102=2.7813$. The columns are longer, 10.907951 and 9.611686, but $\sin\theta=2.7813/104.8438=0.0265$, so they lie only 1.52° apart ([[ex:area]]). Eliminating as in [[eg:two]] gives $x_1=-9.6924/2.7813=-3.48$ and $x_2=14.2515/2.7813=5.12$. To reproduce TAIEX with two nearly parallel columns, the mix shorts 3.48 of Semiconductor against 5.12 of Food.

For larger matrices the **cofactor expansion** defines the determinant step by step, along the first row:

$$\det M=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{1+j}\,m_{1j}\det M^{(1j)}.$$

Each $(-1)^{i+j}\det M^{(ij)}$ is a **cofactor**. Expanding along any other row or column gives the same value. For $n=3$ the absolute value is the volume of the box spanned by the columns (both stated without proof; Strang 2016).

> [!theorem] Row operations and the determinant
> Let $M$ and $K$ be $n\times n$ matrices.
> 1. Swapping two rows changes the sign of the determinant.
> 2. Multiplying one row by a number $a$ multiplies the determinant by $a$.
> 3. Adding a multiple of one row to another leaves the determinant unchanged.
> 4. The determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries.
>
> Moreover,
> $$\begin{gathered}\det(MK)=\det M\,\det K,\\ \det M^{\top}=\det M,\\ \det M^{-1}=1/\det M,\end{gathered} \tag{9.3}$$
> the last when $M^{-1}$ exists. Rules 1–4 are stated without proof (Strang 2016); the Deep Dive below derives (9.3) from them.

Since $\det M^{\top}=\det M$, the rules hold for columns too. If one column is a combination of the others, subtracting that combination (rule 3) leaves a zero column, so $\det M=0$. A square matrix with $\det M=0$ is **singular**; Section 5 shows that its columns are dependent.

> [!example] The determinant of the 7–9 April system {#cof}
> With rows 7, 8 and 9 April and columns Semiconductor, Financial and Food, the augmented matrix $[M\mid\mathbf b]$ of the case is
> $$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}-9.95&-9.89&-8.66&-9.70\\-3.99&0.53&3.77&-4.02\\-4.47&-5.61&-4.17&-5.79\end{array}\right).$$
> Compute $\det M$ by cofactor expansion along the first row.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The three minors are
> > $$\begin{aligned}\det M^{(11)}&=0.53(-4.17)-3.77(-5.61)\\&=18.9396,\\ \det M^{(12)}&=3.99(4.17)+3.77(4.47)\\&=33.4902,\end{aligned}$$
> > and $\det M^{(13)}=24.753$, the determinant of [[eg:two]]. So
> > $$\begin{aligned}\det M&=-9.95(18.9396)\\&\quad+9.89(33.4902)-8.66(24.753)\\&=-71.591922,\end{aligned}$$
> > the sum of −188.44902, 331.218078 and −214.36098. The determinant is not 0, so the three columns are independent, and Section 5 finds exactly one mix.

> [!pitfall] A small determinant does not mean nearly dependent columns
> The determinant scales with the entries: $\det(0.1\,I_9)=10^{-9}$, yet the system $0.1\,\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ has the plain solution $\mathbf x=10\,\mathbf b$. Judge a determinant against the column lengths instead: 24.753 against 33.76 in [[eg:sliver]] is far from 0, and 2.7813 against 104.84 is close. [[LM11]] measures this closeness with the condition number, which tells how strongly a nearly singular matrix magnifies small errors.

> [!deepdive]- The product rule (9.3)
> A row operation on $K$ is a product $EK$, where the **elementary matrix** $E$ is $I_n$ with the same operation applied. By rules 1–3, $\det(EK)=\det E\,\det K$. If $M$ is invertible, Gauss–Jordan elimination (Section 6) writes it as a product $E_1E_2\cdots$ of elementary matrices. Applying the rule once per factor gives $\det(MK)=\det E_1\det E_2\cdots\det K=\det M\det K$.
>
> If $M$ is singular, so is $MK$, whose columns combine those of $M$; both sides are 0. Transposing an elementary matrix keeps its determinant, so $\det M^{\top}=\det M$, and $\det M\det M^{-1}=\det I_n=1$ (Strang 2016).

## Cramer's Rule {lo=3}

With the other columns held fixed, the determinant is linear in each column, as the cofactor expansion along that column shows. It is 0 when two columns are equal, because swapping them changes the sign but not the matrix. Now suppose $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, and replace column $j$ of $M$ by $\mathbf b=\sum_kx_k\mathbf m_k$. By linearity in column $j$,

$$\det M_j(\mathbf b)=\sum_{k}x_k\det M_j(\mathbf m_k)=x_j\det M,$$

because for $k\ne j$ the matrix $M_j(\mathbf m_k)$ holds column $\mathbf m_k$ twice.

> [!theorem] Cramer's rule
> If $M$ is $n\times n$ and $\det M\ne0$, the system $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ has exactly one solution, with entries
> $$x_j=\frac{\det M_j(\mathbf b)}{\det M},\qquad j=1,\dots,n. \tag{9.4}$$
> The derivation above shows that no other solution exists; the Deep Dive of Section 6 shows that (9.4) solves the system.

> [!example] Cramer's rule for the 7–9 April mix {#cramer}
> Solve the system of [[eg:cof]] by (9.4).
>
> > [!solution]
> > Replacing each column of $M$ in turn by $\mathbf b$ and expanding as in [[eg:cof]] gives $\det M_1(\mathbf b)=-23.919201$, $\det M_2(\mathbf b)=-103.581657$ and $\det M_3(\mathbf b)=65.586258$. Dividing each by $\det M=-71.591922$,
> > $$\mathbf x=(0.334105,\ 1.446834,\ -0.916113)^{\top}.$$
> > The mix holds 0.334 of Semiconductor, 1.447 of Financial and −0.916 of Food, and its multiples sum to 0.865, not 1. [[LM7]] (7.12) is the same rule for two Kelly fractions. Each numerator there is the determinant of the covariance matrix with one column replaced by the excess returns.

Cramer's rule is exact and short to write, but slow to compute. A cofactor expansion of an $n\times n$ determinant has $n!$ terms, each a product of $n$ entries, and (9.4) needs $n+1$ determinants: $(n+1)!$ terms. Elimination needs about $\tfrac23n^3$ operations (Section 5 counts them). At $n=3$ that is 24 terms against about 18 operations, and at $n=20$ about $5.1\times10^{19}$ terms against 5,333 ([[ex:cost]]).

> [!zh] 行列式與克拉瑪法則
> $2\times2$ 行列式（determinant）就是兩個行向量（column vector）張成的平行四邊形（parallelogram）面積，正負號表示方向；$3\times3$ 時是平行六面體（parallelepiped）的體積。行列式為 0，表示某一行是其他行的線性組合（linear combination），方程組不會剛好只有一個解。克拉瑪法則（Cramer's rule）把每個未知數寫成兩個行列式的比，公式漂亮，二元、三元時手算很方便。但 $n$ 個未知數要算 $n+1$ 個行列式、共 $(n+1)!$ 項，$n=20$ 時約 $5\times10^{19}$ 項，消去法（elimination）只要約五千次運算。行列式很小也不代表接近奇異（singular），要和行向量的長度比較，[[LM11]] 用條件數（condition number）衡量。

## Gaussian Elimination {lo=4}

Three operations on the equations leave the solutions of a system unchanged, because each can be undone. They swap two equations, multiply one by a non-zero number, or add a multiple of one equation to another. **Gaussian elimination** uses them to make the matrix upper triangular, with zeros below the diagonal. **Back substitution** then solves the last equation first and works upward.

At step $k$ the **pivot** $d_k$ is the diagonal entry of row $k$. For each row $i$ below it, the **multiplier** $\ell_{ik}$ is that row's entry in column $k$ divided by $d_k$. Subtracting $\ell_{ik}$ times row $k$ from row $i$ puts a zero there.

Step $k$ changes the $(n-k)^2$ entries below and to the right of the pivot, each with one multiplication and one subtraction. Summing over the steps,

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}2(n-k)^2=\tfrac23n^3-n^2+\tfrac13n\approx\tfrac23n^3$$

operations, with an error of order $n^2$; the right-hand side and back substitution add terms of the same order.

If a pivot is 0, swap its row with a lower row that has a non-zero entry in that column. Library code goes further. At every step it brings into the pivot position the row whose entry in the pivot column is largest in absolute value. This **partial pivoting** keeps every multiplier between −1 and 1, which limits the growth of rounding errors (Golub and Van Loan 2013).

Elimination also delivers the determinant. Subtracting multiples of rows leaves it unchanged (rule 3), each swap flips its sign (rule 1), and the final matrix is triangular (rule 4). So

$$\det M=\pm\,d_1d_2\cdots d_n,$$

with a minus sign when the number of swaps is odd. With independent columns, elimination always finds a non-zero pivot. Otherwise the current column, zero at and below the pivot row, would be a combination of the earlier columns. So $\det M\ne0$ for independent columns, the converse promised in Section 3.

> [!example] Elimination on the 7–9 April system {#gauss}
> Solve the system of [[eg:cof]] by elimination, and check its determinant.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The first pivot is $d_1=-9.95$. The multipliers $\ell_{21}=-3.99/(-9.95)=0.401005$ and $\ell_{31}=-4.47/(-9.95)=0.449246$ clear column 1. Then $\ell_{32}=-0.259557$, the entry −1.166955 divided by the pivot 4.495940, clears column 2 ([[ex:elim]]). No swap is needed, and partial pivoting would choose the same rows. Back substitution in exact arithmetic gives
> > $$\begin{aligned}x_3&=\frac{-1.466119}{1.600370}=-0.916113,\\ x_2&=\frac{-0.130251-7.242704\,x_3}{4.495940}\\&=1.446834,\\ x_1&=\frac{-9.70+9.89\,x_2+8.66\,x_3}{-9.95}\\&=0.334105,\end{aligned}$$
> > as in [[eg:cramer]]. The exact pivots multiply to $-71.591922=\det M$.

## The Inverse {lo=5}

> [!definition] Inverse
> An $n\times n$ matrix $M$ is **invertible** if some $n\times n$ matrix $K$ satisfies $MK=KM=I_n$. That $K$ is unique and is written $M^{-1}$.

If $K$ and $K_2$ both qualify, then $K=K(MK_2)=(KM)K_2=K_2$, so the inverse is unique. When it exists, the system $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ has the one solution $\mathbf x=M^{-1}\mathbf b$. For a $2\times2$ matrix with $\det M\ne0$, multiplying out checks

$$M^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det M}\begin{pmatrix}m_{22}&-m_{12}\\-m_{21}&m_{11}\end{pmatrix}. \tag{9.5}$$

For larger matrices, **Gauss–Jordan elimination** row-reduces $[M\mid I_n]$ until the left block is $I_n$; the right block is then $M^{-1}$. It solves the $n$ systems $M\mathbf k_j=\mathbf e_j$ at once, whose solutions are the columns of $K=M^{-1}$. Two rules follow from the definition: $(MK)^{-1}=K^{-1}M^{-1}$, since $MKK^{-1}M^{-1}=I_n$, and $(M^{\top})^{-1}=(M^{-1})^{\top}$, by transposing $MM^{-1}=I_n$ with [[LM8]] (8.15).

For an $n\times n$ matrix $M$ the following are equivalent:

$$\begin{aligned}M\text{ is invertible}&\iff\det M\ne0\\&\iff\operatorname{rank}M=n\\&\iff\operatorname{null}M=\{\mathbf 0\}.\end{aligned} \tag{9.6}$$

Here the rank is the number of independent columns, and the null space is the set of solutions of $M\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$ (Section 7). An inverse turns $M\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$ into $\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$. Independent columns give $n$ non-zero pivots and $\det M\ne0$ (Section 5). The Deep Dive below builds the inverse when $\det M\ne0$.

The inverse also tells how the answer moves with the data. Since $\mathbf x=M^{-1}\mathbf b$, column $j$ of $M^{-1}$ is the change in the mix when TAIEX's return on day $j$ rises by one point ([[ex:inverse]]). Had TAIEX fallen 8.70% on 7 April, not 9.70%, the Semiconductor multiple would fall by 0.265 and the Financial one rise by 0.468.

The inverse is a concept first. To solve one system, `np.linalg.solve` runs elimination on $\mathbf b$ alone. `np.linalg.inv` runs it on all $n$ columns of $I_n$, so it costs more. Its $M^{-1}\mathbf b$ is also usually less accurate (Higham 2002a, ch. 14). Use `inv` for concepts such as $\Sigma^{-1}$; `solve` also takes many right-hand sides at once.

```python
import numpy as np

M = np.array([[-9.95, -9.89, -8.66],    # 7 April: Semi, Fin, Food (%)
              [-3.99,  0.53,  3.77],    # 8 April
              [-4.47, -5.61, -4.17]])   # 9 April
b = np.array([-9.70, -4.02, -5.79])     # TAIEX
x = np.linalg.solve(M, b)               # elimination with partial pivoting
print(x.round(6))                 # [ 0.334105  1.446834 -0.916113]
print(round(np.linalg.det(M), 6))       # -71.591922
print(np.linalg.inv(M).round(3))        # the heatmap above
```

> [!kc]
> Why is `np.linalg.inv(M) @ b` the wrong habit for solving one system?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Computing `inv(M) @ b` does more work and usually loses accuracy. `inv` runs elimination on the $n$ columns of $I_n$ and then multiplies, while `solve` runs it on $\mathbf b$ alone. The rounding errors of the computed inverse also pass into $M^{-1}\mathbf b$ (Higham 2002a, ch. 14).

> [!deepdive]- The adjugate formula $M^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}M/\det M$
> Let $\operatorname{adj}M$ be the $n\times n$ matrix whose entry $(j,i)$ is the cofactor $(-1)^{i+j}\det M^{(ij)}$. Entry $(i,i)$ of $M\operatorname{adj}M$ is $\sum_jm_{ij}(-1)^{i+j}\det M^{(ij)}$, the cofactor expansion of $\det M$ along row $i$. For $k\ne i$, entry $(i,k)$ expands the matrix whose row $k$ is replaced by row $i$, which has two equal rows, so it is 0. Hence $M\operatorname{adj}M=(\det M)\,I_n$, and when $\det M\ne0$,
> $$M^{-1}=\frac{\operatorname{adj}M}{\det M}.$$
>
> For $n=2$ this is (9.5). Entry $j$ of $\operatorname{adj}M\,\mathbf b$ expands $\det M_j(\mathbf b)$ along column $j$, so $\mathbf x=M^{-1}\mathbf b$ is (9.4), which therefore solves the system (Strang 2016).

> [!zh] 消去法與反矩陣
> 高斯消去法（Gaussian elimination）用三種不改變解的列運算（row operation），把方程組化成上三角方陣（upper triangular matrix），再從最後一列往上解回去，也就是後置法（back substitution）。每一步的對角元素是樞軸元（pivot），樞軸元相乘（加上換列的正負號）就是行列式（determinant）。NumPy 的 `np.linalg.solve` 做的正是這件事，而且每一步把絕對值最大的列換上來，以控制捨入誤差（rounding error）。反矩陣（inverse matrix）主要是概念工具：$\mathbf x=M^{-1}\mathbf b$ 說明解如何隨資料變動，[[LM7]] 的凱利比例就是 $\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol\mu-r_f\mathbf 1)$。實際解題時先用 `solve`，不要先求反矩陣再相乘。

## Rank and the Number of Solutions {lo=6}

Return to the case with three unknowns, and change the number of days.

> [!definition] Independence, column space, null space and rank
> Vectors $\mathbf m_1,\dots,\mathbf m_n$ are **linearly independent** if $x_1\mathbf m_1+\dots+x_n\mathbf m_n=\mathbf 0$ only for $\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$. For an $m\times n$ matrix $M$, the **column space** $\operatorname{col}M$ is the set of all $M\mathbf x$. The **null space** $\operatorname{null}M$ is the set of all $\mathbf x$ with $M\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$. The **rank** $\operatorname{rank}M$ is the largest number of independent columns. The **dimension** of a space of vectors is the largest number of independent vectors in it, so $\operatorname{rank}M=\dim\operatorname{col}M$.

Elimination works on any $m\times n$ matrix. When a column has no non-zero entry at or below the current row, it gets no pivot, and elimination moves to the next column. The result is an **echelon form**: each row starts further right than the row above, and zero rows come last. The pivot columns are independent and the other columns are combinations of them, so the rank is the number of pivots.

Each column without a pivot holds a **free variable**: give it any value, and back substitution finds the rest. With $\mathbf b=\mathbf 0$, setting one free variable to 1 and the others to 0 gives a null-space vector $\mathbf x_h$. These vectors are independent and span $\operatorname{null}M$, one for each free column, so

$$\operatorname{rank}M+\dim\operatorname{null}M=n. \tag{9.7}$$

A system is solvable when $\mathbf b$ lies in $\operatorname{col}M$. In echelon form, the entries of the transformed $\mathbf b$ beside zero rows of $M$ must then be 0: appending $\mathbf b$ adds no pivot. If $\mathbf x_p$ is one solution and $M\mathbf x_h=\mathbf 0$, then $M(\mathbf x_p+\mathbf x_h)=\mathbf b$, and two solutions always differ by a null-space vector. Hence, for the system $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$,

$$\begin{gathered}\text{solvable}\iff\operatorname{rank}M=\operatorname{rank}[M\mid\mathbf b],\\ \text{solutions}=\mathbf x_p+\operatorname{null}M.\end{gathered} \tag{9.8}$$

By (9.7), a solvable system has exactly one solution when $\operatorname{rank}M=n$, and infinitely many when $\operatorname{rank}M<n$.

> [!deepdive]- Row rank equals column rank
> The **row rank** is the largest number of independent rows. A row operation $EM$, with $E$ invertible, keeps all combinations of the rows and hence the row rank. It also keeps every relation among the columns, since $EM\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$ exactly when $M\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$, and hence the column rank. In echelon form both ranks equal the number of pivots (Strang 2016).

## Perfect Collinearity in Returns, and Python {lo=7,8}

Now let the rows be dates and the columns assets, as in the $T\times N$ return matrix $X$ of [[LM8]]. Its columns are dependent exactly when some fixed mix of the assets returns 0 on every date, a case called **perfect collinearity**. By (9.7),

$$\operatorname{rank}X<N\iff X\mathbf x=\mathbf 0\ \text{ for some }\mathbf x\ne\mathbf 0. \tag{9.9}$$

Take the daily returns of Semiconductor and Financial on the Volume II sector sample: $T=4{,}382$ returns from 2 December 2008 to 8 October 2026. Add a 50/50 mix of the two as a third column. A mix **rebalanced daily** to 50/50 returns $\tfrac12R_{S,t}+\tfrac12R_{F,t}$ on every date. So $\mathbf x=(-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,1)^{\top}$ solves $X\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$, and the rank is 2.

A mix **bought and held** from the close of 1 December 2008 lets its weights drift with prices. Its return on date $t$ is $w_{t-1}R_{S,t}+(1-w_{t-1})R_{F,t}$, where $w_{t-1}$ is Semiconductor's weight at the previous close. That weight rose from 0.50 to 0.858 by 8 October 2026 ([[ex:drift]]). Along the way it fell to 0.457 on 4 August 2011 and peaked at 0.883 on 25 May 2026. No fixed $\mathbf x$ matches a changing weight, so the rank is 3.

Floating point keeps about 16 significant digits ([[LM2]]), so an exact 0 rarely survives. For the matrix of [[eg:mix]], `np.linalg.det` prints about $10^{-14}$. `solve` may return entries of order $10^{15}$ along $\mathbf x_h$ without an error, because LAPACK stops only at an exactly zero pivot. `matrix_rank` applies a tolerance ([[LM12]]) and reports 2. print(abs(np.linalg.det(Mmix)) < 1e-10)   # True, though det is not 0.0
```python
four = np.vstack([M, [9.91, 8.36, 5.72]])           # add 10 April
Mb = np.column_stack([four, [-9.70, -4.02, -5.79, 9.25]])
print(np.linalg.matrix_rank(four), np.linalg.matrix_rank(Mb))   # 3 4
# np.linalg.solve(four, ...) raises LinAlgError:
#   Last 2 dimensions of the array must be square
```

## Summary {-}

- A linear system $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ reads by rows, as lines or planes that meet, and by columns (9.1), as a mix that reaches $\mathbf b$. On 8 and 9 April, 1.035 of Semiconductor and 0.207 of Financial reproduce TAIEX.
- The determinant (9.2) is the signed area or volume of the columns and is multiplicative (9.3). It is 0 exactly when the columns are dependent; judge a small value against their lengths.
- Cramer's rule (9.4) solved the 7–9 April system, $\mathbf x=(0.334105,\,1.446834,\,-0.916113)$, with $(n+1)!$ terms against about $\tfrac23n^3$ operations for elimination.
- Elimination with back substitution and partial pivoting is what `np.linalg.solve` runs. Its pivots multiply to $\pm\det M$, here −71.591922.
- The inverse (9.5) exists exactly under (9.6). It explains answers, such as LM7's $\Sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol\mu-r_f\mathbf 1)=(5.59,\,-0.52)$, while `solve` computes them.
- Rank, (9.7) and (9.8) separate no, one and infinitely many solutions: four, three and two days of the case.
- An exact fit on three days carries no evidence. Financial's multiple ran from −1.479 to 2.069 across the four triples, and the 7–9 April mix gave +10.17% for 10 April, against +9.25%.
- A return matrix loses rank exactly when a fixed mix returns 0 on every date (9.9). A daily rebalanced mix does; a buy-and-hold mix does not. Floating point turns such zeros into about $10^{-14}$, so test with `matrix_rank`.

## Solutions {-}

1. **C** is correct. The swap changes the sign (rule 1), and doubling a row doubles the determinant (rule 2), so the new determinant is $-2\det M=143.183844$. A ignores the swap, and B ignores the doubling.

2. **A** is correct. The matrix $M$ has only three columns, so $\operatorname{rank}M\le3<4$, and by (9.8) there is no solution. B needs both ranks equal to 3, and C needs them equal and below 3.

3. **B** is correct. The rebalanced mix returns $\tfrac12R_{S,t}+\tfrac12R_{F,t}$ on every date, so $(-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,1)^{\top}$ lies in the null space (9.9). The held mix's weights drift (Section 8), so no fixed combination is zero, which is also why C is wrong.

4. The determinant and, by (9.4), the mix are
   $$\begin{aligned}\det M&=(-9.95)(5.72)-(-8.66)(9.91)\\&=-56.914+85.8206=28.9066,\\ x_1&=\frac{(-9.70)(5.72)-(-8.66)(9.25)}{28.9066}\\&=\frac{24.621}{28.9066}=0.85174,\\ x_2&=\frac{(-9.95)(9.25)-(-9.70)(9.91)}{28.9066}\\&=\frac{4.0895}{28.9066}=0.14147.\end{aligned}$$
   By (9.5) the inverse is
   $$M^{-1}=\frac{1}{28.9066}\begin{pmatrix}5.72&8.66\\-9.91&-9.95\end{pmatrix}.$$

   Multiplying it by $\mathbf b$ gives the same mix. Its multiples nearly sum to 1 (0.993), while on 7 and 9 April the same two sectors needed −3.48 and 5.12 ([[eg:sliver]]).

5. Column 1's largest entry in absolute value is 9.91, so the 10 April row moves to the top. The multipliers are $\ell_{21}=-0.451060$ (9 April) and $\ell_{31}=-0.402624$ (8 April). In column 2 the 8 April row now has the larger entry, 3.895933 against −1.839142, so it moves up, and $\ell_{32}=-0.472067$.

   The pivots are 9.91, 3.895933 and 1.276928, and back substitution gives $\mathbf x=(-0.017926,\,2.069318,\,-1.376197)$. Two swaps keep the sign, so the exact pivots multiply to $\det M=49.300524$. Against [[eg:gauss]], changing one day moves Financial's multiple from 1.447 to 2.069.

6. The minors of the 7 April row are $\det M^{(11)}=-28.4856$, $\det M^{(12)}=-60.1835$ and $\det M^{(13)}=-38.6087$, so
   $$\begin{aligned}\det M&=-9.95(-28.4856)\\&\quad+9.89(-60.1835)\\&\quad-8.66(-38.6087)\\&=22.568247,\end{aligned}$$
   the sum of 283.43172, −595.214815 and 334.351342. Without swaps, elimination keeps the first two rows of [[eg:gauss]], so $d_1=-9.95$ and $d_2=4.495940$. After step 1 the 10 April row is $(0,\,-1.490241,\,-2.905186)$; then, in exact arithmetic, $\ell_{32}=-0.331464$ and $d_3=-0.504492$. The exact pivots multiply to 22.568247.

7. (a) Subtract twice row 1 from row 2 and row 1 from row 3, then row 2 from row 3:
   $$\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&2&3&1\\0&0&1&1\\0&0&0&a-2\end{array}\right).$$
   The pivots are in columns 1 and 3, so $\operatorname{rank}M=2$. The column space $\operatorname{col}M$ is the plane of all combinations of $(1,2,1)^{\top}$ and $(3,7,4)^{\top}$; the second column is twice the first. (b) The last row reads $0=a-2$, so the system is solvable only for $a=2$. (c) With $x_2$ free, $\mathbf x_h=(-2,1,0)^{\top}$. The other rows give $x_3=1$ and $x_1=-2-2x_2$, so the solutions are $(-2,0,1)^{\top}+x_2\,(-2,1,0)^{\top}$, and $2+1=3$ as (9.7) requires.

8. Each bond pays its coupon every year and 100 more at maturity, so the matrix is lower triangular:
   $$\begin{pmatrix}102&0&0\\3&103&0\\4&4&104\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_1\\v_2\\v_3\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}100.50\\102.55\\106.25\end{pmatrix}.$$
   No elimination is needed. Forward substitution solves the rows from the top:
   $$\begin{aligned}v_1&=\frac{100.50}{102}=0.985294,\\ v_2&=\frac{102.55-3v_1}{103}=0.966933,\\ v_3&=\frac{106.25-4v_1-4v_2}{104}=0.946549.\end{aligned}$$
   The implied spot rates, 1.4925%, 1.6955% and 1.8480%, rise with maturity like [[LM6]]'s illustrative curve.

9. (a) Each contract's daily P&L is its multiplier times the index move, so
   $$\begin{pmatrix}-60{,}000&-15{,}000\\30{,}000&7{,}500\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\x_2\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf b,$$
   whose matrix has determinant $-450{,}000{,}000+450{,}000{,}000=0$. (b) Both columns are multiples of $(-300,150)^{\top}$, so only targets proportional to it can be reached: $b_1=-2b_2$. (c) The null vector is $\mathbf x_h=(-\tfrac14,1)^{\top}$: short a quarter of a TX contract against one MTX, and the P&L is 0 on every day. So every reachable target has a line of solutions.

10. The two answers agree to about 16 significant digits; their largest difference, of order $10^{-16}$, varies from machine to machine. On the $4\times3$ matrix, `solve` raises `LinAlgError` with the message "Last 2 dimensions of the array must be square". `matrix_rank` gives 3 for $M$ and 4 for $[M\mid\mathbf b]$, so there is no exact solution, as in [[eg:four]].

11. The ranks are 2 for the rebalanced mix and 3 for the buy-and-hold mix. The buy-and-hold weight in Semiconductor ends at 0.858 on 8 October 2026. The largest daily gap between the two mixes' returns is 2.57 percentage points.

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Linear system | 線性方程組 | $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$: $m$ linear equations in $n$ unknowns. |
| Augmented matrix | 增廣矩陣 | $[M\mid\mathbf b]$, the matrix with $\mathbf b$ appended as a last column. |
| Row picture, column picture | 按列讀、按行讀 | Lines or planes that meet; columns combined to reach $\mathbf b$, (9.1). |
| Replicating mix | 複製組合 | Multiples of assets whose returns reproduce a target's; not weights. |
| Determinant | 行列式 | Signed area or volume spanned by the columns, (9.2), (9.3). |
| Cofactor | 餘因子 | $(-1)^{i+j}\det M^{(ij)}$; the cofactor expansion sums entries times cofactors. |
| Cramer's rule | 克拉瑪法則 | $x_j=\det M_j(\mathbf b)/\det M$, (9.4). |
| Gaussian elimination | 高斯消去法 | Row operations to triangular form, then back substitution. |
| Pivot | 樞軸元 | The diagonal entry that clears the entries below it. |
| Back substitution | 後置法 | Solving a triangular system from the last equation up. |
| Partial pivoting | 部分軸元消元法 | Moving the row with the largest entry in the pivot column into the pivot position. |
| Inverse matrix | 反矩陣 | $M^{-1}$ with $MM^{-1}=M^{-1}M=I_n$, (9.5), (9.6). |
| Singular matrix | 奇異矩陣 | A square matrix with $\det M=0$; it has no inverse. |
| Gauss–Jordan elimination | 高斯-約旦消去法 | Row reduction of $[M\mid I_n]$ to $[I_n\mid M^{-1}]$. |
| Linear independence | 線性獨立 | No combination of the vectors with a non-zero coefficient is $\mathbf 0$. |
| Column space | 行空間 | All combinations $M\mathbf x$ of the columns. |
| Null space | 零空間 | All $\mathbf x$ with $M\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$. |
| Rank | 秩 | Number of independent columns, or of pivots, (9.7). |
| Free variable | 自由變數 | An unknown whose column has no pivot. |
| Particular solution | 特解 | One solution $\mathbf x_p$ of $M\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, (9.8). |
| Perfect collinearity | 完全共線性 | A fixed combination of the columns that is 0 on every row, (9.9). |
