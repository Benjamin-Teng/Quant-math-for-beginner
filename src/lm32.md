## Introduction

[[LM27]] measured a book's typical risk by its volatility $\sigma_p$, (27.4). This module measures the tail, by value at risk and expected shortfall, and the path, by drawdowns.

> [!case] Did any model see 7 April coming?

> Was every model wrong, or was the day just rare? How precise is a 99% value at risk in the first place? And what does a one-day number say about the fall from a peak?

## Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall {lo=1,2}

A 99% one-day value at risk is a loss exceeded with probability 1%. Expected shortfall is the mean loss beyond it. Both come from [[LM14]]'s quantile function $F_R^{-1}(u)=\min\{x:F_R(x)\ge u\}$.

> [!definition] Value at risk and expected shortfall
> Let $R$ be the simple return of a position over $m$ sessions, with distribution function $F_R$, and let $0<u<1$. The value at risk and, when $\E\lvert R\rvert<\infty$, the expected shortfall at tail probability $u$ are
> $$\begin{gathered}\operatorname{VaR}_u:=-F_R^{-1}(u),\\ \operatorname{ES}_u:=-\frac1u\int_0^uF_R^{-1}(v)\,dv.\end{gathered} \tag{32.1}$$
> Both are positive for a loss. "99% VaR" is $u=0.01$, and the horizon is one day unless $m$ is named.

For a continuous $F_R$ the integral is a tail mean. By (18.1), $F_R^{-1}(U)$ with $U\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$ has the law of $R$, and $U\le u$ exactly when $F_R^{-1}(U)\le F_R^{-1}(u)$, up to probability 0. So the integral is $\E[R\,\mathbf 1\{R\le F_R^{-1}(u)\}]$, and since $\Pr(R\le F_R^{-1}(u))=u$,

$$\begin{gathered}\operatorname{ES}_u=-\E\big[R\mid R\le F_R^{-1}(u)\big]\\ \text{for continuous }F_R.\end{gathered}$$

This tail mean, with the sign reversed, is [[LM5]]'s (5.11) and [[LM15]]'s at the VaR level.

The historical estimators apply (32.1) to the empirical distribution $\hat F$, mass $1/T$ on each return. Its quantile function is the step $\hat F^{-1}(v)=R_{(i)}$ for $(i-1)/T<v\le i/T$, so the integral takes $j-1$ whole steps and part of step $j$:

$$\begin{gathered}\operatorname{VaR}=-R_{(j)},\qquad j=\lceil uT\rceil,\\ \operatorname{ES}=-\frac1u\Big[\frac1T\sum_{i<j}R_{(i)}+\Big(u-\frac{j-1}{T}\Big)R_{(j)}\Big].\end{gathered} \tag{32.2}$$

When $uT$ is whole, ES is the mean of the $j$ worst returns. Form (32.2) is Acerbi and Tasche's (2002a) tail mean applied to $\hat F$, the book's one historical ES.

> [!zh] 風險值與預期短缺：同一個分位數的兩種讀法
> 風險值（value at risk，VaR）是報酬分布下方分位數（quantile）的負值：99% 的單日風險值，是一天只有 1% 機率會超過的損失。預期短缺（expected shortfall，ES）則問：落到那 1% 的壞日子時，平均損失多少。歷史模擬法（historical simulation）直接用經驗分布（empirical distribution），把報酬由小到大排成順序統計量（order statistic），第 $j=\lceil uT\rceil$ 小的報酬取負號就是風險值。預期短缺把前 $j-1$ 個壞日子全部計入、第 $j$ 個按比例計入再平均，這就是 (32.2)。樣本很小時，幾種平均方法差距不小，本書一律用 (32.2)。

## Normal, Student-t and Tail Models {lo=1,2,8}

A model gives the quantile in closed form. For a normal $R$, (15.6) gives $F_R^{-1}(u)=\mu+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(u)$. With (5.11)'s threshold at that quantile, $z=\Phi^{-1}(u)$ and $\Phi(z)=u$, so

$$\begin{gathered}\operatorname{VaR}_u=-\mu-\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(u),\\ \operatorname{ES}_u=-\mu+\sigma\,\frac{\varphi\big(\Phi^{-1}(u)\big)}{u}.\end{gathered} \tag{32.3}$$

> [!result] Student-t VaR and ES
> Let $R=\mu+\sigma\sqrt{(\nu-2)/\nu}\;Y$ with $Y\sim t_\nu$ and $\nu>2$, [[LM15]]'s scaled model (15.11), and let $q=F_Y^{-1}(u)$. Then
> $$\begin{gathered}\operatorname{VaR}_u=-\mu-\sigma\sqrt{\tfrac{\nu-2}{\nu}}\;q,\\ \operatorname{ES}_u=-\mu+\sigma\sqrt{\tfrac{\nu-2}{\nu}}\;\frac{(\nu+q^2)\,f_Y(q)}{(\nu-1)\,u}.\end{gathered} \tag{32.4}$$

The VaR is (15.11) at $Y$'s quantile, and the ES needs the tail integral of $y\,f_Y(y)$ (Deep Dive). SciPy's `t.fit` reports $\sigma\sqrt{(\nu-2)/\nu}$ as its scale.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: the Student-t tail mean
> With (15.9)'s density, $(\nu+y^2)f_Y(y)$ is a constant times $(1+y^2/\nu)^{-(\nu-1)/2}$. Differentiating gives
> $$\frac{d}{dy}\big[(\nu+y^2)f_Y(y)\big]=-(\nu-1)\,y\,f_Y(y).$$
> For $\nu>1$ the bracket vanishes as $y\to-\infty$, and
> $$\int_{-\infty}^{q}y\,f_Y(y)\,dy=-\frac{(\nu+q^2)\,f_Y(q)}{\nu-1}.$$
> Substituting the integral into
> $$\operatorname{ES}_u=-\mu-\sigma\sqrt{\tfrac{\nu-2}{\nu}}\;\frac1u\int_{-\infty}^qy\,f_Y(y)\,dy$$
> gives (32.4). On TR23's fit SciPy's `quad` agrees to better than $10^{-12}$ (Section 9).

The normal misses both. The $t$ nearly matches the VaR but overshoots the ES, "too heavy from −5%" ([[LM15]]).

VaR needs no moment, ES a first ($\nu>1$, or $\alpha>1$ for a power tail), and the normal fit a variance. TR23's $t$, $\nu=3.065$, has no fourth moment, so no standard error built on kurtosis applies ([[LM5]]'s warning).

[[LM20]]'s Hill estimate (20.10) (Hill 1975) fits a power tail beyond $x_0$,

$$\Pr(-R>x)\approx\frac kT\Big(\frac x{x_0}\Big)^{-\alpha},\qquad x\ge x_0,$$

whose density is proportional to $x^{-\alpha-1}$. Setting the fitted tail equal to $u$ gives the VaR, and for $\hat\alpha>1$ Section 2's tail mean gives the ES:

$$\begin{gathered}\operatorname{VaR}_u=x_0\Big(\frac{k}{uT}\Big)^{1/\hat\alpha},\qquad u\le\frac kT,\\ \operatorname{ES}_u=\frac{\int_{\operatorname{VaR}_u}^{\infty}x^{-\hat\alpha}\,dx}{\int_{\operatorname{VaR}_u}^{\infty}x^{-\hat\alpha-1}\,dx}=\frac{\hat\alpha}{\hat\alpha-1}\operatorname{VaR}_u.\end{gathered}$$

At $x_0=3\%$, $k=108$ and $\hat\alpha=3.63$ the 1% figures are 3.55% and 4.90% (Problem 11).

**The horizon.** The $\sqrt m$ rule multiplies a one-day VaR by $\sqrt m$. It is exact only for a log return over $m$ iid normal days with zero mean, whose sum (19.7) gives $m$ times the daily variance. A non-normal law changes the quantile's scaling, a mean adds $m$ times, and compounding to $e^r-1$ bends the result.

> [!zh] 厚尾與時間尺度
> 常態分布（normal distribution）低估 TR23 的尾部風險（tail risk）：1% 的風險值（value at risk）少了 0.82 個百分點，預期短缺（expected shortfall）少了 1.53 個百分點。Student t 分布有厚尾（heavy tails），風險值接近資料，預期短缺卻偏高。風險值不需要任何動差（moment），預期短缺只需要一階動差存在，也就是自由度（degrees of freedom）或尾部指數（tail index）大於 1。所以即使尾部很厚，只要這個條件成立，預期短缺仍有定義。把單日風險值乘上 $\sqrt m$ 的 $\sqrt m$ 法則（$\sqrt m$ rule），只在每日對數報酬（log return）獨立同分布（iid），且服從平均為零的常態分布時才精確。

## How Precise Is a Value at Risk? {lo=3}

A historical VaR is one order statistic, so it moves from sample to sample. Its precision needs no model for $F_R$.

> [!proposition] A distribution-free interval for a quantile
> Let $T$ returns be iid with a continuous distribution function $F_R$, let $X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(T,u)$, and let $j_1<j_2$ be ranks. Then
> $$\begin{gathered}\Pr\big(R_{(j_1)}\le F_R^{-1}(u)<R_{(j_2)}\big)\\=\Pr(j_1\le X\le j_2-1).\end{gathered} \tag{32.5}$$

*Proof.* By continuity each return lies at or below $F_R^{-1}(u)$ with probability $u$, independently, so the count of such returns has $X$'s law. And $R_{(j)}$ is at or below $F_R^{-1}(u)$ exactly when at least $j$ returns are. So the left event is $\{X\ge j_1\}\cap\{X\le j_2-1\}$. $\blacksquare$

The equal-tail 95% interval takes $j_1$ the largest rank with $\Pr(X\le j_1-1)\le0.025$ and $j_2$ the smallest with $\Pr(X\le j_2-1)\ge0.975$, so its coverage is at least 95%. For large $T$,

$$\operatorname{SE}\big(R_{(\lceil uT\rceil)}\big)\approx\frac{\sqrt{u(1-u)/T}}{f_R\big(F_R^{-1}(u)\big)}, \tag{32.6}$$

for iid returns with a density $f_R$ positive and continuous at the quantile (stated without proof; Glasserman 2003, (9.7)). The interval's ranks estimate the density by their spacing, $(j_2-j_1)/\big(T(R_{(j_2)}-R_{(j_1)})\big)$.

> [!pitfall] A 99% VaR from 250 returns
> Such a VaR is the third worst return, and nothing in the data bounds the true quantile from below. Report it with its interval, or use a longer sample or a model.

## Coherence and Expected Shortfall {lo=4,8}

A risk measure should not punish diversification. Artzner, Delbaen, Eber and Heath (1999) wrote this down as four axioms.

> [!definition] A coherent risk measure
> A risk measure $\mathcal R(\cdot)$ gives a number to a position's profit and loss, written here as a return on one unit of capital. It is **coherent** if, for all positions $R_1$ and $R_2$,
> 1. translation invariance: $\mathcal R(R_1+\zeta)=\mathcal R(R_1)-\zeta$ for a sure return $\zeta$;
> 2. subadditivity: $\mathcal R(R_1+R_2)\le\mathcal R(R_1)+\mathcal R(R_2)$;
> 3. positive homogeneity: $\mathcal R(\eta R_1)=\eta\,\mathcal R(R_1)$ for every $\eta\ge0$;
> 4. monotonicity: $\mathcal R(R_2)\le\mathcal R(R_1)$ when $R_1\le R_2$ in every outcome.

VaR meets axioms 1, 3 and 4: a quantile moves with a sure return, scales with a positive multiple and keeps the order of outcomes. For jointly normal returns and $u<\tfrac12$ it is also subadditive. By (16.7) the sum is normal, and by (16.4) its standard deviation is at most $\sigma_1+\sigma_2$, as [[LM10]] shows for a long-only book. Since $-\Phi^{-1}(u)>0$, (32.3) then gives subadditivity, which can fail without normality.

> [!example] Two bonds {#bonds}
> Two independent bonds each lose 60% with probability 0.8% and gain 2% otherwise (*constructed*). Compare one bond with half in each at $u=1\%$.
>
> > [!solution]
> > Default is rarer than 1%, so one bond's 1% quantile is its gain: VaR $-2.00\%$. By (32.1) its ES is $-\big[0.008\times(-60\%)+0.002\times2\%\big]/0.01=47.60\%$.
> >
> > With half in each, some default has probability $1-0.992^2=1.59\%$, above 1%. So the quantile is one default, $(-60\%+2\%)/2$: VaR 29.00%, against the halves' $-2.00\%$ summed. The ES is 29.20%, below the halves' 47.60%. Diversifying raised VaR and lowered ES.

ES is coherent (Acerbi and Tasche 2002a; stated without proof). It is also a convex minimum, which makes it easy to optimize.

> [!theorem] Expected shortfall as a minimum (Rockafellar and Uryasev 2000)
> Let $R$ have a continuous distribution function and $\E\lvert R\rvert<\infty$. Then
> $$\operatorname{ES}_u=\min_\theta\Big\{\theta+\frac1u\E\big[(-R-\theta)^+\big]\Big\}, \tag{32.7}$$
> attained at $\theta=\operatorname{VaR}_u$. The function minimized is convex in $\theta$, and jointly convex in $\theta$ and the weights when $R=\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf R_t$.

*Proof.* Each $(-\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf R_t-\theta)^+$ is the larger of 0 and an affine function, so it is convex by (29.1), and so is a mean of such terms. The minimum is the Deep Dive's. $\blacksquare$

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: why the minimum is the expected shortfall
> For continuous $F_R$ the derivative in $\theta$ is $1-\Pr(-R>\theta)/u$, which rises with $\theta$ and is zero at $\theta=\operatorname{VaR}_u$. There the value is $\operatorname{VaR}_u$ plus $\E\big[(-R-\operatorname{VaR}_u)^+\big]/u$, which equals $\E\big[-R\,\mathbf 1\{-R>\operatorname{VaR}_u\}\big]/u$, so it is $\operatorname{ES}_u$ (Theorem 1 of Rockafellar and Uryasev's 1999 working paper).
>
> Under $\hat F$ the function is piecewise linear, with kinks at the losses $-R_t$. Its slope $1-\#\{t:-R_t>\theta\}/(uT)$ changes sign at the loss of rank $j$, counted from the largest. When $uT$ is whole it is zero between ranks $j+1$ and $j$. At rank $j$ the value is (32.2): on TR23, 4.689% at 3.567%.

So minimizing ES over long-only weights is a convex problem of [[LM29]]'s (29.3), and under $\hat F$ a linear program (Problem 12).

Basel's 2019 market-risk standard uses ES at 97.5% (BCBS 2019, MAR33.3), where the 1996 rules used a 99% VaR (BCBS 1996b, B.4(b)). Under a normal law with zero mean, $\operatorname{ES}_{0.025}=2.338\sigma$ and $\operatorname{VaR}_{0.01}=2.326\sigma$.

> [!zh] 一致性：分散投資不該增加風險
> 一致性風險量數（coherent risk measure）要滿足四個公理（axiom），其中劣可加性（subadditivity）要求合併兩個部位的風險不超過各自風險之和。風險值（value at risk）不一定滿足：兩檔獨立債券各持一半，至少一檔違約的機率超過 1%，風險值反而從兩半合計的 −2.00% 升到 29.00%。預期短缺（expected shortfall）滿足劣可加性，在這個例子裡從 47.60% 降到 29.20%；它也叫條件風險值（conditional value at risk，CVaR）。預期短缺還能寫成凸函數（convex function）的最小值 (32.7)，所以用歷史報酬的經驗分布（empirical distribution）時，最小化投資組合（portfolio）的預期短缺是線性規劃（linear programming）問題。

> [!kc]
> With the normal values above, why could Basel switch measures without changing a normal book's measured risk much, and why does the switch matter on TR23? Which axiom does [[eg:bonds]]'s VaR break, and does ES keep it there?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > The ratio $2.338/2.326$ is 1.005, so a normal book's measured risk barely moves. TR23's heavier tail makes ES at 2.5% over VaR at 1% 1.05 in the data and 1.11 under the $t$ ([[ex:models]]). The bonds' VaR breaks subadditivity, 29.00% against −2.00%. ES keeps it, 29.20% against 47.60%.

## Value at Risk from Volatility Forecasts {lo=5}

[[LM26]]'s models forecast tomorrow's variance of the log return. A quantile passes through any increasing map, and $R=e^r-1$ is one.

> [!result] Filtered VaR
> Let tomorrow's log return, in decimals, be $r=g+\sigma_{t+1\mid t}\,\xi_{t+1}$, with $\xi$ iid of mean 0, variance 1 and quantile function $F_\xi^{-1}$. Then
> $$\operatorname{VaR}_u=1-\exp\big(g+\sigma_{t+1\mid t}\,F_\xi^{-1}(u)\big), \tag{32.8}$$
> with $F_\xi^{-1}(u)=\Phi^{-1}(u)$ for normal innovations and $\sqrt{(\nu-2)/\nu}\,F_Y^{-1}(u)$ for [[LM26]]'s scaled $t$.

A mean does not pass through the map. The ES of $R$ is

$$\operatorname{ES}_u=1-\frac1u\E\big[e^r\,\mathbf 1\{r\le F_r^{-1}(u)\}\big].$$

For normal $\xi$, completing the square gives the expectation as $e^{g+\sigma^2/2}\,\Phi(z-\sigma)$, with $z=\Phi^{-1}(u)$ as in Section 3 and $\sigma=\sigma_{t+1\mid t}$. For the $t$, a quadrature gives it. EWMA (26.2), with RiskMetrics' $\lambda=0.94$, has $g=0$ and normal innovations. GARCH (26.3) uses its refit's constant mean.

> [!example] The forecasts for 7 April {#filtered}
> On 2 April the forecasts $\sigma_{t+1\mid t}$ were 1.5605% (EWMA) and 1.7222% (GARCH-n), as [[LM26]] prints them, and 1.7040% (GARCH-t) a day. The GARCH refits' means were 0.1020% and 0.1190%, and the $t$'s $\nu=6.39$. Find VaR, ES and how rare the loss was.
>
> > [!solution]
> > By (32.8) the VaRs and ESs are [[ex:case]]'s EWMA and GARCH rows. The log return, −10.20%, lies $-5.98$ forecast SDs from GARCH-n's mean ([[LM26]]'s 5.9 divides the return itself by 1.7222). Its probability is $1.1\times10^{-9}$, once in 3.7 million years of 246.12 sessions.
> >
> > Under GARCH-t the standardized loss is $-6.06$, probability $1.2\times10^{-4}$: once in 8,014 sessions, 32.6 years. Both come from the unrounded fits and probabilities. Both are **model** statements: the law's tail decides.

Over ten days the GARCH variance reverts, (26.4), and is itself random, so a simulation runs each path through (26.3).

> [!example] Ten days under GARCH-t {#mc10}
> Simulate ten sessions from 2 April under the refit of 1 April (experiment 1, `default_rng([1, 20261011])`, 100,000 paths; each step draws all paths' $t$ innovations at once, scaled to variance 1). Compare the ten-day 99% VaR with the $\sqrt{10}$ rule.
>
> > [!solution]
> > | Ten-day 99% VaR from 2 April | % | Batch SE |
> > |:--|--:|--:|
> > | GARCH-t, simulated | 10.5 | 0.07 |
> > | Constant $\sigma_{t+1\mid t}=1.7040\%$ | 10.9 | 0.05 |
> > | (26.4)'s path toward the fit's unconditional 1.116% a day | 9.9 | 0.06 |
> > | The $\sqrt{10}$ rule, $\sqrt{10}\times4.142\%$ | 13.10 | |

The constant $\sigma_{t+1\mid t}$ gives 2.2 points less than 13.10%: the rule scales the whole one-day loss, while ten days add the mean ten times and average their innovations. This run does not separate the two. Mean reversion (persistence 0.922) takes one point more, and the variance's random feedback restores 0.6 (batch SE of the difference 0.04).

A portfolio's Monte Carlo VaR colours normal draws with [[LM12]]'s Cholesky factor (12.8), by (16.7) (the lab). [[LM18]]'s antithetic and control variates, (18.5) and (18.6), sharpen such estimates, and importance sampling (18.7) estimates probabilities deep in the tail (Problem 10). A count misses timing. [[LM21]]'s exact count tests independence: of the exceedance days with a next forecast day, count those followed by another. Under independence that count is $\operatorname{Bin}(\text{trials},x/n)$, and its upper tail gives the p-value. HS's 9 of 56, against 0.68 expected, give $2.6\times10^{-8}$ ([[ex:clusters]]).

Kupiec (1995; §IV of its Federal Reserve working paper) tests the same count with a likelihood ratio, two-sided and asymptotically $\chi^2_1$. Christoffersen (1998; §3 of the author's manuscript) adds a ratio for independence, against exceedances whose chance depends on the day before (a first-order Markov chain), and a joint one ($\chi^2_2$). The lab computes all three beside the exact tests.

One permutation test per rule makes eight tests, and none is significant at the 5% level: the smallest share at or above the realized MDD is R2's, 557 of 10,000. For Brownian motion, the continuous-time limit of a random walk ([[LM36]]), the expected maximum drawdown has a closed form, stated here without proof and with its model.

> [!result] The expected maximum drawdown of a Brownian motion (Magdon-Ismail et al. 2004)
> If $\ln W_t$ is a Brownian motion with zero drift and volatility $\sigma$ a year, the expected maximum drop of $\ln W$ over $\tau$ years is
> $$\sqrt{\pi/2}\;\sigma\sqrt\tau. \tag{32.10}$$

A hypothetical $\sigma=20\%$ over ten years gives 0.793. Then $1-e^{-0.7927}=54.7\%$ is the fall whose log equals that mean. By Jensen's inequality (14.10), the mean percentage fall is lower.

The rules' Sharpe ratios are [[LM33]]'s, their net results [[LM34]]'s and their scenario losses [[LM35]]'s.

> [!zh] 回撤看路徑，不只看單日
> 回撤（drawdown）是財富從歷史高點下跌的幅度，最大回撤（maximum drawdown）取其最大值；從期初資本算起的損失（loss from initial wealth）$\mathrm{DD}^0_t$ 是另一個問題。R2 的最大回撤是 22.97%，從期初算起最多只虧 0.39%。回撤取決於報酬的先後順序，必須看整條財富路徑。排列檢定（permutation test）打亂順序、保留報酬本身；區塊自助重抽法（block bootstrap）保留短期的連續日子。八條規則都沒有在 5% 水準下顯示順序異常。

## Risk Measures in Python {lo=2}

```python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np
from scipy import integrate, stats

def var_es(R, u):                         # (32.2): historical VaR and ES
    x = np.sort(R)
    T = len(x)
    j = int(np.ceil(u * T - 1e-12))       # j = ceil(uT), with a guard
    assert x[j - 1] == np.quantile(R, u, method="inverted_cdf")
    tail = x[: j - 1].sum() / T + x[j - 1] * (u - (j - 1) / T)
    return -x[j - 1], -tail / u

def es_t(R, u):                           # (32.4) on an ML fit, in percent
    nu, loc, sc = stats.t.fit(100 * R)
    q = stats.t.ppf(u, nu)
    es = -loc + sc * (nu + q * q) * stats.t.pdf(q, nu) / ((nu - 1) * u)
    tail = integrate.quad(lambda y: y * stats.t.pdf(y, nu), -np.inf, q,
                          epsabs=1e-14, epsrel=1e-13)[0]
    assert abs(-loc - sc * tail / u - es) < 1e-12     # the Deep Dive
    return es

def min_es(Rw, u):                        # (32.7) as a linear program
    T, N = Rw.shape                       # Rw: T x N returns in percent
    w, th, s = cp.Variable(N), cp.Variable(), cp.Variable(T)
    cons = [s >= 0, s + Rw @ w + th >= 0, w >= 0, cp.sum(w) == 1]
    prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(th + cp.sum(s) / (u * T)), cons)
    prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, tol_gap_abs=1e-12,
               tol_gap_rel=1e-12, tol_feas=1e-12)
    assert prob.status == "optimal" and w.value is not None
    return w.value, prob.value
```

In `min_es` two linear constraints on each slack `s[t]` replace $(-\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf R_t-\theta)^+$, which makes (32.7) a linear program. The lab refits the forecasts with [[LM26]]'s `arch` code.

## Summary {-}

- VaR is minus a quantile and ES the mean loss beyond it, (32.1), estimated historically by (32.2).
- Normal and Student-t forms are (32.3) and (32.4). VaR needs no moment, ES a first.
- The $\sqrt m$ rule needs iid normal log returns with zero mean. On TR23 it overstates against ten iid days and under GARCH-t.
- A quantile's interval (32.5) needs iid returns but no model for $F_R$. From 250 returns a 99% VaR has no distribution-free 95% lower bound.
- VaR can fail subadditivity. ES is coherent and a convex minimum, (32.7).
- Filtered VaR (32.8) turns a variance forecast into a quantile. A backtest tests count and timing.
- Drawdowns (32.9) depend on the order of days. The rules' MDDs run from 18.42% to 33.56%.
