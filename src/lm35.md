## Introduction

[[LM32]] measured losses as statistics of a return distribution: value at risk, expected shortfall and drawdowns. This module asks what named and constructed shocks do to the same books, without a probability. Each shock is one more row of [[LM8]]'s P&L matrix (8.13).

> [!case] Five days, one session
> After its close on 2 April 2025, Taiwan's market did not trade again until 7 April. That session the TAIEX total-return index (TR23) fell 9.70%, its worst day since 2003 ([[LM26]]). Its five sessions to 9 April lost 15.96%, second only to March 2020's 16.51%. 0050 traded at one price all day, 158.40, exactly 10% below its last close: 29.6 million units, none lower.
>
> 00646 and 00642U have no daily limit, and they fell 12.59% and 14.62% ([[ex:case]]). The through case's eight rules lost from 5.06% (R7) to 8.33% (R4). R8 had raised its exposure to 1.319 at its trade of 3 March, and it lost 7.94%. A beta model fitted on 2,362 days, that day included, gives R1 3.62%, under half the actual 7.70%.
>
> What did each rule lose in the four other scenarios? Which shock, not seen yet, would cost a rule a tenth of its wealth? And what would a book of 0050 levered five times have lost by the next open?

## Scenarios from History {lo=1,6}

A **scenario** fixes a return for every fund, the vector $\mathbf R^{\mathrm{sc}}$, and a book with exposures $\mathbf f$ and wealth $W_0$ gains $W_0\,\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf R^{\mathrm{sc}}$ ([[LM8]]'s (8.4)). A **historical scenario** takes it from a past window, and a **hypothetical scenario** builds it.

> [!definition] Scenario window
> On TR23 the **trough** is the lowest close of the named year (2008, 2015, 2020) or of the 20 sessions from the named day (2024, 2025). The **peak** is the highest close after the previous scenario's trough, or after TR23's first close for 2008, and before this trough. The **worst day** is the window's worst return. The **worst $m$ sessions** are the worst compound return over $m$ consecutive sessions ending in (peak, trough].

A span of $m$ sessions is dated from the close before its first return, and it may start before the peak. [[ex:windows]] gives the five windows.

> [!zh] 壓力測試：先把情境的日期定清楚
> 壓力測試（stress testing）問的是「如果某個情境發生，這個投資組合會虧多少」，不問這個情境發生的機率。情境分析（scenario analysis）把一個情境寫成每檔基金的報酬向量，損失就是曝險（exposure）乘上報酬再加總。歷史情境（historical scenario）取自過去的崩跌，但要先用規則定好情境區間（scenario window）：高點、低點、最差的一天、最差的連續 5 天與 20 天，規則寫死之後才算數字。區間的高點不一定是 [[LM1]] 的回撤（drawdown）所用的歷史高點：2024 年 8 月與 2025 年 4 月共用同一個歷史高點，靠區間規則才分成兩個情境。報告「一天」的損失時，也要寫出它跨了幾個日曆日。

## Replaying the Rules {lo=1}

A **frozen replay** holds the book of the window's first close fixed. Each fund's scenario return is its compound return over the window, and the loss splits exactly by fund:

$$\begin{gathered}\Delta W=W_0\,\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf R^{\mathrm{sc}}=W_0\sum_if_iR^{\mathrm{sc}}_i,\\ R^{\mathrm{sc}}_i=\prod_t(1+R_{i,t})-1.\end{gathered} \tag{35.1}$$

Here $f_iR^{\mathrm{sc}}_i$ is fund $i$'s **attribution**, and interest is excluded. A **named-day replay** applies the book of the previous close to one day's returns, on which no rule trades. A rule's **actual path** is its wealth ratio from peak to trough, trades and costs included. R1 to R7 hold no cash, so on 7 April 2025 their wealth fell by exactly $\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf R$ ([[ex:replay]], [[ex:attrib]]).

## Funds That Did Not Exist {lo=2}

In 2008 none of E5's foreign funds existed, and in the 2015 window only 00635U. A hypothetical replay keeps 0050's own returns and puts SPY, TLT, GLD and USO for 00646, 00679B, 00635U and 00642U, with DBO as a second oil proxy. Their adjusted closes are [[LM34]]'s $P^{\mathrm{adj}}_{t\mid t'}$, with $t'$ the latest session FinMind held on 24 October 2026.

00646's and 00679B's 13:30 closes follow the previous New York session, and futures-trust ETFs are valued on European and US futures ([[LM27]]). The pairing rule gives a US close the first USD/TWD row dated after it, and a Taiwan date $t$ the last US session before it, $t^{\mathrm{US}}$:

$$P^{\mathrm{TWD}}_t=P^{\mathrm{adj}}_{t^{\mathrm{US}}\mid t'}\,\mathrm{FX}_{t^{\mathrm{US}}},\qquad R_t=\frac{P^{\mathrm{TWD}}_t}{P^{\mathrm{TWD}}_{t-1}}-1. \tag{35.5}$$

A Taiwan date's return thus compounds every US session since the previous Taiwan date.

## Hypothetical Shocks {lo=3}

A **factor shock** $x$ to TR23 moves fund $i$ by $\beta_ix$, where the **beta** $\beta_i$ is its OLS slope on TR23, [[LM23]]'s (23.1). The betas are in-sample and printed without standard errors: a factor shock is a construction, and its error on a crash day is the model's.

A **partial scenario** fixes one fund, $R_i=x$, and sets each other fund at its mean given that value. For a normal vector, [[LM16]]'s (16.8) gives, fund by fund,

$$\E[R_j\mid R_i=x]=\mu_j+\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{\sigma_{ii}}\,(x-\mu_i). \tag{35.3}$$

For other laws the right side of (35.3) is the best linear predictor (Deep Dive), here with $\bar{\mathbf R}$ and $\hat\Sigma$.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: conditional means without normality
> Let $R_i$, $R_j$ have finite variances. The $\eta$ and $\zeta$ that minimize $\E[(R_j-\eta R_i-\zeta)^2]$ are $\eta=\sigma_{ij}/\sigma_{ii}$ and $\zeta=\mu_j-\eta\mu_i$, the population form of [[LM23]]'s OLS. So the right side of (35.3) is the best linear predictor of $R_j$ for any such law, and normality makes it the conditional mean.
>
> For an elliptical law whose density falls with $d$, these values give the most plausible scenario that fixes $R_i$ (Breuer et al. 2009, §2.2, p. 210, Proposition 1). They compare Kupiec's (1998) options, as they describe them.

> [!example] The beta model on 7 April 2025 {#factor}
> Apply the betas to TR23's −9.70% of 7 April 2025, and compare the partial scenario with 0050 at −10%.
>
> > [!solution]
> > On the 2,362 joint dates 0050's beta is 1.048 (R-squared 0.924) and 00646's 0.527 (0.363). Times −9.70%, the betas give [[ex:case]]'s last column: 00642U −1.92% against −14.62% actual, and R1 −3.62% against −7.70%. With 0050 at −10%, (35.3) moves 00646 by −4.79%, 00679B +0.71%, 00635U −1.46% and 00642U −1.69%.
> >
> > Both shocks are averages of ordinary days. By [[LM27]]'s split (27.8), almost all of 00642U's variance is residual (R-squared under 1%), which a factor shock leaves at its mean. On the current books a shock of −10% to TR23 costs R1 3.77% and R4 7.28%, from the unrounded betas and books.

A **correlation break** changes one correlation of $C$ and keeps the rest. The new $C$ needs $\lambda_N\ge0$, or no return vector has it ([[LM16]]'s (16.4)).

> [!example] A correlation break {#break}
> Raise the correlation of 0050 and 00679B from −0.105 ([[LM27]]) to a constructed +0.30, and redo the partial scenario.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The changed $C$ has $\lambda_N=0.298$, so it stays positive definite. 00679B's conditional move turns from +0.71% to −2.04%. On the current books R3's loss rises from 1.41% to 2.95%, and R7's from 1.40% to 3.00%.
> >
> > 2022 is a real case, an illustration outside the five scenarios, from 17 January to 25 October 2022:
> >
> > | 2022, % | TR23 | 0050 | 00679B | R2, frozen | R4, frozen |
> > |:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
> > | Return | −28.47 | −33.93 | −22.15 | −22.37 | −29.86 |
> >
> > Yet 0050 and 00679B correlated −0.138 daily and +0.019 over 38 ISO weeks, against −0.022 over the full sample's 498 ([[LM27]]). A correlation measures moves around each mean, so two funds can fall together uncorrelated.
> >
> > On the 48 days when 0050 fell two standard deviations below its mean or more, it correlated 0.540 with 00646, against 0.599 overall and 0.669 by [[LM16]]'s (16.10). A one-sided tail narrows a normal spread, but 0050's variance on those days (divisor 48) is 1.449 times the full sample's. So (16.10) lifts the correlation, and the data lie 1.3 normal-theory standard errors (about 0.10) below it: a direction, not a proven difference.

> [!zh] 假設情境：因子衝擊低估了崩盤日
> 因子模型（factor model）把基金報酬寫成 beta 乘上市場報酬，再加上與市場無關的殘差（residual）。因子衝擊（factor shock）只移動市場、殘差停在平均數（mean），所以 2025 年 4 月 7 日它預測 R1 虧 3.62%，實際卻虧 7.70%：00642U 的 R-squared 不到 1%，它的大跌幾乎全在殘差裡。部分情境（partial scenario）用 (35.3)：固定一檔基金的報酬，其他基金取對它做迴歸（regression）的預測值；報酬為常態時，這個值就是條件期望值（conditional expectation）。改動一個相關係數（correlation coefficient）之後，要先檢查相關矩陣（correlation matrix）仍是半正定（positive semidefinite），否則它不是任何報酬的相關矩陣。

## Reverse Stress Testing {lo=4,7}

A **reverse stress test** asks for the least unusual shock that produces a given loss. [[LM16]]'s squared Mahalanobis distance measures how unusual a shock $\mathbf x$ is,

$$d^2=(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu),$$

and the test minimizes it subject to $\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf x=-\ell$.

> [!theorem] The reverse stress test
> Let $\Sigma$ be positive definite and $\mathbf f\ne\mathbf 0$, with $\sigma_p^2=\mathbf f^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf f$. Suppose that the loss lies beyond the book's mean, $\ell+\mathbf f^{\top}\boldsymbol\mu>0$. Then $d^2$ has exactly one minimizer on the hyperplane $\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf x=-\ell$, and the same one on the half-space $\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf x\le-\ell$:
> $$\begin{gathered}\mathbf R^{\mathrm{sc}*}=\boldsymbol\mu-(\ell+\mathbf f^{\top}\boldsymbol\mu)\,\frac{\Sigma\mathbf f}{\sigma_p^2},\\ d^*=\frac{\ell+\mathbf f^{\top}\boldsymbol\mu}{\sigma_p}.\end{gathered} \tag{35.4}$$
> Within $d\le k$ the largest loss is $k\sigma_p-\mathbf f^{\top}\boldsymbol\mu$.

*Proof.* With $\mathbf z=\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu$ and $b=-(\ell+\mathbf f^{\top}\boldsymbol\mu)<0$, minimize $\mathbf z^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf z$ subject to $\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf z=b$. [[LM28]]'s (28.1), with $\Sigma^{-1}$ for $\Sigma$ and the single row $K=\mathbf f^{\top}$, gives $\mathbf z^*=b\,\Sigma\mathbf f/\sigma_p^2$ and $d^{*2}=b^2/\sigma_p^2$. On the half-space, scaling $\mathbf z$ by $b/\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf z\in(0,1]$ reaches the hyperplane without raising $d^2$. For the bound, Cauchy–Schwarz in the inner product $\mathbf u^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf v$ gives $-\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf z\le d\,\sigma_p$, with equality at $\mathbf z=-k\Sigma\mathbf f/\sigma_p$. $\blacksquare$

The bound is Breuer et al.'s worst loss over the ellipsoid $d\le k$ (2009, §2.3, p. 211), who measure plausibility by $d$ (§2.1, p. 209). This book ranks shocks by $d$ without a probability, since [[LM16]]'s law of $d^2$ behind (16.9) holds only for normal returns. The shocks are plug-in estimates with an unmeasured sampling error.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: the reverse stress test with a price limit
> The bounded test minimizes $d^2$ over $\{\mathbf x:\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf x\le-\ell,\ x_{0050}\ge-10\%\}$, a non-empty polyhedron because every book holds a fund besides 0050. The objective is strictly convex and grows without bound, so exactly one minimizer exists. With linear constraints, [[LM29]]'s conditions (29.4) are necessary and sufficient.
>
> With both constraints active, $K\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ for the rows $\mathbf f^{\top}$ and $(1,0,0,0,0)$ of $K$ and $\mathbf b=(-\ell,-10\%)^{\top}$. This $K$ has full row rank, since $\mathbf f$ is no multiple of $(1,0,0,0,0)$. Then (28.1), with $\hat\Sigma^{-1}$ for $\Sigma$ and the shock measured from $\bar{\mathbf R}$, gives
> $$\mathbf x^*=\bar{\mathbf R}+\hat\Sigma K^{\top}\big(K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}\big)^{-1}\big(\mathbf b-K\bar{\mathbf R}\big).$$
> By (29.4) this point is the answer when both multipliers $\nu_1$, $\nu_2$ are non-negative: for R1 they are 37.3 and 0.8877 in percent units. For R8 the limit is inactive, and (35.4) holds. > [!pitfall] The closest shock can be impossible in one day
> R1's shock puts 0050 at −10.47%, beyond its daily limit. Bound the shock by the limit, and compare each entry with the fund's worst day.

```python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np

# Rbar, S: mean and covariance of the 2,362 joint dates (divisor T), in %;
# f: a book's exposures, 0050 first; loss = 10 (%)
sp2 = f @ S @ f
x_closed = Rbar - (loss + f @ Rbar) * (S @ f) / sp2      # (35.4)
d_closed = (loss + f @ Rbar) / np.sqrt(sp2)

Si = np.linalg.inv(S)
Si = (Si + Si.T) / 2                                     # exactly symmetric
x = cp.Variable(len(f))
cons = [f @ x <= -loss, x[0] >= -10.0]                   # 0050's limit
d2 = cp.quad_form(x - Rbar, cp.psd_wrap(Si))
prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(d2), cons)
prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, tol_gap_abs=1e-12, tol_gap_rel=1e-12,
           tol_feas=1e-12)
assert prob.status == "optimal" and x.value is not None
nu = [float(c.dual_value) for c in cons]                 # both >= 0
grad = 2 * Si @ (x.value - Rbar) + nu[0] * f - nu[1] * np.eye(len(f))[0]
print(np.sqrt(d2.value), np.abs(grad).max())             # KKT residual
```

> [!zh] 反向壓力測試：從損失倒推最可能的衝擊
> 一般的壓力測試（stress testing）先給情境再算損失；反向壓力測試（reverse stress test）先定損失，例如一天虧 10%，再從所有造成這個損失的衝擊（shock）中找出最不離譜的一個。「離譜」用 Mahalanobis 距離（Mahalanobis distance）衡量，它同時考慮各基金的波動與相關，大漲和大跌一樣算不尋常。答案有公式解（closed form）(35.4)：距離等於損失加上平均報酬（mean return），再除以投資組合（portfolio）的標準差（standard deviation）。最近的衝擊可能讓 0050 跌超過 10% 的漲跌幅限制（price limit），加上限制後用 cvxpy 求解，再以 KKT 條件（KKT conditions）檢查。距離只用來排序，不是機率。

## Leverage, Ruin and Margin {lo=5,8}

A unit book with scenario return $R^{\mathrm{sc}}_p<0$, scaled to total exposure $L$ with holdings fixed, ends at $1+LR^{\mathrm{sc}}_p$ times its wealth. So it is **ruined**, equity at or below zero, when

$$\begin{gathered}1+LR^{\mathrm{sc}}_p\le0,\\ \text{that is}\quad L\ge L_{\mathrm{ruin}}=-\frac{1}{R^{\mathrm{sc}}_p}.\end{gathered} \tag{35.2}$$

Over a window the test applies on every date, so $R^{\mathrm{sc}}_p$ is then the book's lowest value. R6's frozen books reach it at each window's last close. R3's and R5's 2024 books reach it on 26 July, at −1.40% and −1.47%, and end at +0.11% and −0.07%. Ruin is reported with its date, never floored ([[LM27]]).

[[ex:ruin]] draws (35.2) for R6's book in three replays and for 0050 to the open of 8 April 2025. The lines cross zero at 7.67, 9.90, 16.77 and 7.10. A book of 0050 levered five times lost 50.00% at the lock and 70.45% by the next open. R8's cap, 1.5, and its largest uncapped ratio, 2.14 ([[LM31]]), lie far to the left.

## What a Stress Test Cannot Say {lo=7,8}

A stress test assigns no probability: its replays are a few past paths, and its constructed shocks are chosen, not drawn. The Basel Committee asks for "severe but plausible" scenarios, and describes reverse stress tests as exploring what could make a bank fail (BCBS 2018, Principle 4, p. 6).

The same rules can fail in four ways ([[ex:failures]]). Weak growth shows in [[LM34]]'s net log growth, a drawdown limit meets [[LM32]]'s MDD or $\mathrm{DD}^0_t$ (32.9), and funding and ruin are this module's. Growth and drawdowns come from one realized path, while ruin stays far away in every replay.

> [!pitfall] Surviving the scenarios does not cover unseen losses
> A book that survives every replay has survived five past paths and a few constructions, not every loss. Filtered historical simulation, scenarios generated by empirical likelihood and stress tests of liquidity extend the method beyond this book.

## Summary {-}

- A scenario's P&L, $W_0\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf R^{\mathrm{sc}}$ (35.1), splits exactly by fund.
- Windows are fixed by rule, and a named day can span ten calendar days.
- Frozen, named-day and actual replays differ: R8's own trades raised its 2025 loss.
- Proxies pair a New York close with the next exchange rate, (35.5), and their error is part of the scenario.
- Factor shocks and partial scenarios (35.3) average ordinary days. A correlation break must stay positive semidefinite.
- The reverse stress test (35.4) finds the least unusual shock reaching a loss, ranked by a distance, not a probability.
- A lock moves a sale to the next open. Ruin starts at $L_{\mathrm{ruin}}=-1/R^{\mathrm{sc}}_p$ (35.2), and a margin call comes earlier.
- Weak growth, drawdowns, funding and ruin are four failures of the same rules, and surviving the replays does not cover unseen losses.

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Stress test | 壓力測試 | A book's loss under a named or constructed scenario, with no probability attached. |
| Scenario analysis | 情境分析 | The P&L $W_0\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf R^{\mathrm{sc}}$ of a book under a scenario's return vector, (35.1). |
| Historical scenario | 歷史情境 | A scenario taken from a past window of returns. |
| Hypothetical scenario | 假設情境 | A scenario not taken from the funds' own past: a replay on proxies, a factor shock, a partial scenario, a correlation break or a constructed day. |
| Scenario window | 情境區間 | A historical scenario's dates from its peak to its trough on TR23, fixed by Section 2's rule. |
| Frozen replay | — | A window's returns applied to the book of its first close, held fixed with no trades, (35.1). |
| Named-day replay | — | One day's returns applied to the book of the previous close. |
| Factor shock | 因子衝擊 | A shock to TR23 that moves each fund by its beta times the shock. |
| Partial scenario | 部分情境 | One fund's return fixed and the others set by (35.3). |
| Proxy | 代理資產 | A traded fund that stands in for one that did not exist yet, through (35.5). |
| Exchange rate | 匯率 | Here the USD/TWD mid rate paired with a New York close. |
| Factor model | 因子模型 | Returns as betas times a factor plus residuals; a factor shock moves only the factor. |
| Conditional expectation | 條件期望值 | The mean of one return given another's value; for a normal pair, (35.3). |
| Reverse stress test | 反向壓力測試 | The least unusual shock that reaches a given loss, (35.4). |
| Mahalanobis distance | Mahalanobis 距離 | $d=\sqrt{(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)}$ ([[LM16]]); a ranking of shocks here, not a probability. |
| Price limit | 漲跌幅限制 | A market's bound on a day's price change, set by that market's own rules. |
| Limit-down lock | 鎖跌停 | A session that trades only at the limit-down price. |
| Gap | 跳空 | A price change between one close and the next trade. |
| Overnight | 隔夜 | From one session's close to the next session's open. |
| Leverage | 槓桿 | Total exposure above 1. |
| Ruin | 權益歸零 | Wealth at or below 0, reached from $L_{\mathrm{ruin}}=-1/R^{\mathrm{sc}}_p$, (35.2). |
| Margin call | 保證金追繳 | A demand for funds when a futures account's equity falls to the maintenance margin. |
