## The Checkpoint: Eight Rules with Error Bars {lo=1}

This section repeats Volume IV's checkpoint. [[LM20]]'s (20.9) gives the variance of a Sharpe ratio estimated from $T$ iid returns:

$$\begin{gathered}T\Var\big(\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}\big)\approx\hat V_{\mathrm{SR}},\\ \hat V_{\mathrm{SR}}:=1-\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}\,\widehat{\operatorname{Skew}}+\tfrac14\big(\widehat{\operatorname{Kurt}}-1\big)\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}^2,\\ \operatorname{SE}\big(\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}\big)=\sqrt{\hat V_{\mathrm{SR}}/T}.\end{gathered}$$

Here $\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}=\bar x/s$ is per observation. The plug-in uses (19.3)'s sample skewness and raw kurtosis (divisor $T$), whose normal values are 0 and 3. [[LM20]] derived it by the delta method for iid returns with a finite fourth moment, and it holds for large $T$, here 1,851 daily returns or TR23's 5,849. The normal case gives Lo's IID formula (9), $\sqrt{(1+\mathrm{SR}^2/2)/T}$ (Lo 2002).

The data may lack (20.9)'s fourth moment. [[LM20]]'s Student-t fit to TR23 has 3.065 degrees of freedom, below 4, so its fourth moment is infinite. The rules' raw kurtosis of 10 to 23 has no standard error. (20.9)'s errors, and the tests built on them, may then be too small.

> [!example] R2 by hand {#r2hand}
> R2's daily excess returns have mean $\bar x=0.0564\%$ and $s=0.774\%$, skewness −0.112 and raw kurtosis 13.58. Find the standard error of its Sharpe ratio, a day and a year.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The daily ratio is $0.0564/0.774=0.0729$. By (20.9),
> > $$\begin{aligned}\hat V_{\mathrm{SR}}&=1+0.0729\times0.112\\&\quad+\tfrac14\times12.58\times0.0729^2\\&=1+0.0082+0.0167=1.0249.\end{aligned}$$
> > So the standard error is $\sqrt{1.0249/1{,}851}=0.0235$ a day, and $0.0235\times\sqrt{243.57}=0.367$ a year. The kurtosis term is about twice the skewness term. The ratio times $\sqrt T$, $0.0729\times\sqrt{1{,}851}=3.14$, is the t-stat of the mean excess return with divisor $T$, by [[LM21]]'s identity.

> [!pitfall] Excess kurtosis in a raw-kurtosis formula
> (20.9), PSR and MinTRL take raw kurtosis. `scipy.stats.kurtosis` returns the excess by default (Section 9). TR23's 8.27 in [[LM20]] is raw, its excess 5.27 in [[LM19]], while 0050's 8.12 in [[LM4]] is excess. Say which in every printed kurtosis.

> [!zh] 八條規則各自的誤差範圍
> 夏普比率（Sharpe ratio）是估計值（estimate），有它的標準誤（standard error）。(20.9) 假設每天的報酬彼此獨立、分布相同（iid），並把偏態（skewness）與峰態（kurtosis）放進標準誤：負偏態與厚尾（fat tail）都讓標準誤變大。公式裡的峰態以常態分布（normal distribution）為 3；`scipy` 預設傳回的是超額峰態（excess kurtosis），也就是峰態減 3，直接代入會低估標準誤。八條規則的標準誤都在 0.37 左右，R2 的 95% 信賴區間（confidence interval）涵蓋其他七條規則的估計值。所以光看估計值的排名，說不出哪條規則真的比較好。

## Dependent Days: the HAC Standard Error {lo=2}

The rules' daily excess returns are nearly uncorrelated: $\hat\rho_1$ by (19.5) runs from −0.054 (R1) to 0.002 (R4). Their squares are not. R1's squared deviations $(x_t-\bar x)^2$ have $\hat\rho_1=0.246$, and (20.9)'s kurtosis term rests on those squares.

Write the Sharpe ratio as $h(\bar x,s^2)=\bar x/s$, a function of two means: $\bar x$, and $s^2$, the mean of $(x_t-\bar x)^2$. The delta method (20.8) needs the covariance of the two means. With dependent days that covariance is the long-run covariance of [[LM23]]'s (23.7), divided by $T$.

> [!definition] The HAC standard error of a Sharpe ratio
> Let the per-date terms and the gradient of $h$ be
> $$\begin{gathered}\mathbf v_t=\big(x_t-\bar x,\ (x_t-\bar x)^2-s^2\big)^{\top},\\ \nabla h=\big(1/s,\ -\bar x/(2s^3)\big)^{\top}.\end{gathered}$$
> With (23.7)'s Bartlett estimate
> $$\begin{gathered}\hat\Psi=\hat\Gamma_0+\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\Big(1-\frac k{\ell+1}\Big)\big(\hat\Gamma_k+\hat\Gamma_k^{\top}\big),\\ \hat\Gamma_k=\frac1T\sum_{t=k+1}^T\mathbf v_t\mathbf v_{t-k}^{\top},\end{gathered}$$
> the standard error is
> $$\operatorname{SE}_{\mathrm{HAC}}\big(\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}\big)=\sqrt{\nabla h^{\top}\hat\Psi\,\nabla h/T}. \tag{33.1}$$
> The lag is the book's $\ell=\lfloor4(T/100)^{2/9}\rfloor$ ([[LM23]]): 7 for 1,851 returns, 9 for TR23's 5,849 and 3 for 90 months. No small-sample factor is applied.

(33.1) is stated without proof as a large-$T$ result. It needs stationary returns whose dependence fades (a mixing condition). It also needs a finite moment of order a little above 4 (Ledoit and Wolf 2008, p. 852, for the same delta method), which TR23 may lack (Section 2). It is Lo's (2002) generalized-method-of-moments standard error, his eqs. (14)–(15), with Newey and West's (1987) weights and the book's lag.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: why the HAC error at lag zero is (20.9)
> At $\ell=0$ the estimate is $\hat\Psi=\hat\Gamma_0$, the mean of $\mathbf v_t\mathbf v_t^{\top}$. By (19.3) its diagonal entries are $s^2$ and $s^4\widehat{\operatorname{Kurt}}-s^4$, and its off-diagonal entry is $s^3\widehat{\operatorname{Skew}}$. So
> $$\begin{aligned}\nabla h^{\top}\hat\Gamma_0\nabla h&=\frac{s^2}{s^2}-2\cdot\frac1s\cdot\frac{\bar x}{2s^3}\,s^3\widehat{\operatorname{Skew}}\\&\quad+\frac{\bar x^2}{4s^6}\,s^4\big(\widehat{\operatorname{Kurt}}-1\big),\end{aligned}$$
> which is $\hat V_{\mathrm{SR}}$ of (20.9) term by term.

## Annualizing Autocorrelated Returns {lo=3}

[[LM19]]'s (19.9) annualizes the Sharpe ratio of a sum of $m$ periods. With (19.7)'s ratio written out, it reads

$$\mathrm{SR}(m)=\frac{\sqrt m\,\mathrm{SR}}{\sqrt{1+2\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(1-k/m)\rho_k}}.$$

Lo (2002, eq. (20)) calls the factor that multiplies SR the scale factor. It equals $\sqrt m$ when every $\rho_k$ is 0, and more generally when the weighted sum in the denominator is 0. He derives it for sums of returns with compounding ignored, which fits monthly log returns. [[LM19]] estimated the denominator from (19.8)'s overlapping sums, while here it comes from $\hat\rho_1,\dots,\hat\rho_{11}$, whose noise (25.6) measures.

> [!example] Ninety months {#monthly}
> Annualize the rules' monthly Sharpe ratios by $\sqrt{12}$ and by (19.9). Does the correction matter?
>
> > [!solution]
> > The 90 calendar months run from April 2019 to September 2026, 12 to 23 sessions each. The 19 returns of March 2019 and the 6 of October 2026 are excluded. A month's log excess return is the log change of net wealth between month-ends minus its sessions times $\ln(1+r_f/A_{\mathrm w})$. Every log is defined, since $1+R_t>0$ on every date.
> >
> > R1's monthly ratio is 0.305, so $\sqrt{12}$ gives 1.06. Its ratio $1+2\sum_k(1-k/12)\hat\rho_k$ is 1.309, so (19.9) gives $0.305\times\sqrt{12/1.309}=0.92$. Over the eight rules the scale factors run from 2.74 to 3.33 against 3.46.
> >
> > | Annual Sharpe ratio | R1 | R2 | R4 |
> > |:--|--:|--:|--:|
> > | By $\sqrt{12}$ | 1.06 | 1.05 | 0.62 |
> > | By (19.9) | 0.92 | 0.94 | 0.49 |
> >
> > The correction is itself noisy. R1's ratio has a robust standard error of 0.38 by (25.6), and its excess over 1, 0.309, is 0.78 iid standard errors of 0.395. (19.9)'s ratio and (25.6) are large-$T$ results, and 90 months are a small sample for them. So 90 months cannot tell whether the correction is needed, and the corrected ratio carries the noise of 11 autocorrelations.

The monthly ratios use log returns and [[eg:checkpoint]] uses simple returns, and the two differ before any autocorrelation enters. On daily log excess returns the annual ratios run from 0.54 (R4) to 1.08 (R2), against 0.63 to 1.14.

> [!kc]

## The Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio and the Minimum Track Record {lo=4}

A one-sided test of $H_0:\mathrm{SR}\le\mathrm{SR}_0$, for a fixed benchmark $\mathrm{SR}_0$, divides the gap by (20.9)'s standard error. Bailey and López de Prado (2012) call one minus its p-value (21.2) the probabilistic Sharpe ratio:

$$\operatorname{PSR}(\mathrm{SR}_0)=\Phi\Big(\frac{(\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}-\mathrm{SR}_0)\sqrt{T-1}}{\sqrt{\hat V_{\mathrm{SR}}}}\Big). \tag{33.2}$$

The kurtosis in $\hat V_{\mathrm{SR}}$ is raw, and the approximation is (20.9)'s, with its iid returns, finite fourth moment and large $T$.

PSR is not the probability that the true ratio exceeds $\mathrm{SR}_0$, which would need a prior. In [[LM24]] a flat prior gives a posterior probability equal to the one-sided p-value to the printed digits, but as a statement about $\mu$, not about the data. Shrinking a Sharpe ratio toward a prior uses [[LM24]]'s tools and is not done here.

Setting PSR equal to $1-\alpha$ and solving for $T$ gives the **minimum track record length**, in observations:

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{MinTRL}=1+\hat V_{\mathrm{SR}}\Big(\frac{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)}{\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}-\mathrm{SR}_0}\Big)^2,\\ \widehat{\mathrm{SR}}>\mathrm{SR}_0.\end{gathered} \tag{33.3}$$

The $T-1$ is the authors' (their §2.5 and eq. (11), authors' version), while (20.9) uses $T$. For R2 the z-value is 3.097 with $\sqrt{T-1}$ and 3.098 with $\sqrt T$, larger by $\sqrt{1{,}851/1{,}850}$, that is by 0.027%.

[[LM21]]'s (21.4) asked another question: how long a record a true ratio needs for a two-sided test at level $\alpha$ to reach a given power. With $\hat V_{\mathrm{SR}}=1$ and $\mathrm{SR}_0=0$, and without the "1 +", (33.3) is (21.4) one-sided at power ½ (Problem 7). At its MinTRL, a test of a true ratio equal to the estimate rejects only half the time.

## Comparing Two Sharpe Ratios {lo=5}

R2 leads R1 by $\hat\Delta=+0.162$ a year. [[LM21]]'s (21.3) would test the lead as if the two samples were independent, but the two rules hold the same funds on the same days.

Ledoit and Wolf (2008) write the difference as a function of four means, $\boldsymbol\theta=(\bar x_j,\bar x_1,\overline{x_j^2},\overline{x_1^2})$, the last two uncentred second moments. With $\hat\Psi$ by (23.7) on the per-date terms $\mathbf v_t$ and the book's $\ell=7$,

$$\begin{gathered}h(\boldsymbol\theta)=\frac{\theta_1}{\sqrt{\theta_3-\theta_1^2}}-\frac{\theta_2}{\sqrt{\theta_4-\theta_2^2}},\\ \operatorname{SE}(\hat\Delta)=\sqrt{\nabla h^{\top}\hat\Psi\,\nabla h/T},\\ \mathbf v_t=\big(x_{j,t}-\bar x_j,\ x_{1,t}-\bar x_1,\\ x_{j,t}^2-\overline{x_j^2},\ x_{1,t}^2-\overline{x_1^2}\big)^{\top},\\ \text{count}=\#\Big\{i:\frac{\lvert\hat\Delta_{\text{boot},i}-\hat\Delta\rvert}{\operatorname{SE}(\hat\Delta_{\text{boot},i})}\ge\frac{\lvert\hat\Delta\rvert}{\operatorname{SE}(\hat\Delta)}\Big\},\\ \text{p-value}=\frac{\text{count}+1}{B+1}.\end{gathered} \tag{33.6}$$

The first two lines give a normal p-value and interval as (21.2) does, under (33.1)'s conditions. The last two lines are their bootstrap test (§3.2.2). It resamples blocks of $b$ consecutive return pairs on [[LM22]]'s circle, which keeps the two rules' joint dependence. Each resample's standard error is centred at its own means and uses its complete blocks (Götze and Künsch 1996, via Ledoit and Wolf).

Unlike (22.4), the resamples come from the data, not from a null world, so each is centred at $\hat\Delta$. The "+1" keeps the p-value above 0 (their Remark 3.2, eq. (9)). The interval $\hat\Delta\pm\hat F^{-1}_{\text{boot}}(0.95)\operatorname{SE}(\hat\Delta)$, with the 95% quantile of the centred statistics, is a symmetric studentized interval, each statistic being divided by its own standard error.

Three settings are the book's. First, the data's standard error uses Bartlett weights at $\ell=7$. Ledoit and Wolf use a kernel estimate with a factor $T/(T-4)$ and, in the bootstrap, Andrews and Monahan's (1992) prewhitened quadratic-spectral kernel.

Second, $b=22$ is [[LM22]]'s rule, the largest stationary estimate over the eight rules' squared demeaned returns (16.66 to 22.41), where Ledoit and Wolf calibrate $b$ (their Algorithm 3.1). As in [[LM22]] it sets the circular blocks too, though the circular estimates reach 25.65, so experiment 3 includes $b=26$.

Third, Ledoit and Wolf do not say how to cut a resample when $b$ does not divide $T$. Here $T=1{,}851=84\times22+3$, so a resample draws 85 blocks, keeps the first 1,851 rows and takes its block covariance from the 84 complete blocks.

## Deflating the Best of Eight {lo=6}

R2 is the best of eight, picked after the fact. Suppose $n$ independent rules all have true ratio zero. By (20.9) each estimate is then about normal with standard deviation $1/\sqrt T$, which PSR's convention writes $1/\sqrt{T-1}$. So their maximum is about that standard deviation times $Z_{(n)}$, the largest of $n$ iid standard normals. [[LM21]]'s (21.9), with its $m$ tests read as $n$ trials, gives $\Pr(Z_{(n)}\le z)=\Phi(z)^n$. Hence

$$\begin{gathered}\E Z_{(n)}=\int_0^\infty\big(1-\Phi(z)^n\big)\,dz-\int_{-\infty}^0\Phi(z)^n\,dz,\\ \mathrm{DSR}=\operatorname{PSR}\big(\E Z_{(n)}/\sqrt{T-1}\big).\end{gathered} \tag{33.4}$$

The **deflated Sharpe ratio** (Bailey and López de Prado 2014) is PSR at that benchmark. It assumes $n$ independent trials, each with true ratio zero under $H_0$. The null scale $1/\sqrt{T-1}$ is the book's, while their eq. (1) scales by the standard deviation of the trials' own estimates.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: the expected maximum from (21.9)
> Let $Y$ have a finite mean and distribution function $F_Y$, so that $y(1-F_Y(y))\to0$ as $y\to\infty$ and $yF_Y(y)\to0$ as $y\to-\infty$. Integration by parts on each half-line gives
> $$\begin{gathered}\int_0^\infty y\,dF_Y=\int_0^\infty(1-F_Y)\,dy,\\ \int_{-\infty}^0y\,dF_Y=-\int_{-\infty}^0F_Y\,dy.\end{gathered}$$
> Adding the two halves gives $\E Y$, and $F_Y=\Phi^n$ gives (33.4).

Bailey and López de Prado derive an approximation in their Appendix A.1 (eq. (1) of the authors' 31 July 2014 version), stated here without proof:

$$\begin{aligned}\E Z_{(n)}&\approx(1-\gamma_{\mathrm E})\,\Phi^{-1}(1-1/n)\\&\quad+\gamma_{\mathrm E}\,\Phi^{-1}\big(1-1/(ne)\big),\\ \gamma_{\mathrm E}&=0.5772.\end{aligned}$$

The module computes the exact integral instead. Setting DSR equal to $1-\alpha$ gives the record needed after $n$ trials (Problem 7):

$$\begin{gathered}T\ge1+\Big(\frac{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)\sqrt{\hat V_{\mathrm{SR}}}+\E Z_{(n)}}{\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}}\Big)^2,\\ \widehat{\mathrm{SR}}>0.\end{gathered} \tag{33.5}$$

At $n=1$, (33.5) is (33.3) against zero. With $\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}\le0$ no record length suffices.

## Haircuts and Sizing {lo=7}

Harvey and Liu (2015) correct a Sharpe ratio for multiple testing by adjusting the two-sided p-value (21.2) of its t-stat $\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}\sqrt T$ for $n$ tests. They convert the adjusted p-value into a Sharpe ratio:

$$\begin{gathered}\text{adjusted p-value}=1-(1-\text{p-value})^n,\\ \text{or}\ \ \min(1,\,n\,\text{p-value}),\\ \mathrm{HSR}=\frac{\Phi^{-1}(1-\text{adjusted p-value}/2)}{\sqrt T}.\end{gathered} \tag{33.7}$$

The first form is for $n$ independent tests (their eq. (4), Šidák's form in [[LM21]]), the second Bonferroni's (21.6). The **haircut** is $1-\mathrm{HSR}/\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}$.

Holm's (21.7) gives adjusted p-values too. In increasing order, the p-values are multiplied by $n$, then by $n-1$, and so on, kept at their running maximum and capped at 1. Harvey and Liu apply Holm with p-values from a factor model (section "Multiple Testing Framework"), so Holm on the eight rules' own p-values is the book's adaptation ([[ex:haircut]]). Their false-discovery version (BHY) is not used: [[LM21]] owns the false discovery rate.

## Sharpe Statistics in Python {lo=8}

```python
import numpy as np
from arch.bootstrap import optimal_block_length
from scipy import stats

def moments(x):                       # divisor T throughout, (19.3)
    m, s = x.mean(), x.std(ddof=0)
    c = x - m
    return m, s, (c**3).mean() / s**3, (c**4).mean() / s**4

def lrv(V, lag):                      # (23.7), Bartlett, V centred
    S = V.T @ V / len(V)
    for k in range(1, lag + 1):
        G = V[k:].T @ V[:-k] / len(V)
        S = S + (1 - k / (lag + 1)) * (G + G.T)
    return S

def se_hac(x, lag):                   # (33.1); lag 0 gives (20.9)
    m, s, _, _ = moments(x)
    c = x - m
    g = np.array([1 / s, -m / (2 * s**3)])
    V = np.column_stack([c, c**2 - s**2])
    return np.sqrt(g @ lrv(V, lag) @ g / len(x))

# x: one rule's 1,851 daily excess returns
m, s, sk, ku = moments(x)
assert np.isclose(sk, stats.skew(x, bias=True))
assert np.isclose(ku, stats.kurtosis(x, fisher=False, bias=True))  # raw
lag = int(4 * (len(x) / 100) ** (2 / 9))                 # 7
obl = optimal_block_length((x - m) ** 2)  # columns stationary, circular
b = round(obl["stationary"].iloc[0])      # the book: largest over rules

rng = np.random.default_rng([1, 20261011])  # once per experiment
nb = -(-len(x) // b)                        # ceil(T / b) blocks
sr_boot = []
for i in range(9999):
    starts = rng.integers(0, len(x), size=nb)
    idx = ((starts[:, None] + np.arange(b)) % len(x)).ravel()[: len(x)]
    sr_boot.append(x[idx].mean() / x[idx].std())
```

`scipy.stats.kurtosis` returns the excess kurtosis unless `fisher=False`, and its default `bias=True` divides by $T$, as (19.3) does, so pass both. arch's `optimal_block_length` returns a stationary and a circular column, and [[LM22]]'s rule reads the stationary one.

## Summary {-}

- By (20.9) with raw kurtosis each rule's Sharpe ratio has a standard error of about 0.37 a year.
- (33.1) applies (23.7)'s long-run variance. Dependence moves the ratio's error little, the volatility's by half.
- Lo's correction (19.9) needs autocorrelations that 90 months cannot estimate precisely.
- PSR (33.2) is one minus a one-sided p-value. MinTRL (33.3) solves it for the record.
- At 5%, Ledoit and Wolf's test (33.6) rejects no rule's equality with R1, before or after Holm.
- DSR (33.4) deflates the best of $n$ trials, and correlated rules count as fewer.
- Haircuts (33.7) cut R2's ratio by 21%, and at a fixed volatility (4.3)'s fraction by the same 21%.

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Sharpe ratio | 夏普比率 | $(\mu-r_f)/\sigma$ per period; $\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}=\bar x/s$ its estimate. |
| Raw kurtosis | 峰態 | $\frac1T\sum_t(x_t-\bar x)^4/s^4$, 3 for normal returns, (19.3). |
| Excess kurtosis | 超額峰態 | Raw kurtosis minus 3; `scipy.stats.kurtosis`'s default. |
| HAC estimator | 變異數非齊一與自我相關一致估計量 | (23.7)'s long-run covariance, here of a statistic's per-date terms. |
| Long-run variance | 長期變異數 | $T$ times the variance of a mean of dependent terms; the book's own Chinese form. |
| Scale factor | — | Lo's factor in (19.9) that turns a period's Sharpe ratio into the ratio over $m$ periods. |
| Probabilistic Sharpe ratio (PSR) | PSR | One minus the one-sided p-value of $H_0:\mathrm{SR}\le\mathrm{SR}_0$, (33.2). |
| Minimum track record length (MinTRL) | MinTRL | The observations at which PSR reaches $1-\alpha$, (33.3). |
| One-sided test | 單邊檢定 | A test whose alternative lies on one side of the null value. |
| Studentized confidence interval | Student化信賴區間 | An interval from the quantiles of a statistic divided by its own standard error. |
| Circular blocks | 循環區塊 | Blocks of consecutive days on a circle, day $T$ followed by day 1 ([[LM22]]). |
| Order statistic | 順序統計量 | A sample's value of a given rank; $Z_{(n)}$ is the largest of $n$. |
| Selection bias | 選擇偏誤 | The excess of a picked estimate over its true value, because it was picked as the best. |
| Deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR) | DSR | PSR at the benchmark $\E Z_{(n)}/\sqrt{T-1}$ of $n$ trials, (33.4). |
| Effective number of trials | — | The $n$ at which (33.4) equals the expected maximum of correlated trials. |
| Haircut | haircut | $1-\mathrm{HSR}/\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}$, the share of a Sharpe ratio removed for multiple testing. |
| Haircut Sharpe ratio (HSR) | HSR | The ratio whose single-test p-value equals the adjusted one, (33.7). |
