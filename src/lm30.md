## Introduction

[[LM28]] and [[LM29]] solved for weights as if the means $\boldsymbol\mu$ and the covariance matrix $\Sigma$ were known. In practice both are estimated. This module measures the error in the inputs, finds which error matters, and tests the repairs.

> [!case] Five funds, two years, and positions nobody should hold
> On 27 February 2019 the through case reads its first window: 504 daily returns of five funds. Fed the window's means and covariances, the tangency formula of [[LM28]] puts 62.6% in 00646 and shorts 14.7% of 00642U. Its in-sample Sharpe ratio is 0.88, against 0.33 for equal weights.
>
> Now suppose those estimates were the truth, and draw another 504 days from them, 10,000 times. Re-estimated on the new days, the position in 00646 has a standard deviation of 67.1 points. The portfolio's true Sharpe ratio averages 0.423. If only the covariances had to be estimated, it would average 0.87686, close to the possible 0.880. The simulation's standard errors are 0.5 points, 0.003 and 0.00002.
>
> Out of sample the through case agrees. From March 2019 to October 2026, minimum variance, which uses no means, realized 9.96% volatility net of costs, against 12.48% for equal weights. Shrinking its covariance matrix changed that by 0.01 points. Which errors matter, and which repairs work?

## How Far Plug-in Weights Fall from the Truth {lo=1}

A simulation can separate the errors, because it knows the truth. Take the first window's estimates as the true $\boldsymbol\mu$ and $\Sigma$ of iid normal daily returns. Their annual means are 7.5, 10.0, 2.6, 0.8 and −1.4%, the volatilities 13.5, 11.9, 8.5, 9.4 and 23.2%, and the correlations run from −0.30 to 0.54 ([[LM28]]). Samples colour standard normals with $\Sigma$'s Cholesky factor ([[LM12]]).

Chopra and Ziemba (1993) found errors in means far more damaging to optimal portfolios than errors in variances or covariances, as [[LM7]] reported. Experiment 1 measures the split.

With a riskless asset, (28.8) gives the best exposures $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e/\delta$. At $\delta=\mathbf 1^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e=11.27$ they are the tangency weights of [[ex:case]], with $\mathrm{SR}^*=0.880$. A sample gives three plug-ins at that fixed $\delta$:

$$\begin{gathered}\hat{\mathbf f}=\frac{\hat\Sigma^{-1}\hat{\mathbf e}}{\delta}\ \text{(both estimated)},\\ \frac{\Sigma^{-1}\hat{\mathbf e}}{\delta}\ \text{(means only)},\\ \frac{\hat\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e}{\delta}\ \text{(covariances only)}.\end{gathered}$$

Each is judged by its true Sharpe ratio, with the model's $\mathbf e$ and $\Sigma$:

$$\mathrm{SR}(\mathbf f)=\frac{\sqrt A\,\mathbf f^{\top}\mathbf e}{\sqrt{\mathbf f^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf f}}.$$

By (16.4), $\mathbf f^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf f$ is the true variance of a portfolio built from estimates. A Sharpe ratio estimated from returns is itself noisy ((20.9)); [[LM33]] tests the rules' Sharpe ratios, while here the model knows the true one.

The comparison uses exposures, not weights that sum to 1. In 2,115 of 10,000 samples (21%, SE 0.4 points) the sum $\mathbf 1^{\top}\hat{\mathbf f}$ is zero or negative, so normalized weights would flip sign. [[ex:scatter]] shows the first 1,000 samples.

With $\Sigma$ known, (28.9) gives $\hat{\mathbf f}-\mathbf w^{\mathrm{tan}}=\Sigma^{-1}(\hat{\mathbf e}-\mathbf e)/\delta$, and $\hat{\mathbf e}-\mathbf e\sim\mathcal N_5(\mathbf 0,\Sigma/T)$. So by (16.4) the means-only exposures have

$$\Cov\Big(\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\hat{\mathbf e}}{\delta}\Big)=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma\,\Sigma^{-1}}{\delta^2T}=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}}{\delta^2T}.$$

For 00646 this covariance gives an SD of 65.7 points, almost all of the spread in [[ex:scatter]].

> [!pitfall] A fixed model measures sampling error alone
> The model's truth is an in-sample estimate: $\mathrm{SR}^*=0.880$ is the first window's own optimum. Its returns are iid normal, and its means stay fixed, while real means move ([[LM24]]: the sector means of a later half spread almost twice as widely). The model is a best case; R1's and R3's realized volatilities are the evidence.

> [!zh] 平均報酬的誤差最傷
> 把估計值當成真值直接代入最適化公式，就是代入原則（plug-in principle）的做法。實驗一假設第一個視窗的估計值就是真值，再重抽 504 天：同時估計平均與共變異數時，真實夏普比率（Sharpe ratio）平均只剩 0.423；只估計共變異數時則有 0.87686，幾乎沒有損失。原因在於平均超額報酬（excess return）的估計誤差（estimation error）只隨年數下降：兩年資料讓 00646 年化平均的標準誤（standard error）達 8.2 個百分點，共變異數矩陣的誤差卻隨天數下降。這個模擬只量到抽樣誤差（sampling error）；真實的平均報酬還會移動，所以實際情況只會更糟。

## Equal Weights as a Benchmark {lo=2}

Equal weights estimate nothing. By (10.6) the variance of the 1/N portfolio is $\overline{\operatorname{var}}/N+(1-1/N)\,\overline{\operatorname{cov}}$, so its risk depends only on the average variance and covariance. Its weights carry no estimation error, but they ignore every difference between the assets.

DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (2009) compared 14 models with 1/N on seven datasets. None was consistently better in Sharpe ratio, certainty-equivalent return or turnover (their abstract). Optimizing should win only with a long estimation window, an ex ante best Sharpe ratio well above 1/N's, and few assets (p. 1919).

Experiment 2 asks how long a window the plug-in needs. It draws 2,000 samples at each of seven lengths from 126 to 8,064 days (`default_rng([2, 20261011])`), plotted in [[ex:years]].

On E5, out of sample and net of costs, R1 realized 12.48% and R3 9.96% on the 1,851 daily returns from 5 March 2019 to 8 October 2026. R3's windows had predicted 8.84% on average, 4.97% to 10.42% ([[LM28]]): an optimized in-sample variance is biased down.

Costs, turnover and net growth of the rules are [[LM34]]'s numbers, and the standard errors of their statistics [[LM33]]'s; this module reports only volatility. Equal weights are also the $c=0$ end of Section 4's shrinkage.

>
> > [!answer]-
> > 1/N's ratio is fixed, while the plug-in's varies. Errors in the means often point the exposures in a poor direction, so its ratio has a long left tail (mean 0.423, median 0.489). It falls below 0.327 in 31.4% of the samples.

## Shrinking the Covariance Matrix {lo=3,4,8}

Minimum-variance weights divide by the small eigenvalues of $\hat\Sigma$, (11.8), and those are too small on average. With a known mean, (11.4) gives $\lambda_N(\hat\Sigma)\le\mathbf q^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf q$ for the true last eigenvector $\mathbf q$, and $\E[\mathbf q^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf q]=\lambda_N(\Sigma)$.

> [!definition] Shrinkage toward the scaled identity
> For a weight $0\le c\le1$ on the data, with $\bar\lambda=\operatorname{tr}\hat\Sigma/N$,
> $$\begin{gathered}\hat\Sigma_c:=c\,\hat\Sigma+(1-c)\,\bar\lambda I,\\ \hat\Sigma_c\mathbf q_k=\big(c\lambda_k+(1-c)\bar\lambda\big)\mathbf q_k,\\ \kappa_c=\frac{c\lambda_1+(1-c)\bar\lambda}{c\lambda_N+(1-c)\bar\lambda}.\end{gathered} \tag{30.1}$$
> The target $\hat\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}=\bar\lambda I$ has the trace of $\hat\Sigma$.

By (11.3), shrinkage keeps the eigenvectors and moves every eigenvalue toward $\bar\lambda$, the small ones up and the large ones down, so $\kappa$ falls. For $c<1$ each eigenvalue is at least $(1-c)\bar\lambda>0$: $\hat\Sigma_c$ is positive definite even when $\hat\Sigma$ is singular. At $c=0$ the minimum-variance portfolio is 1/N. Unstable eigenvectors of close eigenvalues, (11.7), stay unrepaired.

> [!theorem] The oracle weight
> Let $\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ be a nonrandom matrix, and let $\hat\Sigma$ satisfy two conditions:
> 1. it is unbiased, $\E\hat\Sigma=\Sigma$ (a known mean);
> 2. its noise $\sigma^2_{\hat\Sigma}:=\E\lVert\hat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_F^2$ is finite (finite fourth moments).
>
> If $\lVert\Sigma-\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}\rVert_F^2+\sigma^2_{\hat\Sigma}>0$, then over nonrandom $c$ the error $\E\lVert c\hat\Sigma+(1-c)\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}-\Sigma\rVert_F^2$ is smallest at
> $$c^*=\frac{\lVert\Sigma-\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}\rVert_F^2}{\lVert\Sigma-\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}\rVert_F^2+\sigma^2_{\hat\Sigma}}. \tag{30.2}$$
> For iid normal returns with a known mean, (18.4) gives $\sigma^2_{\hat\Sigma}=(\lVert\Sigma\rVert_F^2+(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2)/T$.

*Proof.* The error is $c(\hat\Sigma-\Sigma)+(1-c)(\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}-\Sigma)$, and its squared Frobenius norm is a sum of squared entries. The cross term has expectation zero, because $\E(\hat\Sigma-\Sigma)=0$ and the second matrix is nonrandom. Hence

$$\begin{aligned}&\E\big\lVert c\hat\Sigma+(1-c)\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}-\Sigma\big\rVert_F^2\\&\quad=c^2\sigma^2_{\hat\Sigma}+(1-c)^2\lVert\Sigma-\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}\rVert_F^2,\end{aligned}$$

variance plus squared bias, (20.1)'s split entry by entry. This parabola in $c$ is smallest where its derivative $2c\sigma^2_{\hat\Sigma}-2(1-c)\lVert\Sigma-\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}\rVert_F^2$ is zero, at (30.2).

The oracle has the shape of [[LM24]]'s (24.3), with the target's squared distance from the truth in the prior variance's place.

The oracle needs $\Sigma$. Ledoit and Wolf (2004) estimate its parts:

$$\begin{gathered}\hat c=1-\frac{\min\big(\hat\sigma^2_{\hat\Sigma},\ \lVert\hat\Sigma-\bar\lambda I\rVert_F^2\big)}{\lVert\hat\Sigma-\bar\lambda I\rVert_F^2},\\ \hat\sigma^2_{\hat\Sigma}=\frac1{T^2}\sum_{t=1}^T\big\lVert(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R})(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R})^{\top}-\hat\Sigma\big\rVert_F^2.\end{gathered} \tag{30.3}$$

The denominator estimates the oracle's, and $1-\hat c$ estimates the noise's share $1-c^*$ (Deep Dive). As $N$ and $T$ grow with $N/T$ bounded, $\hat c$ is consistent and the shrunk matrix attains the oracle's loss asymptotically, under moment conditions. This is stated without proof (Theorems 3.2 and 3.4 of their 2001 working paper). Here $N/T$ runs from 5/504 on E5 to 28/20 on U28.

Forecasting the matrix day by day, with the recursion (26.2) applied to $\mathbf R_t\mathbf R_t^{\top}$, is [[LM31]]'s method.

> [!deepdive]- Deep Dive: why Ledoit and Wolf's noise estimate works
> With a known mean, $\hat\Sigma$ is the average of $T$ iid matrices $M_t=(\mathbf R_t-\boldsymbol\mu)(\mathbf R_t-\boldsymbol\mu)^{\top}$ with mean $\Sigma$. Entry by entry, (18.2) gives $\E\lVert\hat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_F^2=\E\lVert M_t-\Sigma\rVert_F^2/T$. In (30.3) the sum over $T$ is the sample spread of the $M_t$, and the second $1/T$ is (18.2)'s.
>
> The expansion in the proof of (30.2), applied to $(\hat\Sigma-\Sigma)+(\Sigma-\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}})$, gives the Pythagorean identity $\E\lVert\hat\Sigma-\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}\rVert_F^2=\lVert\Sigma-\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}\rVert_F^2+\sigma^2_{\hat\Sigma}$ (§2 of the 2001 working paper). So $\lVert\hat\Sigma-\bar\lambda I\rVert_F^2$ estimates the oracle's denominator, apart from the error in $\bar\lambda$; the minimum in (30.3) keeps $\hat c\ge0$.
>
> Stated without proof, the consistency result assumes (§3 of the working paper):
> 1. the ratio $N/T$ stays bounded as both grow;
> 2. the returns have bounded eighth moments;
> 3. a condition holds on products of the returns' entries.

> [!pitfall] Ledoit and Wolf's estimate reacts to tails
> TR23's Student $t$ fit has 3.065 degrees of freedom, so its kurtosis is infinite ([[LM15]]). In E5's window to 2026-03-31, $\hat c$ fell to 0.781 against 0.983 with the normal noise. Say what $\hat c$ responds to; never call it the right amount of shrinkage.

> [!zh] 收縮：用一點偏誤換掉大量雜訊
> 樣本共變異數矩陣（covariance matrix）不偏，但雜訊大；目標 $\bar\lambda I$ 沒有雜訊，卻有偏誤。收縮估計量（shrinkage estimator）取兩者的加權平均（weighted average），權重的選擇是偏誤及變異數之抵換（bias–variance tradeoff）：使 Frobenius 範數（Frobenius norm）下的均方誤差（mean squared error）最小。收縮（shrinkage）保留特徵向量（eigenvector），把每個特徵值（eigenvalue）往平均值拉，小的變大、大的變小，所以條件數（condition number）下降。28 個產業只用 60 筆報酬時，條件數從 438 降到 68.1，樣本外波動率從 13.85% 降到 12.04%；E5 用 504 天估 5 檔基金，收縮幾乎沒有影響。Ledoit–Wolf 的估計值會對厚尾（heavy tail）起反應，不能把它當成「正確的收縮量」。

## Constraints as Implicit Shrinkage {lo=5}

R3 forbids short positions. Jagannathan and Ma (2003) showed that this acts on the covariance matrix. The problem is

$$\min_{\mathbf w}\ \mathbf w^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf w\quad\text{subject to}\quad\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf w=1,\ \ \mathbf w\ge\mathbf 0.$$

The feasible set is closed and bounded, so a minimizer exists. With $\hat\Sigma$ positive definite the objective is strictly convex, so by (29.3) the minimizer $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}}$ is unique. [[LM29]]'s KKT conditions (29.5), halved, read

$$\hat\Sigma\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}}=\nu_0\mathbf 1+\boldsymbol\nu,\qquad\boldsymbol\nu\ge\mathbf 0,\qquad\nu_iw^{\mathrm{lo}}_i=0.$$

Each multiplier is half of (29.5)'s, as Jagannathan and Ma scale them. Multiplying by $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}\top}$ gives $\nu_0=\sigma_p^2$. By (27.5), a held fund's covariance with the portfolio is $\sigma_p^2$, an excluded one's $\sigma_p^2+\nu_i$.

> [!proposition] The constraint as an adjusted matrix
> Let $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}}$, $\nu_0$ and $\boldsymbol\nu$ satisfy these conditions, and define
> $$\hat\Sigma_\nu:=\hat\Sigma-\big(\boldsymbol\nu\mathbf 1^{\top}+\mathbf 1\boldsymbol\nu^{\top}\big). \tag{30.4}$$
> Then $\hat\Sigma_\nu$ is positive semidefinite and $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}}$ is one of its unconstrained minimum-variance portfolios (Jagannathan and Ma 2003, Proposition 1). If $\hat\Sigma$ is positive definite, so is $\hat\Sigma_\nu$, and $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}}$ is its only one.

*Derivation.* For any $\mathbf x$ put $\mathbf y=\mathbf x-(\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf x)\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}}$. Expanding $\mathbf y^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf y$ with the KKT conditions and $\boldsymbol\nu^{\top}\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}}=0$ gives

$$\mathbf x^{\top}\hat\Sigma_\nu\mathbf x=\mathbf y^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf y+\nu_0\,(\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf x)^2\ge0.$$

On the budget $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf x=1$ this is at least $\nu_0$, the value at $\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}}$, where $\mathbf y=\mathbf 0$. With $\hat\Sigma$ positive definite and $\nu_0>0$, the sum is zero only if $\mathbf y=\mathbf 0$ and $\mathbf 1^{\top}\mathbf x=0$, that is $\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$. On the budget, equality needs $\mathbf y=\mathbf 0$, that is $\mathbf x=\mathbf w^{\mathrm{lo}}$.

The adjusted matrix lowers an excluded fund's covariances by $\nu_i+\nu_j$ and its variance by $2\nu_i$: the constraint shrinks the entries that made the fund a short.

The unconstrained minimum-variance weights of $\hat\Sigma_\nu$ equal the long-only weights to rounding error (below $10^{-15}$). > > cvxpy's dual values give the same $\boldsymbol\nu$ to $3.7\times10^{-10}$ of the largest $\nu_i$. In the second half the long-only portfolio realized 12.14%, against 11.78% unconstrained.

In experiment 3, paired on the same samples, the constraint lowers the true volatility against shrinkage by 0.38 points (SE 0.03) at 30 returns and 0.06 (SE 0.02) at 60. It raises it by 0.49 points (SE 0.01) at 120 and 1.2981 (SE 0.0005) at 2,191: wrong constraints cost when data are long. > [!zh] 不准放空，等於修改共變異數矩陣
> 加上非負性條件（nonnegativity condition）的最小變異數投資組合（minimum-variance portfolio），等於另一個矩陣 $\hat\Sigma_\nu$ 的無限制最小變異數解。被排除的資產與投資組合的共變異數偏高，它的乘數 $\nu_i$ 為正，這條限制條件（constraint）就是有效限制（active constraint）。調整後的矩陣把它的共變異數降低 $\nu_i+\nu_j$、變異數降低 $2\nu_i$，所以限制條件本身就是一種收縮（shrinkage）。資料少時它有幫助；資料多而真實的最適解需要放空時，它反而有成本。

## Starting from a Reference Portfolio: Black–Litterman {lo=6}

Means are the weak input, so Black and Litterman (1992) start from a reference portfolio and let views move it. If an investor with risk aversion $\delta$ holds $\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}$ optimally, (28.8) gives $\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}=\hat\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e/\delta$. Reversed, it gives the **implied excess returns**

$$\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}:=\delta\,\hat\Sigma\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}.$$

The reference here is R2, [[LM27]]'s *constructed* mix of 40% 0050, 20% 00646, 30% 00679B and 10% 00635U; the book has no market weights for these funds. The risk aversion $\delta=\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf e}/\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\top}\hat\Sigma\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}$ makes R2's implied mean excess return equal its window mean.

The prior is $\mathbf e\sim\mathcal N_5(\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}},\hat\Sigma/n_0)$: the reference is worth $n_0$ observations, as in [[LM24]]. The literature's scalar is $1/n_0$, with no consensus value. Idzorek (2007) reports values near 0, from 0.01 to 0.05, 1, and about one over the number of observations, each from other authors.

A view is a linear statement about excess returns, with an error: $K\mathbf e=b+\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\Xi)$ is independent of $\mathbf e$. Here $K=(-1,1,0,0,0)$, so the view says that 00646 beats 0050 by $b$.

> [!proposition] The Black–Litterman posterior for one view
> Under this prior and view, the posterior mean of $\mathbf e$ and its exposures $\mathbf f_{\mathrm{BL}}=\hat\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e_{\mathrm{BL}}/\delta$ are
> $$\begin{gathered}\mathbf e_{\mathrm{BL}}=\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}+\hat\Sigma K^{\top}\,\frac{c\,(b-K\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}})}{K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}},\\ c=\frac{K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}/n_0}{K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}/n_0+\Xi},\\ \mathbf f_{\mathrm{BL}}=\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}+K^{\top}\,\frac{c\,(b-K\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}})}{\delta\,K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}}.\end{gathered} \tag{30.5}$$
> A data view, $\Xi=K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}/T$, gives $c=T/(T+n_0)$, (24.3)'s weight.

*Derivation.* By (16.4) and the independence of $\varepsilon$, each $e_i$ and the observed view $K\mathbf e+\varepsilon$ form a normal pair, with covariance $(\hat\Sigma K^{\top})_i/n_0$ and view variance $K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}/n_0+\Xi$. By (16.8), the mean of $e_i$ given that the view equals $b$ is the prior mean plus the covariance over the view variance times $b-K\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}$: (30.5)'s first line. Multiplying by $\hat\Sigma^{-1}/\delta$, with $\hat\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}/\delta=\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}$, gives the last.

The exposures move along $K^{\top}$ only. He and Litterman (1999) state the general case: the unconstrained optimum is the scaled reference plus a weighted sum of view portfolios. With one row of $K$ per view and $\Xi$ their error covariance,

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf e_{\mathrm{BL}}&=\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}\\&\quad+\hat\Sigma K^{\top}\big(K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}+n_0\Xi\big)^{-1}(\mathbf b-K\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}).\end{aligned}$$

The exposures here use $\hat\Sigma$, not the posterior covariance of $\mathbf e$. [[ex:bl]] traces them as $c$ rises.

> [!pitfall] The reference carries the prior
> Black–Litterman moves away from $\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}$ only as far as the views push, so a poor reference is a poor prior. R2 is *constructed*, and $\delta$ fits R2's own window mean; report both beside every such portfolio.

> [!zh] 從參考投資組合出發
> Black–Litterman 不直接採用樣本平均，而是先假設參考投資組合（reference portfolio）是最適的，反推出隱含超額報酬（implied excess returns）$\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}=\delta\hat\Sigma\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}$，作為事前分布（prior distribution）的中心。觀點（view）是對超額報酬的線性陳述，帶有誤差；事後分布（posterior distribution）的平均把兩者依精確度加權，與 [[LM24]] 的 (24.3) 同形。只有一個觀點時，部位只沿著觀點的方向移動，本例只有 0050 與 00646 改變。參考組合 R2 是建構的，事前分布的好壞取決於它。

## Resampling, Factor Structure and Repair {lo=7,8}

Michaud (1989) argued that mean–variance optimization tends to maximize the effects of errors in its inputs, and can do worse than equal weights. Resampled efficiency, first proposed by Michaud (1998), averages instead, as Michaud and Michaud (2008) describe it. It draws means and covariances around the estimates, computes each draw's frontier, and averages the weights of matched portfolios.

Experiment 4 is a variant (`default_rng([4, 20261011])`). It averages R4's long-only maximum-Sharpe portfolio (29.6) over 1,000 iid bootstrap resamples of the first window's rows ([[LM22]]). In the 12 where no fund's mean exceeds $r_f$, R3's minimum variance stands in. The average holds every fund, from 3.8% (00642U) to 35.2% (00646), SEs 0.4 and 0.9 points.

Yet each fund is excluded in 18% (00646) to 72% (00642U) of the resamples, SEs 1 to 2 points: averaging spreads weights that each resample concentrates.

A factor structure cuts the parameters. The single-index matrix of Sharpe (1963) is [[LM27]]'s (27.8) with uncorrelated residuals,

$$\hat\Sigma_{\mathrm F}=\sigma^2_{\mathrm{mkt}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^{\top}+\operatorname{diag}\big(\hat\sigma^2_{\mathrm{res},1},\dots,\hat\sigma^2_{\mathrm{res},N}\big),$$

with (23.1)'s betas on the TAIEX price index and residual variances with divisor $T$. For U28 it has 57 parameters against the sample's 406, yet its portfolio realized 12.67% in the second half, against the sample's 11.78% ([[ex:halves]]): a wrong structure costs. Ledoit and Wolf shrink toward this matrix (2003) or toward constant correlations (2004, *Journal of Portfolio Management*); nonlinear shrinkage is outside this book.

> [!example] Repairing LM10's pairwise matrix {#repair}
> [[LM10]]'s constructed $C_{\text{pw}}$ has entries 0.982, 0.199 and −0.444 and $\det C_{\text{pw}}=-0.374592$. Repair it three ways.
>
> > [!solution]
> > Its eigenvalues are 2.0149, 1.1471 and −0.1621. Shrinking toward $I$ gives eigenvalues $c\lambda_k+1-c$, the smallest nonnegative up to $c=1/(1-\lambda_3)=0.8605$, with off-diagonal entries 0.845, 0.171 and −0.382. Clipping $\lambda_3$ to 0 and rescaling to a unit diagonal gives 0.852, 0.154 and −0.385.
> >
> > The nearest correlation matrix (Higham 2002b) gives 0.864, 0.139 and −0.379, PSD to rounding; a second solver, cvxpy's CLARABEL, agrees to $1.4\times10^{-7}$. The Frobenius distances to $C_{\text{pw}}$ are 0.216, 0.211 and 0.209.
> >
> > The nearest matrix minimizes $\lVert M-C_{\text{pw}}\rVert_F^2$ over PSD matrices $M$ with unit diagonal. That set is closed, bounded and convex, and the objective strictly convex, so the minimizer exists and is unique. Rousseeuw and Molenberghs (1993) compare methods based on shrinking, on eigenvalues and on geometry. *Constructed* (LM10's).

The Summary's table chooses a repair by the ratio $T/N$ of observations to assets. Picking the best of several estimators judged on one split is a search, which [[LM34]] corrects.

> [!zh] 重抽樣、因子結構與修補
> 重抽樣（resampling）把資料重抽很多次，每次求最適權重再取平均；平均後每檔基金都有部位，但每次重抽都圍繞同一組樣本平均。單一指標模型（single index model）用市場的貝他係數（beta）描述共變異數，28 個產業只要 57 個參數，但結構不對時樣本外波動率反而較高。成對估計的相關矩陣（correlation matrix）可能不是半正定（positive semidefinite）：可以往單位矩陣收縮、把負的特徵值（eigenvalue）截為零，或找最近的相關矩陣。

## Estimation-Robust Portfolios in Python {lo=4}

```python
import cvxpy as cp
import numpy as np
import polars as pl
from sklearn.covariance import LedoitWolf

est = pl.read_csv("data/e5_estimation.csv", schema_overrides={"fund": pl.Utf8})
wide = est.pivot(on="fund", index="date", values="R").sort("date")
funds = ["0050", "00646", "00679B", "00635U", "00642U"]
first = wide.filter(pl.col("date") <= pl.lit("2019-02-27")).tail(504)
x = first.select(funds).to_numpy()              # 504 x 5 daily returns
lw = LedoitWolf(assume_centered=False).fit(x)
c_hat = 1 - lw.shrinkage_                       # 0.9675
A = 242.951422                                  # E5's estimation frame
S = A * np.cov(x, rowvar=False, ddof=0)         # annual inputs for solvers
w = cp.Variable(5)
cons = [cp.sum(w) == 1, w >= 0]
prob = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(cp.quad_form(w, cp.psd_wrap(S))), cons)
prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL, tol_gap_abs=1e-12, tol_gap_rel=1e-12,
           tol_feas=1e-12)
assert prob.status == "optimal"
nu = cons[1].dual_value / (2 * A)               # Jagannathan and Ma's scale
```

`LedoitWolf(assume_centered=False)` removes the mean, divides by $T$ and shrinks toward $\bar\lambda I$ only; `shrinkage_` is the target's weight $1-\hat c$. `OAS` is another estimator, after Chen et al. (2010); its source says it omits their $2/p$ factor. The dual values of `w >= 0` belong to $\mathbf w^{\top}(A\hat\Sigma)\mathbf w$, so dividing by $2A$ gives the daily $\boldsymbol\nu$. `psd_wrap` asserts positive semidefiniteness without testing it.

## Summary {-}

- Plug-in weights inherit the means' errors: on E5's model the mean true Sharpe ratio is 0.423 of 0.880, but 0.87686 with the means known.
- A mean's error falls only with years, (20.2); a covariance matrix's falls with days, (18.4).
- Equal weights carry no estimation error; optimizing beats them only with a long window, a large gap in Sharpe ratios and few assets.
- Shrinkage (30.1) pulls the eigenvalues toward $\bar\lambda$; the oracle (30.2) trades bias against noise, and (30.3) estimates it.
- The long-only constraint is minimum variance on the adjusted matrix (30.4).
- Black–Litterman (30.5) moves the reference along the views with (24.3)'s weight.
- Resampling spreads weights, a factor model cuts parameters, and three repairs make a correlation matrix PSD.

| Long-only constraint | short positions built on noise | small: 30 to 60 returns in experiment 3 | bias when the truth needs shorts |
| Reference portfolio | errors in means | any, given a credible reference | the reference is the prior |
| Single-index matrix | too many parameters | small, with many assets | a wrong structure (12.67% against 11.78%) |
| Resampling | weights that jump | any; an illustration here | still centred on the window's means |
| Equal weights | every estimate | short windows, a small best Sharpe ratio | ignores all differences |

## Solutions {-}

1. **C** is correct: by (20.2) the SE is $\sigma/\sqrt\tau=20/\sqrt4=10.0$ points. A divides by the square root of the 972 days, B by the years instead of their square root.

2. **A** is correct: $\bar\lambda=2.5$, and (30.1) gives $0.8\times4+0.2\times2.5=3.70$ and $0.8\times1+0.2\times2.5=1.30$, so $\kappa_c=2.85$. B reads shrinkage as a rotation that keeps the eigenvalues. C multiplies $\kappa=4$ by $c$.

3. **B** is correct: the target equals the truth, so $\lVert\Sigma-\Sigma_{\mathrm{tgt}}\rVert_F^2=0$ and (30.2) puts all weight on the target. A borrows the form $T/(T+N)$ from (24.3). C would need a noiseless sample matrix.

4. **C** is correct: $c=504/(504+1{,}512)=0.25$, and (30.5) moves the view's value from 0% by $0.25\times4\%=1.0\%$. A swaps $c$ and $1-c$. B weighs the two equally.

5. **A** is correct: by (9.5), $(\Sigma^{-1})_{11}=\sigma_2^2/\det\Sigma=1/(\sigma_1^2(1-\rho^2))$, or $\operatorname{VIF}/\sigma_1^2=5.263/0.04=131.6$ by (23.9). B ignores $\rho$; C uses $1-\rho$. The factor $1-\rho^2$ is the product of the correlation matrix's eigenvalues 1.9 and 0.1: the small one drives the inverse, and shrinkage lifts it.

6. **B** is correct: the diagonal of $\boldsymbol\nu\mathbf 1^{\top}+\mathbf 1\boldsymbol\nu^{\top}$ is $2\nu_i$. A takes one of the two terms. C forgets the diagonal; each covariance with asset 3 falls by $\nu_3+\nu_j$.

7. Section 4's proof gives (30.2). Here $\bar\lambda=2.5$, so $\lVert\Sigma-2.5I\rVert_F^2=1.5^2+1.5^2=4.5$. By (18.4) the noise is $(16+1+25)/100=0.42$, and $c^*=4.5/4.92=0.9146$.

8. Section 5 gives (30.4). Here $\hat\Sigma$ has entries 1, 1.6 and 4, and the unconstrained weights are $(4-1.6)/(1+4-3.2)=133.3\%$ and −33.3%. At $\mathbf w=(1,0)$ the KKT conditions hold with $\nu_0=(\hat\Sigma\mathbf w)_1=1$ and $\nu_2=1.6-1=0.6\ge0$, so the long-only weights are (1, 0). Then $\hat\Sigma_\nu$ has entries 1, $1.6-0.6=1$ and $4-1.2=2.8$, with minimum-variance weights $(2.8-1)/(1+2.8-2)=100\%$ and 0.

9. Section 6 gives (30.5). Here $\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}=4\times(0.04\times0.5,\ 0.01\times0.5)=(8\%,2\%)$, so $K\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}=-6\%$ and $K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}=0.05$, and the factor $c(b-K\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}})/K\hat\Sigma K^{\top}$ is $0.5\times0.06/0.05=0.6$. Then
   $$\begin{aligned}\mathbf e_{\mathrm{BL}}&=(8\%,2\%)+0.6\,(-0.04,\,0.01)\\&=(5.6\%,\,2.6\%),\\ \mathbf f_{\mathrm{BL}}&=(50\%,50\%)+\tfrac{0.6}{4}(-1,\,1)\\&=(35\%,\,65\%).\end{aligned}$$

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Plug-in portfolio | 代入投資組合 | Weights computed from estimates as if they were the truth, such as $\hat\Sigma^{-1}\hat{\mathbf e}/\delta$. |
| Estimation error | 估計誤差 | The difference between an estimate and the quantity it estimates. |
| Sampling error | 抽樣誤差 | The part of the estimation error that comes from a finite sample of a fixed model. |
| Shrinkage estimator | 收縮估計量 | A weighted average of an estimator and a target, such as $\hat\Sigma_c$ of (30.1). |
| Oracle weight | — | $c^*$ of (30.2), the best nonrandom weight on the data, which needs the truth. |
| Ledoit–Wolf estimator | — | The shrunk matrix with $\hat c$ of (30.3). |
| Frobenius norm | Frobenius 範數 | $\lVert M\rVert_F$, the square root of the sum of squared entries ([[LM18]]). |
| Condition number | 條件數 | $\kappa=\lambda_1/\lambda_N$ of a positive definite matrix; $\kappa_c$ after shrinkage. |
| Adjusted covariance matrix | — | $\hat\Sigma_\nu$ of (30.4), whose unconstrained minimum-variance portfolio is the long-only one. |
| Reference portfolio | 參考投資組合 | The weights $\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}$ that Black–Litterman's prior starts from. |
| Implied excess returns | 隱含超額報酬 | $\mathbf e_{\mathrm{eq}}=\delta\hat\Sigma\mathbf w_{\mathrm{ref}}$, the means under which the reference is optimal. |
| View | 觀點 | A linear statement $K\mathbf e=b+\varepsilon$ about excess returns, with an error. |
| Resampled efficiency | — | Averaging optimal weights over draws of the inputs (Michaud and Michaud 2008). |
| Single index model | 單一指標模型 | The covariance matrix $\hat\Sigma_{\mathrm F}$ of market betas and uncorrelated residuals. |
| Nearest correlation matrix | — | The PSD matrix with unit diagonal closest to a given one in the Frobenius norm. |
