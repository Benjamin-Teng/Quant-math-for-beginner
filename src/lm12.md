## Introduction

Volume II has built vectors ([[LM8]]), linear systems ([[LM9]]), covariance matrices ([[LM10]]) and eigenvectors ([[LM11]]). This module closes the volume with three factorizations that software uses every day. Least squares splits a response into a fitted part and a residual orthogonal to it. The singular value decomposition (SVD) measures rank and conditioning on the data matrix itself. The Cholesky factor of a covariance matrix turns correlated returns into uncorrelated ones.

> [!case] Can you rebuild TAIEX from its sectors?
> A desk analyst wants to know how much of TAIEX moves with semiconductors, without a file of index weights. FinMind serves daily closes of 30 TWSE sector indices, and 28 of them contain no other sector index. She regresses TAIEX's last 250 daily returns, 25 September 2025 to 8 October 2026, on the returns of these 28 sectors, with an intercept.
>
> The fit reproduces TAIEX almost perfectly: R-squared (Section 2) is 0.99912. The coefficient on Semiconductor is 0.542, on ElectronicPartsComponents 0.098, on FinancialInsurance 0.084 and on ComputerPeripheralEquipment 0.054. The 28 coefficients sum to 0.990 ([[ex:case]]).
>
> A colleague runs the same regression on the 22 daily returns of December 2008. That fit is exact, with R-squared 1, and it stays exact when the Semiconductor column is removed. Which fit tells more, and why is the perfect one worthless?

## Least Squares as a Projection {lo=1}

Least squares is geometry before it is algebra, so start with one column in the plane.

> [!example] The closest multiple of one vector {#proj1}
> Find the multiple $\beta\mathbf x$ of $\mathbf x=(2,1)^{\top}$ that is closest to $\mathbf y=(1,3)^{\top}$, its residual and the distance.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The multiples of $\mathbf x$ form a line through the origin ([[ex:proj]]). The point of the line closest to $\mathbf y$ is the foot of the perpendicular, so the residual must be orthogonal to $\mathbf x$: $\mathbf x^{\top}(\mathbf y-\beta\mathbf x)=0$. Hence $\hat\beta=\mathbf x^{\top}\mathbf y/\mathbf x^{\top}\mathbf x=5/5=1$.
> >
> > The fitted vector is $\hat{\mathbf y}=(2,1)^{\top}$, and the residual is $\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=\mathbf y-\hat{\mathbf y}=(-1,2)^{\top}$, with $\mathbf x^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=-2+2=0$. The distance is $\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\rVert=\sqrt5=2.236$. Every other multiple is farther away: $1.5\mathbf x$ lies at distance 2.5. [[LM8]] found the same closest multiple in its proof of (8.6), with the roles of the two vectors exchanged.

### The normal equations

Now let $X$ be any $T\times N$ matrix with columns $\mathbf x_1,\dots,\mathbf x_N$. Its **column space** $\operatorname{col}X$ is the set of all combinations $X\boldsymbol\beta=\beta_1\mathbf x_1+\dots+\beta_N\mathbf x_N$ ([[LM9]]). Least squares finds the point of $\operatorname{col}X$ closest to a response $\mathbf y$ (Strang 2016, §4.2). It minimizes $\lVert\mathbf y-X\boldsymbol\beta\rVert^2$, the sum of the squared misses over the $T$ rows.

> [!theorem] Least squares
> Let $X$ be a $T\times N$ matrix and $\mathbf y$ a vector in $\mathbb R^T$. A vector $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ minimizes $\lVert\mathbf y-X\boldsymbol\beta\rVert^2$ if and only if its residual $\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}:=\mathbf y-X\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ is orthogonal to every column of $X$:
> $$X^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=\mathbf 0,\quad\text{that is,}\quad X^{\top}X\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=X^{\top}\mathbf y. \tag{12.1}$$
> When $X$ has full column rank, the minimizer is unique: $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=(X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top}\mathbf y$.

*Proof.* Suppose that $X^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=\mathbf 0$, and take any $\boldsymbol\beta$. Then $\mathbf y-X\boldsymbol\beta=\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}+X(\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}-\boldsymbol\beta)$ is a sum of two orthogonal vectors, and Pythagoras gives

$$\lVert\mathbf y-X\boldsymbol\beta\rVert^2=\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\rVert^2+\lVert X(\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}-\boldsymbol\beta)\rVert^2\ge\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\rVert^2 .$$

Conversely, expand the squared length and differentiate with the rules of [[LM10]]:

$$\begin{gathered}\lVert\mathbf y-X\boldsymbol\beta\rVert^2=\mathbf y^{\top}\mathbf y-2\boldsymbol\beta^{\top}X^{\top}\mathbf y+\boldsymbol\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\boldsymbol\beta,\\ \nabla_{\boldsymbol\beta}\lVert\mathbf y-X\boldsymbol\beta\rVert^2=2X^{\top}X\boldsymbol\beta-2X^{\top}\mathbf y .\end{gathered}$$

The gradient vanishes at any minimizer ([[LM7]]), which is (12.1). With full column rank, $X^{\top}X$ is positive definite ([[LM10]]), hence invertible. $\blacksquare$

The theorem names the **fitted values** $\hat{\mathbf y}:=X\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$, the point of $\operatorname{col}X$ closest to $\mathbf y$. Equations (12.1) are the **normal equations**, "normal" meaning perpendicular. Section 5 shows that they have a solution even without full column rank.

### The projection matrix

With full column rank, $\hat{\mathbf y}=X(X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top}\mathbf y$, so one matrix sends every response to its fitted values:

$$\begin{gathered}\Pi:=X(X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top},\\ \Pi^{\top}=\Pi=\Pi^2,\qquad \operatorname{tr}\Pi=N.\end{gathered} \tag{12.2}$$

The **projection matrix** $\Pi$ is symmetric, by the rule $(MK)^{\top}=K^{\top}M^{\top}$ of [[LM8]] and because the inverse of the symmetric matrix $X^{\top}X$ is symmetric. It is idempotent, $\Pi^2=\Pi$, because the middle factor $X^{\top}X$ of $\Pi^2$ cancels one inverse: projecting twice changes nothing. Its trace counts the columns. For two matrices whose products $KM$ and $MK$ are both square, the trace is cyclic, $\operatorname{tr}(KM)=\sum_i\sum_jk_{ij}m_{ji}=\operatorname{tr}(MK)$. Here $k_{ij}$ and $m_{ij}$ are the entries of $K$ and $M$. Hence

$$\operatorname{tr}\Pi=\operatorname{tr}\big((X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top}X\big)=\operatorname{tr}I_N=N.$$

The residual is $\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=(I-\Pi)\mathbf y$, and $I-\Pi$ projects on the directions orthogonal to every column.

### The intercept and R-squared

A fit with an intercept uses the design $(\mathbf 1\ X)$, whose first column is $\mathbf 1$. Its first normal equation, $\mathbf 1^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=0$, says that the residuals sum to zero. Averaging $\mathbf y=\hat\alpha\mathbf 1+X\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}+\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}$ over the rows therefore gives $\bar y=\hat\alpha+\bar{\mathbf R}^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$, and subtracting leaves $\tilde{\mathbf y}=\tilde X\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}+\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}$. The other normal equations, $X^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=\mathbf 0$, combine with the first into $\tilde X^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=\mathbf 0$. Substituting $\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=\tilde{\mathbf y}-\tilde X\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ and dividing by $T$:

$$\hat\Sigma\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=\tfrac1T\tilde X^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf y},\qquad \hat\alpha=\bar y-\bar{\mathbf R}^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}. \tag{12.3}$$

The slopes depend only on covariances, and the intercept on the means and the slopes.

Centring splits $\tilde{\mathbf y}$ into two orthogonal pieces, the centred fit $\tilde X\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=\hat{\mathbf y}-\bar y\mathbf 1$ and the residual. **R-squared** is the share of the centred response's squared length that the fit reproduces ([[LM23]] treats it as a statistic). By Pythagoras,

$$\begin{aligned}\text{R-squared}&:=1-\frac{\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\rVert^2}{\lVert\tilde{\mathbf y}\rVert^2}\\&=\frac{\lVert\hat{\mathbf y}-\bar y\mathbf 1\rVert^2}{\lVert\tilde{\mathbf y}\rVert^2}=\cos^2\theta,\end{aligned} \tag{12.4}$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between $\tilde{\mathbf y}$ and the centred fit ([[LM8]]). With one column, $\cos\theta$ is the absolute value of the sample correlation of $\mathbf x$ and $\mathbf y$, so R-squared is the squared correlation.

> [!example] A line through four points {#line4}
> Fit $\mathbf y=\hat\alpha\mathbf 1+\hat\beta\mathbf x+\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}$ to $\mathbf x=(0,1,2,3)^{\top}$ and $\mathbf y=(1,3,2,6)^{\top}$. Check (12.1), and compute R-squared in two ways.
>
> > [!solution]
> > The means are 1.5 and 3, so $\tilde{\mathbf x}=(-1.5,-0.5,0.5,1.5)^{\top}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf y}=(-2,0,-1,3)^{\top}$. By (12.3), $\hat\beta=\tilde{\mathbf x}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf y}/\tilde{\mathbf x}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf x}=7/5=1.4$ and $\hat\alpha=3-1.4\times1.5=0.9$.
> >
> > The fitted values are $(0.9,2.3,3.7,5.1)^{\top}$ and the residuals $(0.1,0.7,-1.7,0.9)^{\top}$. The residuals sum to zero, and $\mathbf x^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=0+0.7-3.4+2.7=0$, as (12.1) requires.
> >
> > The squared lengths are $\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\rVert^2=4.2$ and $\lVert\tilde{\mathbf y}\rVert^2=14$, so R-squared is $1-4.2/14=0.7$. As a squared cosine it is $(\tilde{\mathbf x}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf y})^2/(\lVert\tilde{\mathbf x}\rVert^2\lVert\tilde{\mathbf y}\rVert^2)=49/70=0.7$.

> [!pitfall] Without an intercept, R-squared is another number
> Formula (12.4) needs $\mathbf 1^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=0$, which only an intercept guarantees. Fit [[eg:line4]]'s data through the origin instead: $\hat\beta=25/14=1.786$ and $\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\rVert^2=5.357$, a worse fit than 4.2. Yet the uncentred share $1-\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\rVert^2/\lVert\mathbf y\rVert^2=1-5.357/50=0.893$ exceeds 0.7. The centred formula gives 0.617, and no angle stands behind it. Compare fits by their residuals, and quote R-squared only for fits with an intercept.

> [!kc]
> In [[eg:proj1]] the residual is orthogonal to the fitted vector. Why does the theorem demand orthogonality to every column of $X$, not only to $\hat{\mathbf y}$?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Orthogonality to the fitted vector alone also holds at bad points. At $\beta=0$ the fit is $\mathbf 0$, and every residual is orthogonal to $\mathbf 0$, yet the distance $\lVert\mathbf y\rVert=\sqrt{10}=3.162$ exceeds the minimum $\sqrt5$. If a column has $\mathbf x_j^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\ne0$, the gradient is not zero, and a small step along that column shortens the residual.

## Reading TAIEX from Its Sectors {lo=2}

Before reading the case's coefficients, consider why such a fit can be nearly exact.

### The index identity

TWSE computes TAIEX as the total market value of its member stocks divided by a base value, times the base level (TWSE IndexS02). It computes the sector indices the same way (TWSE IndexS03). Suppose nothing but prices changes between two closes, so that the market value $\mathrm{MV}_{i,t}$ of member $i$ grows by its return, $\mathrm{MV}_{i,t}=\mathrm{MV}_{i,t-1}(1+R_{i,t})$. With the same base value on both days,

$$\begin{gathered}1+y_t=\frac{\sum_i\mathrm{MV}_{i,t}}{\sum_i\mathrm{MV}_{i,t-1}}=\sum_iw_{i,t-1}(1+R_{i,t}),\\ w_{i,t-1}:=\frac{\mathrm{MV}_{i,t-1}}{\sum_j\mathrm{MV}_{j,t-1}} .\end{gathered}$$

So TAIEX's return is a weighted average of its members' returns, $\mathbf w_{t-1}^{\top}\mathbf R_t$ as in [[LM8]], with weights set at the previous close. The same identity holds inside each sector index. Suppose every member of TAIEX belongs to exactly one of the 28 sectors. Grouping the members by sector then turns TAIEX's return into an average of sector returns, weighted by the sectors' market values at the previous close.

Two things break the identity: members or share counts that change, which alter the base value, and members outside the 28 sectors. TWSE added four sector indices with base date 30 June 2023 (綠能環保類, 數位雲端類, 運動休閒類 and 居家生活類; TWSE IndexS03). FinMind serves no daily series for them, so from July 2023, which includes the whole last-250 window, some TAIEX members lie outside U28.

A third thing, weights that drift every day with relative prices, leaves the identity intact. The drift keeps any constant mix from reproducing the identity. The coefficients of a fit are therefore the constant weights that best reproduce TAIEX's daily moves in the window, never "the sector weights".

> [!zh] 最小平方法：投影，不是權重
> 最小平方法（least squares）把 $\mathbf y$ 投影（projection）到設計矩陣（design matrix）的行空間（column space）上：殘差（residual）必須和每一行都正交（orthogonal），這就是正規方程（normal equations）。加上截距（intercept）以後，判定係數（R-squared）等於中心化（centring）後的 $\mathbf y$ 與中心化配適值（fitted value）夾角（angle）餘弦（cosine）的平方。加權股價指數的報酬本來就是成分股報酬的市值加權平均，所以對 28 個類股指數迴歸（regression），判定係數幾乎是 1；這是會計恆等式（accounting identity），不是預測能力。係數是「這段期間最能重現大盤每日漲跌的固定權重」，不是類股市值權重：真正的權重每天隨股價漂移，而且有七個係數是負的。逐年重做時半導體的係數從 0.189 升到 0.554，這是穩定性檢查（stability check），不是樣本外檢定（out-of-sample test）。

> Why can a least-squares coefficient be slightly negative when every sector's market-value weight is positive?
>
> > [!answer]-
> > Least squares minimizes the squared misses and imposes no sign. The true weights drift, and correlated columns such as the electronics sub-industries partly substitute for one another. A small negative coefficient can therefore lower the sum of squared residuals. In the last 250 returns, seven coefficients are negative, the smallest −0.0081 (InformationService).

## The Singular Value Decomposition {lo=3}

[[LM11]] decomposed the symmetric matrix $\hat\Sigma$. The SVD decomposes a rectangular data matrix directly.

### From a circle to an ellipse

An invertible $2\times2$ matrix maps the unit circle to an ellipse. For

$$M=\begin{pmatrix}4&4\\-2&4\end{pmatrix},$$

the ellipse has semi-axes of lengths 6 and 4 ([[ex:svd]]). Two perpendicular unit vectors, $\mathbf v_1$ and $\mathbf v_2$, map onto the axes: $M\mathbf v_k=s_k\mathbf u_k$, where $\mathbf u_1$ and $\mathbf u_2$ are perpendicular unit vectors and $s_1=6$, $s_2=4$. So $M=USV^{\top}$ acts in three steps: $V^{\top}$ carries each $\mathbf v_k$ to a coordinate axis, $S$ stretches the axes by $s_k$, and $U$ carries them to the $\mathbf u_k$.

> <div data-chart="lm12-svd"></div>
>
> Note: $M$ of [[eg:svd22]]. The unit circle (grey) maps to the ellipse with semi-axes $s_1=6$ and $s_2=4$. The right singular vectors $\mathbf v_1,\mathbf v_2$ map to $s_1\mathbf u_1$ and $s_2\mathbf u_2$, the axes of the ellipse.

### The decomposition

Let $M$ be a real $T\times N$ matrix. The matrix $M^{\top}M$ is symmetric and positive semidefinite, since $\boldsymbol\beta^{\top}M^{\top}M\boldsymbol\beta=\lVert M\boldsymbol\beta\rVert^2\ge0$ for every $\boldsymbol\beta$. By the spectral theorem of [[LM11]], it has orthonormal eigenvectors $\mathbf v_1,\dots,\mathbf v_N$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1\ge\dots\ge\lambda_N\ge0$. Set $s_k:=\sqrt{\lambda_k}$ and, when $s_k>0$, $\mathbf u_k:=M\mathbf v_k/s_k$. These vectors are orthonormal:

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf u_j^{\top}\mathbf u_k&=\frac{\mathbf v_j^{\top}M^{\top}M\mathbf v_k}{s_js_k}=\frac{\lambda_k\,\mathbf v_j^{\top}\mathbf v_k}{s_js_k}\\&=\begin{cases}1, & j=k,\\ 0, & j\ne k.\end{cases}\end{aligned}$$

When $s_k=0$, $\lVert M\mathbf v_k\rVert^2=\lambda_k=0$, so $M\mathbf v_k=\mathbf 0$. Because the $\mathbf v_k$ form an orthonormal basis, $M=\sum_kM\mathbf v_k\mathbf v_k^{\top}$, and only the terms with $s_k>0$ survive.

> [!theorem] Singular value decomposition
> Let $M$ be a real $T\times N$ matrix, with singular values $s_1\ge s_2\ge\dots\ge0$ (the square roots of the eigenvalues of $M^{\top}M$) and orthonormal eigenvectors $\mathbf v_k$ of $M^{\top}M$. Then
> $$M=\sum_{s_k>0}s_k\,\mathbf u_k\mathbf v_k^{\top},\qquad \mathbf u_k=M\mathbf v_k/s_k,$$
> where the $\mathbf u_k$ are orthonormal in $\mathbb R^T$ and the number of positive $s_k$ is $\operatorname{rank}M$. When $\operatorname{rank}M=N$, stacking the pieces gives the **thin SVD** $M=USV^{\top}$: $U$ is $T\times N$ with $U^{\top}U=I_N$, $S=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_N)$ and $V$ is orthogonal.

The number of positive $s_k$ is the rank, because $M\boldsymbol\beta=\sum_{s_k>0}s_k(\mathbf v_k^{\top}\boldsymbol\beta)\,\mathbf u_k$ ranges over the span of those $\mathbf u_k$ (Strang 2016, ch. 7).

> [!example] The SVD of a $2\times2$ matrix {#svd22}
> Compute the SVD of the matrix $M$ above by hand.
>
> > [!solution]
> > First,
> > $$M^{\top}M=\begin{pmatrix}20&8\\8&32\end{pmatrix},$$
> > with trace 52 and determinant 576. By [[LM11]]'s $2\times2$ formula, the eigenvalues are $26\pm\sqrt{26^2-576}=26\pm10$: $\lambda_1=36$ and $\lambda_2=16$, so $s_1=6$ and $s_2=4$.
> >
> > For $\lambda_1=36$ the first row of $M^{\top}M-36I$ is $(-16,8)$, so $\mathbf v_1=(1,2)^{\top}/\sqrt5$. Orthogonality and the sign rule give $\mathbf v_2=(2,-1)^{\top}/\sqrt5$. Then $\mathbf u_1=M\mathbf v_1/6=(2,1)^{\top}/\sqrt5$ and $\mathbf u_2=M\mathbf v_2/4=(1,-2)^{\top}/\sqrt5$.
> >
> > Check: $\lvert\det M\rvert=\lvert16+8\rvert=24=s_1s_2$, because the orthogonal factors have determinants $\pm1$.

### Principal components from the data matrix

Apply the SVD to the centred data matrix, scaled by $1/\sqrt T$:

$$\frac{\tilde X}{\sqrt T}=USV^{\top}\;\Longrightarrow\;\hat\Sigma=\frac{\tilde X^{\top}\tilde X}{T}=VS^2V^{\top}. \tag{12.5}$$

Compare [[LM11]]'s $\hat\Sigma=Q\Lambda Q^{\top}$. The eigenvalues are the squared singular values, $\lambda_k=s_k^2$. When the eigenvalues are distinct, the right singular vectors are the principal directions, $\mathbf v_k=\mathbf q_k$ under the same sign rule. The SVD also gives the score series: $\tilde X\mathbf v_k=\sqrt T\,s_k\mathbf u_k$. This route never forms $\tilde X^{\top}\tilde X$, which matters for accuracy.

### Conditioning

For a matrix of full column rank, the **condition number** is $\kappa(M):=s_1/s_N$. On a symmetric positive definite matrix the singular values are the eigenvalues, so $\kappa$ agrees with [[LM11]]'s $\lambda_1/\lambda_N$. Because $\tilde X^{\top}\tilde X=T\,VS^2V^{\top}$,

$$\kappa(\tilde X^{\top}\tilde X)=\kappa(\hat\Sigma)=\frac{s_1^2}{s_N^2}=\kappa(\tilde X)^2. \tag{12.6}$$

The normal equations work with $\tilde X^{\top}\tilde X$, so they square the condition number. Floating-point arithmetic carries about 16 significant digits ([[LM2]]), so rounding changes a matrix by a relative amount of order $10^{-16}$. By [[LM11]]'s bound (11.9), such a change in a positive definite matrix moves the solution by a relative amount of at most about $\kappa\times10^{-16}$. Hence, as a rule of thumb, a condition number $\kappa$ can cost about $\log_{10}\kappa$ of the 16 digits.

`np.linalg.lstsq` works on the matrix it is given and returns that matrix's singular values (NumPy reference). A call on $\tilde X$ therefore never squares $\kappa(\tilde X)$ by forming $\tilde X^{\top}\tilde X$. When the residual is small, the accuracy of its slopes depends on $\kappa(\tilde X)$ rather than on $\kappa(\tilde X)^2$.

## Rank Deficiency, Numerical Rank and the Pseudoinverse {lo=4,7}

### More coefficients than returns

December 2008 has 22 daily returns, and the design $(\mathbf 1\ X)$ has 29 columns. Its rank is at most 22, and NumPy finds exactly 22, so by (9.7) of [[LM9]] the null space has dimension $29-22=7$. The columns then span all of $\mathbb R^{22}$: every response, TAIEX or not, is fitted exactly. R-squared is 1 by arithmetic, not by evidence.

The normal equations then have infinitely many solutions, because adding a vector of the null space changes no fitted value. They form [[LM9]]'s particular solution plus null space, and software needs a rule to pick one.

### The pseudoinverse

> [!theorem] Minimum-norm least squares
> Let $M$ be a real $T\times N$ matrix with the SVD of Section 4, and let $\mathbf y$ be in $\mathbb R^T$. Define the **pseudoinverse**
> $$M^{+}:=\sum_{s_k>0}\frac{1}{s_k}\,\mathbf v_k\mathbf u_k^{\top}. \tag{12.7}$$
> In matrix form $M^{+}=VS^{-1}U^{\top}$, where $U$, $S$ and $V$ keep only the positive singular values and their vectors. Then $M^{+}\mathbf y$ solves the normal equations $M^{\top}M\boldsymbol\beta=M^{\top}\mathbf y$, and every other solution has a larger norm.

The vector $M^{+}\mathbf y$ solves the normal equations. Transposing the theorem of Section 4 gives $M^{\top}=\sum_{s_k>0}s_k\mathbf v_k\mathbf u_k^{\top}$, and the orthonormal $\mathbf u_k$ then give $M^{\top}M=\sum_{s_k>0}s_k^2\mathbf v_k\mathbf v_k^{\top}$. Since the $\mathbf v_k$ are orthonormal too,

$$M^{\top}MM^{+}\mathbf y=\sum_{s_k>0}s_k\,\mathbf v_k\mathbf u_k^{\top}\mathbf y=M^{\top}\mathbf y.$$

At full column rank, $M^{+}=(M^{\top}M)^{-1}M^{\top}$ gives the unique solution of Section 2.

> [!deepdive]- Why $M^{+}\mathbf y$ has the smallest norm
> Two solutions of the normal equations differ by a vector $\mathbf x_h$ with $M^{\top}M\mathbf x_h=\mathbf 0$, so $\lVert M\mathbf x_h\rVert^2=\mathbf x_h^{\top}M^{\top}M\mathbf x_h=0$. By Section 4, $M\mathbf x_h=\sum_{s_k>0}s_k(\mathbf v_k^{\top}\mathbf x_h)\,\mathbf u_k$ vanishes exactly when $\mathbf x_h$ is orthogonal to every $\mathbf v_k$ with $s_k>0$. The vector $M^{+}\mathbf y$ is a combination of these $\mathbf v_k$, so it is orthogonal to $\mathbf x_h$, and Pythagoras gives
> $$\lVert M^{+}\mathbf y+\mathbf x_h\rVert^2=\lVert M^{+}\mathbf y\rVert^2+\lVert\mathbf x_h\rVert^2 .$$
> Every other solution is longer, and $M^{+}\mathbf y$ is the only solution orthogonal to $\operatorname{null}M$.

### Numerical rank

In exact arithmetic the rank is the number of non-zero singular values. Floating-point arithmetic keeps about 16 significant digits ([[LM2]]), so a singular value that is zero in exact arithmetic is computed as a number of order $10^{-16}s_1$. Software therefore counts the singular values above a tolerance: the **numerical rank** at tolerance $\delta$ is the number of $s_k>\delta s_1$. By default, `np.linalg.matrix_rank` uses $\delta=\max(T,N)\times2.2\times10^{-16}$, and `np.linalg.pinv` treats every $s_k\le10^{-15}s_1$ as zero (NumPy reference).

Such a tolerance answers a question about arithmetic, not about data. [[LM11]] found Electronic nearly a combination of its eight sub-industries, a relation that a tolerance near $10^{-15}$ cannot see. That near-dependence is why U28 excludes the aggregates: adding Electronic raises $\kappa(\tilde X)$ from 10.40 to 48.33, and to 642.0 in 2016 ([[ex:cond]]).

> [!example] Electronic and its eight sub-industries {#elec9}
> Standardize the daily returns of Electronic and its eight sub-industries (divisor $T$), and divide the standardized matrix by $\sqrt T$. Find its numerical rank at $\delta=0.1$ and at $\delta=0.01$, on the full sample and in 2016.
>
> > [!solution]
> > On the full sample the smallest singular value is $s_9=0.0346\,s_1$, and in 2016 it is $0.00277\,s_1$ ([[ex:rank]]). At $\delta=0.01$ the numerical rank is therefore 9 on the full sample and 8 in 2016; at $\delta=0.1$ it is 8 in both. `matrix_rank` reports 9 in every calendar year.
> >
> > Scale matters too: for the centred returns without standardizing (the covariance scale), the full-sample ratio is 0.0329 instead of 0.0346. A numerical rank needs both its tolerance and its scale.

> [!pitfall] "Full rank" is not "well determined"
> `np.linalg.matrix_rank` reports 9 for Electronic and its sub-industries in every year, yet in 2016 the smallest singular value is 0.3% of the largest. Coefficients on such nearly dependent columns move a lot when the data move a little ([[LM11]]). Report $s_N/s_1$ or $\kappa$ next to any rank, and choose $\delta$ for the precision of the data, not of the computer.

The calls of this section, on the December 2008 design:

```python
import numpy as np

# X: 22 x 28 sector returns of December 2008; y: TAIEX's 22 returns
design = np.column_stack([np.ones(len(y)), X])       # (1 X), 22 x 29
b_lstsq = np.linalg.lstsq(design, y, rcond=None)[0]   # minimum norm
b_pinv = np.linalg.pinv(design) @ y                   # the same, (12.7)
# s comes in descending order, and the rows of Vh are the v_k
U, s, Vh = np.linalg.svd(design, full_matrices=False)
print(np.linalg.matrix_rank(design))                  # 22
print(np.abs(b_lstsq - b_pinv).max() < 1e-12)         # True
```

> [!zh] 奇異值分解與數值秩
> 奇異值分解（singular value decomposition）把任何矩陣寫成「正交變換（orthogonal transformation，即旋轉或鏡射）、沿座標軸伸縮、再一次正交變換」：可逆（invertible）的 2×2 矩陣把單位圓映成橢圓，奇異值（singular value）就是橢圓的半軸長。把報酬矩陣中心化（centring）並除以 $\sqrt T$，對 $\tilde X/\sqrt T$ 做奇異值分解，樣本共變異數矩陣（sample covariance matrix）的特徵值（eigenvalue）就等於奇異值的平方；特徵值互不相同時，右奇異向量（right singular vector）就是主成分（principal component）方向。正規方程（normal equations）先算 $\tilde X^{\top}\tilde X$，會把條件數（condition number）平方，所以軟體直接在 $\tilde X$ 上計算。2008 年 12 月只有 22 個報酬，卻有 29 個係數，秩（rank）只有 22，任何反應變數（response）都能完全配適，判定係數（R-squared）等於 1 只是算術結果。擬反矩陣（pseudoinverse）從無限多組解中挑出範數（norm）最小的一組；拿掉半導體一樣完全配適，係數卻全變了。數值秩（numerical rank）取決於容許誤差（tolerance）與尺度（scale），`matrix_rank` 說「滿秩（full rank）」不代表係數估得準。

## Cholesky, Whitening and Colouring {lo=5,6}

### The $2\times2$ case

[[LM7]] classified a symmetric $2\times2$ matrix by completing the square, (7.4). Read as a factorization of a $2\times2$ covariance matrix $\hat\Sigma$, the completed square says

$$\begin{gathered}\begin{pmatrix}\hat\sigma_{11}&\hat\sigma_{21}\\\hat\sigma_{21}&\hat\sigma_{22}\end{pmatrix}=LL^{\top},\\ L=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\hat\sigma_{11}}&0\\\hat\sigma_{21}/\sqrt{\hat\sigma_{11}}&\sqrt{\det\hat\Sigma/\hat\sigma_{11}}\end{pmatrix}.\end{gathered}$$

The factor $L$ is lower triangular. Its diagonal is real and positive exactly when $\hat\sigma_{11}>0$ and $\det\hat\Sigma>0$, which is [[LM7]]'s test for a positive definite matrix.

### The factorization

> [!theorem] Cholesky factorization
> Let $M$ be a symmetric $N\times N$ matrix. Then $M$ is positive definite if and only if $M=LL^{\top}$ for a lower-triangular $L$ with positive diagonal entries, and that $L$ is unique. Proof: Deep Dive below; see also Strang (2016, §6.5) and Horn and Johnson (2013, ch. 7).

For $M=\hat\Sigma$, multiply out $LL^{\top}$. Entry $(i,j)$ with $i\ge j$ is $\sum_{p\le j}l_{ip}l_{jp}=\hat\sigma_{ij}$, and solving for the last term gives the entries column by column:

$$\begin{gathered}l_{jj}=\Big(\hat\sigma_{jj}-\sum_{p<j}l_{jp}^2\Big)^{1/2},\\ l_{ij}=\frac{1}{l_{jj}}\Big(\hat\sigma_{ij}-\sum_{p<j}l_{ip}l_{jp}\Big)\quad(i>j).\end{gathered} \tag{12.8}$$

The leading $j\times j$ block of $LL^{\top}$ uses only the leading block of $L$, so each leading principal minor of $\hat\Sigma$ is $l_{11}^2\cdots l_{jj}^2$. The pivots $d_j$ of [[LM9]] and [[LM10]], ratios of successive leading minors, are therefore $d_j=l_{jj}^2$. If $\hat\Sigma$ is only semidefinite, some $l_{jj}^2$ is zero or, in floating point, tiny or negative. December 2008's $\hat\Sigma$, 22 returns on 28 sectors, has rank 21, at most $T-1$ ([[LM10]]), and `np.linalg.cholesky` raises `LinAlgError` on it.

> [!deepdive]- Existence and uniqueness of the Cholesky factor
> Here $\mathbf x$ is a vector of $\mathbb R^N$, $K$ the leading block of $M$ of size $N-1$, $\mathbf a$ the rest of the last column and $m_{NN}$ the corner entry. The factor of $K$ is $L_K$, and $\mathbf l^{\top}$ is the last row of $L$ without its corner.
>
> *If.* When $M=LL^{\top}$ with $L$ invertible, $\mathbf x^{\top}M\mathbf x=\lVert L^{\top}\mathbf x\rVert^2>0$ for $\mathbf x\ne\mathbf 0$.
>
> *Only if, by induction on $N$.* For $N=1$ the square root of the positive entry is the only choice. For $N>1$ write
> $$\begin{gathered}M=\begin{pmatrix}K&\mathbf a\\\mathbf a^{\top}&m_{NN}\end{pmatrix},\\ L=\begin{pmatrix}L_K&\mathbf 0\\\mathbf l^{\top}&l_{NN}\end{pmatrix}.\end{gathered}$$
> The block $K$ is positive definite, so exactly one $L_K$ exists. The last column requires $\mathbf l=L_K^{-1}\mathbf a$, and the corner $l_{NN}^2=m_{NN}-\mathbf l^{\top}\mathbf l$. Take $\mathbf x$ with $-K^{-1}\mathbf a$ above a last entry 1. Positive definiteness then gives
> $$\begin{aligned}0<\mathbf x^{\top}M\mathbf x&=m_{NN}-\mathbf a^{\top}K^{-1}\mathbf a\\&=m_{NN}-\mathbf l^{\top}\mathbf l,\end{aligned}$$
> so exactly one $l_{NN}>0$ fits. $\blacksquare$

### A chain of regressions

Each column of $L$ has a regression meaning. Define $\mathbf z_t:=L^{-1}(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R})$, so that $\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R}=L\mathbf z_t$. The sample covariance of $\mathbf z_t$ is $L^{-1}\hat\Sigma L^{-\top}=I_N$: its entries are uncorrelated, with variance 1. Row $j$ of $L\mathbf z_t$ reads

$$R_{j,t}-\bar R_j=\sum_{p<j}l_{jp}z_{p,t}+l_{jj}z_{j,t}.$$

The series $z_{p,t}$ with $p<j$ span the same space as the first $j-1$ centred returns, and $z_{j,t}$ is uncorrelated with them. So the sum is the least-squares fit of asset $j$ on the assets before it, and $l_{jj}z_{j,t}$ is its residual. Each $l_{jj}$ is a residual standard deviation, and $l_{11}$ is the first asset's own.

>
> > [!solution]
> > Column 1: $l_{11}=\sqrt{2.323354\times10^{-4}}=0.0152426$ and $l_{21}=1.066603\times10^{-4}/0.0152426=0.006998$. Column 2: $l_{22}=(1.481729\times10^{-4}-0.006998^2)^{1/2}=0.009960$.

> > The ratio $l_{21}/l_{11}=\hat\sigma_{21}/\hat\sigma_{11}=0.4591$ is the slope of FinancialInsurance on Semiconductor. ### Whitening

The vector $\mathbf z_t$ is the **whitened** return. It comes from a triangular solve of $L\mathbf z_t=\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R}$, worked down the rows, not from an inverse (on inverses, see Higham 2002a, ch. 14):

$$\begin{gathered}\mathbf z_t=L^{-1}(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R}),\qquad \frac1T\sum_t\mathbf z_t\mathbf z_t^{\top}=I_N,\\ \lVert\mathbf z_t\rVert^2=(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R})^{\top}\hat\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R}).\end{gathered} \tag{12.9}$$

The middle identity is $L^{-1}\hat\Sigma L^{-\top}=I_N$, and the last uses $\hat\Sigma^{-1}=L^{-\top}L^{-1}$. The same factor solves a system with matrix $\hat\Sigma$ by two triangular solves, down the rows of $L$ and then up those of $L^{\top}$.

Since $\mathbf z^{\top}\mathbf z=\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf z\mathbf z^{\top})$ for any vector and the trace is linear,

$$\frac1T\sum_t\lVert\mathbf z_t\rVert^2=\operatorname{tr}\Big(\frac1T\sum_t\mathbf z_t\mathbf z_t^{\top}\Big)=\operatorname{tr}I_N=N,$$

exactly, in every sample, with divisor $T$. A day with $\lVert\mathbf z_t\rVert^2$ far above $N$ is unusual for the joint pattern of its returns, not only for their size. Statisticians call $\lVert\mathbf z_t\rVert^2$ the squared Mahalanobis distance ([[LM16]]). The vector $\mathbf z_t$ depends on the order of the assets, because the order changes $L$; its squared length does not, by (12.9).

### Colouring

Run backwards, the factor colours: if vectors $\mathbf z$ have identity sample covariance, then $L\mathbf z$ has sample covariance $LI_NL^{\top}=\hat\Sigma$. A standard normal draw is a random number with the density $\varphi$ of [[LM5]]. Applied to $N$ independent draws, $L$ gives a simulated vector of centred returns with covariance $\hat\Sigma$; [[LM16]] and [[LM18]] develop such simulations.

Whitening in NumPy and SciPy:

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import solve_triangular

# X5: T x 5 daily returns of S5, one row per date
Xc = X5 - X5.mean(axis=0)                    # centred returns
Sigma = Xc.T @ Xc / len(Xc)                  # covariance matrix, divisor T
L = np.linalg.cholesky(Sigma)                # lower factor
Z = solve_triangular(L, Xc.T, lower=True).T  # whitened returns, (12.9)
print((Z**2).sum(axis=1).mean())             # 5 up to rounding: N
# scipy.linalg.cholesky(Sigma) returns the upper factor, L transposed
```

> [!zh] Cholesky 分解：把相關的報酬變成不相關
> 對稱矩陣（symmetric matrix）是正定（positive definite）的，若且唯若它能寫成 $LL^{\top}$，其中 $L$ 是對角線為正的下三角矩陣（lower triangular matrix），而且這個 $L$ 唯一；二階的情形就是 [[LM7]] 的配方法。$L$ 的每一行都有迴歸（regression）的意義：$l_{jj}$ 是第 $j$ 個資產對排在前面的資產迴歸後的殘差標準差（residual standard deviation）。用 $L$ 把中心化（centring）報酬解回去，就是白化（whitening）：白化後各分量互不相關、變異數為 1，$\lVert\mathbf z_t\rVert^2$ 的平均恰好等於資產個數 $N$。2025 年 4 月 7 日五個類股的 $\lVert\mathbf z\rVert^2$ 是 89.30，是 4,382 天中最大的一天；資產順序改變時 $\mathbf z$ 會變，長度不變，而漲跌幅限制讓這個數字低估了衝擊。反過來用 $L$ 乘上互不相關、變異數為 1 的亂數，就是著色（colouring），可以模擬出共變異數（covariance）為 $\hat\Sigma$ 的報酬，留待 [[LM16]] 與 [[LM18]] 處理。

- Least squares is a projection: the residual is orthogonal to every column, (12.1), and $\Pi$ is symmetric and idempotent with trace $N$, (12.2). With an intercept, R-squared is $\cos^2\theta$, (12.4).
- TAIEX on the 28 sector indices of U28 fits the last 250 returns with R-squared 0.99912, because an accounting identity nearly holds. The coefficients are the window's best constant mix; Semiconductor's drifted from 0.189 in 2009 to 0.554 in 2026.
- The SVD follows from the spectral theorem for $M^{\top}M$. For data $\lambda_k=s_k^2$, (12.5), and the normal equations square the condition number, (12.6).
- With more coefficients than returns, every response fits exactly. The pseudoinverse (12.7) picks the minimum-norm solution; a numerical rank depends on its tolerance and scale.
- A positive definite $\hat\Sigma$ has a unique Cholesky factor (12.8), a chain of regressions. Whitened returns (12.9) have identity covariance and mean squared length $N$.

## Solutions {-}

1. **B** is correct. The columns of $\Pi$ lie in $\operatorname{col}X$, so $\Pi$ has rank $N<T$ and is singular: it sends every residual to $\mathbf 0$. A holds by the transpose rule, and C is (12.2).

2. **C** is correct. With rank 22 equal to the number of rows, the columns span $\mathbb R^{22}$, so every response fits exactly. A fails because the exact fit is not unique: [[eg:dec08eg]] finds two exact fits that disagree. B is an out-of-sample claim that no in-sample fit can support.

3. **A** is correct. A Cholesky factor exists exactly when the matrix is positive definite. Matrix A has $4>0$ and $\det=20-4=16>0$; its factor has rows $(2,0)$ and $(1,2)$. Matrix B is positive semidefinite but singular, $\det=36-36=0$, so the recursion gives $l_{22}=0$, not a positive number. Matrix C is indefinite, $\det=4-9=-5$, so $l_{22}^2$ would be negative.

4. The means are 0.5 and 1.5, so $\tilde{\mathbf x}=(-1.5,-0.5,0.5,1.5)^{\top}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf y}=(-1.5,-0.5,-0.5,2.5)^{\top}$. Then $\hat\beta=6/5=1.2$ and $\hat\alpha=1.5-1.2\times0.5=0.9$. The fitted values are $(-0.3,0.9,2.1,3.3)^{\top}$ and the residuals $(0.3,0.1,-1.1,0.7)^{\top}$: they sum to zero, and $\mathbf x^{\top}\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=-0.3+0-1.1+1.4=0$. R-squared is $1-1.8/9=0.8$, and as a squared cosine $6^2/(5\times9)=0.8$.

5. Since $\mathbf x^{\top}\mathbf x=2$, $\Pi=\mathbf x\mathbf x^{\top}/2$ has $\tfrac12$ in the four entries of its leading $2\times2$ block and zeros elsewhere. Multiplying out confirms $\Pi^2=\Pi$, and the trace is $\tfrac12+\tfrac12=1$, the number of columns. The projection is $\Pi\mathbf y=(2,2,0)^{\top}$, and the residual $(1,-1,2)^{\top}$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf x$, since $1-1+0=0$.

6. Here $M^{\top}M$ has rows $(13,6)$ and $(6,4)$, trace 17 and determinant 16, so its eigenvalues are $8.5\pm7.5$: $s_1=4$ and $s_2=1$. The first row of $M^{\top}M-16I$ is $(-3,6)$, so $\mathbf v_1=(2,1)^{\top}/\sqrt5$. The sign rule turns $(1,-2)^{\top}/\sqrt5$ into $\mathbf v_2=(-1,2)^{\top}/\sqrt5$. Then $\mathbf u_1=M\mathbf v_1/4=(1,2)^{\top}/\sqrt5$ and $\mathbf u_2=M\mathbf v_2=(-2,1)^{\top}/\sqrt5$. Check: $\lvert\det M\rvert=4=s_1s_2$, and $\kappa(M)=4$.

7. The second equation is twice the first, so the solutions are $(5,0)$ plus any multiple of the null vector $(-2,1)$. The one orthogonal to $(-2,1)$ is $(1,2)$, with norm $\sqrt5$. The matrix $M^{\top}M$ has eigenvalues 25 and 0, so $s_1=5$ and $\mathbf v_1=\mathbf u_1=(1,2)^{\top}/\sqrt5$. By (12.7), $M^{+}=\mathbf v_1\mathbf u_1^{\top}/5$, and $M^{+}\mathbf y=(1,2)^{\top}$.

8. The eigenvalues are $\lambda_k=s_k^2$: 5.876, 1.819, 1.316, 0.874 and 0.485, all times $10^{-4}$. Then $\kappa(\tilde X)=0.024240/0.006964=3.481$ and $\kappa(\hat\Sigma)=3.481^2=12.12$. Since $\log_{10}12.12=1.08$, the normal equations might lose about one digit, and [[ex:cond]] finds a gap below $10^{-14}$.

9. By (12.8), $l_{11}=\sqrt{0.040425}=0.20106$, $l_{21}=-0.002866/0.20106=-0.014254$ and $l_{22}=(0.018214-0.014254^2)^{1/2}=0.134204$. The ratio $l_{21}/l_{11}=\sigma_{12}/\sigma_1^2=-0.0709$ is the slope of 00679B's returns on 0050's in [[LM7]]'s sample. The entry $l_{22}$ is 00679B's volatility once the part explained by 0050 is removed: 13.42% against its own 13.50%.

10. With 0050 first, $z_1=-0.30/0.20106=-1.492$ and $z_2=(0.10-0.014254\times1.492)/0.134204=0.587$, so $\lVert\mathbf z\rVert^2=2.57$. With 00679B first, the factor has $l_{11}=\sqrt{0.018214}=0.134959$, $l_{21}=-0.002866/0.134959=-0.021236$ and $l_{22}=(0.040425-0.021236^2)^{1/2}=0.199935$. Then $z_1=0.10/0.134959=0.741$ and $z_2=(-0.30+0.021236\times0.741)/0.199935=-1.422$, and $\lVert\mathbf z\rVert^2=2.57$ again: the entries differ, the squared length does not, as (12.9) requires.

11. R-squared is 0.99912. The three slope vectors agree to better than $10^{-14}$, and $\kappa(\tilde X)=17.04$ and $\kappa(\tilde X^{\top}\tilde X)=290.2$, as in [[eg:cond28]].

12. The smallest yearly R-squared is 0.99800, in 2021. At $\delta=0.01$ the numerical rank of Electronic and its sub-industries is 9 in 2009, 2010 and 2018 and 8 in the other 15 years. In 2010 the ratio $s_9/s_1$ is 0.01004, just above the tolerance, so a slightly larger $\delta$ would give 8. `matrix_rank` gives 9 in all 18: its default tolerance cannot see that the nine series are nearly dependent.

## Glossary {-}

| Term | 中文 | Meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Least squares | 最小平方法 | Choosing coefficients that minimize the sum of squared residuals. |
| Column space | 行空間 | All combinations $X\boldsymbol\beta$ of the columns of $X$. |
| Normal equations | 正規方程 | $X^{\top}X\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=X^{\top}\mathbf y$, (12.1): the residual is orthogonal to every column. |
| Projection matrix | 投影矩陣 | $\Pi$, (12.2): symmetric, idempotent, with trace equal to the number of columns. |
| Residual | 殘差 | $\hat{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=\mathbf y-\hat{\mathbf y}$, the part of the response the fit misses. |
| R-squared | 判定係數 | Share of the centred response's squared length that a fit with an intercept reproduces, (12.4). |
| Singular value decomposition | 奇異值分解 | $M=\sum_{s_k>0}s_k\mathbf u_k\mathbf v_k^{\top}$ with orthonormal $\mathbf u_k$ and $\mathbf v_k$ and singular values $s_1\ge s_2\ge\dots\ge0$; at full column rank, $M=USV^{\top}$. |
| Condition number | 條件數 | $\kappa(M)=s_1/s_N$; forming $M^{\top}M$ squares it, (12.6). |
| Numerical rank | 數值秩 | Number of singular values above $\delta s_1$, for a stated tolerance $\delta$. |
| Null space | 零空間 | All vectors that a matrix maps to $\mathbf 0$. |
| Pseudoinverse | 擬反矩陣 | $M^{+}=\sum_{s_k>0}\mathbf v_k\mathbf u_k^{\top}/s_k$, (12.7); $M^{+}\mathbf y$ is the minimum-norm least-squares solution. |
| Cholesky factorization | Cholesky 分解 | $\hat\Sigma=LL^{\top}$ with $L$ lower triangular with a positive diagonal, (12.8). |
| Whitening | 白化 | $\mathbf z_t=L^{-1}(\mathbf R_t-\bar{\mathbf R})$, (12.9): uncorrelated entries with variance 1. |
| Colouring | 著色 | $L\mathbf z$: vectors with identity covariance turned into vectors with covariance $\hat\Sigma$. |
