- Binary bet: gain b and loss a per unit staked (book-wide), so f* = p/a − q/b, break-even probability π = a/(a+b), risk ρ = f·a. The old ratio b := G/L disappears. ## Reviewer's findings (verbatim, condensed formatting)

**Critical**
1. PP15 solution (l.752), "positive for this positively skewed bet": the +2%/−1% bet at p=0.5 is symmetric about its mean (±1.5%), so its skewness is 0; pG³−qL³ = 3.5×10⁻⁶ is positive only because the mean is positive — rewrite as "positive because the bet's mean is positive". Example "Adding moments" (l.478), "small positive skewness (0.037) and so m₃>0": m₃ = 4.712×10⁻⁷, of which the central (skew) part is 0.697×10⁻⁷ (15%) and 3μ̂s² is 4.009×10⁻⁷ (85%); m₃ would be positive even with zero skew.

**Major**
2. l.128 says the 284 days with |f*R|>10% are "why the exact optimum differs from the rule", but normal returns with the same μ̂ and s would have about 399 such days (13.7%) and their exact optimum is 5.6514, essentially the rule's 5.6516. The real cause is excess kurtosis (8.12). l.585 ("because the ±10% days fatten 0050's tails") and l.480 ("those days make the fourth moment large") overstate the two ±10% days: they supply only 26% of m₄; without them the rule still overshoots, 5.916 against 5.744 (75% of the full 0.23 gap), and excess kurtosis is still 5.97.
3. Under the module's own definition (l.80 counts kinks as critical points), l.166 "a strictly concave function has at most one critical point" and glossary l.763 "a critical point is a global maximum" are false: h(x)=min(x,2x)−x² is strictly concave with critical points at 0 (a kink) and ½, maximum at ½. Add "differentiable".
4. "2.7 to 8.6" (l.39, 562, 588, 593, 673) uses the quadratic rule. Shifting the mean to μ̂±1.96 SE and solving the exact condition gives 2.72 to 7.62. Then at l.571, against a true value of 7.62, 5.42 is λ=0.71 and keeps about 92%, not λ=0.63 and 86%.
5. PP10 solution (l.742): "each extra unit of permitted leverage would add about 2 percentage points" is wrong; the unconstrained maximum is only 0.2 pp above the corner (5.0% vs 4.8%), and +0.1 of leverage adds exactly 0.15 pp. Restate as a marginal rate: 0.02 per unit at f=0.8; a cap above 1 adds nothing.
6. G, L are the bet's gain and loss in outcome 4, overview l.21, the §3 KC, §5, the Summary, PP5, PP15, the Glossary, two 導讀 boxes and the widget in src/lm04.js — clashing with G = growth factor and with LM3 (3.10)–(3.11), where L is the fund's multiple. b := G/L (l.335) is also an interval endpoint (§4, §6) and a coefficient in Example 2 (l.136). π is both the break-even probability (l.322) and the profit π(x) (l.134). r is the financing rate (Example 10) and the IRR (PP7 and a pitfall), while LM4 itself uses r_f at l.118. D is both the domain (l.68, 180) and the relative entropy D(p‖π) (l.384). μ̂ is never defined in the notation note. The l.59 note is inaccurate.
7. Exhibit 8 plots every 5th window (436 of 2,174), so its visible range is 0.99 (18 Jan 2024) to 8.50 (12 Sep 2018), while the text (l.564) cites 0.94 (17 Jan 2024) to 8.87 (13 Aug 2018). Keep the extremes in the plotted series.
8. Exhibit 4 omits 2014 (+2.37%, f*=7.97) and 2026 to 8 Oct (+78.71%, f*=8.80); rows 2015–2025 compound to +447.8%, not the +902.25% in the last row. Label it "full calendar years 2015–2025", as LM3 does.
9. The Sharpe ratio is never defined in LM1–4 (l.118 should add "zero risk-free rate"); skewness and kurtosis are taught in LM19; the 1.96 interval in LM20; the intermediate value theorem (l.189, 414) is not in LM2 or LM3 — state it next to the extreme value theorem. Introduce each briefly with a pointer.
