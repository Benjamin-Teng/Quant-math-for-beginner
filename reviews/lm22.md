All checks used my own code on the raw TR23 CSV (5,850 closes, $T=5{,}849$; $\bar R=0.062505\%$, $s_{\mathrm u}=1.208468\%$, t-stat 3.955678) and on U28 through `u28()`. I imported no author script. ### C1: confirmed; kept critical

**Experiment 18, reproduced.** It gives 0 of 10,000 hits. The null law of t has SD 1.084791 and a largest $\lvert t\rvert$ of 3.9148.

**Independent run.** I drew 1,000,000 stationary resamples ($\ell=145$, centred $R$, seed `[2222, 1]`, own vectorized draws):

- 190 hits, so $p=0.000190\pm0.000014$.
- The exact 95% interval is 0.000164 to 0.000219.
- A normal reading of the null SD gives $2\Phi(-3.9557/1.0848)=0.00027$.
- The chance of zero hits in 10,000 at $p=0.00019$ is 0.15, so experiment 18's outcome is unremarkable.
- The one-sided 95% bound after zero hits is $1-0.05^{1/B}=2.996/B=0.0003$.

**Refutation attempt.** Under (22.4)'s literal definition at $B=10{,}000$ the printed p-value is 0, which is below $1/B$. But the module itself treats (22.4) as an estimate of an ideal quantity: it gives the estimate a Monte Carlo SE, and E1 and the §2 KC make the same distinction. So the sentence states a bound on the ideal p-value, and that bound is false by 13 Monte Carlo SEs. It also teaches the rule "zero hits gives $p<1/B$".

**Severity.** Critical by the review prompt's definition (a reader learns a wrong number and a wrong rule). The 5%-level decision is unchanged, so the editor may grade it major.

**L338.** The iid null SD is 0.013045, so 0.160615 lies 12.3 SEs out and the conclusion holds. The fix removes the faulty rule there as well.
