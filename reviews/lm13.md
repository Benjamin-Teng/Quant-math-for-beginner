## Adversarial check of the major findings

- **M1: confirmed.** For every event $A$, $\emptyset\cap A=\emptyset$ and $\Pr(\emptyset\cap A)=0=\Pr(\emptyset)\Pr(A)$. So the empty event is disjoint from every event and independent of it. To refute the finding, one could argue that in TR23's empirical model every non-empty event has probability at least 1/5,849. That covers only the data, though, and the summary bullet restates the general definition (13.6). The module itself makes the condition the point of its §4 pitfall, so the bullet contradicts lines 24, 236, 244 and 250.
- **M2: confirmed.** In the stated model, with a 10% prior and 0.55 against 0.5, $\Pr(\text{good})=0.505$ and $\Pr(\text{two good})=0.1\times0.3025+0.9\times0.25=0.25525\ne0.505^2=0.255025$. Equivalently, $\Pr(\text{good}_2\mid\text{good}_1)=0.50545$. So the months are dependent, as the reviewer says, and the stated assumption contradicts the model the solution uses. The printed numbers are right: likelihood ratio 1.406409, posterior odds 0.156268, posterior 13.515%. They rest on independence within each type. A charitable reading attaches "independent" to each manager. Even so, the module defines independence only under $\Pr$, so the literal reading is the one a reader learns from. The fix keeps every number.

The sentence names the fallacy "after the court", while the name refers to a prosecutor's argument.
