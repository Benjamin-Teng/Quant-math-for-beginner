## Reviewer's findings (verbatim, condensed formatting)

### Critical

- **1.** §7, Example cap, L594/L603 (also L568, L602): the text calls f1+f2≤2 "a cap on gross exposure", then says "allowing a 1% short in 00679B would add about 0.17 points." f1+f2 is net exposure; it equals gross only when both are long. ν2 = 0.166 prices the short only because a net cap then lets 0050 rise to 2.01 (re-solved: f = (2.01, −0.01), +0.166 pt). Under a true gross cap |f1|+|f2|≤2 the optimum stays at (2,0), so the gain is 0; moving to (1.99, −0.01) loses 0.127 pt. "Cap only" (4.58, −2.58) also has gross exposure 7.17.

### Major

- **2.** Exhibit kkt (L607–616), L597 "enumerates the candidates", L621 "each rejected one fails a different test": only 5 of the 7 consistent active sets are listed. Missing: f1≥0 only → (0, −1.40), ν1 = −0.224, infeasible, 3.28%; f1≥0 and f2≥0 → (0,0), feasible, ν1 = −0.2275 < 0 (ν2 = +0.0255), 1.50%. Three of the four listed rejections fail the same test (feasibility). Add both rows and reword the pitfall; lab cell 25 has the same omission.
- **3.** L335 "the constraints are linear, so a candidate is the global answer" is false for Example budget (L366–376), whose constraint fᵀΣf = σ_T² is quadratic: the Lagrange conditions give two candidates ±(0.274, 2.038) with λ = ±4.15, and the solution never rejects the minimizer. State the λ>0 choice, or pose the budget as fᵀΣf ≤ σ_T² (a convex problem).
- **4.** L231 and L756 say third- and fourth-order terms ("fat tails") cost two points of growth. At full Kelly the 202.0 bp gap splits into 34.8 bp from the dropped squared daily mean, ½A·mean(R_p)², and 167.2 bp from higher-order terms (3rd +49.5, 4th −187.2, 5th and above −29.5). In the unlevered rows the whole gap is the squared-mean term (0050: +1.21 bp; higher-order −0.24 bp).
- **5.** f is both a generic function (§§2–5, the KCs, PP1–6, (7.13)) and the Kelly exposures f1, f2, **f** (§§3, 6–8); g is both a constraint (L320–333, (7.13), "∇V=λ∇g" in Exhibit levels) and log growth (L209; g_{f1} at L599) — in Example cap the objective is g and the variables are f, the reverse of (7.13)'s letters; V is variance and the value function V(c) (L347); r = r_f/A (L207) though the book reserves r for log returns; A is never defined (L207/209); T is the sample size, σ_T the target volatility and w^T the tangency weights — at L530 `f* = L*w^T` reads as a transpose; D is the domain, det H, D_V and D_u. Consistent: λ, ν, 𝓛, `L* = f1*+f2*`, w and **w**.
- **6.** Three formulas are neither derived nor labelled "stated without proof" (covariance first appears in LM16): the portfolio-variance formula (L73); Cov(R1, w1R1+w2R2) = w1σ1²+w2σ12 (L88); Var(R1−R2) (L411). Expanding the sample variance of w1R1+w2R2 derives them in two lines. Smaller gaps: eigenvalues for 0.0179/0.0408 (L676), though the 2×2 closed form would do; matrix–vector products in Σ⁻¹(μ−r_f**1**) (L529) and H⁻¹∇g (L680); **1** and **μ** are never defined; the deep dive uses a 3×3 determinant (L382).
